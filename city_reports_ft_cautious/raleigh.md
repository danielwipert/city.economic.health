# Raleigh-Cary

**Grade: A (Very Good) | 71.0th percentile | November 2026**

---

Raleigh-Cary has earned an overall grade of A, with a score of 71.0 out of 100, making it a very good location for businesses, driven in part by its strong labor demand, with employment growth of 2.3% over the past year. **Labor Demand**
The city is adding jobs at a rate of 2.3% over the past year, with workers' hours also rising by 1.0%, indicating a strong labor market with genuine demand for workers. This means that businesses are likely to face competition for talent, but also have access to a growing and engaged workforce.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 2.7% makes it harder for employers to hire, as there are fewer available workers, but it also indicates a strong and stable economy. With a score of 94 out of 100, Raleigh-Cary has lower unemployment than most metros, making it a challenging but desirable market for employers.

**Wage Growth**

This slower wage growth may make it easier for businesses to manage costs, but could also impact worker purchasing power and attraction.

**Cost of Living**
Housing in Raleigh-Cary is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $213 and hourly pay of $38.12, and it's getting even more affordable as prices have fallen by 3.2% over the past year, earning a score of 80 out of 100. This makes the city an attractive location for workers and businesses looking for a cost-effective place to live and operate.

**Labor Force Growth**
The pool of workers in Raleigh-Cary is growing at a rate of 0.9% over the past year, with a score of 80 out of 100, indicating a relatively fast expansion of the workforce, which is good news for businesses looking to hire. This growth in the labor force provides a steady supply of potential employees, making it easier for companies to find the talent they need.

This decline in building permits may lead to a more competitive housing market, making it harder for workers to find affordable housing and potentially impacting the city's attractiveness to businesses.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Raleigh-Cary are taking 64 days to sell, which is getting slower, with a 6.7% increase over the past year, earning a score of 18 out of 100, indicating a softer housing market. This slower pace of home sales may give buyers more time to find a home, but it also suggests a less competitive market, which could impact the city's appeal to relocating workers and businesses.

**Office Economy**
Raleigh-Cary has a large and thriving office-based workforce, with a score of 98 out of 100, making it an ideal location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. The city's strong office economy provides a deep talent pool and a network of like-minded businesses, making it an attractive location for companies looking to establish or expand their operations.

Raleigh-Cary's biggest strength is its strong labor demand and low unemployment rate, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to hire and grow. However, the city's biggest risk is the decline in building permits, which may lead to a future housing shortage and impact the city's affordability and attractiveness to workers and businesses.
