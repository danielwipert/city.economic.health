# Raleigh-Cary

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 81.8th percentile | August 2026**

---

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking in the 81.8th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 9.41, and its highly developed office economy, ranking in the 98th percentile. The combination of a 2.64% employment growth rate and a 1.410% weekly hours deviation from trend underscores the city's strong job market.

**Labor Demand**
The Raleigh-Cary metro area boasts a 2.64% employment growth rate and a 1.410% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a surge in job creation, with hours worked above the trend line, suggesting a healthy and growing labor market. The labor demand composite score of 9.41 further reinforces this assessment, placing the city in the top tier.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Raleigh-Cary stands at 3.10%, ranking in the 94th percentile, which indicates a tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is relatively small, and wage pressure may be higher. However, this also suggests a strong local economy with a high level of job security.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in Raleigh-Cary is 3.78%, ranking in the 52nd percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. While this is not exceptionally high, it still suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, and worker purchasing power is increasing. This moderate wage growth may help balance the need for businesses to attract and retain talent while managing labor costs.

**Cost of Living**
Raleigh-Cary has a cost of living score ranking in the 86th percentile, with a PSF of $217/sqft and average hourly earnings of $37.84, resulting in a ratio of 5.73. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The fact that PSF is decreasing by 2.2% year-over-year further enhances the city's affordability.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Raleigh-Cary is growing at a rate of 0.37% year-over-year, ranking in the 58th percentile. While this growth rate is not exceptionally high, it still indicates that the labor force supply is expanding, albeit slowly. This moderate growth suggests that businesses may face some challenges in finding new talent, but the overall labor market remains relatively stable.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Raleigh-Cary has increased by 31.25% year-over-year, ranking in the 86th percentile. This significant increase suggests that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. As a result, the city is likely to remain an attractive location for workers and businesses alike.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Raleigh-Cary is 54 days, with a year-over-year increase of 3.9%, ranking in the 62nd percentile. This indicates a relatively competitive market, but one that is still accessible for relocating workers. The rising days on market suggests a healthy normalization of the housing market, rather than a sign of demand erosion.

**Office Economy**
Raleigh-Cary has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking in the 98th percentile. This makes the city an ideal location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. However, it may be less suited for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented economies.

The Raleigh-Cary metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a strong labor market, moderate wage growth, and relatively low cost of living, making it an attractive location for talent and investment. However, the tight labor market and potential for wage pressure may pose a risk for businesses looking to hire and expand in the area, and decision-makers should carefully consider these factors when evaluating the city as a potential location.
