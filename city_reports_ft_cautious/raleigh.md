# Raleigh-Cary

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 72.0th percentile | September 2026**

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 72.0th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. **Labor Demand**
The Raleigh-Cary metro area exhibits a strong labor demand, with a 2.23% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.207% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a significant increase in job creation, with workers also putting in more hours, suggesting a thriving economy. The labor demand composite score of 8.63 further reinforces this, placing the city in the top tier.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Raleigh-Cary stands at 3.20%, which is in the top tier, ranking it in the 84th percentile. This low unemployment rate signifies a tight labor market, making it more challenging for businesses to hire new employees. As a result, companies may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain top talent.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in Raleigh-Cary is 3.16%, which is below average, ranking it in the 38th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies that labor costs for employers are increasing, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is also growing, albeit not as rapidly as in other metros. This could be a consideration for businesses looking to manage labor expenses.

**Cost of Living**
Raleigh-Cary has a cost of living score that ranks it in the 76th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $215/sqft to $38.60/hr, or 5.57. The fact that the PSF is falling by 2.3% year-over-year relative to wages makes the city more affordable compared to its peers. This affordability advantage can be a significant talent attraction factor, as businesses may not need to offer substantial wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Raleigh-Cary is growing at a rate of 0.48% year-over-year, which is above average, ranking it in the 68th percentile. This expansion in the labor force supply suggests that the city's hiring capacity is increasing, providing businesses with a larger pool of potential employees. However, the growth rate is not exceptionally high, indicating a moderate increase in workforce availability.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Raleigh-Cary has decreased by 5.68% year-over-year, which is below average, ranking it in the 36th percentile. This decline in residential building permits may signal a tightening of the housing supply, potentially leading to decreased affordability and increased competition for workforce accommodation in the future.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Raleigh-Cary is 59 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.3%. This places the city near the median in terms of housing market speed, ranking it in the 52nd percentile. For workers relocating to this city, the market is relatively accessible, with homes not selling excessively quickly, providing a reasonable window for finding suitable housing.

**Office Economy**
Raleigh-Cary boasts a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking it in the 98th percentile. This makes the city an ideal location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. Conversely, it may be less suited for industries with primarily industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Raleigh-Cary metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, a deep professional talent pool, and relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for corporate investment. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the potential tightening of the housing supply due to the decline in building permits, which could impact future affordability and workforce accommodation.
