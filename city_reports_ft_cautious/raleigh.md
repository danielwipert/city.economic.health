# Raleigh-Cary

**Grade: A (Very Good) | 71.0th percentile | November 2026**

---

Raleigh-Cary earns an overall grade of A, with a strong economy driven by 2.3% job growth over the past year. The city's labor market is thriving, with low unemployment and a large office-based workforce. This makes it an attractive location for businesses and workers alike.

**Labor Demand**
The city saw 2.3% job growth over the past year, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a strong chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 2.7%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring challenging for employers due to a tight labor market.

**Wage Growth**
Pay grew 3.2% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers face relatively stable labor costs, but workers have less bargaining power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.6 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, priced at $213/sqft, down 3.2% from last year, making it more affordable than most metros. This attracts workers and helps businesses retain talent.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 0.9% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers have a growing pool of potential hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 22.7% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze and rising prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 64 days to sell, up 6.7% from last year, indicating a softer market. This gives buyers more time to find a home, but may slow down relocating workers.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce, better than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which thrive in this environment.

The city's biggest strength is its thriving labor market, driven by strong job growth and low unemployment. However, its biggest risk is a potential housing supply squeeze, which could drive up prices and make it harder for workers to afford homes.
