# San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 50.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has a below-average overall grade of B-, with 50.3 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by strong hiring, with 1.3% employment growth over the past year. This growth is accompanied by a 0.5% increase in weekly hours, indicating genuine demand for labor.

**Labor Demand**
The city has 1.3% job growth and 0.5% higher weekly hours, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job with decent hours.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.7%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for businesses to staff, but also indicates weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 7.8% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers enjoy more purchasing power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 13.4 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, at $583/sqft and $43.46/hr pay, less affordable than most metros. This makes it harder to attract and keep workers, especially with prices only slightly decreasing.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking 0.8% over the past year, slower than most metros. This creates a structural headwind for hiring, making it harder for businesses to find new employees.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are down 16.7% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This signals a potential supply squeeze, putting future affordability and workforce attraction at risk.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 48 days to sell, up 2.1% from last year, about average. This indicates a relatively balanced housing market, but with homes selling slightly slower, which can be good for buyers.

**Office Economy**
The city has a larger office-based workforce than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which can tap into a deep knowledge-economy talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong wage growth, driven by a tight labor market. However, its biggest risk is the high cost of living, which can make it difficult to attract and retain workers, especially if housing prices do not decrease significantly.
