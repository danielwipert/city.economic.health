# Cleveland

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 52.6th percentile | November 2026**

---

Cleveland has a B- overall grade, with a score of 52.6 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by its slow wage growth, with pay rising only 1.8% over the past year. This is a concern for workers and employers alike.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 0.6% more jobs over the past year, with weekly hours also rising 0.6%, about average for most metros. This means people looking for work have decent chances, and employers can find talent.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.4%, about average for most metros. This makes hiring neither very easy nor very hard, with a balanced job market.

**Wage Growth**
Pay grew 1.8% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers have lower labor costs, but workers have less bargaining power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 4.2 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, with homes costing $143/sqft and pay at $34.42/hr, about average for most metros. This means the city is neither a standout nor a laggard in attracting workers with its affordability.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 1.8% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers have a growing pool of potential hires to choose from.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 6.8% over the past year, about average for most metros. This signals that future housing supply might not keep up with demand, potentially driving up prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 55 days to sell, up 7.8% from last year, a healthier housing market than most metros. This means people moving to the city have a reasonable time to find a home, and the market is not too tight.

**Office Economy**
The city has a larger office-based workforce than most metros, with a strong presence of tech, finance, and professional services. This suits businesses that need specialized knowledge-economy talent.

Cleveland's biggest strength is its fast labor force growth, giving employers a growing pool of potential hires. However, its biggest risk is slow wage growth, which can make it hard for workers to afford the rising cost of living.
