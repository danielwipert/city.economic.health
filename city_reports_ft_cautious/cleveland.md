# Cleveland

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 52.6th percentile | November 2026**

---

Cleveland, with an overall grade of B- and a composite score ranking at the 52.6th percentile among 50 US metros, is characterized by its near-median labor demand and below-average wage growth. The city's economic character is most defined by its labor force growth, which is in the top tier at 92nd percentile with a 1.80% year-over-year increase, and its wage growth, which is below average at 20th percentile with a 1.81% year-over-year change. These metrics suggest a city with a growing workforce but struggling to keep pace with wage increases.

**Labor Demand**
Cleveland's employment growth rate is 0.57% year-over-year, combined with a 0.558% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, signaling a genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a growing economy. However, the near-median percentile rank of 58th suggests that this growth is not exceptionally strong compared to other metros.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Cleveland is 3.40%, which is near the median and suggests a relatively balanced labor market. This rate indicates that the market is neither extremely tight nor overly slack, implying that businesses may face moderate challenges in hiring but without extreme wage pressure. A business trying to hire in Cleveland can expect a manageable but not highly competitive hiring environment.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 1.81%, Cleveland is experiencing below-average wage increases, ranking at the 20th percentile. This slow wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies weaker bargaining power for workers and potentially less purchasing power. This could be a mixed bag for businesses, offering a flat cost environment but also potentially affecting consumer demand.

**Cost of Living**
Cleveland's cost of living, with a PSF of $143/sqft and an earnings ratio of $34.42/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.15, places it near the median in terms of affordability, ranking at the 50th percentile. However, the 13.5% year-over-year increase in PSF suggests that the city is becoming less affordable. This means that while Cleveland may not require significant wage premiums currently, its attractiveness for talent without such premiums may decrease if affordability continues to decline.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Cleveland is growing at a rate of 1.80% year-over-year, which is in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This significant expansion in the labor force supply indicates that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to find talent. This is a positive sign for hiring capacity and future workforce needs.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -6.79%, indicating a contraction in housing supply. This decrease suggests that the future affordability of housing in Cleveland may be at risk, potentially impacting the city's ability to attract and accommodate a growing workforce. This could become a structural headwind for businesses looking to relocate or expand in the city.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Cleveland are currently sitting on the market for 55 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.8%. This increase in days on market, placing the city above the average at the 60th percentile, suggests a slowing market. For workers relocating to Cleveland, this could mean a more accessible and less competitive housing market, potentially easing the transition for new hires.

**Office Economy**
With an above-average share of professional and office workers, ranking at the 62nd percentile, Cleveland has a deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. This makes the city an attractive location for knowledge-economy businesses but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

In conclusion, Cleveland offers businesses a growing labor force and a currently manageable hiring environment, but it also presents challenges such as below-average wage growth and decreasing affordability. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker to factor in is the potential future affordability issue due to the contraction in housing supply, which could impact both the attractiveness of the city to talent and the overall cost of living.
