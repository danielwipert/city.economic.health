# Cleveland

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 47.9th percentile | August 2026**

---

Cleveland earns an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 47.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 4.66 ranking it in the 36th percentile, and its stagnant wage growth, with a year-over-year change of -0.32% ranking it in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. These metrics suggest a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

**Labor Demand**
Cleveland's employment growth rate is 0.57% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.437% from its own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The negative hours deviation suggests that businesses may be absorbing the workload with existing staff rather than hiring new employees.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Cleveland is 3.40%, ranking it in the 64th percentile, which indicates a relatively tight market. This means that businesses may face challenges in finding and hiring qualified workers, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight labor market also suggests that there may be upward pressure on wages.

**Wage Growth**
Cleveland's year-over-year wage growth is -0.32%, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This stagnant wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are not increasing significantly, but it also means that workers have weak bargaining power and limited purchasing power. This could impact the overall attractiveness of the city to businesses and workers alike.

**Cost of Living**
Cleveland's cost of living is near the median, with a percentile rank of 50th, and a price-to-salary ratio of $143/sqft to $33.82/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.23. The city's cost of living is not particularly advantageous or disadvantageous, but the 13.5% year-over-year increase in PSF suggests that affordability may be decreasing. This could impact the city's ability to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Cleveland is growing at a rate of 1.80% year-over-year, ranking it in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This expanding labor force supply suggests that businesses may have an easier time finding qualified workers, and the city may be more attractive to businesses looking to hire.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Cleveland is increasing by 0.56% year-over-year, ranking it near the median at the 54th percentile. This moderate growth in housing supply suggests that the city's affordability and workforce accommodation may improve in the future, but the slow pace of growth may not keep up with demand.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market in Cleveland is 55 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.8%, ranking it in the 72nd percentile. This suggests that the housing market is slowing down, and workers relocating to the city may find it more accessible. However, the relatively slow pace of sales may also indicate a less competitive market.

**Office Economy**
Cleveland's professional and office worker share is 3.04, ranking it in the 62nd percentile. This suggests that the city has a relatively deep talent pool in professional and office sectors, making it suitable for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. However, it may be less suited for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

In conclusion, Cleveland offers businesses a relatively tight labor market and a growing labor force, but its low labor demand and stagnant wage growth pose significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited labor demand, which may impact businesses' ability to expand or relocate.
