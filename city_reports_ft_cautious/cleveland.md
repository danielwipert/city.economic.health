# Cleveland

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 50.7th percentile | October 2026**

---

Cleveland earns an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 50.7th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its near-median labor demand and low wage growth, with a labor demand composite score of 5.27 and wage growth of +0.88% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile. These metrics suggest a mixed economic environment, with some positive trends but also areas of concern.

**Labor Demand**
Cleveland's employment growth rate is +0.57% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.170%, indicating a near-median labor demand composite score. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit a modest one, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This suggests that the city is experiencing some level of economic expansion, but not at a rapid pace.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Cleveland is 3.40%, ranking above average at the 60th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. However, this also suggests that the local consumer demand is relatively strong.

**Wage Growth**
Wage growth in Cleveland is +0.88% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies that workers have limited bargaining power and purchasing power. This could impact the overall attractiveness of the city to businesses and workers alike.

**Cost of Living**
Cleveland's cost of living is near median, with a percentile rank of 50th, and a PSF-to-earnings ratio of $143/sqft to $34.26/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.17. The city's PSF is increasing by +13.5% year-over-year, which may impact affordability. This means that the city is neither particularly affordable nor expensive relative to its peers, and businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to attract talent.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Cleveland is growing at a rate of +1.80% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 94th percentile, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive trend suggests that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to hire and expand their operations.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Cleveland is decreasing by -0.61% year-over-year, ranking near median at the 48th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This trend signals that the city's housing market may become less affordable in the future, potentially impacting workforce accommodation and attraction.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market in Cleveland is 55 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.8%, ranking above average at the 64th percentile. This suggests that the housing market is becoming less competitive, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, this trend may also indicate a slowing demand for housing.

**Office Economy**
Cleveland's professional and office worker share is 2.86, ranking above average at the 60th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses in these sectors, but less suitable for industries with limited specialized roles, such as industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

In conclusion, Cleveland offers a mixed economic environment with some positive trends, such as labor force growth and a deep professional talent pool. However, the city's low wage growth and tightening housing supply are significant concerns that businesses should factor into their location decisions, as they may impact labor costs, talent attraction, and overall competitiveness.
