# Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 73.6th percentile | November 2026**

---

The city of Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos earns an overall grade of A+, with a score of 73.6 out of 100. This city's economy is defined by strong job growth, with a 1.1% increase in employment over the past year. The city's weekly hours are also up 0.9%, indicating a high demand for labor.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is 1.1% over the past year, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job, and employers are hiring at a strong pace.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.7%, about average for the 50 metros. This makes hiring neither very easy nor very hard, with a balanced labor market.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 5.4% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see their paychecks increase.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 6.0 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, at $226 per square foot and $37.91 per hour, more affordable than most metros. This attracts and keeps workers, as the cost of living is getting better, with home prices down 8.5% over the past year.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is growing 1.0% over the past year, faster than most metros. This helps employers find the workers they need, as the labor pool is expanding.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 15.3% over the past year. This signals a growing housing supply, which should improve affordability and help keep prices in check.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 84 days to sell, up 6.3% from a year ago. This is a weaker housing market, making it harder for people to move here and find a home quickly.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is larger than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which can tap into a deep talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong labor demand, driven by high job growth and rising weekly hours. However, its biggest risk is a softening housing market, with homes taking longer to sell, which could make it harder for people to relocate to the city.
