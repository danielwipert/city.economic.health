# Boston-Cambridge-Newton

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 45.0th percentile | October 2026**

---

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it at the 45.0th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor force growth of 2.87% YoY and its high building permits growth of 72.27% YoY, indicating a expanding workforce and housing supply. The labor demand composite score of 4.13, however, suggests a more nuanced economic environment.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of -0.12% YoY and weekly hours deviation of -0.351% from the city's own trend signal a contraction in labor demand, indicating a lack of genuine demand expansion. This combination suggests that the city is experiencing a slowdown in job creation, which may lead to a surplus of workers. The labor demand composite score of 4.13, ranking at the 26th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 3.90% is relatively low, ranking at the 26th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with some slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, this means that it may be moderately challenging to find qualified candidates, and wage pressure may be present. However, the market is not extremely tight, allowing for some flexibility in hiring.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of 2.93% is moderate, ranking at the 32nd percentile. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not extremely quickly, and worker purchasing power is increasing at a steady pace. While this is not ideal for employers looking to minimize labor costs, it is beneficial for workers and the local economy.

**Cost of Living**
The cost of living in Boston-Cambridge-Newton, with a PSF of $459/sqft and an earnings ratio of 10.02, ranks at the 22nd percentile, indicating that the city is relatively expensive. The fact that PSF is falling by 3.0% YoY is a positive sign, but the overall affordability score suggests that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of 2.87% YoY, ranking at the 98th percentile, indicating a rapidly expanding workforce supply. This growth suggests that the city has a strong hiring capacity, with a large and increasing pool of potential employees. This is a significant advantage for businesses looking to hire in the area.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in building permits is 72.27%, ranking at the 94th percentile, indicating a significant expansion in housing supply. This growth suggests that the city is experiencing an increase in developer confidence, which will lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market is 43 days, with a year-over-year increase of 19.4%, ranking at the 98th percentile. This suggests that the housing market is slowing down, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, the relatively low days on market still indicates a competitive market, and relocating workers may face some challenges in finding housing.

**Office Economy**
The share of professional and office workers in the city is 2.70, ranking at the 56th percentile, indicating a moderately deep talent pool. This city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors, but may not be ideal for industries that require a large amount of industrial or logistics workers.

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area offers a unique combination of a strong and growing labor force, expanding housing supply, and a moderately deep professional talent pool. However, the city's relatively high cost of living and tight labor market may pose significant challenges for businesses looking to hire and attract talent without offering wage premiums, making it essential for decision-makers to carefully consider these factors when evaluating this location.
