# Boston-Cambridge-Newton

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 44.1th percentile | August 2026**

---

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 44.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. **Labor Demand**

This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the small increase in employment is accompanied by a decrease in hours worked. **Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.90%, ranking in the below-average tier at the 34th percentile. This indicates a relatively tight labor market with some slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire in this city. As a result, businesses may face some wage pressure when trying to attract and retain talent.

The year-over-year wage growth in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 2.89%, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. At the same time, workers in the city are experiencing a modest increase in purchasing power.

This rapid expansion of the labor force supply suggests that businesses have a large and growing pool of potential workers to draw from, making it easier to hire and staff operations.

**Building Permits**

This surge in building permits indicates that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodation options for the workforce in the future.

This rising days on market, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile, suggests a slowing market that is becoming more accessible for relocating workers. However, the relatively fast pace of sales still indicates a competitive market.

**Office Economy**
Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a deep professional talent pool, ranking in the near-median tier at the 58th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that require a high concentration of office workers. However, it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a growing labor force and expanding housing supply, making it an attractive location for companies looking to hire and staff operations. However, the city's high cost of living and moderate wage growth rate pose significant challenges for businesses trying to attract and retain talent without offering substantial wage premiums, which is the single biggest risk or constraint that decision-makers should factor in when considering this location.
