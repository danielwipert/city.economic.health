# Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 57.2th percentile | September 2026**

---

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 57.2th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the 74th percentile, and its top-tier wage growth, at 82nd percentile with a 5.78% year-over-year increase. These metrics signal a city with genuine job market expansion and rising labor costs.

**Labor Demand**
The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has seen employment growth of 1.11% year-over-year and a 0.294% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a sign of a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 6.15 further reinforces this, placing the city in the 74th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford stands at 4.70%, which ranks in the 12th percentile, indicating a relatively high level of unemployment compared to other metros. This suggests that the job market has some slack, making it easier for businesses to hire, but it also implies weaker local consumer demand. Businesses looking to staff up may find a more accessible labor pool here compared to tighter markets.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 5.78%, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is experiencing fast wage growth, ranking it in the 82nd percentile. This rapid increase in wages signals rising labor costs for employers but also means workers have increasing purchasing power, which can be beneficial for local businesses relying on consumer spending.

**Cost of Living**
Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a cost of living score that ranks it in the 78th percentile, with a PSF of $223/sqft and average hourly earnings of $33.52, resulting in a ratio of 6.65. The fact that PSF is falling by 2.6% year-over-year contributes to the city's affordability. This makes the city more attractive for talent without needing significant wage premiums, as the cost of living is relatively more affordable compared to its peers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is growing at a rate of 0.22% year-over-year, indicating a slowly expanding workforce supply. While this growth is positive, its slow pace may present a structural headwind for hiring, especially if businesses are looking to rapidly expand their operations.

**Building Permits**
The city has seen a year-over-year decrease in building permits of 11.73%, ranking it in the 28th percentile. This decline suggests that the housing supply is not expanding as quickly as demand, potentially leading to a supply squeeze and future affordability issues for the workforce.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford are currently sitting on the market for a median of 75 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 2.6%. This relatively fast market, ranking in the 64th percentile, means that homes are selling quickly, which can be challenging for relocating workers to find accommodation.

**Office Economy**
With an office and professional worker share ranked in the 90th percentile, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses in these sectors but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand and top-tier wage growth, making it an attractive location for companies looking to tap into a growing and skilled workforce. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential future affordability and housing supply issues signaled by the decline in building permits, which could impact the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce.
