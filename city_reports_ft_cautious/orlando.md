# Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 57.2th percentile | September 2026**

---

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 57.2th percentile among 50 US metros. The combination of these metrics suggests a city with a robust job market and rising labor costs.

**Labor Demand**
The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has seen employment growth of 0.80% year-over-year and weekly hours 0.783% above its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a sign of a healthy and growing economy. **Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford stands at 4.70%, ranking it in the 12th percentile, indicating a relatively loose labor market. This means that businesses may find it easier to hire in this city, as there is a larger pool of available workers. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand compared to cities with tighter labor markets.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 6.12%, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is experiencing fast wage growth, ranking it in the 90th percentile. This rapid wage growth will lead to rising labor costs for employers but will also increase worker purchasing power, benefiting the local economy. Businesses should factor in these increasing labor costs when considering relocation or expansion in this city.

**Cost of Living**
Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a cost of living score in the 71st percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $223/sqft to $34.05/hr, or 6.55. The civilian labor force in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is growing at a rate of 0.22% year-over-year, indicating a slowly expanding workforce supply. While this growth is positive, its slow pace may pose a structural headwind for hiring in the long term, as the labor pool is not expanding rapidly.

This decrease suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This trend may signal a potential risk for businesses relying on a readily available and affordable housing stock for their employees.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford are currently sitting on the market for a median of 75 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 2.6%. This relatively fast pace of home sales, combined with the decrease in days on market, indicates a competitive housing market that may be challenging for relocating workers. However, the market is not extremely heated, suggesting some accessibility for those looking to move to the area.

**Office Economy**
With an office economy score ranking it in the 90th percentile, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a deep talent pool in professional and office sectors. This makes the city particularly well-suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ operations, but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

In conclusion, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the tightening housing supply, signaled by the decline in building permits, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.
