# Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 60.2th percentile | October 2026**

---

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 60.2th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the 72nd percentile, and its top-tier wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 6.12%. These metrics signal a city with a robust job market and rising earnings.

**Labor Demand**
The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has seen employment growth of 0.80% year-over-year and a deviation of weekly hours 0.783% above its own 12-month trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours worked are increasing, indicating a strong and growing labor market. This suggests that businesses can expect to find a talented and available workforce.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford stands at 4.60%, which ranks in the 24th percentile, indicating a relatively loose labor market. This means that there is some slack in the market, making it easier for businesses to hire, but it also implies weaker local consumer demand. For a business trying to hire, this market offers a relatively accessible pool of potential employees.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 6.12%, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is experiencing fast wage growth, ranking it in the 90th percentile. This rapid increase in wages implies rising labor costs for employers but also means that workers have strong purchasing power, which can be beneficial for businesses relying on local consumer spending.

**Cost of Living**
Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a cost of living score ranking it in the 71st percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $222/sqft to $34.05/hr, or 6.52. Given that the PSF is falling by 3.1% year-over-year, this city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. This affordability is a talent attraction advantage, as businesses can attract workers without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is growing at a rate of 0.38% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the hiring capacity for businesses is likely to remain strong, as more workers are entering or remaining in the labor market.

**Building Permits**
The city has seen a year-over-year decrease in residential building permits of 15.65%, ranking it in the 22nd percentile. This decline signals that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This trend may pose a risk to businesses relying on a readily available and affordable housing supply for their employees.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford are currently sitting on the market for a median of 77 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 2.5%. This relatively fast market, ranking in the 61st percentile, means that homes are selling quickly, which can be competitive for workers relocating to the city. However, this also indicates a healthy and active real estate market.

**Office Economy**
With an office and professional worker share ranking it in the 90th percentile, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. This city is well-suited for knowledge-economy businesses but may be less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

In conclusion, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion and top-tier wage growth, making it an attractive location for companies looking to tap into a talented and relatively affordable workforce. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, signaled by the sharp decline in building permits, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.
