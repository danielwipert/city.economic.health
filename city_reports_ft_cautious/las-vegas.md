# Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas

**Grade: B (Average) | 59.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Las Vegas earns a B grade, with an overall score of 59.8. The city's economy is defined by strong job growth, with a 1.7% increase over the past year. This growth is a notable strength, outpacing most metros.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 1.7% more jobs over the past year, with weekly hours rising 1.1%. This means people looking for work have a better chance of finding a job, and employers are hiring at a strong pace.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.8%, near the middle of the 50 metros. This makes hiring neither extremely easy nor difficult, with a balanced labor market.

**Wage Growth**
Hourly earnings rose 3.6% over the past year, about average for the metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see modest pay gains.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 7.8 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $258/sqft and pay at $32.90/hr. This affordability is about average, making it neither a major draw nor a deterrent for workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 0.6% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers may face challenges finding new hires, with a shrinking labor pool.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 4.9% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze, which could drive up prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 59 days to sell, up 1.7% from last year. This slightly slower pace indicates a relatively healthy housing market, with buyers having more time to find a home.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce, better than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which can tap into a deep talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong job growth, driven by a diverse economy. However, its biggest risk is a shrinking labor force, which may limit future hiring and growth.
