# Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas

**Grade: B (Average) | 59.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Las Vegas earns a B grade, with an overall score of 59.8. The city's economy is defined by strong job growth, with a 1.7% increase over the past year. This growth is driven by a diverse range of industries.

**Labor Demand**
The city sees 1.7% job growth and 1.1% more weekly hours, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.8%, near the middle of the 50 metros. This makes hiring a bit challenging, but not extremely difficult.

**Wage Growth**
Pay grew 3.6% over the past year, about average for the metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see some pay raises.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 7.8 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $258/sqft and pay at $32.90/hr, and prices are falling. This makes the city more attractive for workers, as affordability is improving.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 0.6% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This makes it harder for employers to find new hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 4.9% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply shortage, which could drive up prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 59 days to sell, up 1.7% from last year. This indicates a slightly softening housing market, giving buyers a bit more time to find a home.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce, better than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which can tap into a deep talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong job growth, driven by a diverse range of industries. However, its biggest risk is a shrinking workforce, which could make it harder for employers to find new hires.
