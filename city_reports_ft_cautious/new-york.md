# New York Newark-Jersey City

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 38.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of C, indicating a very poor economic performance. The city's labor demand is weak, with employment growth of only 0.2% over the past year. This slow growth is a major concern for the local economy.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is 0.2% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means people looking for work may struggle to find jobs, and employers may not need to compete as hard for talent.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.5%, about average for the 50 metros. This makes hiring neither very easy nor very hard, as there are still workers available but not an oversupply.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 1.8% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers' labor costs are not rising quickly, but workers' paychecks are not growing much either.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 12.3 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, at $498/sqft and $40.54/hr pay, making the city less affordable than most metros. This makes it harder for the city to attract and keep workers, especially as housing costs are high.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking 0.5% over the past year, about average for the 50 metros. This means employers may face some challenges hiring, as the labor pool is not expanding.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 20.7% over the past year, stronger than most metros. This signals that future housing supply will increase, which could help make the city more affordable.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 53 days to sell, down 1.9% from a year ago, indicating a weaker housing market. This means people moving to the city may find it easier to buy a home, but the market is not very healthy.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is about average for the 50 metros. This suits businesses that need a mix of professional and other skills, but may not be ideal for those seeking a deep tech or finance talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong growth in new home approvals, which could help address affordability concerns. However, its biggest risk is its weak labor demand, which may make it hard for people to find jobs and for employers to grow their businesses.
