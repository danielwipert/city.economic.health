# San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 47.1th percentile | November 2026**

---

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has a C+ overall grade, with a score of 47.1 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by its strong wage growth, with pay rising 5.6% over the past year. This is a key factor in the city's economic landscape.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is 0.3% over the past year, about average for most metros. This means employers are hiring, but not at a rapid pace.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.4%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for employers to find workers, but also indicates weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 5.6% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means higher labor costs for employers, but more money in workers' paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 12.4 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $630/sqft and pay at $50.70/hr, making it less affordable than most metros. This makes it harder to attract and keep workers, especially with prices only falling 3.5% over the past year.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking 1.4% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers will face a structural headwind when trying to hire.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 183.3% over the past year, stronger than most metros. This signals a future increase in housing supply, which could improve affordability.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 38 days to sell, down 9.5% from last year, making it a weaker housing market. This means relocating workers may struggle to find a home, as the market is too tight.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is about average for most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which can tap into a deep talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong wage growth, driven by a highly skilled workforce. However, its biggest risk is its high cost of living, which makes it hard to attract and keep workers, and may drive up labor costs for employers.
