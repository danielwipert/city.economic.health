# San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 47.1th percentile | November 2026**

---

The city earns a C+ grade. San Francisco's economy is defined by strong wage growth, with pay rising 5.6% over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 0.3% more jobs over the past year. Workers' hours rose 0.5%, indicating a stable job market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.4%. This makes it harder for employers to hire, as the labor market is relatively tight.

**Wage Growth**
Pay rose 5.6% over the past year. This means higher labor costs for employers, but more money in workers' paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
Housing costs $630 per square foot, down 3.5% from last year, with hourly pay at $50.70. The city is less affordable than most metros.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 1.4% over the past year. This slows hiring, as the labor pool is decreasing.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals rose 183.3% over the past year. This signals a strong increase in future housing supply.

**Days on Market**
Homes sell in 38 days, down 9.5% from last year. The housing market is too tight, making it hard for buyers to find homes.

**Office Economy**
The city has an average share of office-based jobs. This suits businesses like tech, finance, and professional services.

The city's biggest strength is its strong wage growth. Its biggest risk is a shrinking labor force, making it harder for businesses to hire and grow.
