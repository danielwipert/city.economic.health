# San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 47.1th percentile | November 2026**

---

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has a C+ overall grade, with a score of 47.1 out of 100. This city's economy is defined by strong wage growth, with pay rising 5.6% over the past year. The area's high cost of living is a notable challenge.

**Labor Demand**
Job growth is 0.3% over the past year, about average for most metros. This means employers are hiring, but not at a rapid pace.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.4%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for employers to find workers, but also indicates weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 5.6% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see bigger paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 12.4 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $630/sqft and pay at $50.70/hr. This high cost of living makes it hard to attract and keep workers, and it's getting slightly more affordable as prices fall 3.5% over the past year.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking 1.4% over the past year, slower than most metros. This makes it harder for employers to find new hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 183.3% over the past year. This signals a strong increase in future housing supply, which should improve affordability.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 38 days to sell, down 9.5% from last year. This fast pace makes it tough for relocating workers to find a home, indicating a too-tight housing market.

**Office Economy**
The office-based workforce is about average for most metros. This suits businesses that need a mix of professional and other skills.

The city's biggest strength is its strong wage growth, driven by a highly skilled workforce. However, its biggest risk is the high cost of living, which can make it hard to attract and retain workers.
