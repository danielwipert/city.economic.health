# Louisville-Jefferson County

**Grade: D (Emergency) | 20.8th percentile | August 2026**

---

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 20.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 2.99. The city's economic character is most defined by its low employment growth rate of -0.07% and high unemployment rate of 5.20%, indicating a challenging labor market. These metrics suggest that the city is experiencing a contraction in labor demand.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of -0.07% and weekly hours deviation of +1.215% signal a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a decline in job creation, despite workers putting in slightly more hours. This is a concerning sign for businesses looking to expand or relocate to the area.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 5.20% is relatively high, indicating a labor market with some slack. This means that businesses may find it easier to hire workers, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. With an unemployment rate ranking in the 2nd percentile, the market is not tight, and businesses may have an advantage in terms of hiring and wage negotiations.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of +1.61% is below average, ranking in the 20th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power is also not increasing significantly. This may be a concern for businesses looking to attract and retain top talent.

**Cost of Living**
The city's cost of living, with a PSF of $178/sqft and an earnings ratio of 5.69, ranks in the 47th percentile, indicating a near-median affordability score. This means that the city is neither extremely affordable nor expensive, and businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to attract talent. However, the stable YoY change in PSF suggests that affordability is not improving.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of +0.08% year-over-year, indicating a slowly expanding workforce supply. This modest growth rate suggests that hiring capacity is not severely constrained, but businesses may still face challenges in finding skilled workers.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in building permits is -31.66%, indicating a sharp decline in housing supply expansion. This signals that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market is 43 days, with a year-over-year increase of +10.3%, ranking in the 94th percentile. This suggests that the housing market is slowing down, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, this may also indicate a weakening demand for housing.

**Office Economy**
The share of professional and office workers is 0.38, ranking in the 4th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool. This suggests that the city is not well-suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies. Instead, the city may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area offers businesses a labor market with some slack and moderate wage growth, but also faces challenges such as low employment growth and a declining housing supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand and limited talent pool, which may make it difficult to attract and retain skilled workers.
