# Louisville-Jefferson County

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 27.8th percentile | September 2026**

---

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 27.8th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a labor demand composite score of 2.99, and its slow wage growth, at +1.61% year-over-year. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is -0.07% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +1.215%. This combination signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, rather than genuine demand expansion. This is a concern for businesses looking to hire, as it may indicate a lack of job creation and a tight labor market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is 4.80%, ranking it in the 34th percentile. This indicates a market with some slack, but not excessively so. For a business trying to hire, this means that while it may not be extremely difficult to find workers, there is still some competition for talent, and wages may be subject to upward pressure.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in Louisville-Jefferson County is +1.61%, which is below average. This slow wage growth means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. This could impact the local consumer demand and the overall economic vitality of the area.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living ratio of $178/sqft to $31.29/hr, Louisville-Jefferson County ranks in the 39th percentile for affordability. This means that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which could make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is rising by +0.6% year-over-year relative to wages further exacerbates this issue.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Louisville-Jefferson County is growing at a rate of +0.25% year-over-year, which is above average. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire. However, the slow labor force growth rate may not be enough to keep up with demand, potentially leading to some labor market tightness.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Louisville-Jefferson County has decreased by -31.66% year-over-year. This sharp decline signals that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This is a concern for businesses that may need to attract workers from other areas.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Louisville-Jefferson County is 44 days, with a year-over-year increase of +12.8%. This ranks in the top tier, indicating a slower market. For a worker relocating to this city, this means that they may have more time to find a home and negotiate a better price, making the city more accessible to newcomers.

**Office Economy**
The share of professional and office workers in Louisville-Jefferson County is 0.38, ranking it in the 4th percentile. This indicates a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors, making the city less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, the city may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area offers a relatively slow-growing economy with some challenges in labor demand and affordability. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the tight labor market and the potential for future affordability issues, which could impact the ability to attract and retain talent. Despite these challenges, the city's expanding labor force and slower housing market may provide some opportunities for businesses that can navigate these complexities.
