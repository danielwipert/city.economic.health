# Salt Lake City-Murray

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 77.6th percentile | November 2026**

---

Salt Lake City-Murray earns an overall grade of A+, with a strong economy driven by 2.7% job growth over the past year. The city's labor market is thriving, with employment growth outpacing most metros. This growth is fueled by a diverse range of industries.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 2.7% more jobs over the past year, with weekly hours slightly below average. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job, and employers are hiring at a strong pace.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.2%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring a bit challenging, as there are fewer available workers, and employers may face upward pressure on wages.

**Wage Growth**
Hourly earnings rose 4.9% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers enjoy higher paychecks and increased purchasing power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 6.5 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, priced at $260 per square foot, with wages at $39.78 per hour. This affordability is a strength, making the city attractive to workers and allowing employers to offer competitive wages without breaking the bank.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 1.2% over the past year, outpacing most metros. This expansion provides a steady supply of potential employees for businesses looking to hire.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals jumped 25.9% over the past year, a strong increase. This surge in building permits signals a growing housing supply, which should help keep prices in check and make the city more attractive to relocating workers.

**Days on Market**
Homes typically take 63 days to sell, with sales slowing 6.8% over the past year. This slower pace indicates a softer housing market, making it slightly easier for buyers to find a home, but potentially worrying for sellers.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce, surpassing most metros. This makes it an ideal location for businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which require a deep pool of skilled workers.

The city's biggest strength is its thriving labor market, driven by strong job growth and a diverse range of industries. However, the biggest risk is the slowing housing market, which may make it harder for people to buy or sell homes, potentially affecting the city's attractiveness to relocating workers.
