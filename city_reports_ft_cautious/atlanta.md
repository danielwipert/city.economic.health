# Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell

**Grade: B (Average) | 52.0th percentile | September 2026**

---

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 52.0th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low unemployment rate of 3.20% and a labor force growth rate of 0.88% YoY. The city's economic character is most defined by these two metrics, indicating a tight labor market with an expanding workforce. Specifically, the unemployment rate and labor force growth rate suggest a city with a strong job market and a growing pool of potential employees.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate in Atlanta is -0.11% YoY, combined with a weekly hours deviation of +0.454% from its own 12-month baseline, signaling a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This indicates that while there may not be significant job growth, existing workers are taking on more hours, suggesting some level of demand but not genuine expansion. The labor demand composite score of 3.69 falls in the bottom tier, at the 12th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Atlanta is 3.20%, which ranks in the top tier at the 84th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city may face significant challenges in finding available workers, potentially leading to wage pressure. The low unemployment rate suggests that workers have considerable bargaining power.

**Wage Growth**
Wage growth in Atlanta is +2.99% YoY, which is near the median, ranking at the 42nd percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not at an alarming rate, and worker purchasing power is increasing, though not dramatically. This balance could be beneficial for businesses looking to manage labor costs while still attracting and retaining talent.

**Cost of Living**
Atlanta has a cost of living score that ranks in the 67th percentile, with a PSF of $195/sqft and average hourly earnings of $37.03/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.27, which is above average. However, the PSF is falling by -0.5% YoY, which is a key driver of its relatively high affordability score. This means that Atlanta is more affordable than many of its peer cities, which could be a significant advantage for talent attraction without needing to offer wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The labor force in Atlanta is growing at a rate of +0.88% YoY, which is in the top tier, ranking at the 82nd percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a growing pool of potential employees. This growth can help mitigate some of the challenges posed by the low unemployment rate.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Atlanta is increasing by +7.37% YoY, which ranks above average at the 60th percentile. This growth in residential building permits signals that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. This is a positive indicator for future workforce attraction and retention.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Atlanta is 59 days, with a YoY increase of +3.5%, ranking near the median at the 48th percentile. This suggests a relatively balanced market that is not overly competitive, making it accessible for workers relocating to the city. The rising DOM indicates a healthy normalization of the market rather than a sign of demand erosion.

**Office Economy**
Atlanta's professional and office worker share composite score places it near the median, at the 42nd percentile. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, though it may not be as strong as in other cities. Atlanta is well-suited for knowledge-economy businesses but may be less ideal for those requiring a large industrial or logistics workforce.

In conclusion, Atlanta offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market with an expanding workforce, moderate wage growth, and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the low unemployment rate, which could lead to significant challenges in hiring and potentially drive up labor costs. Despite this, Atlanta's growing labor force and expanding housing supply are positive indicators for its future as a business location.
