# Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood

**Grade: D (Emergency) | 29.6th percentile | October 2026**

---

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 29.6th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 4.59. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with employment growth of +0.68% and weekly hours deviation of -0.658%, as well as its stagnant wage growth of +1.41%. The combination of these metrics suggests a lack of genuine demand expansion in the labor market.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of +0.68% and weekly hours deviation of -0.658% signal a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. This combination suggests that the labor market is not strong, with fewer jobs being added and hours worked below trend. The labor demand composite score of 4.59, ranking in the 30th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 4.20% indicates a near-median labor market, neither extremely tight nor extremely slack. This rate suggests that businesses may face some challenges in hiring, but it is not an insurmountable obstacle. However, the market is not loose enough to provide a significant advantage in terms of ease of hiring.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of +1.41% is stagnant, ranking in the 16th percentile. This slow wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also means that worker purchasing power is not increasing significantly. As a result, businesses may not face strong pressure to increase wages, but workers may not have the disposable income to drive local consumer demand.

**Cost of Living**
The city's cost of living, with a PSF of $215/sqft and an earnings ratio of 6.60, ranks in the 14th percentile, indicating that it is relatively expensive. The fact that PSF is rising by 1.9% YoY relative to wages further exacerbates the affordability issue. This means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums to compensate for the high cost of living.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -7.20% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This decline in labor force growth poses a significant structural headwind for hiring, making it challenging for businesses to find and recruit talent.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in building permits is -5.72%, indicating a tightening of housing supply. This decline in permits suggests that the city's future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of housing is not expanding to meet demand.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market is 42 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.7%, ranking in the 82nd percentile. This slower market means that homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city to find housing.

**Office Economy**
The city's professional talent pool is relatively shallow, ranking in the 16th percentile. This suggests that the city is not well-suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. Instead, the city may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area offers businesses a relatively slow-paced and affordable housing market, but it is constrained by a lack of labor demand, stagnant wage growth, and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the city's limited labor pool and lack of specialized talent, which may hinder a business's ability to grow and expand in the area.
