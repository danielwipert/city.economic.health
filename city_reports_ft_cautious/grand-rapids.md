# Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood

**Grade: D (Emergency) | 26.1th percentile | November 2026**

---

The city earns a D grade, with an overall score of 26.1. Job growth is weak, at just 0.1% over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city added few jobs, 0.1% over the past year. Workers' hours fell 0.6%, a weak job market.

**Unemployment**
Unemployment is 3.7%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring harder for employers.

**Wage Growth**
Pay rose 1.2% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means lower labor costs, but weaker worker paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
Housing costs $214 per square foot, up 2.4% from a year ago, versus $32.90 hourly pay. This makes the city less affordable than most metros.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 8.5% over the past year, the weakest growth. This makes hiring even harder for employers.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 31.1% over the past year, a sharp decline. This signals a future housing supply squeeze.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 43 days to sell, up 4.9% from a year ago, a healthier market. This gives buyers more time to find a home.

**Office Economy**
The city's office workforce is smaller than most metros, a weakness. This suits industrial or logistics businesses better than tech or finance.

The city's biggest strength is its relatively low unemployment, making it attractive to workers. However, its weak job growth and shrinking workforce pose significant risks for employers.
