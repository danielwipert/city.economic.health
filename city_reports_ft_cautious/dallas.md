# Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington

**Grade: A- (Good) | 60.0th percentile | September 2026**

---

The Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area has an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking it at the 60.0th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the top tier at the 86th percentile, and its highly affordable cost of living, ranked at the 94th percentile. This combination signals a city with genuine demand expansion and a talent attraction advantage.

**Labor Demand**
The Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area has an employment growth rate of +1.26% year-over-year and a weekly hours deviation of +0.463% from its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a sign of a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 6.53 further reinforces this trend, placing the city in the top tier at the 86th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is 4.60%, which ranks at the 28th percentile, indicating a below-average level of slack in the labor market. This means that the market is relatively tight, making it harder for businesses to hire, and potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. As a result, businesses may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent in this market.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is +3.44%, which ranks near the median at the 50th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, and that workers have some, but not exceptional, bargaining power. As a result, businesses can expect to see some upward pressure on labor costs, but it is not likely to be a major constraint.

**Cost of Living**
The cost of living in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is highly affordable, with a percentile rank of 94, driven by a PSF of $199/sqft that is falling by -2.0% year-over-year, and an hourly earnings rate of $38.32. This means that the city offers a significant talent attraction advantage, as workers can afford a high standard of living without requiring large wage premiums. The ratio of PSF to earnings is 5.19, further reinforcing the city's affordability.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is growing at a rate of +0.79% year-over-year, which ranks above average at the 76th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, providing businesses with a growing pool of potential employees to hire from. As a result, businesses can expect to have a relatively easy time finding qualified workers to fill open positions.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is falling by -13.44% year-over-year, which ranks below average at the 24th percentile. This decline in permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability concerns and make it more difficult for workers to relocate to the city. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering relocation assistance or other incentives to attract top talent.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is 58 days, with no year-over-year change. This relatively fast pace of home sales suggests that the market is competitive, and workers relocating to the city may face challenges in finding affordable housing. However, the lack of change in days on market suggests that the market is not becoming significantly more competitive, which could be a positive sign for businesses looking to attract talent.

**Office Economy**
The share of professional and office workers in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is 4.18, which ranks at the 92nd percentile, indicating a deep and talented pool of workers in these sectors. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less suited for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces. As a result, businesses in these sectors can expect to find a highly qualified and talented workforce in the city.

In conclusion, the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area offers businesses a strong and growing economy, with a talented and expanding workforce, and a highly affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses in this city is the tightening housing supply, which could lead to future affordability concerns and make it more difficult to attract and retain top talent.
