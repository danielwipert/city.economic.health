# Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 63.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

The Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area earns a B+ grade, with an overall score of 63.3. This city's economy is defined by strong job growth, with a 1.0% increase over the past year. The city's weekly hours are also up 0.7%, indicating a genuine demand for labor.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is 1.0% over the past year, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.1%, about average for the 50 metros. This makes hiring neither easy nor hard, with a balanced labor market.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 4.7% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see higher paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.2 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $197/sqft and pay at $37.92/hr, making it more affordable than most metros. This attracts and keeps workers, with affordability improving as prices fall 2.5% over the past year.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 0.6% over the past year, faster than most metros. This helps employers find new hires, with a growing labor pool.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 8.4% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze, with prices potentially rising.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 64 days to sell, about the same as last year, a weaker housing market. This is a softening market, making it harder for people to move here and find a home.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is larger than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, with a deep talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong labor demand, driven by job growth and rising weekly hours. However, its biggest risk is a potential housing supply squeeze, driven by falling building permits, which could drive up prices and make it harder to attract workers.
