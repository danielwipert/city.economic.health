# Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington

**Grade: B (Average) | 51.2th percentile | July 2026**

---

The Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 51.2th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average labor demand and cost of living. **Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of 0.57% and weekly hours deviation of 0.116% above trend indicate genuine demand expansion in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area. This combination signals that the city is experiencing an increase in jobs and hours worked, suggesting a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.21, ranking in the 66th percentile, further supports this assessment.

The unemployment rate in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is 4.00%, ranking in the 36th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight market implies upward pressure on wages.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is 2.07%, ranking in the 30th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace than in many other metros. As a result, worker purchasing power is increasing, but not as rapidly as in areas with faster wage growth.

**Cost of Living**
Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington has a cost of living ranking in the 74th percentile, with a PSF of $202/sqft and hourly earnings of $36.94, resulting in a ratio of 5.47. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The affordability of the city can be a major draw for businesses looking to relocate or expand.

The civilian labor force in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is growing at a rate of 0.19% year-over-year, ranking in the 70th percentile, indicating an expanding workforce supply. However, the growth rate is not exceptionally high, and businesses may still face some challenges in finding the right talent.

This decline suggests that the city may face future affordability and workforce accommodation challenges, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand. This could lead to increased competition for housing and upward pressure on prices.

The median days on market for homes in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is 51 days, with a year-over-year increase of 2.0%, ranking in the 52nd percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced market, where homes are selling at a moderate pace. For workers relocating to the city, this means that the housing market is neither extremely competitive nor overly accessible, providing a relatively stable environment for finding a home.

**Office Economy**
The share of professional and office workers in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is 4.18, ranking in the 94th percentile, indicating a deep and talented pool of knowledge-economy workers. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which rely on specialized talent. However, it may be less suited for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area offers businesses a unique combination of genuine labor demand expansion, relatively affordable cost of living, and a deep talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, which could lead to future affordability and workforce accommodation challenges, potentially offsetting some of the city's advantages.
