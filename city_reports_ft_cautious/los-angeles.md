# Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim

**Grade: B (Average) | 55.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 55.3th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong labor demand and building permits growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 7.11, combining a 1.83% employment growth rate and a 0.253% weekly hours deviation above trend, signals genuine demand expansion. This, along with a 27.28% year-over-year increase in building permits, defines the city's current economic character.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of 1.83% and weekly hours deviation of 0.253% above trend indicate a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 7.11, ranking in the 86th percentile, further reinforces this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 4.40% ranks in the 64th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market with some slack. This rate suggests that while it may be slightly challenging for businesses to find talent, it's not excessively difficult, and wage pressure is moderate. For a business trying to hire in this market, it's essential to be competitive with salaries and benefits.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of 3.57% is near the median, ranking in the 46th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but not excessively, and worker purchasing power is relatively stable. As a result, businesses can expect to pay slightly higher wages, but the impact on their bottom line should be manageable.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living ratio of $642/sqft to $41.84/hr, ranking in the 4th percentile, Los Angeles is relatively expensive compared to its peers. This low affordability score means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums, as the high cost of living could offset the benefits of working in the area.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -1.27% year-over-year, ranking in the 24th percentile. This decline in labor force supply suggests that hiring capacity may be constrained, and businesses may face challenges in finding the talent they need. As a result, companies may need to consider alternative strategies, such as upskilling or reskilling existing employees.

**Building Permits**
The 27.28% year-over-year increase in building permits, ranking in the 88th percentile, signals that housing supply is expanding. This growth in permits suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may improve, making it easier for businesses to attract and retain talent in the area.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market is 57 days, with a 0.0% year-over-year change, ranking in the 30th percentile. This relatively fast market means that homes are selling quickly, which can make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering relocation assistance or other incentives to attract talent.

**Office Economy**
With an office/professional worker share composite score of 2.08, ranking in the 30th percentile, Los Angeles has a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses that require a deep pool of specialized office or tech talent, such as finance, consulting, or HQ decisions.

In conclusion, Los Angeles offers businesses a strong labor demand and expanding housing supply, driven by a significant increase in building permits. However, the city's high cost of living and contracting labor force supply pose significant risks, and decision-makers should carefully consider these factors when evaluating the area as a potential location. The single biggest constraint for businesses in Los Angeles may be the need to offer wage premiums to attract and retain talent, given the city's relatively low affordability.
