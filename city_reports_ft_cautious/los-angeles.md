# Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim

**Grade: B (Average) | 55.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area has an overall grade of B, with a score of 55.3 out of 100. This city's economy is defined by strong job growth, with employment increasing by 1.8% over the past year. The weekly hours worked are also up by 0.3%, indicating a genuine demand for labor.

**Labor Demand**
The city has job growth of 1.8% and rising weekly hours. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.4%, lower than most metros. This makes it harder for employers to hire, as there are fewer people looking for work.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing by 3.6% over the past year, about average for most metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see some improvement in their paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 15.3 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $642/sqft and pay at $41.84/hr. This high cost of living makes it hard to attract and keep workers, as the city is less affordable than most metros.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking by 1.3% over the past year, slower than most metros. This makes it harder for employers to hire, as the labor pool is decreasing.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up by 27.3% over the past year. This signals a strong future housing supply, which could improve affordability and attract more workers.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 57 days to sell, about the same as last year. This indicates a weak housing market, making it harder for people to move here and for owners to sell their homes.

**Office Economy**
The city has a smaller office-based workforce than most metros. This suits businesses that are not heavily reliant on professional services, tech, or finance.

The city's biggest strength is its strong job growth, driven by increasing employment and weekly hours. However, its biggest risk is the high cost of living, which makes it hard to attract and keep workers, and may drive up labor costs for employers.
