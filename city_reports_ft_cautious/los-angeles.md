# Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 48.8th percentile | July 2026**

---

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 48.8th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the top tier at the 84th percentile, and its extremely high cost of living, ranked at the 4th percentile. The labor demand is driven by a 1.83% year-over-year employment growth rate and a -0.627% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend.

**Labor Demand**
The combination of a 1.83% employment growth rate and a -0.627% deviation in weekly hours signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are not excessively above trend despite job growth. This indicates that the labor market is experiencing real growth rather than just a redistribution of work among fewer workers. The top-tier labor demand composite score of 6.23 further supports this interpretation.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 4.80%, which ranks at the 28th percentile, indicating a below-average but not extremely tight labor market. This means there is some slack in the market, making it somewhat easier for businesses to hire compared to cities with very low unemployment rates. However, the market is not so loose that it would lead to significantly weaker consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate is 4.18%, ranking near the median at the 54th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising but not at an alarming pace. At the same time, it supports worker purchasing power, contributing to a stable consumer demand environment.

**Cost of Living**
Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is extremely expensive, with a cost of living percentile rank of 4, indicating it is one of the least affordable cities. The PSF to earnings ratio is $662/sqft vs $41.42/hr, resulting in a ratio of 15.98. Although the PSF is falling by 2.5% year-over-year, the city remains very costly. This high cost of living poses a significant challenge for talent attraction, as businesses may need to offer wage premiums to compensate for the expensive living conditions.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is contracting at a rate of -1.87% year-over-year, ranking at the 8th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is shrinking. Businesses looking to expand or establish operations in this area may face challenges in finding sufficient labor.

**Building Permits**
The city is experiencing an 18.09% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking at the 80th percentile. This significant expansion in housing supply is a positive signal for future affordability and workforce accommodation, as it may help mitigate some of the pressure on housing costs and availability.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes is 50 days, with a year-over-year increase of 6.4%, ranking at the 68th percentile. This indicates a slightly slower market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as they may have more time to find suitable housing without facing extreme competition.

**Office Economy**
The share of jobs in professional and office sectors is moderate, with a composite score ranking at the 48th percentile. This suggests a decent but not exceptionally deep talent pool for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations. The city is somewhat suited for these types of businesses but may not offer the same level of specialized talent as cities with a higher concentration of professional and office jobs.

In conclusion, Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim offers a strong labor demand environment, which is a significant advantage for businesses looking to expand or establish operations. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the extremely high cost of living, which poses challenges for talent attraction and may require wage premiums to compensate for the expensive living conditions.
