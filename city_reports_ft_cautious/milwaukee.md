# Milwaukee-Waukesha

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 44.7th percentile | July 2026**

---

The Milwaukee-Waukesha metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 44.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its tight unemployment market, with a rate of 3.20% ranking in the top tier. The combination of these two metrics suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to hire and expand.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate in Milwaukee-Waukesha is -0.36% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.511% from the city's own 12-month baseline, indicating a contraction in labor demand. This combination signals a lack of genuine demand expansion, with payrolls contracting and hours below trend. The labor demand composite score of 3.80 falls in the bottom tier, reinforcing this conclusion.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Milwaukee-Waukesha is 3.20%, which ranks in the top tier, indicating a tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city will face significant competition for talent, leading to potential wage pressure. With a market this tight, hiring may be more difficult and expensive.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate in Milwaukee-Waukesha is +1.56%, which is below average, ranking in the 24th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not rapidly, and worker purchasing power is increasing, but not strongly. This may lead to a relatively stable cost environment for businesses, but with limited bargaining power for workers.

**Cost of Living**
Milwaukee-Waukesha has a cost of living score that ranks in the 20th percentile, indicating that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. The PSF to earnings ratio is $230/sqft vs $36.18/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.36, and PSF is rising 2.2% year-over-year. This means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums, as the cost of living is relatively high.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Milwaukee-Waukesha is growing at a rate of -0.14% year-over-year, indicating a slight contraction in the labor force supply. This implies that the hiring capacity for businesses may be limited, and the labor pool is not expanding rapidly. However, the 60th percentile rank suggests that this contraction is not as severe as in some other cities.

**Building Permits**
The number of residential building permits in Milwaukee-Waukesha is increasing by 9.94% year-over-year, indicating an expansion in housing supply. This suggests that developer confidence is rising, and future affordability and workforce accommodation may improve. The above-average rank of 64th percentile reinforces this positive outlook.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Milwaukee-Waukesha is 33 days, with a year-over-year increase of 13.8%, ranking in the top tier. This indicates a slower market, where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. The high percentile rank of 92nd suggests that this market is particularly buyer-friendly.

**Office Economy**
The share of professional and office workers in Milwaukee-Waukesha is 1.76, ranking in the 28th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics companies, and less suited for tech, finance, or consulting firms.

The Milwaukee-Waukesha metro area offers businesses a relatively tight labor market with moderate wage growth, but also a challenging environment for hiring and expansion due to low labor demand. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the limited labor force growth and the relatively high cost of living, which may require wage premiums to attract talent.
