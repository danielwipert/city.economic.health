# Pittsburgh

**Grade: B (Average) | 56.9th percentile | November 2026**

---

Pittsburgh earns an overall grade of B, ranking in the 56.9th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score defined largely by its low unemployment rate of 3.00% and a cost of living score that indicates affordability, with a PSF to wages ratio of 4.95. The city's labor demand and office economy metrics, however, suggest areas of concern, with employment growth at -0.54% YoY and a professional worker share ranking below average. These numbers suggest a complex economic landscape that offers both advantages and challenges for businesses considering location decisions.

**Labor Demand**
Pittsburgh's labor demand composite score is 3.17, reflecting a combination of -0.54% employment growth YoY and +0.372% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, indicating a contraction in job growth but with hours slightly above trend, which may signal a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. This scenario suggests that while there is some resilience in the workforce, the overall trend is not towards genuine demand expansion. The low labor demand score, ranking in the 12th percentile, underscores the challenges in finding and retaining talent in a market with slowing job growth.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.00%, ranking in the 96th percentile, which indicates a very tight labor market with little slack. This tight market implies that businesses may face significant challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure as a result of the low unemployment rate. For companies looking to expand or relocate, this means preparing for potentially higher labor costs and a more competitive hiring environment.

**Wage Growth**
Wage growth in Pittsburgh is +3.55% YoY, placing it near the median in the 44th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising but not at an alarming rate, while workers see an increase in their purchasing power. However, the moderate pace may not be sufficient to attract top talent from other markets with faster wage growth, potentially impacting the city's ability to draw in highly skilled workers.

**Cost of Living**
Pittsburgh's cost of living, with a PSF of $168/sqft (decreasing by -1.8% YoY) compared to average hourly earnings of $33.92, results in a ratio of 4.95, ranking in the 88th percentile for affordability. This high percentile score indicates that Pittsburgh is more affordable than most of its peer cities, making it an attractive location for talent without the need for significant wage premiums to offset living costs. The decreasing PSF YoY is a key driver of this affordability, enhancing the city's appeal for both workers and businesses.

**Labor Force Growth**
The labor force in Pittsburgh is growing at a rate of +0.22% YoY, which is near the median, ranking in the 58th percentile. This slow but positive growth indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, albeit at a modest pace. While this growth is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, the slow rate may not be sufficient to meet the demands of rapidly expanding industries or to significantly alleviate hiring pressures in a tight labor market.

**Building Permits**
Pittsburgh has seen a +26.35% YoY increase in building permits, ranking in the 86th percentile. This significant increase signals that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate workforce growth over the medium term. The strong growth in building permits is a positive indicator for future workforce attraction and retention, as it suggests that the city is actively addressing potential housing supply constraints.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Pittsburgh is 57 days, with a +5.6% YoY increase, ranking in the 52nd percentile. This indicates a moderate pace of home sales, neither extremely fast nor slow. For workers relocating to Pittsburgh, this suggests a relatively accessible housing market, though the slight increase in days on market may indicate a normalization of the market rather than a significant cooling.

**Office Economy**
Pittsburgh's professional and office worker share ranks in the 20th percentile, indicating a less deep talent pool in these sectors compared to other cities. This suggests that Pittsburgh may be less suited for businesses requiring a large pool of professional, tech, finance, or consulting talent, but could be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs, such as manufacturing or logistics. The city's economic profile is more aligned with industries that do not heavily rely on a large office economy workforce.

In conclusion, Pittsburgh offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market, moderate wage growth, and an affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for certain types of industries. However, the city's slow labor force growth, low labor demand, and less deep professional talent pool present significant challenges, particularly for businesses reliant on a large, skilled workforce. The single biggest risk for decision-makers is navigating the tight labor market and potentially limited talent pool, which could impact hiring and expansion plans.
