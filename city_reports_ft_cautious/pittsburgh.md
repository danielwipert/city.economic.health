# Pittsburgh

**Grade: B (Average) | 56.9th percentile | November 2026**

---

Pittsburgh earns an overall grade of B, with a score of 56.9 out of 100, indicating it is an average city among the 50 metros, with its low unemployment rate of 3.0% being a notable strength. The city's economy is currently defined by a mix of strengths and weaknesses, including a relatively affordable cost of living, with a price per square foot of $168 and hourly pay of $33.92, and a score of 88 out of 100 in this area.

**Labor Demand**
Pittsburgh is losing jobs at a rate of 0.5% over the past year, and workers' hours are rising by 0.4%, indicating a weak job market with remaining workers putting in extra hours. This means that employers may struggle to find new talent, but those already employed are shouldering a heavier workload.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.0%, which is lower than most metros, earning a score of 96 out of 100, making it harder for employers to hire as the labor market is tight. This low unemployment rate indicates that workers have more bargaining power, and employers may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent.

**Wage Growth**
Wages in Pittsburgh rose by 3.5% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 44 out of 100, meaning that employers' labor costs are increasing, but not as quickly as in other cities. This moderate wage growth suggests that workers' paychecks are growing, but not at a rapid pace.

**Cost of Living**
Pittsburgh has a relatively affordable cost of living, with a price per square foot of $168 and hourly pay of $33.92, earning a score of 88 out of 100, and the price per square foot is actually falling by 1.8% over the past year. This makes Pittsburgh an attractive location for workers and businesses, as the cost of living is more manageable compared to other metros.

**Labor Force Growth**
The labor force in Pittsburgh is growing at a rate of 0.2% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 58 out of 100, indicating a relatively stable workforce. This slow growth means that hiring may be moderately challenging, but the city is not experiencing a significant brain drain or influx of new workers.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals in Pittsburgh increased by 26.4% over the past year, earning a score of 86 out of 100, signaling a strong demand for housing and a potential increase in future housing supply. This surge in building permits suggests that developers are confident in the city's growth prospects and are investing in new housing stock.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Pittsburgh are taking 57 days to sell, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 52 out of 100, and this time is actually increasing by 5.6% over the past year. This moderate pace indicates a relatively balanced housing market, where buyers have some time to find a home, but the market is not overly soft.

**Office Economy**
Pittsburgh has a relatively small office-based workforce, with a score of 20 out of 100, indicating that it may not be the best fit for businesses in the tech, finance, or professional services sectors. This smaller office workforce means that companies in these industries may find it more challenging to attract specialized talent in Pittsburgh.

The bottom line is that Pittsburgh's biggest strength is its low unemployment rate and relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for workers and businesses. However, the city's biggest risk is its weak labor demand, with job losses and rising hours worked by remaining employees, which may indicate a challenging job market and potential difficulties for employers looking to hire new talent.
