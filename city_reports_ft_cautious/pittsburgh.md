# Pittsburgh

**Grade: B (Average) | 56.9th percentile | November 2026**

---

Pittsburgh earns a B grade, with 3.0% unemployment. The city's economy is defined by low job growth.

**Labor Demand**
The city lost 0.5% of its jobs over the past year. Workers' hours rose 0.4%, but job losses mean remaining workers are putting in extra hours.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.0%. This makes it hard for employers to hire, with low unemployment.

**Wage Growth**
Pay rose 3.5% over the past year. This means rising labor costs for employers, but better paychecks for workers.

**Cost of Living**
Housing is affordable, with $168/sqft and $33.92/hr pay. Prices are falling 1.8% from a year ago, making it even more affordable.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 0.2% over the past year. This slow growth means a limited pool of new workers for hiring.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals rose 26.4% over the past year. This signals a stronger future housing supply and improving affordability.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 57 days to sell, up 5.6% from a year ago. This is a relatively healthy housing market, with homes selling at a moderate pace.

**Office Economy**
The office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits industrial or logistics businesses, but not tech or finance companies.

The city's biggest strength is its low unemployment and affordable housing. Its biggest risk is slow job growth and a limited office workforce.
