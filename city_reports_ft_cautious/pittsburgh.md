# Pittsburgh

**Grade: B (Average) | 58.6th percentile | October 2026**

---

Pittsburgh earns an overall grade of B, ranking 58.6th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low unemployment rate of 3.90% and a cost of living score that indicates relative affordability. The city's labor demand composite score of 4.06, combining a -0.05% employment growth rate and a +0.173% weekly hours deviation, suggests a mixed signal. The low unemployment rate and moderate wage growth are key defining characteristics of Pittsburgh's current economic landscape.

**Labor Demand**
Pittsburgh's employment growth rate is -0.05%, indicating a slight contraction, while weekly hours are running +0.173% above trend, suggesting some level of demand. However, the combination of these two metrics signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, rather than genuine demand expansion. This mixed signal warrants caution for businesses considering location decisions.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.90%, ranking in the top tier at the 90th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This tight market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure likely. For a business trying to hire in Pittsburgh, the low unemployment rate implies a competitive environment for talent acquisition.

**Wage Growth**
Wage growth in Pittsburgh is +3.33% year-over-year, placing it near the median at the 44th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively, while workers are experiencing some improvement in purchasing power. The implication is a relatively stable cost environment for businesses, with workers having moderate bargaining power.

**Cost of Living**
Pittsburgh's cost of living, with a PSF of $170/sqft and average hourly earnings of $33.44, results in a ratio of 5.08, ranking above average at the 74th percentile in terms of affordability. This means the city is relatively more affordable compared to its peers, which can be a significant talent attraction advantage without necessitating wage premiums. The -1.2% year-over-year decrease in PSF relative to wages further enhances the city's affordability.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Pittsburgh is growing at a rate of +1.07% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the hiring capacity for businesses is improving, as there are more potential workers entering the labor market. This trend is beneficial for companies looking to expand their operations in the city.

**Building Permits**
Residential building permits in Pittsburgh are increasing by +24.71% year-over-year, signaling an expansion in future housing supply. This growth in permits suggests that developer confidence is high, and the city is likely to see improvements in affordability and an easier time accommodating a growing workforce. The increasing supply of housing will help mitigate potential workforce attraction challenges.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Pittsburgh is 54 days, with a year-over-year increase of +3.9%. This indicates a slightly slower market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as it may provide them with more options and less competitive pressure. However, the market remains relatively competitive, and relocating workers should still anticipate a brisk sales environment.

**Office Economy**
Pittsburgh's professional and office worker share ranks below average at the 20th percentile, indicating a less deep talent pool in these sectors compared to other cities. This suggests that Pittsburgh is less suited for businesses requiring a large pool of tech, finance, or consulting talent but may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs, such as manufacturing or logistics.

In conclusion, Pittsburgh offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market, moderate wage growth, and relative affordability, making it an attractive location for certain types of industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the city's below-average labor demand and office economy metrics, which may limit its appeal for businesses heavily reliant on professional and office sectors.
