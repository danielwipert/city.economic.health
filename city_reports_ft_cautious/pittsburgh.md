# Pittsburgh

**Grade: A- (Good) | 61.1th percentile | September 2026**

---

Pittsburgh earns an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking it at the 61.1th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 3.70% and its labor force growth rate of +1.61% YoY, indicating a tight labor market with an expanding workforce. These metrics suggest a city with a strong foundation for business growth, but also potential challenges in hiring and retaining talent.

**Labor Demand**
Pittsburgh's employment growth rate is -0.02% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating -0.470% from its own trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 4.27, which ranks below average at the 26th percentile. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion or survivor squeeze. The negative employment growth rate indicates a decrease in job openings, which may affect businesses looking to expand their operations.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.70%, ranking it at the 90th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring and retaining talent, as the low unemployment rate puts upward pressure on wages. With such a tight market, companies may need to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract and retain employees.

**Wage Growth**
Pittsburgh's wage growth rate is +4.48% YoY, ranking near the median at the 58th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. As a result, workers in Pittsburgh are experiencing an increase in purchasing power, which can have a positive impact on local consumer demand.

**Cost of Living**
Pittsburgh's cost of living, with a PSF of $172/sqft and an hourly wage of $33.21, resulting in a ratio of 5.18, ranks near the median at the 57th percentile. This means that the city is neither particularly affordable nor expensive relative to its peers. The stable YoY change in PSF (+0.0%) suggests that the cost of living is not increasing significantly, making it easier for businesses to attract talent without needing to offer large wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Pittsburgh is growing at a rate of +1.61% YoY, ranking it at the 90th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, providing businesses with a growing pool of potential employees. As the labor force continues to grow, companies may find it easier to hire and staff their operations.

**Building Permits**
Pittsburgh has seen a +25.84% YoY increase in building permits, ranking it above average at the 78th percentile. This significant increase in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which can improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. As a result, businesses can expect a more stable and affordable housing market for their employees.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market in Pittsburgh is 53 days, with a +6.0% YoY increase, ranking it above average at the 72nd percentile. This means that homes are sitting on the market for a relatively longer period, making it a more accessible market for workers relocating to the city. The rising DOM indicates a healthy normalization of the market, rather than a demand erosion.

**Office Economy**
Pittsburgh's office economy, with a professional worker share ranking at the 20th percentile, indicates a relatively shallow talent pool in professional and office sectors. This makes the city less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies. However, it may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Pittsburgh offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market, moderate wage growth, and an expanding workforce, making it an attractive location for companies looking to grow. However, the biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential challenge in hiring and retaining talent due to the low unemployment rate, which may require companies to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract and retain employees.
