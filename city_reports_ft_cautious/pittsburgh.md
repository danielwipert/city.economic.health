# Pittsburgh

**Grade: B (Average) | 56.9th percentile | November 2026**

---

Pittsburgh earns a B grade, with an overall score of 56.9. The city's economy is defined by its low unemployment rate of 3.0%. Job growth is slower than most metros, at -0.5% over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is -0.5% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means people looking for work may face challenges finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.0%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring harder for employers due to a tight labor market.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing at 3.5% over the past year, about average for most metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see modest paycheck growth.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.0 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, priced at $168/sqft, down 1.8% from last year, making it more affordable than most metros. This attracts and retains workers, as the cost of living is relatively low.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is growing at 0.2% over the past year, about average for most metros. This means employers have a moderately expanding labor pool to hire from.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 26.4% over the past year, stronger than most metros. This signals a growing housing supply, which may improve affordability and attract more workers.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 57 days to sell, up 5.6% from last year, selling slower. This indicates a relatively balanced housing market, but rising days on market may give buyers more time to find a home.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses with more industrial or logistics-focused operations, rather than tech or finance companies.

The city's biggest strength is its low unemployment rate, making it attractive for workers. However, its biggest risk is its slow job growth, which may limit opportunities for people looking for work.
