# Pittsburgh

**Grade: B (Average) | 56.9th percentile | November 2026**

---

Pittsburgh earns an overall grade of B, with a score of 56.9 out of 100, indicating it is an average city for business and economic growth. The city's economy is currently defined by its low unemployment rate of 3.0%, which is a strength, and its slower employment growth, with a decline of 0.5% over the past year, which is a weakness.

**Labor Demand**
Pittsburgh is losing jobs, with a decline of 0.5% over the past year, and workers' hours are only slightly rising, by 0.4%, indicating a weak job market. This means that the city is not adding jobs at a pace that is competitive with most other metros, making it a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.0%, which is lower than most other metros, earning a score of 96 out of 100, indicating a tight labor market. This low unemployment rate makes it harder for employers to hire, as there are fewer available workers, and may lead to upward pressure on wages.

**Wage Growth**
Wages in Pittsburgh rose by 3.5% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, earning a score of 44 out of 100. This moderate wage growth means that employers' labor costs are increasing, but not at a rapid pace, while workers are seeing some improvement in their paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
The cost of living in Pittsburgh is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $168 and hourly pay of $33.92, earning a score of 88 out of 100, indicating that it is more affordable than most metros. The fact that the price per square foot is falling by 1.8% over the past year makes it an even more attractive option for workers and businesses.

**Labor Force Growth**
The labor force in Pittsburgh is growing slowly, with an increase of 0.2% over the past year, earning a score of 58 out of 100, which is about average for the 50 metros. This slow growth means that the pool of available workers is not expanding rapidly, which may make it challenging for businesses to find the talent they need.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals in Pittsburgh increased by 26.4% over the past year, earning a score of 86 out of 100, indicating a strong and growing housing market. This surge in building permits suggests that the city's housing supply will expand in the future, making it more attractive to workers and businesses.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Pittsburgh are taking 57 days to sell, which is about average for the 50 metros, earning a score of 52 out of 100, and this time is increasing by 5.6% over the past year. This moderate pace of home sales indicates a relatively healthy housing market, but one that is not overly tight or soft.

**Office Economy**
Pittsburgh's office-based workforce is smaller than most other metros, earning a score of 20 out of 100, which may make it less suitable for businesses in the tech, finance, or professional services sectors. This smaller office workforce means that businesses looking for a deep talent pool in these areas may need to look elsewhere.

The bottom line is that Pittsburgh's biggest strength is its affordable cost of living, making it an attractive option for workers and businesses, while its biggest risk is its slow employment growth, which may make it challenging for businesses to expand and find the talent they need. Overall, Pittsburgh is an average city for business and economic growth, with both strengths and weaknesses that businesses should consider when making location decisions.
