# Pittsburgh

**Grade: B (Average) | 56.9th percentile | November 2026**

---

Pittsburgh earns a B grade, with an overall score of 56.9. The city's economy is defined by low unemployment, at 3.0%. Housing affordability is also a strength, with homes costing $168 per square foot, down 1.8% from last year.

**Labor Demand**
Job growth is -0.5% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means fewer job openings for people looking for work.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.0%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring harder for employers due to a tight labor market.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 3.5% over the past year, about average for most metros. This means moderate labor cost increases for employers and steady paychecks for workers.

**Cost of Living**
Homes cost 5.0 hours of local pay per square foot, with prices down 1.8% from last year, making it more affordable than most metros. This attracts and retains workers, with affordability improving.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 0.2% over the past year, about average for most metros. This means a stable labor pool for employers, with no significant hiring challenges.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals rose 26.4% over the past year, stronger than most metros. This signals increasing housing supply and potentially lower prices ahead.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 57 days to sell, up 5.6% from last year, with sales slowing. This indicates a softening housing market, making it slightly easier for buyers to find homes.

**Office Economy**
The office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits industries like manufacturing and logistics, but may not be ideal for tech or finance companies.

Pittsburgh's biggest strength is its low unemployment rate, making it a competitive labor market. However, its biggest risk is a relatively small office-based workforce, which may limit its appeal to certain types of businesses.
