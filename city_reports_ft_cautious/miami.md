# Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach

**Grade: A- (Good) | 65.2th percentile | November 2026**

---

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has an overall grade of A-, with a score of 65.2 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by strong wage growth, with hourly earnings rising 8.8% over the past year. This is one of the highest pay growth rates among major US metros.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.2%, lower than most metros. This makes it harder for employers to hire, as there are fewer people looking for work.

**Wage Growth**
Hourly earnings have risen 8.8% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers enjoy stronger paychecks.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce has grown 0.9% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers have a growing pool of potential hires to choose from.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals have risen 11.3% over the past year, stronger than most metros. This signals a growing housing supply, which could help keep prices in check.

**Days on Market**
Homes typically take 79 days to sell, down 8.1% from a year ago, making the housing market healthier than most metros. This is a good sign for people moving to the city, as homes are selling relatively quickly.

**Office Economy**
The city has a larger office-based workforce than most metros, with a strong presence of tech, finance, and professional services. This makes it a good fit for businesses in these sectors, such as headquarters or consulting firms.

The city's biggest strength is its strong wage growth, driven by a tight labor market. However, its biggest risk is a high cost of living, which can make it hard to attract and keep workers, especially as home prices remain elevated.
