# Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 51.0th percentile | November 2026**

---

The Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue metro area has a below-average overall grade of B-. The city's economy is defined by strong job growth, with a 0.7% increase over the past year. This growth is accompanied by a rise in weekly hours worked.

**Labor Demand**
The city has 0.7% job growth and 0.4% higher weekly hours. This means people looking for work have a better chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.9%. This makes hiring easier for employers.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 5.5% over the past year. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see higher paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 8.8 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $423/sqft and pay at $47.99/hr. This affordability is about average, making it neither a major draw nor a deterrent for workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 1.4% over the past year. This means employers have a growing pool of potential hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 13.6% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 51 days to sell, up 6.2% from last year. This indicates a healthier housing market, with more time for buyers to find homes.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services.

The city's biggest strength is its strong labor force growth, making it easier for employers to hire. However, its biggest risk is the falling building permits, which may lead to a housing supply shortage and higher prices.
