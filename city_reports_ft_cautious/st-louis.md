# St. Louis

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 73.7th percentile | November 2026**

---

St. Louis earns an overall grade of A+, with a score of 73.7 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by strong labor demand, with 1.0% job growth over the past year. This growth is paired with a low unemployment rate of 3.4%.

**Labor Demand**
The city sees 1.0% job growth and 1.0% higher weekly hours, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job with decent hours.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.4%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring a bit harder for employers, but it's a good sign for workers who want a job.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 4.3% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, but workers see more money in their paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 4.3 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $163/sqft and pay at $38.21/hr, more affordable than most metros. This attracts workers and helps them keep more of their pay.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking 0.1% over the past year, near the middle of metros. This makes hiring a bit harder for employers who need more workers.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 7.4% over the past year, about average for metros. This signals a steady housing supply and stable prices ahead.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 51 days to sell, up 13.3% from last year, a healthier market than most. This means people moving to the city have a decent chance of finding a home without rushing.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses that don't rely on a large pool of office workers, such as industrial or logistics companies.

The city's biggest strength is its affordability, with low housing costs making it attractive to workers. However, its biggest risk is a relatively small office-based workforce, which may limit its appeal to certain types of businesses.
