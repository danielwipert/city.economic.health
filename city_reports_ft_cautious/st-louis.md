# St. Louis

**Grade: A (Very Good) | 64.1th percentile | August 2026**

---

St. Louis earns an overall grade of A, ranking in the 64.1th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average labor demand and cost of living. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.58, combining a +0.49% employment growth rate and a +0.598% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, signals genuine demand expansion. Additionally, its cost of living, with a PSF of $164/sqft and hourly earnings of $37.84, resulting in a ratio of 4.33, places it in the top tier for affordability, ranking in the 84th percentile.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of +0.49% and weekly hours deviation of +0.598% from its own trend indicate a genuine demand expansion in St. Louis, suggesting that the city is experiencing an increase in jobs and hours worked. This combination signals a healthy labor market where businesses can find the talent they need. The labor demand composite score of 5.58 further reinforces this, placing the city above average in terms of labor demand.

**Unemployment**
St. Louis has an unemployment rate of 3.80%, which ranks in the 78th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with relatively low unemployment. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. However, it also suggests a strong local economy with a high level of employment.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in St. Louis is +2.84%, which ranks below average in the 38th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but at a slower pace than in many other cities. While this may help keep costs under control for businesses, it also means that worker purchasing power is not increasing as rapidly as it could be.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living ratio of 4.33, derived from a PSF of $164/sqft and hourly earnings of $37.84, St. Louis is highly affordable, ranking in the 84th percentile. This affordability is a significant talent attraction advantage, as workers can maintain a high standard of living without requiring large wage premiums. The fact that PSF is decreasing by -0.6% YoY further enhances the city's affordability.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in St. Louis is growing at a rate of +0.69% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the city has a structural advantage in terms of hiring capacity, as the labor pool is increasing. Businesses looking to expand or relocate may find it easier to find the talent they need in St. Louis.

**Building Permits**
St. Louis has seen a +16.20% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, which ranks in the 76th percentile. This significant increase in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. This is a positive sign for businesses considering relocation, as it indicates that the city is likely to remain attractive to workers.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market in St. Louis is 44 days, with a year-over-year change of +0.0%, ranking in the 26th percentile. This relatively fast market suggests that homes are selling quickly, which could make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the stable year-over-year change indicates a balanced market that is not becoming significantly more competitive.

**Office Economy**
St. Louis has an office economy percentile rank of 48th, indicating a near-median depth of professional talent pool. The city is suited for businesses that require a balanced mix of professional and non-professional roles, but may not be the best fit for those requiring a deep pool of specialized office talent, such as tech or finance companies.

In conclusion, St. Louis offers businesses a unique combination of above-average labor demand, high affordability, and a growing labor force, making it an attractive location for companies looking to expand or relocate. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the relatively tight labor market, which may lead to challenges in hiring and upward pressure on wages.
