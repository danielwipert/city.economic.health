# St. Louis

**Grade: A (Very Good) | 66.0th percentile | September 2026**

---

St. Louis earns an overall grade of A, ranking in the 66.0th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong labor demand and highly affordable cost of living. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.58, combining a +0.49% employment growth rate and a +0.598% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, signals genuine demand expansion. This, coupled with its top-tier cost of living percentile rank of 92, makes St. Louis an attractive location for businesses.

**Labor Demand**
St. Louis exhibits a +0.49% employment growth rate and a +0.598% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, indicating a genuine demand expansion. This combination suggests that the city is experiencing an increase in jobs and hours worked, pointing to a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 5.58 further reinforces this, ranking in the 64th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in St. Louis stands at 3.80%, placing it in the top tier with an 82nd percentile rank. This tight labor market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the low unemployment rate translates to higher competition for available talent and potential upward pressure on wages. For a business trying to hire in St. Louis, this means being prepared to offer competitive compensation packages.

**Wage Growth**
St. Louis experiences a year-over-year wage growth rate of +2.84%, which ranks in the 38th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. While this is below the national average, it still suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, albeit at a slower pace. This moderate wage growth supports worker purchasing power without overly inflating labor costs for businesses.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living percentile rank of 92, St. Louis is highly affordable, boasting a PSF of $164/sqft that has decreased by 1.2% year-over-year, coupled with an average hourly earnings of $37.84. This affordability advantage means that businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs, making it an attractive location for companies looking to relocate or expand.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in St. Louis is growing at a rate of +0.54% year-over-year, ranking in the 70th percentile. This expansion in the labor force supply indicates that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, which is beneficial for businesses looking to hire. The positive growth rate suggests that St. Louis can support the hiring needs of expanding businesses.

**Building Permits**
St. Louis has seen an 18.86% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, placing it in the 70th percentile. This significant rise in permits signals that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. The increase in building permits is a positive indicator for future workforce attraction and retention.

**Days on Market**
Homes in St. Louis currently sit on the market for a median of 45 days, with a year-over-year change of +0.0%. This relatively fast market, ranking in the 28th percentile, means that relocating workers may face a competitive environment when searching for housing. However, the stable days on market suggest a balanced housing market that is neither overly hot nor cold.

**Office Economy**
St. Louis has a professional and office worker share that places it near the median, with a 48th percentile rank. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, although it may not be as robust as in other major metropolitan areas. The city is well-suited for a variety of businesses but may not offer the same level of specialized talent as larger, more knowledge-economy dominant metros.

In conclusion, St. Louis offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, highly affordable cost of living, and a growing labor force, making it an attractive location for relocation or expansion. However, the tight labor market and moderate wage growth rate pose challenges for hiring and labor costs, which decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating St. Louis as a potential business location.
