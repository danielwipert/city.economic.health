# St. Louis

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 73.7th percentile | November 2026**

---

St. Louis earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 73.7th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. The city's economic character is defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score of 6.69, and its highly affordable cost of living, with a percentile rank of 98th. Specifically, the labor demand is driven by a 0.98% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.019% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend.

**Labor Demand**
The combination of a 0.98% employment growth rate and a 1.019% increase in weekly hours above trend signals genuine demand expansion in St. Louis. This indicates that the city is experiencing a real increase in labor demand, with more jobs being added and hours worked above the historical trend. As a result, businesses can expect a competitive labor market with opportunities for growth.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in St. Louis stands at 3.40%, which corresponds to an above-average percentile rank of 78th. This suggests a relatively tight labor market with some slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire new talent. However, the unemployment rate is not excessively low, which could mitigate some of the wage pressure associated with very tight labor markets.

**Wage Growth**
St. Louis experiences a year-over-year wage growth rate of 4.35%, which is above average, ranking 62nd percentile. This moderate to fast wage growth rate implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing worker purchasing power. As a result, businesses should factor in higher labor costs, while also benefiting from a workforce with growing disposable income.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living percentile rank of 98th, St. Louis is highly affordable relative to its peers, featuring a PSF of $163/sqft (decreasing by 1.8% year-over-year) compared to average hourly earnings of $38.21. This affordability advantage is a significant talent attraction factor, as workers can maintain a high standard of living without requiring excessive wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in St. Louis is growing at a rate of -0.09% year-over-year, indicating a slightly contracting labor pool. This modest decline suggests that the labor supply is not expanding rapidly, which could pose a structural headwind for hiring in the long term. However, the decline is relatively small, and the city's strong labor demand and affordability may still attract new talent.

**Building Permits**
The number of residential building permits in St. Louis has increased by 7.38% year-over-year, signaling an expansion of future housing supply. Although the growth rate is near the median, it still suggests that developer confidence is present, and housing affordability may improve over time. This, in turn, should support the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market in St. Louis stands at 51 days, with a year-over-year increase of 13.3%. This indicates a relatively slow market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as they have more time to find suitable housing. The rising days on market also suggests a healthy normalization of the housing market, rather than a demand-driven slowdown.

**Office Economy**
With an office economy percentile rank of 38th, St. Louis has a relatively shallow professional talent pool compared to other cities. This makes it less suited for businesses requiring a deep pool of specialized office workers, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, the city may still be attractive to industries with less specialized labor requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, St. Louis offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, affordable cost of living, and moderate wage growth, making it an attractive location for companies seeking to expand or relocate. However, the slightly contracting labor force and relatively shallow office economy talent pool pose risks that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating the city's potential for long-term growth and success.
