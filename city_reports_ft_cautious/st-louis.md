# St. Louis

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 73.7th percentile | November 2026**

---

St. Louis earns an overall grade of A+ with a score of 73.7 out of 100, making it an excellent location for businesses, driven in part by its strong labor demand, with employment growth of 0.98% over the past year. The city's top-tier labor demand, combined with its highly affordable cost of living, makes it an attractive location for companies looking to expand or relocate.

**Labor Demand**
St. Louis is adding jobs at a rate of 0.98% over the past year, with workers also putting in 1.0% more hours, indicating a strong and growing job market. This means that employers are likely to face a competitive hiring environment, but also have access to a talented and busy workforce, with a score of 82, better than most of the 50 metros.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in St. Louis is 3.40%, which is lower than many other cities, making it harder for employers to find available workers, but also indicating a strong and healthy economy, with a score of 78, above average. This low unemployment rate suggests that workers have more bargaining power and may be able to command higher wages, which could be a challenge for employers.

**Wage Growth**
Wages in St. Louis have risen by 4.35% over the past year, which is a relatively moderate pace, with a score of 62, above average. This means that employers may face some upward pressure on labor costs, but workers will also see their paychecks grow, which could be a boon for the local economy.

**Cost of Living**
The cost of living in St. Louis is highly affordable, with a price per square foot of $163 and hourly pay of $38.21, and it's getting even better, with housing prices falling by 1.8% over the past year, earning a score of 98, top tier. This makes St. Louis an attractive location for workers and employers alike, as the low cost of living means that workers can afford a high quality of life without requiring excessively high wages.

**Labor Force Growth**
The labor force in St. Louis is shrinking by 0.09% over the past year, which is a relatively slow pace, with a score of 54, near median. This means that employers may face some challenges in finding new workers, but the slow pace of decline also means that the city's workforce is relatively stable.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals in St. Louis have risen by 7.38% over the past year, which is a moderate pace, with a score of 58, near median. This suggests that the city's housing supply is likely to grow in the future, which could help keep housing prices affordable and attract more workers to the area.

**Days on Market**
Homes in St. Louis are taking 51 days to sell, which is getting slower, with a 13.3% increase over the past year, earning a score of 84, top tier. This indicates a healthy housing market, where buyers have a reasonable amount of time to find a home, and sellers can still expect to sell their properties relatively quickly.

**Office Economy**
St. Louis has a relatively small office-based workforce, with a score of 38, below average, which may make it less suitable for businesses that rely heavily on tech, finance, or professional services. However, this also means that the city may have opportunities for growth and development in these sectors, which could attract new businesses and talent to the area.

The biggest strength of St. Louis is its highly affordable cost of living, which makes it an attractive location for workers and employers alike, with a top-tier score of 98. However, the city's biggest risk is its slow labor force growth, which could make it challenging for employers to find new workers, with a score of 54, near median.
