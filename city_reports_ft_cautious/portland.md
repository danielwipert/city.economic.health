# Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 49.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

The city of Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro has a C+ overall grade, with a score of 49.3 out of 100. This city's economy is defined by slower hiring, with employment growth at -0.6% over the past year. The unemployment rate is 4.1%, lower than most metros.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is -0.6% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means people looking for work may face challenges finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.1%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring harder for employers, as there are fewer available workers.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing at 5.7% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see bigger paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 7.1 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, at $302/sqft (down 2.3% from a year ago) and $42.38/hr pay, more affordable than most metros. This attracts workers, as the cost of living is relatively low and getting better.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is growing at -0.6% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers may struggle to find new hires, as the labor pool is shrinking.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 20.1% over the past year. This signals a growing housing supply, which may improve affordability and attract more workers.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 71 days to sell, about the same as a year ago. This is a relatively slow housing market, making it easier for people to find a home, but potentially softer than ideal.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses that don't rely on a large pool of office workers, such as industrial or logistics companies.

The city's biggest strength is its fast pay growth, making it attractive to workers. However, its biggest risk is slower workforce growth, making it challenging for employers to hire new staff.
