# Columbus

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 47.7th percentile | November 2026**

---

Columbus has a C+ overall grade, with a score of 47.7 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by its low unemployment rate of 3.3%. This is a major strength, but labor demand is a weakness, with job growth at -0.1% over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
Job growth is -0.1% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means people looking for work may face challenges finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.3%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring harder for employers, as there are fewer available workers.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing at 4.5% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see bigger paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.9 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with a price of $205 per square foot and hourly pay at $34.52. This is about average for affordability, making it neither a major draw nor a deterrent for workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking at -2.0% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers may struggle to find new hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are down 3.0% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze, which could drive up prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 50 days to sell, up 6.4% from last year. This is a relatively healthy housing market, giving buyers time to find a home without excessive competition.

**Office Economy**
The office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses with more industrial or logistics-focused operations, rather than tech or finance companies.

The city's biggest strength is its low unemployment rate, making it an attractive place for workers. However, its biggest risk is the shrinking labor force, which poses a challenge for employers looking to hire and grow their businesses.
