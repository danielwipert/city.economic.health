# Columbus

**Grade: B (Average) | 52.4th percentile | August 2026**

---

Columbus, with an overall grade of B and a composite score ranking it at the 52.4th percentile among 50 US metros, is characterized by its low unemployment rate of 3.30% and a near-median labor demand composite score of 4.57. The city's economic character is also defined by its stagnant labor force growth and declining building permits, which may pose challenges for businesses looking to expand. Specifically, the combination of a low unemployment rate and near-median labor demand signals a tight labor market with moderate job growth.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate in Columbus is +0.02% year-over-year, with weekly hours deviating +0.246% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit a modest one, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. The labor demand composite score of 4.57, ranking at the 40th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Columbus is 3.30%, ranking at the 96th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the low unemployment rate translates to higher wage pressure. With such a tight market, companies may need to offer competitive salaries to attract and retain talent.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in Columbus is +3.31%, ranking at the 46th percentile, indicating a moderate pace of wage growth. This rate of growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, while workers are experiencing a modest increase in purchasing power. As a result, businesses may need to balance their labor costs with the need to attract and retain skilled workers.

**Cost of Living**
Columbus has a cost of living score ranking at the 59th percentile, with a PSF of $207/sqft and an hourly earnings rate of $34.40, resulting in a ratio of 6.02. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The stable PSF growth rate of +0.0% year-over-year further supports the city's affordability.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Columbus is contracting at a rate of -0.43% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This decline in labor force growth poses a structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may struggle to find qualified workers to fill open positions. As a result, companies may need to invest in training and development programs to upskill existing employees.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Columbus has declined by -25.53% year-over-year, ranking at the 10th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This sharp decline in permits signals a potential supply squeeze in the future, which could lead to decreased affordability and make it more challenging for workers to relocate to the city. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact on their workforce accommodation and retention strategies.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market in Columbus is currently 40 days, with a year-over-year increase of +5.3%, ranking at the 84th percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, this trend may also signal a normalization of the market, rather than a decline in demand.

**Office Economy**
Columbus has a professional and office worker share ranking at the 14th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city may be less suited for businesses in the tech, finance, or consulting industries, which typically require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool. Instead, Columbus may be more attractive to businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

In conclusion, Columbus offers businesses a relatively affordable location with a tight labor market and moderate wage growth, making it an attractive option for companies looking to expand. However, the city's declining labor force growth and sharp decline in building permits pose significant risks to its long-term viability as a business location, and decision-makers should carefully consider these factors when evaluating Columbus as a potential location.
