# Columbus

**Grade: B (Average) | 51.6th percentile | August 2026**

---

Columbus, with an overall grade of B and a composite score ranking it at the 51.6th percentile among 50 US metros, is characterized by its low unemployment rate of 3.30% and a near-median labor demand composite score of 4.57. The city's economic character is most defined by its tight labor market and moderate labor demand growth, with employment growth at +0.02% year-over-year and weekly hours deviating +0.246% from its own trend. These metrics suggest a city with a strong but not overheating economy.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of +0.02% and hours deviation of +0.246% indicate a moderate expansion of labor demand, signaling genuine demand for workers rather than a survivor squeeze. This combination suggests that businesses are adding jobs, albeit slowly, and workers are putting in slightly more hours than usual, indicating a healthy but not booming labor market. The labor demand composite score of 4.57, ranking at the 40th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 3.30% ranks at the 96th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in Columbus may face significant competition for workers, potentially driving up wages and making it harder to attract talent. The low unemployment rate suggests that workers have strong bargaining power in this market.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of +3.31% ranks at the 46th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not extremely quickly, and that workers are seeing some improvement in their purchasing power. However, the moderate pace of wage growth may not be sufficient to keep up with cost of living increases or to attract top talent from other cities.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living ratio of 6.02, based on $207 per square foot and $34.40 per hour, Columbus ranks at the 59th percentile in terms of affordability. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which could be a talent attraction advantage for businesses without needing to offer significant wage premiums. The stable year-over-year change in PSF versus wages also supports this assessment.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -0.43% year-over-year, ranking at the 36th percentile. This indicates that the labor force supply is shrinking, which could create a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion in the city. The declining labor force growth rate may limit the city's ability to support rapid business growth or expansion.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in building permits is -37.46%, ranking at the 2nd percentile, indicating a sharp decline in housing supply expansion. This suggests that the city's housing market may be tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The low ranking in building permits is a concern for businesses relying on a steady supply of new housing for their employees.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market is 40 days, with a year-over-year increase of +5.3%, ranking at the 84th percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are taking longer to sell, which could be beneficial for workers relocating to the city as it may provide more time to find a suitable home. However, this trend may also signal a normalization of the housing market rather than a significant downturn in demand.

**Office Economy**
With an office and professional worker share ranking at the 14th percentile, Columbus has a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more appropriate for industries with different skill requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Columbus offers businesses a relatively affordable and stable economic environment, with a tight labor market and moderate labor demand growth. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses in this city is the shrinking labor force supply and the tightening housing market, which could limit the city's ability to support rapid business growth or expansion. Decision-makers should carefully consider these factors when evaluating Columbus as a potential business location.
