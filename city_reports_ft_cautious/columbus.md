# Columbus

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 54.1th percentile | September 2026**

---

Columbus, with an overall grade of B+ and a composite score ranking it at the 54.1th percentile among 50 US metros, is characterized by its low unemployment rate of 3.20% and a labor demand composite score of 4.88, which ranks below average at the 36th percentile. The city's economic character is most defined by its tight labor market and moderate wage growth. Specifically, the combination of a +0.06% employment growth rate and +0.492% weekly hours deviation signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit at a slow pace.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate in Columbus is +0.06% year-over-year, accompanied by a +0.492% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline. This combination indicates a genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth, suggesting that the economy is absorbing new workers. However, the pace of this expansion is slow, ranking below average at the 36th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Columbus is 3.20%, ranking at the 96th percentile, which indicates a very tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city will face significant competition for talent, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. The tight market implies that hiring may be challenging, but it also suggests a skilled and engaged workforce.

**Wage Growth**
Wage growth in Columbus is +4.15% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 56th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not at an alarming pace. For workers, this translates into increased purchasing power, which can be beneficial for local consumer demand.

**Cost of Living**
Columbus has a cost of living score that ranks at the 67th percentile, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $205/sqft to $34.84/hr, or 5.88. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, especially since the PSF is falling by -0.5% year-over-year. This affordability is a talent attraction advantage, as workers can be drawn to the city without requiring significant wage premiums to offset living costs.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Columbus is shrinking at a rate of -1.35% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 22nd percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies or consider upskilling existing employees to mitigate this challenge.

**Building Permits**
The number of residential building permits in Columbus has decreased by -14.98% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 26th percentile. This sharp decline in permits suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This trend may erode the city's attractiveness for relocating workers and businesses alike.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Columbus are currently sitting on the market for a median of 44 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.3%. This ranks at the 86th percentile, indicating a slower market. For workers relocating to this city, the slower pace of home sales could make it easier to find and purchase a home, as the market is less competitive compared to faster-paced markets.

**Office Economy**
Columbus has a professional and office worker share that ranks at the 12th percentile, with a composite score of 0.82. This indicates a relatively shallow talent pool in professional and office sectors, making the city less suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations that require deep knowledge-economy talent. However, it may be more accommodating to industries with different workforce needs, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Columbus offers businesses a unique blend of a tight labor market, moderate wage growth, and relative affordability, which can be attractive for talent retention and recruitment. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply, which could pose significant challenges for future hiring and workforce accommodation, potentially offsetting the city's current advantages.
