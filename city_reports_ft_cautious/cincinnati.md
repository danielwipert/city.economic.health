# Cincinnati

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 60.0th percentile | November 2026**

---

Cincinnati earns a B+ grade, with an overall score of 60.0. The city's economy is defined by strong wage growth, with hourly earnings rising 8.9% over the past year. This rapid pay growth is a key characteristic of the local economy.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 0.4% more jobs over the past year, weaker than most metros. This slower hiring pace makes it easier for employers to find workers.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.6%, lower than most metros. This tight labor market makes hiring more challenging for employers.

**Wage Growth**
Hourly earnings grew 8.9% over the past year, faster than most metros. This rapid pay growth increases labor costs for employers but boosts workers' purchasing power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.2 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, priced at $189/sqft, down 0.5% from a year ago, making it more affordable than most metros. This affordability attracts and retains workers, with costs improving over time.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 1.2% over the past year, slower than most metros. This decline in the labor pool creates a structural headwind for hiring.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 20.2% over the past year. This decline signals a potential future supply squeeze and rising housing prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 44 days to sell, up 15.8% from a year ago, indicating a healthier housing market. This slower sales pace gives buyers more time to find a home, making the market more attractive for relocating workers.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This smaller office sector makes it less suitable for tech, finance, and professional services businesses.

The city's biggest strength is its rapid wage growth, driven by a tight labor market. However, its biggest risk is a shrinking labor pool, which creates challenges for employers trying to hire and grow their businesses.
