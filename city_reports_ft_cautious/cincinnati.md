# Cincinnati

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 60.0th percentile | November 2026**

---

Cincinnati earns an overall grade of B+, scoring 60.0 out of 100, with its economy defined by a unique combination of strong wage growth and affordability. With wages rising 8.9% over the past year and a cost of living score of 92, indicating that housing is more affordable than in most metros, Cincinnati presents an attractive environment for both workers and employers.

**Labor Demand**
Cincinnati added jobs at a rate of 0.4% over the past year, but this growth is slower than in most metros, with a score of 38. The city's workers are also putting in slightly fewer hours, down 0.1% from their 12-month average, indicating a weaker labor demand than in most cities.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Cincinnati stands at 3.6%, which is lower than in most metros, earning a score of 74. This low unemployment rate makes it harder for employers to hire, as the labor market is relatively tight, with more job openings than available workers.

**Wage Growth**
Wages in Cincinnati rose 8.9% over the past year, which is faster than in most metros, with a score of 98. This rapid wage growth means that employers face rising labor costs, but workers benefit from increased purchasing power and a higher standard of living.

**Cost of Living**
Housing in Cincinnati is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $189 and average hourly earnings of $36.19, and the cost of living score of 92 indicates it is more affordable than in most metros. The fact that housing prices per square foot are falling by 0.5% over the past year further improves affordability, making Cincinnati an attractive location for workers and businesses.

**Labor Force Growth**
The labor force in Cincinnati shrank by 1.2% over the past year, which is slower growth than in most metros, earning a score of 26. This decline in the workforce presents a challenge for employers looking to hire, as the pool of available workers is shrinking.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals in Cincinnati decreased by 20.2% over the past year, which is a weaker pace of home-building than in most metros, with a score of 14. This decline in building permits suggests that the future housing supply may not keep pace with demand, potentially leading to increased costs and reduced affordability.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Cincinnati are selling in 44 days, which is getting slower by 15.8% over the past year, but this still indicates a healthy housing market, with a score of 98. This moderate pace of home sales suggests that buyers have a reasonable amount of time to find a home, and sellers can expect to sell their properties without undue delay.

**Office Economy**
Cincinnati's office-based workforce is smaller than in most metros, with a score of 36, indicating that the city may not be as suited for businesses in the tech, finance, or professional services sectors. However, this also means that Cincinnati may offer opportunities for businesses looking to establish a presence in a city with a more diverse economy.

The bottom line is that Cincinnati's biggest strength lies in its affordable cost of living and rapid wage growth, making it an attractive location for workers and businesses alike. However, the city's biggest risk is its slow labor force growth and declining building permits, which could lead to future challenges in hiring and housing affordability.
