# Cincinnati

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 69.3th percentile | July 2026**

---

Cincinnati earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking at the 69.3th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 3.10% and its strong wage growth of +5.40% year-over-year. These metrics suggest a tight labor market with rising labor costs.

**Labor Demand**
Cincinnati's employment growth rate is +0.45% year-over-year, while weekly hours are -0.694% below trend. This combination signals a moderate labor demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs, but hours worked are slightly below the 12-month baseline. This indicates genuine demand expansion, rather than a survivor squeeze.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Cincinnati is 3.10%, ranking in the top tier at the 94th percentile. This tight labor market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as there is less slack in the workforce. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive wages to attract top talent.

**Wage Growth**
Wages in Cincinnati are growing at a rate of +5.40% year-over-year, which is above average, ranking at the 70th percentile. This fast wage growth signals rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing purchasing power for workers. Companies should factor in these rising costs when considering relocation or expansion.

**Cost of Living**
Cincinnati has a cost of living score ranking at the 86th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $194/sqft to $35.49/hr, or 5.47. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. As a result, businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Cincinnati is shrinking at a rate of -0.97% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 34th percentile. This contraction in labor supply may pose a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. Companies should plan accordingly and consider strategies to attract talent from other areas.

**Building Permits**
Residential building permits in Cincinnati are increasing at a rate of +40.25% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 88th percentile. This expansion in housing supply signals improving affordability and accommodation for the workforce. As a result, businesses can expect a more stable and growing pool of potential employees.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Cincinnati is 37 days, with a year-over-year increase of +15.6%, ranking at the 96th percentile. This slower market means that workers relocating to the city may have an easier time finding housing, as homes are sitting on the market for longer periods. However, this may also indicate a normalization of the market rather than a demand erosion.

**Office Economy**
Cincinnati's professional and office worker share ranks at the 40th percentile, indicating a moderate depth of talent in these sectors. The city is relatively well-suited for businesses that require a general workforce, but may not be the best fit for companies that rely heavily on specialized tech, finance, or consulting talent.

In conclusion, Cincinnati offers businesses a unique combination of low unemployment, strong wage growth, and relatively affordable living costs. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor force, which may pose challenges for hiring and talent attraction in the long term.
