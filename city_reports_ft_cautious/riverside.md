# Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 36.2th percentile | September 2026**

---

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 36.2th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its below-average labor demand and high wage growth. **Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of +0.79% is modest, while the weekly hours deviation of -0.845% indicates hours worked are below the city's own 12-month trend. This combination signals a labor market that is not experiencing genuine demand expansion, as the increase in jobs is not accompanied by an increase in hours worked, suggesting some level of inefficiency or strain in the workforce. The labor demand composite score of 4.56, ranking in the 38th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 5.70% is relatively high, ranking in the 14th percentile, indicating a labor market with some slack. This means that while hiring might be somewhat easier due to the available workforce, the local consumer demand may be weaker compared to areas with lower unemployment rates. For a business trying to hire in this market, the higher unemployment rate could provide a larger pool of potential candidates.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of +6.02% is strong, ranking in the 88th percentile, indicating fast-rising labor costs for employers. This rapid wage growth is beneficial for worker purchasing power, as it suggests that employees in the area are seeing significant increases in their earnings. However, for employers, this means a faster increase in labor costs, which could impact profitability.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living ratio of 9.33, based on $332/sqft (which is decreasing by -1.2% YoY) compared to $35.60/hr, Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario ranks in the 35th percentile for affordability. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, making it less attractive for talent without offering wage premiums to offset the higher cost of living.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is shrinking at a rate of -1.75% year-over-year, ranking in the 12th percentile. This contraction in the labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as it indicates a dwindling pool of potential workers. For businesses looking to expand or establish operations in this area, the shrinking labor force could limit hiring capacity and growth potential.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -13.30%, indicating a tightening in housing supply. This decrease in building permits suggests that the future affordability of housing in the area may worsen, and the ability to accommodate a growing workforce could be compromised, potentially affecting the attractiveness of the city for both businesses and workers.

**Days on Market**
Homes are currently sitting on the market for a median of 63 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -4.5%. This relatively fast pace of home sales, ranking near the median in terms of days on market, suggests a competitive housing market that could be challenging for relocating workers to find accommodation quickly.

**Office Economy**
The share of jobs in professional and office sectors is 0.16, ranking in the 0th percentile, indicating a very shallow talent pool in these areas. This makes the city less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations, but potentially more suitable for industries with different workforce requirements, like logistics or manufacturing.

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area offers a mixed bag for businesses, with strong wage growth indicating a dynamic workforce but also posing challenges for labor costs. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the combination of a shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply, which could severely limit the area's ability to accommodate business growth and attract new talent without significant wage premiums or investment in local development.
