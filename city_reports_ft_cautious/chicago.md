# Chicago-Naperville-Elgin

**Grade: D (Emergency) | 22.5th percentile | November 2026**

---

Chicago-Naperville-Elgin earns a D grade. The city's economy is defined by slow job growth, with a 0.1% decline over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city lost jobs over the past year. Workers' hours rose 0.7%, but job losses mean remaining workers are putting in extra hours.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.4%. This makes it easier for employers to hire, but also signals weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay rose 3.2% over the past year. This slow wage growth means flat labor costs for employers, but weak bargaining power for workers.

**Cost of Living**
Housing is less affordable, with $219 per square foot and $37.37 hourly pay. Prices rose 3.8% over the past year, making it harder for workers to afford homes.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 2.0% over the past year. This slower growth makes it harder for employers to find new hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 5.0% over the past year. This signals a potential supply squeeze and future affordability issues.

**Days on Market**
Homes sell in 37 days, down 2.6% from last year. This fast pace makes it hard for relocating workers to find a home.

**Office Economy**
The city has an average-sized office-based workforce. This suits businesses like tech, finance, and professional services, but is not a standout.

The city's biggest risk is its slow job growth and shrinking workforce. Its biggest strength is its large and diverse economy, which could attract new businesses and talent.
