# Fresno

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 41.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Fresno has a C grade, with an overall score of 41.8. The city's economy is defined by its high unemployment rate of 7.3%. Job growth is 1.9% over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city has 1.9% job growth and -0.8% weekly hours change, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a decent chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 7.3%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for businesses to hire, but weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 4.2% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see higher paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 7.7 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with a price of $261/sqft and $33.92/hr pay, about average for most metros. This means the city has average affordability, making it neither a major draw nor a deterrent for workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew -0.5% over the past year, near the middle of most metros. This means employers face a relatively stable labor pool, but not an expanding one.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 6.9% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze, putting upward pressure on prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 61 days to sell, up 3.4% from last year. This is a softer housing market, making it harder for people to move here and for owners to sell.

**Office Economy**
The office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses with more industrial or logistics-focused operations, rather than tech or finance.

The city's biggest strength is its job growth, with 1.9% more jobs added over the past year. However, its biggest risk is its high unemployment rate, which signals weaker local consumer demand and a less attractive environment for businesses.
