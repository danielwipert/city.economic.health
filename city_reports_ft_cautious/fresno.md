# Fresno

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 41.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Fresno has a C grade, with 1.9% job growth over the past year. The city's economy is defined by its mixed labor market, with stronger hiring than most metros.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 1.9% more jobs over the past year. Workers' hours are 0.8% below average, indicating a strong job market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 7.3%, making it hard for employers to hire. This high rate means employers have an easier time staffing.

**Wage Growth**
Pay rose 4.2% over the past year. Faster pay growth means higher labor costs for employers and more money for workers.

**Cost of Living**
Housing costs $261 per square foot, unchanged from last year, with $33.92 hourly pay. The city's affordability is about average.

**Labor Force Growth**
The labor pool shrank 0.5% over the past year. A shrinking workforce makes hiring harder.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 6.9% over the past year. Weaker home-building signals a future housing supply squeeze.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 61 days to sell, up 3.4% from last year. The housing market is weakening, with homes selling slower.

**Office Economy**
The city has a small office-based workforce. This suits industrial or logistics businesses better than tech or finance.

Fresno's biggest strength is its stronger hiring, with 1.9% job growth. The city's biggest risk is its high unemployment rate, making it harder for employers to find workers.
