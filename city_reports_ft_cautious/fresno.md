# Fresno

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 41.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Fresno has a C grade, with an overall score of 41.8. The city's economy is defined by its high unemployment rate of 7.3%. Job growth is 1.9% over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city has 1.9% job growth and weekly hours are slightly falling. This means people looking for work have a decent chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 7.3%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for businesses to hire, but weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 4.2% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see bigger paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 7.7 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with a price of $261 per square foot and $33.92 hourly pay. This affordability is about average, making it neither a major draw nor a deterrent for workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking 0.5% over the past year, near the middle of metros. This means employers may face some challenges hiring, but not extreme ones.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are down 6.9% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze, which could drive up prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 61 days to sell, getting slower. This is a weaker housing market, making it harder for people to move here and find a home.

**Office Economy**
The office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses with more industrial or logistics-focused operations.

The city's biggest strength is its job growth, with 1.9% more jobs available. However, its biggest risk is its high unemployment rate, which signals weaker local consumer demand and a less attractive environment for businesses.
