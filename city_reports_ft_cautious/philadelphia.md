# Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington

**Grade: B (Average) | 50.6th percentile | July 2026**

---

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking 50.6th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its labor demand and labor force growth metrics, which stand at 74th and 84th percentiles, respectively. The city's labor demand is characterized by a 0.19% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.039% deviation in weekly hours above its own 12-month trend. These numbers suggest a city with genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours worked are increasing.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of 0.19% and weekly hours deviation of 1.039% above trend signal a genuine demand expansion in the city, indicating that jobs are being added and workers are putting in more hours. This combination suggests a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 5.61, ranking at the 74th percentile, further reinforces this interpretation.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington stands at 4.10%, placing it near the median at the 58th percentile. This rate indicates a relatively balanced market, neither too tight nor too slack, suggesting that businesses may face moderate challenges in hiring but without extreme wage pressure. The market's balance means that hiring can be managed with strategic planning.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate is -0.06%, ranking at the 4th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow pace suggests that labor costs for employers are not increasing rapidly, but it also means that worker purchasing power is not strengthening. For businesses, this could mean a flat cost environment, but for workers, it translates to weak bargaining power.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living ratio of $232/sqft to $32.67/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.10, and a percentile rank of 22, the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. This expense profile may require businesses to offer wage premiums to attract talent, as the city's affordability does not serve as a natural attractor.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of 1.49% year-over-year, placing it in the top tier at the 84th percentile. This positive growth indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a favorable condition for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a structural advantage in terms of hiring capacity.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in residential building permits is 9.53%, ranking above average at the 70th percentile. This increase signals that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate workforce growth. The growth in building permits is a positive indicator for future workforce attraction and retention.

**Days on Market**
Homes are currently sitting on the market for 39 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 4.9%. This decrease indicates a competitive market that is slightly cooling, which could make it more accessible for relocating workers to find homes. However, the market remains relatively fast-moving, which could still pose challenges for some buyers.

**Office Economy**
The share of jobs in professional and office sectors, combined with its growth trend, places the city at the 46th percentile. This ranking suggests a moderately deep talent pool suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ operations, though it may not be as robust as in other metros. The city is less suited for industries that require a large workforce in industrial or logistics roles.

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro offers businesses a growing labor force and expanding housing supply, which are significant advantages for recruitment and retention. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's relatively expensive cost of living and stagnant wage growth, which may necessitate offering wage premiums to attract and retain top talent, potentially increasing labor costs.
