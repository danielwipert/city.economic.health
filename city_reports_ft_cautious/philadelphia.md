# Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington

**Grade: A (Very Good) | 69.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington earns an overall grade of A, with a strong economy driven by labor demand. The city's employment growth is up 0.5% over the past year. Weekly hours are also rising, by 1.0%.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is 0.5% over the past year, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.6%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring harder for employers, as there are fewer unemployed workers to choose from.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 3.5% over the past year, about average for the 50 metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see modest paycheck growth.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 6.9 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, priced at $227/sqft, with pay at $32.96/hr, and prices are down 1.3% from a year ago. This affordability is about average for the 50 metros, making it easier to attract workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is growing 1.0% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers have a growing pool of potential workers to hire from.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 11.5% over the past year. This signals a growing housing supply, which should help keep prices in check.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 48 days to sell, up 11.6% from a year ago. This slower pace is still a healthy housing market, giving buyers time to find a home.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is larger than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which require a deep talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong labor demand, driven by job growth and rising weekly hours. Its biggest risk is a relatively low wage growth, which may limit workers' purchasing power and employers' ability to attract top talent.
