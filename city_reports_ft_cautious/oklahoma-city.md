# Oklahoma City

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 44.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Oklahoma City has a C+ overall grade, with a score of 44.8 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by its slow job growth, with only 0.5% more jobs added over the past year. This slow growth is a key challenge for the city.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 0.5% more jobs over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means people looking for work may face a tough job market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.7%, about average for the 50 metros. This makes hiring neither very easy nor very hard for employers.

**Wage Growth**
Pay grew 4.0% over the past year, about average for the 50 metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see decent pay raises.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.1 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, priced at $170/sqft, with pay at $33.63/hr, making it more affordable than most metros. This attracts workers and helps businesses retain them.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 0.2% over the past year, about average for the 50 metros. This means employers may face some challenges finding new hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals rose 7.5% over the past year, stronger than most metros. This signals a growing housing supply and potentially more affordable prices ahead.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 60 days to sell, up 9.1% from a year ago, indicating a softer market. This means buyers have more time to find a home, but sellers may struggle to close deals quickly.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses with more industrial or logistics-focused operations.

Oklahoma City's biggest strength is its affordable cost of living, making it attractive to workers. Its biggest risk is its slow job growth, which can make it hard for people to find work and for businesses to hire.
