# Oklahoma City

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 44.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Oklahoma City earns a C+ grade. The city's economy is defined by slow job growth, with only 0.5% more jobs over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 0.5% more jobs over the past year. Workers' hours are falling, 1.1% below average, indicating a weak job market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.7%. This makes it about average for employers to hire, neither very easy nor very hard.

**Wage Growth**
Pay rose 4.0% over the past year. This means employers face average cost pressure, while workers see decent paycheck growth.

**Cost of Living**
Housing is affordable, with $170 per square foot and $33.63 hourly pay. This affordability is unchanged from last year, remaining a strength.

**Labor Force Growth**
The worker pool shrank 0.2% over the past year. This slight decline makes hiring slightly more challenging.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals rose 7.5% over the past year. This signals a stronger future housing supply, a positive sign.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 60 days to sell, up 9.1% from last year. This slower pace indicates a weaker housing market.

**Office Economy**
The city's office workforce is small. This suits industrial or logistics businesses better than tech or finance companies.

The city's biggest strength is its affordable housing. Its biggest risk is a slow and shrinking labor market, making it hard to attract and retain workers.
