# Oklahoma City

**Grade: C+ (Poor) | 42.2th percentile | July 2026**

---

Oklahoma City has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 42.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 4.20% and its high wage growth rate of +5.07% year-over-year. These two metrics suggest a tight labor market with rising labor costs.

**Labor Demand**
Oklahoma City's employment growth rate is +0.15% year-over-year, while weekly hours are -0.117% below trend. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the small increase in employment is offset by a decrease in hours worked, indicating a potential survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Oklahoma City is 4.20%, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This tight labor market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure to attract and retain talent.

**Wage Growth**
Wage growth in Oklahoma City is +5.07% year-over-year, ranking it above average at the 68th percentile. This fast wage growth signals rising labor costs for employers, but also means good news for worker purchasing power, as employees see their earnings increase.

**Cost of Living**
Oklahoma City has a cost of living score that ranks it in the top tier at the 82nd percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $172/sqft to $33.58/hr, or 5.12. This means the city is relatively affordable, which can be a talent attraction advantage without requiring wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Oklahoma City is shrinking at a rate of -1.07% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor pool. This structural headwind for hiring means businesses may face challenges in finding qualified workers.

**Building Permits**
Residential building permits in Oklahoma City are decreasing at a rate of -6.14% year-over-year. This decline in permits signals a tightening housing supply, which may lead to decreased affordability and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Oklahoma City are currently sitting on the market for 51 days, with a year-over-year increase of +13.3%. This slower market, ranking in the top tier at the 80th percentile, means that workers relocating to the city may find a more accessible and less competitive housing market.

**Office Economy**
Oklahoma City's professional talent pool is relatively shallow, ranking in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. This makes the city less suited for businesses requiring a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

In conclusion, Oklahoma City offers a unique combination of a tight labor market with rising wages and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering locating in the city is the shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply, which may limit hiring capacity and workforce accommodation.
