# Oklahoma City

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 47.4th percentile | September 2026**

---

Oklahoma City has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 47.4th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its high wage growth rate of 4.86% year-over-year and its relatively low unemployment rate of 4.30%, which suggests a tight labor market. These metrics indicate a city with rising labor costs and potential challenges in hiring.

**Labor Demand**
Oklahoma City's employment growth rate is 0.43% year-over-year, and its weekly hours deviation from trend is -0.070%, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 4.83, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This combination signals a modest expansion of jobs, but with hours worked slightly below trend, it may indicate some level of survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand is not strongly indicative of genuine demand expansion.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Oklahoma City is 4.30%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile, which means the labor market is relatively tight. This tightness implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring due to the scarcity of available workers, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. The low unemployment rate suggests that the city's workforce is largely employed, making it harder for companies to find and recruit new talent.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in Oklahoma City is 4.86%, placing it above average at the 66th percentile. This rate of wage growth is relatively fast, suggesting that labor costs for employers are rising. For workers, this means increased purchasing power, which can be beneficial for the local economy. However, for businesses, it implies a need to budget for higher labor costs.

**Cost of Living**
Oklahoma City has a cost of living score that ranks it above average at the 71st percentile, with a price per square foot of $171 and average hourly earnings of $33.54, resulting in a ratio of 5.10. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The affordability of Oklahoma City gives it a talent attraction advantage, as workers can maintain a good standard of living without requiring significant wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Oklahoma City is growing at a rate of -0.50% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This negative growth rate indicates that the labor force supply is contracting, which could pose a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion. A shrinking labor pool limits the potential for companies to find new employees, making growth more challenging.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Oklahoma City has increased by 17.78% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 68th percentile. This significant increase in residential building permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. The rise in building permits is a positive signal for future workforce attraction and retention.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Oklahoma City is 55 days, with a year-over-year increase of 10.0%, ranking above average at the 74th percentile. This longer days on market indicates a slower, more buyer-friendly market. For workers relocating to Oklahoma City, this means a more accessible and less competitive housing market, potentially making relocation easier.

**Office Economy**
Oklahoma City's share of professional and office workers is 0.60, ranking in the bottom tier at the 6th percentile. This suggests a relatively shallow talent pool in professional and office sectors, making the city less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, it may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Oklahoma City offers a relatively affordable cost of living and a growing housing supply, which are attractive for talent retention and business expansion. However, the city's tight labor market and rising wage growth pose significant challenges for businesses looking to hire and expand, making labor costs and recruitment strategies critical considerations for any company deciding to locate or grow operations in Oklahoma City.
