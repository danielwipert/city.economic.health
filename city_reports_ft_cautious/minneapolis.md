# Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 37.1th percentile | November 2026**

---

The Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington metro area has an overall grade of C-, with a score of 37.1 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by slower job growth, with employment increasing by 1.1% over the past year. This growth is near the middle compared to other metros.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is 1.1% over the past year, with weekly hours falling by 0.8%. This means employers may not be hiring as aggressively as in other cities.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.5%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for employers to find workers, but also indicates weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 0.7% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers may not face significant labor cost pressure, but workers may not see much increase in their paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.0 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $204/sqft and pay at $40.79/hr. This is more affordable than most metros, making it easier to attract and keep workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking by 0.7% over the past year, slower than most metros. This makes it harder for employers to find new workers, as the labor pool is decreasing.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 10.9% over the past year. This signals that housing supply may increase, potentially improving affordability and making it easier for workers to find homes.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 43 days to sell, up 4.9% from last year. This indicates a healthier housing market, with more time for buyers to find homes, but still relatively fast sales.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This makes it less suitable for businesses that rely on a large pool of professional and technical workers.

The city's biggest strength is its affordable cost of living, making it attractive to workers and employers alike. However, its biggest risk is its slow labor force growth, which may limit the availability of skilled workers for businesses to hire.
