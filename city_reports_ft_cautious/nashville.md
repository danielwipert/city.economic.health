# Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin

**Grade: B (Average) | 56.1th percentile | November 2026**

---

Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin earns a B grade, with an overall score of 56.1. The city's economy is defined by its low unemployment rate of 2.9%. This is a major strength, making it harder for businesses to hire.

**Labor Demand**
Job growth is 1.1% over the past year, near the middle of metros. This means employers are hiring, but not at a rapid pace.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 2.9%, better than most metros. This makes hiring challenging for businesses due to a tight labor market.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 3.7% over the past year, about average for metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see decent pay raises.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 7.2 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $259/sqft and pay at $35.86/hr. This is about average for metros, making it neither a major draw nor a deterrent for workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 0.6% over the past year, among the weakest metros. This makes it harder for employers to find new hires, as the labor pool is shrinking.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 8.9% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This signals a potential future housing supply shortage, which could drive up prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 63 days to sell, up 3.3% from last year, indicating a softer market. This gives buyers more time to find a home, but also means sellers may struggle to close deals quickly.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce, better than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which can tap into a deep talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its low unemployment rate, making it an attractive place for workers. However, its biggest risk is a shrinking labor force, which poses a challenge for employers trying to hire and grow their businesses.
