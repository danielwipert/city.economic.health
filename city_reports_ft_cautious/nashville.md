# Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 71.7th percentile | August 2026**

---

Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking in the 71.7th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its top-tier labor demand, with an 86th percentile ranking, and its strong wage growth, at +5.97% year-over-year. These metrics signal a city with a robust job market and increasing labor costs.

**Labor Demand**
Nashville's employment growth rate is +1.56% year-over-year, combined with a -0.141% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in slightly fewer hours than trend, suggesting a healthy labor market. The labor demand composite score of 6.34 further reinforces this assessment, ranking in the top tier at the 86th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The city's unemployment rate is 3.30%, ranking in the top tier at the 86th percentile, indicating a tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the low unemployment rate translates to more competition for available talent and potential upward pressure on wages. In practical terms, companies looking to hire in Nashville may need to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract top candidates.

**Wage Growth**
Nashville is experiencing strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of +5.97% in average hourly earnings, ranking in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing purchasing power for workers. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs when considering relocation or expansion in Nashville.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living ratio of $260/sqft to $35.48/hr, and a year-over-year decrease of -0.8% in PSF, Nashville ranks in the 65th percentile for affordability. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The falling PSF relative to wages is a key driver of this affordability, enhancing the city's appeal for businesses and workers alike.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Nashville is growing at a rate of -0.14% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This slight contraction in labor force supply may pose a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is not expanding. Businesses may need to consider strategies to attract and retain talent in this environment.

**Building Permits**
The city is experiencing a -5.10% year-over-year change in residential building permits, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This decline in permits suggests that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability challenges and constraints on workforce accommodation. As a result, businesses may need to factor in potential housing costs and availability when considering relocation or expansion in Nashville.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market in Nashville is 58 days, with a year-over-year increase of +5.5%. This indicates a relatively accessible market for workers relocating to the city, as homes are not selling extremely quickly. The rising days on market may signal a healthy normalization of the housing market, but businesses should still consider the potential impact on relocating employees.

**Office Economy**
Nashville has a deep professional talent pool, ranking in the top tier at the 88th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require specialized knowledge-economy talent. Conversely, the city may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

In conclusion, Nashville offers businesses a robust job market, strong wage growth, and a relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for talent and investment. However, the city's tightening housing supply and slight contraction in labor force growth pose potential risks and constraints that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating Nashville as a business location.
