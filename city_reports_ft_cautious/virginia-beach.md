# Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 35.6th percentile | November 2026**

---

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C-, with a score of 35.6 out of 100. This city's economy is currently defined by slower hiring, with employment growth at -0.2% over the past year. The city's wage growth is a notable exception, at 4.5% over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is -0.2% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means people looking for work may face a tough job market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.8%, about average for the 50 metros. This makes hiring neither very easy nor very hard for employers.

**Wage Growth**
The city's pay growth is 4.5% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see bigger paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 6.4 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, at $222/sqft and $34.92/hr pay, making it less affordable than most metros. This makes it harder to attract and keep workers, and affordability is getting worse.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce growth is -1.4% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers may struggle to find new hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are down 17.5% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze and rising prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 45 days to sell, up 2.3% from last year, making the market about average. This is a relatively healthy market, but homes are selling slightly slower.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses that don't rely heavily on tech, finance, or professional services.

The city's biggest strength is its wage growth, which benefits workers but may increase costs for employers. The biggest risk is the slow labor force growth, which can make it hard for businesses to find the workers they need.
