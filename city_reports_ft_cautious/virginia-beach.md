# Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 41.2th percentile | October 2026**

---

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 41.2th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low labor demand and labor force growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 3.36 and labor force growth rate of -2.61% YoY are the two metrics that most define its current economic character, indicating a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or hire. With an employment growth rate of -0.55% YoY, the city is experiencing a contraction in job opportunities.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of -0.55% YoY and weekly hours deviation of +0.174% signal a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, rather than genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city's labor market is not adding new jobs, and the existing workforce is shouldering more responsibility. The labor demand composite score of 3.36, ranking in the bottom tier at 14th percentile, further reinforces this notion.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 3.70% is relatively low, ranking in the 66th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with some slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, the low unemployment rate means that it may be harder to find available workers, and there may be upward pressure on wages to attract top talent. However, the rate is not extremely low, suggesting that there is still some flexibility in the labor market.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of +5.51% is relatively fast, ranking in the 78th percentile, indicating rising labor costs for employers. This rapid wage growth is good for worker purchasing power, as employees will have more disposable income to spend in the local economy. However, it may pose a challenge for businesses looking to keep labor costs under control.

**Cost of Living**
The city's cost of living, with a PSF of $223/sqft and an earnings ratio of $35.71/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.24, is near the median, ranking 53rd percentile. This means that the city is neither extremely affordable nor expensive relative to its peers. The fact that PSF is rising 2.8% YoY relative to wages may make it slightly less attractive for talent attraction without wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -2.61% YoY, indicating that the workforce supply is shrinking. This decline in labor force growth poses a significant implication for hiring capacity, as businesses may face challenges in finding qualified workers to fill open positions. The low labor force growth rate, ranking in the bottom tier at 12th percentile, further exacerbates this issue.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in building permits is -10.45%, indicating that housing supply is tightening. This decline in building permits signals that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing units is not keeping pace with demand. **Days on Market**
The current median days on market is 44 days, with a YoY direction of +0.0%, indicating a relatively stable market. For a worker relocating to this city, the days on market suggests a moderately competitive market, neither extremely fast nor slow. The ranking of 35th percentile in days on market suggests that the market is not extremely favorable for buyers or sellers.

**Office Economy**
The city's professional talent pool is relatively shallow, ranking 24th percentile, indicating a smaller share of jobs in professional and office sectors. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics-dominant companies, and less suited for tech, finance, or consulting firms that rely on specialized office workers.

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area offers a mixed bag for businesses, with low labor demand and labor force growth posing significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the shrinking labor force, which may limit the city's ability to support business expansion or hiring. Despite some positive metrics, such as fast wage growth and a relatively low unemployment rate, the city's overall economic character is defined by its labor market constraints.
