# Providence-Warwick

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 43.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

Providence-Warwick has a C overall grade, with 43.3 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by slow employment growth, at 0.6% over the past year. This is near the middle compared to other metros.

**Labor Demand**
Job growth is 0.6% over the past year, near the middle. This means employers can find workers, but not many new jobs are being added.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.3%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for employers to hire, but weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 3.5% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means lower labor costs for employers, but weaker worker purchasing power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 9.7 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, at $357/sqft and $36.70/hr pay. This is less affordable than most metros, making it harder to attract workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 1.7% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers have a growing pool of potential workers.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals rose 15.6% over the past year. This signals a future increase in housing supply, which could improve affordability.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 39 days to sell, up 5.4% from last year. This is a healthy housing market, with homes still selling relatively quickly.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce, better than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services.

The city's biggest strength is its growing workforce, making it easier for employers to hire. However, its biggest risk is its high cost of living, which is getting worse, making it harder to attract and keep workers.
