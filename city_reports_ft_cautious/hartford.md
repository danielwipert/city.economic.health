# Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 73.0th percentile | November 2026**

---

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area earns an overall grade of A+, with a strong economy driven by job growth and low unemployment. The city's employment growth is up 1.9% over the past year, outpacing most metros. This growth is fueled by a diverse range of industries.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is 1.9% over the past year, with weekly hours rising 0.7%, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a strong chance of finding a job, and employers are likely to find skilled workers.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 2.7%, lower than most metros. This low rate makes hiring challenging, as there are fewer available workers, and employers may face upward pressure on wages.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 5.0% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers enjoy increasing purchasing power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 6.1 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, with a price of $246/sqft and hourly pay of $40.13, about average for the 50 metros. This average affordability makes it neither a major draw nor a deterrent for workers, with prices rising 1.6% over the past year.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is growing 2.8% over the past year, faster than most metros. This growth gives employers a larger pool of potential workers to hire from, making it easier to staff open positions.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are down 53.9% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This decline signals a potential future housing shortage, which could drive up prices and make it harder for workers to find affordable homes.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 37 days to sell, up 2.8% from a year ago, with homes selling slightly slower. This indicates a relatively healthy housing market, giving buyers a reasonable amount of time to find a home, but not so long that it becomes a buyer's market.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This makes it less suited for businesses that rely heavily on professional services, tech, or finance, but may still support other types of industries.

The city's biggest strength is its strong labor demand, driven by job growth and low unemployment, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to hire. However, its biggest risk is the decline in building permits, which could lead to a future housing shortage and make it harder to attract and retain workers.
