# Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 73.0th percentile | November 2026**

---

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area has an overall grade of A+, with a score of 73.0 out of 100. This city's economy is defined by strong job growth, with a 1.9% increase in employment over the past year. The unemployment rate is also notably low, at 2.7%.

**Labor Demand**
The city has 1.9% job growth and rising weekly hours. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 2.7%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring harder for employers due to a tight labor market.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 5.0% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see bigger paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 6.1 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with a price of $246/sqft and $40.13/hr pay. This cost of living is about average for the 50 metros, making it neither a major advantage nor disadvantage for attracting workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is growing 2.8% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers have a growing pool of potential workers to hire from.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are down 53.9% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze, which could drive up prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 37 days to sell, with sales slowing down. This indicates a relatively healthy housing market, but one that is softening slightly.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This makes it less suited for businesses like tech, finance, or professional services that rely on a deep knowledge-economy talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong labor demand, with low unemployment and rising weekly hours making it an attractive place for workers. However, its biggest risk is the sharp decline in new home approvals, which could lead to a housing supply squeeze and drive up prices, making it harder for people to afford homes and for employers to attract workers.
