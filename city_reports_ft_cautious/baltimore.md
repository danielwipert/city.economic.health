# Baltimore-Columbia-Towson

**Grade: A- (Good) | 64.7th percentile | November 2026**

---

Baltimore-Columbia-Towson earns an A- grade. The city's economy is defined by low unemployment and strong wage growth, with wages rising 6.4% over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city lost 0.2% of its jobs over the past year. Workers' hours rose slightly, but job losses mean a weaker hiring market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.5%. This makes it hard for employers to hire, with low unemployment driving up wage pressure.

**Wage Growth**
Pay rose 6.4% over the past year. This means higher labor costs for employers, but fatter paychecks for workers.

**Cost of Living**
Housing is affordable, with $213 per square foot and $37.72 hourly pay. Prices fell 2.7% over the past year, making it even more affordable.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 0.4% over the past year. This expanding labor pool helps hiring, with more workers available.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 2.8% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 46 days to sell, up 12.2% from last year. This slower pace is still a relatively healthy housing market.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is about average. This suits businesses with mixed needs, not just tech or finance.

The city's biggest strength is its low unemployment and strong wage growth. Its biggest risk is a potential housing supply squeeze due to falling building permits.
