# Baltimore-Columbia-Towson

**Grade: A- (Good) | 64.7th percentile | November 2026**

---

The city of Baltimore-Columbia-Towson earns an overall grade of A-, with a score of 64.7. Its economy is defined by low unemployment, at 3.5%, and strong wage growth, at 6.4% over the past year. This suggests a competitive job market with rising pay.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is -0.2% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means people looking for work may face challenges finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.5%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring harder for employers, as there are fewer available workers.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 6.4% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers see bigger paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.6 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, at $213/sqft (down 2.7% from a year ago) and $37.72/hr pay, making it more affordable than most metros. This attracts and keeps workers, as the cost of living is getting better.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 0.4% over the past year, faster than most metros. This helps employers trying to hire, as the labor pool is expanding.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 2.8% over the past year. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze, which could drive up prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 46 days to sell, up 12.2% from a year ago, indicating a healthier housing market. This means people moving here have more time to find a home, and the market is not too tight.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is about average compared to other metros. This suits businesses that need a mix of office and non-office workers, but may not be ideal for tech or finance companies.

The city's biggest strength is its low unemployment rate, which drives up wages and attracts workers. However, its biggest risk is the slow job growth, which may limit opportunities for people looking for work.
