# Denver-Aurora-Centennial

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 34.7th percentile | November 2026**

---

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a C- overall grade, with 34.7 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by slow hiring, with only 0.2% employment growth over the past year. This slow growth is a major challenge.

**Labor Demand**
The city has 0.2% job growth and 1.2% more weekly hours, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a decent chance of finding a job with decent hours.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.3%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for businesses to hire, but also means local consumer demand may be weaker.

**Wage Growth**
Pay grew 1.9% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers have lower labor costs, but workers have less bargaining power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 6.4 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $277/sqft and pay at $43.06/hr, about average for the 50 metros. The cost of living is getting more affordable as prices fall 3.5% over the past year.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 2.4% over the past year, among the weakest. This makes it harder for employers to find new hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 18.6% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze and higher prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 60 days to sell, getting faster, but still a weaker housing market. This means people moving to the city may struggle to find a home.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce, better than most metros. This suits businesses like tech, finance, and professional services.

The city's biggest strength is its large office-based workforce, making it a good fit for businesses like tech and finance. However, its biggest risk is the slow workforce growth, making it hard for employers to find new hires.
