# Memphis

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 54.9th percentile | November 2026**

---

Memphis has a B- overall grade, with a score of 54.9 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by its strong labor demand, with employment growth of 0.2% over the past year. This growth is accompanied by a 1.2% increase in weekly hours.

**Labor Demand**
The city has job growth of 0.2% and rising weekly hours. This means people looking for work have a better chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.1%, near the middle of other metros. This makes hiring neither very easy nor very hard for employers.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing at 3.6% over the past year, about average for other metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see modest pay raises.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 4.6 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $152/sqft and pay at $33.13/hr. This affordability is a strength, making it easier to attract and keep workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking at 1.7% over the past year, slower than most metros. This makes it harder for employers to find new hires.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 113.0% over the past year. This signals a strong future housing supply and potentially lower prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 73 days to sell, getting slower. This is a weak housing market, making it harder for people to move here.

**Office Economy**
The city has a small office-based workforce, smaller than most metros. This suits businesses that are not heavily reliant on tech, finance, or professional services.

The city's biggest strength is its affordability, with low housing costs making it attractive to workers. Its biggest risk is a shrinking workforce, making it harder for employers to hire and grow their businesses.
