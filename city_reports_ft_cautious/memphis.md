# Memphis

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 40.1th percentile | September 2026**

---

The city of Memphis has an overall grade of C with a composite score ranking it 40.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 2.11, and its high cost of living affordability, with a ratio of $153/sqft to $33.22/hr, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile. The labor demand and cost of living metrics are crucial in understanding the city's current economic state, with the labor demand indicating a contraction and the cost of living suggesting an attractive environment for talent.

**Labor Demand**
Memphis has an employment growth rate of -0.49% and a weekly hours deviation of +1.586% from its own trend. This combination signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, rather than genuine demand expansion. The low labor demand composite score of 2.11, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile, further supports this conclusion.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Memphis is 4.70%, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced market with some slack, making it easier for businesses to hire compared to extremely tight markets. However, the practical implication for a business trying to hire in Memphis is that it may not face extreme wage pressure, but it still needs to compete for talent.

**Wage Growth**
Memphis has a year-over-year wage growth rate of +4.52%, ranking above average at the 68th percentile. This indicates moderate to fast wage growth, which implies rising labor costs for employers but increased purchasing power for workers. As a result, businesses in Memphis may need to adjust their compensation packages to remain competitive.

**Cost of Living**
Memphis has a cost of living ratio of $153/sqft to $33.22/hr, with the price per square foot falling by -4.4% year-over-year. With a top-tier ranking at the 98th percentile, the city is highly affordable relative to its peers. This means that Memphis has a significant talent attraction advantage, as it can offer a lower cost of living without requiring wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Memphis is contracting at a rate of -0.92% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 34th percentile. This indicates a shrinking labor pool, which poses a structural headwind for hiring and may limit the city's ability to support rapid business growth.

**Building Permits**
Memphis has a year-over-year change in building permits of +5.41%, ranking near the median at the 52nd percentile. This suggests that housing supply is expanding, which may improve affordability and support workforce accommodation in the future. However, the current pace of growth is moderate, and it remains to be seen whether it will be sufficient to meet demand.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market in Memphis is 68 days, with a year-over-year increase of +6.2%. This indicates a slower market, ranking in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile. For a worker relocating to Memphis, this means that the housing market is relatively accessible, with fewer bidding wars and more time to find a suitable home.

**Office Economy**
Memphis has a professional and office worker share of 0.50, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This indicates a relatively shallow talent pool, making the city less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy workforce, such as tech or finance companies. However, Memphis may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The city of Memphis offers businesses an attractive cost of living and a relatively balanced labor market, but it is constrained by low labor demand and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the limited capacity for rapid business growth due to the contracting labor pool, which may require businesses to invest in talent development or relocation incentives to attract and retain workers.
