# Memphis

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 54.9th percentile | November 2026**

---

Memphis has a below-average overall grade of B-, with a score of 54.9 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by its affordable cost of living, with homes costing $152 per square foot, equivalent to 4.6 hours of pay. This affordability is a major strength, with the city scoring better than most metros in this area.

**Labor Demand**
The city has 0.2% job growth over the past year, with weekly hours rising 1.2%, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a relatively strong job market, with employers hiring at a good pace.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.1%, about average for the 50 metros. This makes hiring neither particularly easy nor hard, with a balanced labor market.

**Wage Growth**
Pay is growing 3.6% over the past year, about average for the 50 metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see decent, but not exceptional, pay raises.

**Cost of Living**
Homes cost $152 per square foot, or 4.6 hours of pay, making the city more affordable than most metros. This attracts and retains workers, as the cost of living is relatively low and getting better, with home prices falling 1.9% over the past year.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking 1.7% over the past year, slower than most metros. This makes it harder for employers to hire, as the labor pool is declining.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 113.0% over the past year, stronger than most metros. This signals a future increase in housing supply, which should improve affordability and help attract workers.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 73 days to sell, getting slower, with a 7.3% increase over the past year. This is a weaker housing market, making it harder for people to move here and find a home.

**Office Economy**
The office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses with more industrial or logistics-focused operations, rather than tech, finance, or professional services.

The city's biggest strength is its affordable cost of living, making it attractive to workers and businesses alike. However, the biggest risk is the shrinking labor force, which poses a challenge for employers looking to hire and grow their operations.
