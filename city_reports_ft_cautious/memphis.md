# Memphis

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 35.3th percentile | October 2026**

---

The city of Memphis has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 35.3th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a labor demand composite score of 2.11, and its high cost of living affordability, with a ratio of $153/sqft to $33.22/hr, ranking in the top tier at the 90th percentile. These metrics signal a complex economic environment with both challenges and opportunities.

**Labor Demand**
Memphis has an employment growth rate of -0.49% and weekly hours deviation of +1.586% from its own trend, indicating a contraction in labor demand with hours above trend, which suggests a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This combination signals that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The labor demand composite score of 2.11 ranks in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile, further emphasizing the challenges in the labor market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Memphis is 4.60%, ranking below average at the 34th percentile, indicating a moderate level of slack in the labor market. This means that while it may not be extremely difficult to hire, there is still some competition for talent. For a business trying to hire in Memphis, this rate suggests that there are available workers, but it may not be as easy to find the right candidates as in cities with higher unemployment rates.

**Wage Growth**
Memphis has a year-over-year wage growth rate of +4.52%, ranking above average at the 68th percentile, indicating relatively fast wage growth. This implies that employer labor costs are rising, and worker purchasing power is increasing. As a result, businesses may face higher labor costs, but workers will have more disposable income to spend in the local economy.

**Cost of Living**
Memphis has a cost of living ratio of $153/sqft to $33.22/hr, with the price per square foot falling by -2.5% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 90th percentile. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The falling price per square foot is a key driver of this affordability, enhancing the city's talent attraction advantage.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Memphis is contracting at a rate of -2.77% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile. This indicates that the labor force supply is shrinking, presenting a structural headwind for hiring and potentially limiting the city's ability to support business growth.

**Building Permits**
Memphis has a year-over-year change in building permits of +5.41%, ranking near the median at the 53rd percentile. This suggests that housing supply is slowly expanding, which could improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce in the future. However, the moderate pace of expansion may not be enough to keep up with demand, potentially leading to future affordability challenges.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market in Memphis is 72 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.5%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. This indicates a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, this slower pace could also signal weakening demand in the housing market.

**Office Economy**
Memphis has an office economy percentile rank of 4th, indicating a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This makes the city less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but potentially more suitable for industries with different skill requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The city of Memphis offers a complex economic environment with challenges in labor demand and labor force growth, but also opportunities in its affordable cost of living and relatively fast wage growth. The single biggest risk or constraint for a business considering location in Memphis is the shrinking labor force, which could limit the city's ability to support long-term growth and require strategic hiring and talent retention strategies.
