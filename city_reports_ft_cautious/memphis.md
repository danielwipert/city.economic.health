# Memphis

**Grade: A- (Good) | 60.2th percentile | September 2026**

---

Memphis has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking it at the 60.2th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its top-tier wage growth and highly affordable cost of living, with a cost of living ratio of 4.67, driven by a PSF of $153/sqft that is decreasing by 4.4% YoY, paired with an hourly wage of $32.74. These metrics suggest a city with strong labor market dynamics and attractive affordability.

**Labor Demand**
Memphis exhibits a labor demand composite score of 5.81, driven by employment growth of 0.06% YoY and weekly hours 1.422% above its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth, indicating that the labor market is experiencing organic growth rather than a survivor squeeze. This is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand or establish operations in the city.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Memphis stands at 4.70%, placing it near the median among its peers at the 40th percentile. This rate suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, it's not so high as to indicate significant weakness in local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 6.63%, Memphis is experiencing fast wage growth, ranking it in the top tier at the 90th percentile. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers but also indicates strong worker purchasing power, which can be beneficial for businesses reliant on local consumer spending.

**Cost of Living**
Memphis is highly affordable, with a cost of living percentile rank of 98th, driven by its decreasing PSF and favorable ratio of $153/sqft to $32.74/hr. This affordability is a significant talent attraction advantage, as businesses can recruit and retain workers without needing to offer substantial wage premiums to offset high living costs.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Memphis is shrinking at a rate of -0.92% YoY, indicating a contracting labor supply. This contraction poses a structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may face challenges in finding sufficient skilled workers to meet their needs.

**Building Permits**
Residential building permits in Memphis are increasing by 5.95% YoY, suggesting that housing supply is expanding. This expansion is a positive signal for future affordability and workforce accommodation, as it indicates that the city is likely to maintain or improve its affordability over time.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Memphis are currently sitting on the market for 68 days, with a year-over-year increase of 6.2%. This longer days on market metric, ranking in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, suggests a slower, more buyer-friendly market. For workers relocating to Memphis, this could mean a more accessible and less competitive housing market.

**Office Economy**
With an office/professional worker share ranked at the 2nd percentile, Memphis has a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This makes the city less suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations that require deep knowledge-economy talent pools, but it may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs.

In conclusion, Memphis offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth, highly affordable cost of living, and expanding housing supply, making it an attractive location for certain types of operations. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's contracting labor force supply, which could pose significant challenges for hiring and workforce expansion plans.
