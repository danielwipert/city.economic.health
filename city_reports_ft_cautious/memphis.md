# Memphis

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 35.2th percentile | October 2026**

---

The city of Memphis has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 35.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 2.11, and its high cost of living affordability, with a ratio of $153/sqft to $33.22/hr, ranking in the top tier at the 90th percentile. The labor demand and cost of living metrics are crucial in understanding the city's current economic state, with the labor demand being in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile.

**Labor Demand**
Memphis has an employment growth rate of -0.49% and a weekly hours deviation of +1.586% from its own trend. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the city is experiencing job losses while hours are above trend, indicating a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This suggests that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Memphis is 4.60%, which is below average, ranking at the 34th percentile. This indicates that the market has some slack, but it is not extremely loose, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire. The practical implication for a business trying to hire in Memphis is that it may face some competition for talent, but it is not an extremely tight market.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate in Memphis is +4.52%, which is above average, ranking at the 68th percentile. This indicates that wages are rising faster than in most cities, which means that employer labor costs are increasing, but worker purchasing power is also improving. This could be beneficial for businesses that want to attract and retain top talent.

**Cost of Living**
Memphis has a cost of living ratio of $153/sqft to $33.22/hr, with the PSF falling by -2.5% YoY, making it a very affordable city, ranking in the top tier at the 90th percentile. This means that the city has a talent attraction advantage, as workers can afford to live comfortably without requiring wage premiums. This could be a significant draw for businesses looking to relocate or expand.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Memphis is shrinking, with a year-over-year growth rate of -2.77%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile. This indicates that the labor pool is contracting, which could create a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion. Businesses may need to consider this when planning their workforce needs.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Memphis is increasing, with a year-over-year change of +5.41%, ranking near the median at the 52nd percentile. This suggests that housing supply is expanding, which could improve affordability and accommodation for the workforce. However, the growth rate is not extremely high, so it may not be enough to keep up with demand.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market in Memphis is 72 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.5%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. This indicates that homes are sitting on the market for a relatively long time, making it a slower and more accessible market for workers relocating to the city. However, this could also signal a weakening demand for housing.

**Office Economy**
Memphis has a relatively shallow professional talent pool, with an office economy percentile rank of 4th percentile. This means that the city is not well-suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, it may be more suitable for businesses with industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The city of Memphis offers businesses an affordable cost of living and a relatively low unemployment rate, making it an attractive location for companies looking to reduce costs. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses is the city's low labor demand and shrinking labor force, which could create challenges for hiring and expansion. Decision-makers should carefully consider these factors when evaluating Memphis as a potential location for their business.
