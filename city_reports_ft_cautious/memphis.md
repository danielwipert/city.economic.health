# Memphis

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 48.6th percentile | July 2026**

---

Memphis, with an overall grade of B- and a composite score ranking it 48.6th percentile out of 50 US metros, is characterized by its unique combination of strong wage growth and an affordable cost of living, with a cost of living ratio of $155/sqft vs $33.25/hr, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile. The city's economic character is also defined by its labor demand, which is in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile due to a -0.11% employment growth rate and a +1.092% weekly hours deviation from its own trend. These metrics signal a complex economic environment that warrants closer examination.

**Labor Demand**
Memphis has a labor demand composite score of 3.05, ranking in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile, due to a -0.11% employment growth rate and a +1.092% weekly hours deviation from its own trend. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the city is experiencing a decline in employment growth despite an increase in weekly hours worked. This suggests a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Memphis is 4.10%, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced labor market, neither too tight nor too slack. For a business trying to hire in Memphis, this means that while it may not be extremely difficult to find talent, there is still some competition for workers, and wages may be subject to moderate pressure.

**Wage Growth**
Memphis is experiencing a strong wage growth of +7.68% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 94th percentile. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing purchasing power for workers. As a result, businesses may need to adjust their compensation packages to remain competitive, while also benefiting from a more affluent local consumer base.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living ratio of $155/sqft vs $33.25/hr and a percentile rank of 98th, Memphis is an extremely affordable city relative to its peers. This means that businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs. The city's affordability is a major talent attraction advantage, making it an attractive location for companies looking to relocate or expand.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Memphis is contracting at a rate of -1.15% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 26th percentile. This decline in labor force supply implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is shrinking. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to compensate for this trend.

**Building Permits**
Memphis is experiencing a +14.68% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking above average at the 74th percentile. This expansion in housing supply signals improving affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce. As a result, businesses can expect a more stable and growing pool of potential employees in the future.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market in Memphis is 64 days, with a +10.3% year-over-year increase, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are taking longer to sell. For a worker relocating to Memphis, this means a more accessible and less competitive housing market, making it easier to find and purchase a home.

**Office Economy**
With an office/professional worker share of 0.48, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile, Memphis has a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This makes the city less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, Memphis may be more attractive to industries with different talent requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Memphis offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth, an affordable cost of living, and an expanding housing supply. However, the city's labor demand contraction, declining labor force supply, and limited professional talent pool pose significant risks and constraints that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating Memphis as a potential business location.
