# San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 40.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has an overall grade of C, with a score of 40.3 out of 100. This city's economy is defined by strong job growth, with a 1.6% increase over the past year. However, it also faces challenges like slow pay growth and high costs.

**Labor Demand**
The city has 1.6% job growth and 1.1% higher weekly hours, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a good chance of finding a job with decent hours.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.2%, about average for the 50 metros. This makes hiring neither very easy nor very hard for employers.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is -0.5% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers have lower labor costs, but workers have less bargaining power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 15.4 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $821/sqft and pay at $53.28/hr, less affordable than most metros. This makes it hard for workers to afford homes, and employers may need to offer higher wages.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew -0.3% over the past year, about average for the 50 metros. This means employers face some challenges in finding new workers, but it's not a major obstacle.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 50.0% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This signals a potential future housing shortage and higher prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 37 days to sell, down 2.6% from a year ago, weaker than most metros. This is a relatively fast housing market, but not healthy, as it may indicate a shortage of homes for sale.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce, better than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which can find a deep talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its strong job growth, making it a good place for people to find work. However, its biggest risk is its high cost of living, which can make it hard for workers to afford homes and for employers to attract and retain talent.
