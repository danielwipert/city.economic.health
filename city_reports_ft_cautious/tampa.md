# Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 54.8th percentile | October 2026**

---

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 54.8th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, driven by a 0.92% employment growth rate and a 0.120% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, as well as its strong wage growth of 5.13% year-over-year. These metrics signal a city with genuine demand expansion and rising labor costs.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of 0.92% and weekly hours deviation of 0.120% above trend indicate a labor market with genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 5.71, ranking at the 60th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 4.70% ranks at the 16th percentile, indicating a relatively loose labor market. This means that there is some slack in the market, making it easier for businesses to hire workers without facing intense competition. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand compared to tighter labor markets.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of 5.13% is above average, ranking at the 72nd percentile. This signals fast wage growth, which implies rising labor costs for employers but also increasing purchasing power for workers. Businesses should factor in the potential for higher labor costs when considering this location.

**Cost of Living**
With a cost of living ratio of 6.22, based on $232/sqft (decreasing by 6.1% year-over-year) versus $37.29/hr, Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater ranks at the 83rd percentile in terms of affordability. This means the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of 0.34% year-over-year, ranking at the 70th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire. The growing labor force provides a structural advantage for companies seeking to expand their operations.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in building permits is -10.10%, ranking at the 31st percentile. This decline suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability challenges and constraints on workforce accommodation. Businesses should consider the potential long-term implications of a tightening housing market on their ability to attract and retain talent.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market is 76 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 3.8%. This ranks at the 66th percentile, indicating a relatively fast-paced market. For workers relocating to this city, the housing market is competitive, but the decreasing days on market suggests some normalization, making it slightly more accessible.

**Office Economy**
With an office/professional worker share composite score of 3.55, ranking at the 82nd percentile, Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses requiring specialized professional roles but may be less ideal for those with primarily industrial or logistics-dominated operations.

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area offers businesses a unique combination of above-average labor demand, strong wage growth, and relative affordability. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the tightening housing market, signaled by the decline in building permits, which could lead to future affordability challenges and impact the city's ability to attract and retain talent.
