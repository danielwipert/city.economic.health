**Grade: B (Average) | 52.5th percentile | September 2026**

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 52.5th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven significantly by its above-average labor demand and top-tier cost of living. **Labor Demand**
The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area exhibits a labor demand composite score of 5.71, placing it in the 60th percentile, driven by a +0.92% employment growth rate and a +0.120% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours worked are increasing. The positive trend in both employment and hours worked suggests a strong and growing labor market.

This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. The low unemployment rate suggests that the local labor market has little slack, making it harder for companies to find and recruit talent.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in the Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area is +5.13%, ranking in the 72nd percentile, which is considered above average. This fast wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers but also indicates strong worker purchasing power. As wages grow rapidly, businesses may need to adjust their compensation packages to attract and retain talent, while workers benefit from increased disposable income.

Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a cost of living score in the 84th percentile, making it one of the more affordable cities relative to its peers. This relatively fast market, with homes selling quickly, may pose challenges for relocating workers, as they may face competition and pressure to make rapid purchasing decisions. The fast-paced housing market could be a consideration for businesses relocating employees to the area.

**Office Economy**
The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater places it in the top tier at the 82nd percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy workers but may be less ideal for those primarily involved in industrial or logistics sectors.

However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tight labor market and the potential for hiring challenges due to the low unemployment rate and contracting labor force supply, which could limit the city's ability to support rapid business growth and require strategic workforce planning.
