# Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 54.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an overall grade of B-, scoring 54.3 out of 100 across 50 US metros, with its economy defined by a mix of average job growth and a relatively affordable cost of living, as seen in its score of 71 out of 100 for cost of living. The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.1% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, but workers' hours are falling by 0.2%, indicating a somewhat sluggish job market. This means that while the city is not losing jobs, the pace of job growth is not particularly strong, and employers may not be demanding as much labor as they could be.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 4.3%, which is higher than most metros, scoring 26 out of 100, making it easier for employers to hire but also indicating weaker local consumer demand. This higher unemployment rate means that employers have a larger pool of potential workers to choose from, but it also suggests that the local economy may not be as robust as others.

**Wage Growth**
Pay rose by 4.0% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, scoring 56 out of 100, meaning that employers' labor costs are increasing at a moderate pace, while workers are seeing some growth in their paychecks. This moderate wage growth suggests that the city's economy is experiencing some upward pressure on wages, but it is not overly inflationary.

**Cost of Living**
The city's cost of living is relatively affordable, with a score of 71 out of 100, thanks to a price per square foot of $232 and hourly pay of $36.77, and housing prices have fallen by 5.7% over the past year, making it more attractive to workers. This affordability is a significant advantage for the city, as it can help attract and retain talent, especially compared to more expensive metros.

**Labor Force Growth**
The pool of workers in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is growing at a rate of 0.8% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, scoring 76 out of 100, indicating a strong and expanding workforce that can support hiring and economic growth. This growing labor force is a significant strength for the city, as it provides a larger pool of potential workers for employers to draw from.

**Building Permits**

This moderate growth in building permits suggests that the city's housing market is likely to remain relatively stable, with some new supply coming online to meet demand.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater are taking 77 days to sell, which is about average for the 50 metros, scoring 58 out of 100, and the days on market are falling by 2.5% over the past year, indicating a relatively healthy housing market. This moderate pace of home sales suggests that the city's housing market is balanced, with neither too much nor too little supply, making it easier for buyers and sellers to navigate.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is larger than most metros, scoring 84 out of 100, making it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This strong office economy is a significant advantage for the city, as it provides a deep pool of skilled workers and a supportive business environment for companies in these industries.

The bottom line is that Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater's biggest strength is its relatively affordable cost of living, which can help attract and retain talent, while its biggest risk is its higher-than-average unemployment rate, which may indicate a weaker local economy. Overall, the city's mix of average job growth, moderate wage growth, and affordable cost of living make it a solid, if not spectacular, choice for businesses and workers alike.
