# Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 54.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater earns a B- overall grade. The city's economy is defined by its affordable cost of living, with homes costing $232 per square foot, equivalent to 6.3 hours of pay. This affordability is a notable strength, especially since home prices have fallen 5.7% over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 1.1% more jobs over the past year, with weekly hours slightly below average. This means employers can find workers, but may not see much upward pressure on wages or hours.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.3%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for employers to hire, but also means local consumer demand may be weaker.

**Wage Growth**
Pay grew 4.0% over the past year, about average for most metros. This means employers face moderate labor cost increases, while workers see some improvement in their paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 6.3 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, priced at $232 per square foot, making the city more affordable than most metros. This attracts and retains workers, as the cost of living is getting better due to falling home prices.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew 0.8% over the past year, faster than most metros. This helps employers find the talent they need, as the labor pool is expanding.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals rose 3.7% over the past year, a moderate increase. This signals a steady future housing supply, which should help keep prices in check.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 77 days to sell, down 2.5% from last year, indicating a relatively healthy market. This means people moving to the city can find a home without too much hassle, as the market is not too tight.

**Office Economy**
The city has a large office-based workforce, bigger than most metros. This suits businesses in tech, finance, and professional services, which can tap into a deep talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its affordable cost of living, making it attractive to workers and businesses alike. However, its higher unemployment rate poses a risk, as it may indicate weaker local consumer demand and slower economic growth.
