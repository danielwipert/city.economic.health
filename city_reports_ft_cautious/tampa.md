# Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater

**Grade: B (Average) | 50.3th percentile | September 2026**

---

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking 50.3th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average labor demand and top-tier cost of living. The city's economic character is defined by its 60th percentile labor demand composite score, which combines a +0.92% employment growth rate and a +0.120% weekly hours deviation from its own trend. This suggests a genuine demand expansion, with jobs being added and hours running above trend.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of +0.92% and weekly hours deviation of +0.120% signal a genuine demand expansion, indicating that the city is experiencing an increase in jobs and working hours. This combination suggests that the labor market is strong, with employers adding jobs and workers putting in more hours. The labor demand composite score of 5.71, ranking in the 60th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 4.80% is relatively high, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile, indicating a labor market with some slack. This means that businesses may find it easier to hire workers, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The relatively high unemployment rate suggests that the market is not as tight as in other cities, giving employers more flexibility in hiring.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of +5.13% is above average, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating fast wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but workers are also experiencing increased purchasing power. The strong wage growth may attract top talent, but also increases the cost burden on employers.

**Cost of Living**
The city's cost of living is relatively affordable, with a percentile rank of 84th, driven by a PSF of $233/sqft that has decreased by 5.7% year-over-year, combined with an average hourly earnings of $37.29/hr. This affordability advantage can attract talent without requiring wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force has contracted by -0.50% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This may pose a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of workers is not expanding. Businesses may need to consider strategies to attract workers from other areas or invest in training and development programs.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -10.10%, ranking below average at the 30th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This may lead to future affordability challenges and make it more difficult for workers to relocate to the city, potentially affecting workforce accommodation and attraction.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market is 74 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -3.9%, ranking in the 76th percentile. This suggests a relatively fast-paced market, where homes are selling quickly, making it competitive for relocating workers to find housing. However, the decreasing trend may indicate a normalization of the market.

**Office Economy**
The city has a deep professional talent pool, ranking in the 82nd percentile, with a share of jobs in professional and office sectors of 3.58. This makes it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less suited for industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area offers businesses a unique combination of above-average labor demand and affordability, making it an attractive location for talent attraction and business growth. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability challenges and affect workforce accommodation, making it essential to factor this into long-term business plans.
