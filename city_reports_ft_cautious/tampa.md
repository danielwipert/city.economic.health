# Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater

**Grade: B (Average) | 53.9th percentile | August 2026**

---

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 53.9th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average wage growth and highly affordable cost of living. The city's economic character is defined by a wage growth rate of 5.33% year-over-year and a cost of living ratio of $236/sqft to $37.12/hr, resulting in a top-tier affordability score. These metrics suggest a city with a strong labor market and attractive living costs.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 0.56% year-over-year, combined with a 0.240% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, indicating a genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing modest job growth with hours worked increasing, suggesting a healthy labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.32 places the city near the median, indicating a balanced labor market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 4.70%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile, indicating a relatively loose labor market. This means that businesses may find it easier to hire in this city compared to others, as there is less competition for workers. However, the looser labor market may also contribute to weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 5.33%, placing it above average at the 78th percentile. This rapid wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers may be rising, but it also indicates strong worker purchasing power. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs, but they can also expect a more affluent local consumer base.

**Cost of Living**
Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a cost of living ratio of $236/sqft to $37.12/hr, with the price per square foot falling 4.8% year-over-year, resulting in a top-tier affordability score at the 82nd percentile. This means that the city is highly affordable relative to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring wage premiums. The falling price per square foot is a key driver of this affordability, suggesting a favorable housing market.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is growing at a rate of 0.52% year-over-year, placing it above average at the 62nd percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire in the city. The growing labor force suggests that the city has a structural advantage in terms of hiring capacity.

**Building Permits**
The number of residential building permits in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is increasing by 3.19% year-over-year, placing it near the median at the 52nd percentile. This moderate growth in building permits suggests that the housing supply is expanding, which should help maintain affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. The increase in permits is a positive sign for future affordability and workforce attraction.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 72 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 1.4%. This suggests that the housing market is relatively competitive, but still accessible for relocating workers. The decreasing days on market indicates a moderately hot market, which may pose some challenges for workers looking to relocate to the city.

**Office Economy**
Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share of 3.38, placing it in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a highly skilled and specialized workforce. However, the city may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth, highly affordable living costs, and a growing labor force, making it an attractive location for talent and investment. However, the city's relatively loose labor market and moderate housing market growth may pose some challenges for businesses looking to hire and accommodate a growing workforce, and decision-makers should factor in these risks when considering a location in this city. Overall, the city's strengths make it a compelling choice for businesses looking to tap into a skilled and affordable workforce.
