# Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater

**Grade: B (Average) | 51.7th percentile | August 2026**

---

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking at the 51.7th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong wage growth and affordable cost of living. The city's economic character is defined by a wage growth rate of 5.33% year-over-year and a cost of living ratio of $236/sqft to $37.12/hr, resulting in a top-tier affordability score. These metrics suggest a city with a growing economy and attractive living conditions.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 0.56% year-over-year, combined with a 0.240% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, indicating a genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing modest job growth, with hours worked above the trend line, suggesting that employers are increasing their workforce and workload. The labor demand composite score of 5.32 places the city near the median, indicating a stable but not exceptional labor market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 4.70%, ranking at the 14th percentile, indicating a relatively loose labor market. This means that there is some slack in the market, making it easier for businesses to hire workers without facing intense competition. However, this also implies that local consumer demand may be weaker due to the higher unemployment rate.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 5.33%, placing the city above average at the 78th percentile. This rapid wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but it also means that workers have stronger purchasing power. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs, but they can also benefit from a more affluent local consumer base.

**Cost of Living**
Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a cost of living ratio of $236/sqft to $37.12/hr, with a year-over-year decrease of 4.8% in PSF, resulting in a top-tier affordability score at the 82nd percentile. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The decreasing PSF relative to wages is a key driver of this affordability, enhancing the city's talent attraction advantage.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is growing at a rate of 0.52% year-over-year, placing the city above average at the 62nd percentile. This positive growth rate indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, providing a favorable condition for businesses looking to hire. The growing labor force can help mitigate potential labor shortages and support business expansion.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is -9.95%, ranking below average at the 30th percentile. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability challenges and constraints on workforce accommodation. The decrease in permits signals a potential risk for businesses relying on a steady supply of housing for their employees.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 72 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 1.4%. This relatively fast market, with homes selling quickly, may pose challenges for relocating workers. However, the slight decrease in days on market could indicate a normalization of the housing market, making it more accessible for workers to find homes.

**Office Economy**
Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share composite score ranking at the 82nd percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that require specialized knowledge-economy talent. However, it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth, affordable cost of living, and a growing labor force, making it an attractive location for talent and business expansion. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, signaled by the decline in building permits, which may lead to future affordability challenges and impact workforce accommodation.
