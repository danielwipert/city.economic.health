# Kansas City

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 61.4th percentile | October 2026**

---

Kansas City has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 61.4th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 7.35%, and its labor demand composite score of 5.99, which ranks in the 70th percentile. These metrics suggest a city with a robust job market and rising labor costs.

**Labor Demand**
Kansas City's employment growth rate of 0.73% and weekly hours deviation of 0.665% above its own trend signal genuine demand expansion, indicating that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours. This combination suggests a healthy labor market with increasing demand for workers. The labor demand composite score of 5.99, ranking in the 70th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The city's unemployment rate of 3.90% ranks in the 64th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight market implies upward pressure on wages.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 7.35%, Kansas City is experiencing fast wage growth, ranking in the 92nd percentile. This rapid growth implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing purchasing power for workers. As a result, businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, while workers will have more disposable income to spend in the local economy.

**Cost of Living**
Kansas City's cost of living, with a PSF of $195/sqft and an earnings ratio of 5.36, ranks in the 80th percentile, indicating that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. This affordability advantage can attract talent without requiring wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to hire and retain workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The city's labor force growth rate of -2.09% indicates that the supply of workers is contracting, ranking in the 20th percentile. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the shrinking labor pool may limit the availability of skilled workers. Businesses may need to invest in training and development programs to attract and retain talent.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in building permits of -3.34% suggests that housing supply is tightening, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This tightening of supply may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for housing, potentially affecting the city's ability to attract and retain workers.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market is 50 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 2.0%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile. This fast-paced market means that homes are selling quickly, making it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. Businesses may need to offer relocation assistance or flexible housing options to attract talent.

**Office Economy**
Kansas City's professional and office worker share ranks in the 28th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. The city is less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies, but may be more attractive to industries with different skill requirements.

In conclusion, Kansas City offers a strong job market with rising labor costs, making it an attractive location for businesses that can adapt to these conditions. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the contracting labor force, which may limit the availability of skilled workers and require businesses to invest in training and development programs to attract and retain talent.
