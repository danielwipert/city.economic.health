# Kansas City

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 69.0th percentile | August 2026**

---

Kansas City earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 69.0th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. The city's economic character is largely defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 7.38%, and its low unemployment rate of 3.70%, which together signal a highly competitive labor market. These metrics suggest that Kansas City is an attractive location for businesses looking to tap into a skilled and productive workforce.

**Labor Demand**
Kansas City's labor demand is characterized by an employment growth rate of 0.69% and a weekly hours deviation of 0.321% above its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a period of job growth, with hours worked increasing above trend, suggesting a strong and vibrant labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.59 further reinforces this assessment, placing the city in the above-average category.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Kansas City stands at 3.70%, which is in the top tier, ranking it at the 80th percentile. This low unemployment rate indicates a tight labor market, making it more challenging for businesses to hire new employees. As a result, companies may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain top talent in this competitive environment.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 7.38%, Kansas City is experiencing rapid wage increases, placing it in the top tier at the 98th percentile. This fast wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers, but it also translates to increased purchasing power for workers, potentially boosting local consumer demand. Businesses should factor in these higher labor costs when considering Kansas City as a location.

**Cost of Living**
Kansas City's cost of living, with a PSF of $197/sqft and an earnings ratio of $36.13/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.45, is considered above average in terms of affordability, ranking at the 80th percentile. This means that the city offers a relatively affordable environment for talent, potentially attracting workers without the need for significant wage premiums. The modest increase in PSF of 2.1% YoY, however, suggests that affordability might be slowly eroding.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Kansas City is growing at a rate of 0.50% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the city's labor pool is increasing, providing a structural advantage for hiring and business expansion. Companies looking to grow their workforce may find Kansas City an attractive location due to its expanding labor force.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Kansas City is -1.26%, indicating a slight tightening in housing supply. This decrease in building permits may signal future affordability challenges and potential constraints on workforce accommodation, as the supply of new housing units is not keeping pace with demand. Businesses should consider the potential long-term implications of this trend on their ability to attract and retain talent.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Kansas City are currently selling at a median of 47 days on market, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.1%. This relatively fast pace of home sales, placing the city in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile, suggests a competitive housing market that may be challenging for relocating workers. The quick turnover in the housing market could make it difficult for new hires to find and secure housing, potentially impacting a company's ability to attract talent from outside the area.

**Office Economy**
Kansas City's professional and office worker share is ranked at the 18th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs, like logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Kansas City offers businesses a highly competitive labor market with strong wage growth and low unemployment, making it an attractive location for companies looking to tap into a skilled and productive workforce. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the potential for future affordability challenges and workforce accommodation issues due to the tightening housing supply, which could impact the city's ability to attract and retain top talent over the long term.
