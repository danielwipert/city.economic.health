# Kansas City

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 62.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Kansas City earns an overall grade of B+, scoring 62.8 out of 100, with its strong wage growth and affordable cost of living standing out as key defining features of its economy right now, including a 7.6% rise in hourly earnings over the past year. With a score better than most of the 50 metros in several areas, Kansas City presents an attractive environment for businesses and workers alike, particularly with its low unemployment rate of 3.4%.

**Labor Demand**
Kansas City added jobs at a rate of 0.5% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, and workers' hours rose by 0.2%, indicating a stable job market. This balance suggests that while the city is not experiencing rapid job growth, it is maintaining a steady pace, which is a positive sign for employers and employees.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Kansas City is 3.4%, which is lower than most metros, scoring 70 out of 100, making it harder for employers to hire but also indicating a strong labor market. This low unemployment rate suggests that workers have more bargaining power, which could lead to higher wages and better working conditions.

**Wage Growth**
Hourly earnings in Kansas City rose by 7.6% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, scoring 92 out of 100, resulting in higher labor costs for employers but more purchasing power for workers. This strong wage growth is a significant advantage for workers, as it allows them to keep up with the cost of living and enjoy a higher standard of living.

**Cost of Living**
The cost of living in Kansas City is more affordable than most metros, with a score of 82 out of 100, and a price per square foot of $194, compared to an hourly pay of $36.10, although housing prices are rising at a rate of 1.6% per year. Despite the rising prices, the city's affordability is a significant strength, making it an attractive location for workers and businesses looking for a lower cost of living.

**Labor Force Growth**

This decline in the labor force is a weakness, as it can limit the city's ability to support business growth and expansion.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals in Kansas City increased by 96.0% over the past year, which is stronger than most metros, scoring 92 out of 100, signaling a significant expansion in future housing supply. This surge in building permits is a positive sign for the city's housing market, as it will provide more options for workers and help keep housing costs under control.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Kansas City take an average of 52 days to sell, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 50 out of 100, and the market is getting slightly slower, with a 2.0% increase in days on market. This moderate pace suggests a relatively healthy housing market, where buyers have a reasonable amount of time to find a home, and sellers can expect to sell their properties without excessive delays.

**Office Economy**
Kansas City's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros, scoring 32 out of 100, which may make it less suitable for businesses that rely heavily on professional and technical services. However, the city's economy is still diverse, and its strengths in other areas, such as affordability and wage growth, can make it an attractive location for a variety of businesses.

Kansas City's biggest strength is its strong wage growth, which provides workers with more purchasing power and makes the city an attractive location for businesses looking to recruit and retain top talent. However, the city's shrinking labor force is a significant risk, as it can limit business growth and expansion, making it essential for the city to address this challenge to maintain its economic momentum.
