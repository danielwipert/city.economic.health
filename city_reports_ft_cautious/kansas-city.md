# Kansas City

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 69.3th percentile | September 2026**

---

Kansas City earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking at the 69.3th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score of 5.99, and its top-tier wage growth of 7.35% year-over-year. These metrics signal a city with a robust job market and rising earnings.

**Labor Demand**
Kansas City's employment growth rate is 0.73% year-over-year, combined with a 0.665% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This indicates that the city's labor market is experiencing a real increase in demand for workers.

**Unemployment**
The city's unemployment rate is 3.80%, ranking at the 78th percentile, which means the market is relatively tight. This tightness implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as well as upward pressure on wages. However, it also suggests a skilled and engaged workforce.

**Wage Growth**
Wage growth in Kansas City is 7.35% year-over-year, placing it in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This fast wage growth means that employer labor costs are rising, but it also translates to increased purchasing power for workers. This can be beneficial for businesses looking to attract and retain top talent.

**Cost of Living**
Kansas City has a cost of living score that ranks at the 80th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $196/sqft to $36.37/hr, or 5.39. This means the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The affordability of Kansas City gives businesses a talent attraction advantage, as they may not need to offer wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Kansas City is growing at a rate of 0.34% year-over-year, which indicates that the workforce supply is slowly expanding. This expansion suggests that businesses will have a slightly easier time finding new hires, as the labor pool is growing, albeit slowly.

**Building Permits**
The city has seen a year-over-year decrease in building permits of 3.34%, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This decrease in permits suggests that the housing supply may be tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Kansas City is 50 days, with no year-over-year change. This relatively fast market means that relocating workers may face competition when looking for housing, making it potentially tougher for businesses to attract talent from other areas.

**Office Economy**
Kansas City's professional and office worker share ranks at the 28th percentile, indicating a less deep talent pool in these sectors compared to other cities. This makes Kansas City less suited for businesses requiring a large number of specialized office or tech workers, but it may still be attractive for other types of industries.

In conclusion, Kansas City offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion and top-tier wage growth, making it an attractive location for companies looking to tap into a skilled and engaged workforce. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential tightening of the housing supply due to decreasing building permits, which could impact future affordability and workforce accommodation.
