# Kansas City

**Grade: A+ (Excellent) | 70.4th percentile | July 2026**

---

Kansas City earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 70.4th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. The city's economic character is largely defined by its exceptionally high wage growth rate of 8.26% year-over-year and its low unemployment rate of 3.50%, which together signal a highly competitive labor market. These metrics suggest that Kansas City is an attractive location for businesses looking to tap into a skilled and productive workforce.

**Labor Demand**
Kansas City's employment growth rate of 0.25% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of 0.049% above its own 12-month baseline, indicates a moderate expansion of labor demand. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, rather than a survivor squeeze, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. The labor demand composite score of 4.70 corresponds to a near-median percentile rank of 54th, suggesting a stable but not exceptionally strong labor market.

**Unemployment**
The city's unemployment rate of 3.50% is notably low, corresponding to a top-tier percentile rank of 80th. This tight labor market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as well as upward pressure on wages. As a result, companies looking to staff up in Kansas City may need to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract top talent.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 8.26%, Kansas City is experiencing rapid wage inflation, ranking it in the 98th percentile. This fast wage growth will lead to rising labor costs for employers, but it also means that workers will have increased purchasing power, which can benefit local businesses. As a result, companies should factor in higher labor costs when considering a location in Kansas City.

**Cost of Living**
Kansas City's cost of living, with a PSF of $197/sqft and an hourly wage of $35.77, corresponds to a ratio of 5.51, ranking it in the 82nd percentile for affordability. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The fact that PSF is rising by 1.0% year-over-year, however, may indicate some upward pressure on housing costs.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Kansas City is contracting at a rate of -0.70% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This decline in labor force growth may pose a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of available workers is decreasing. As a result, businesses may need to invest in training and development programs to attract and retain talent in the city.

**Building Permits**
The city is experiencing a significant increase in residential building permits, with a year-over-year change of 51.78%, ranking it in the 92nd percentile. This surge in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. As a result, Kansas City is likely to remain an attractive location for workers and businesses alike.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market in Kansas City is 45 days, with no year-over-year change. This corresponds to a below-average percentile rank of 30th, indicating a relatively fast-paced market. For workers relocating to the city, this means that they may face a competitive housing market, with homes selling quickly.

**Office Economy**
Kansas City's professional and office worker share corresponds to a percentile rank of 30th, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. As a result, the city may be less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies. However, it may be more attractive to industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Kansas City offers businesses a highly competitive labor market with exceptional wage growth and low unemployment, making it an attractive location for companies looking to tap into a skilled and productive workforce. However, the city's shrinking labor force and competitive housing market may pose challenges for hiring and talent attraction, and businesses should carefully consider these factors when making a location decision. Overall, Kansas City's strengths make it a compelling choice for businesses, but its weaknesses require careful planning and strategy to overcome.
