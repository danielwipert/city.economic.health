# Kansas City

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 61.3th percentile | October 2026**

---

Kansas City has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 61.3th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 7.35%, and its labor demand composite score of 5.99, which indicates genuine demand expansion. These metrics suggest a city with a growing economy and increasing labor costs.

**Labor Demand**
Kansas City's employment growth rate is 0.73% year-over-year, and its weekly hours deviation from trend is 0.665%, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 5.99 further supports this conclusion, ranking the city in the 70th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Kansas City is 3.90%, which is relatively low and indicates a tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. With an unemployment rate ranking in the 64th percentile, the city's labor market has some slack, but not much.

**Wage Growth**
Wage growth in Kansas City is strong, with a year-over-year increase of 7.35%, ranking in the 92nd percentile. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing purchasing power for workers. As a result, businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, but can also expect a skilled and productive workforce.

**Cost of Living**
Kansas City has a cost of living score ranking it in the 80th percentile, indicating that it is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The city's price-to-salary ratio is $195/sqft vs $36.37/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.36. This affordability makes it an attractive location for talent, as workers can maintain a high standard of living without requiring excessive wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Kansas City is shrinking, with a year-over-year growth rate of -2.09%, ranking in the 20th percentile. This contraction in the labor force supply implies that businesses may face structural headwinds in hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. This could lead to increased competition for talent and upward pressure on wages.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Kansas City has decreased by 3.34% year-over-year, ranking in the 40th percentile. This decline in permits suggests that the city's housing supply is not expanding rapidly, which could lead to future affordability concerns and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Kansas City is 50 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 2.0%, ranking in the 16th percentile. This relatively fast pace of home sales indicates a competitive market, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find affordable housing. However, this also suggests a strong demand for housing and a vibrant local economy.

**Office Economy**
Kansas City's professional and office worker share is relatively low, ranking in the 28th percentile. This suggests that the city's talent pool is not as deep in specialized roles, making it less suited for businesses that require a high concentration of tech, finance, or consulting talent. However, the city may be more attractive to businesses with industrial or logistics-oriented operations.

In conclusion, Kansas City offers a strong and growing economy with increasing labor costs, making it an attractive location for businesses that can adapt to a competitive labor market. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor force, which could lead to increased competition for talent and upward pressure on wages, making it essential to factor this into their location decision.
