# Kansas City

**Grade: B+ (Above Average) | 62.8th percentile | November 2026**

---

Kansas City earns a B+ overall grade, with a strong economy driven by fast pay growth, at 7.6% over the past year. The city's low unemployment rate of 3.4% is also a notable strength. This combination makes Kansas City an attractive location for businesses and workers alike.

**Labor Demand**
Job growth is 0.5% over the past year, about average for most metros. This means employers may not face extreme pressure to hire, but workers still have decent job prospects.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.4%, lower than most metros. This makes hiring challenging for employers, as there are fewer available workers.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 7.6% over the past year, faster than most metros. This means employers face rising labor costs, while workers enjoy higher paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.4 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, at $194/sqft and $36.10/hr pay, making it more affordable than most metros. This attracts and retains workers, as the cost of living is relatively low.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 2.1% over the past year, slower than most metros. This makes it harder for employers to find new hires, as the labor pool is shrinking.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals rose 96.0% over the past year, stronger than most metros. This signals a future increase in housing supply, which could improve affordability.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 52 days to sell, up 2.0% from last year, about average for most metros. This indicates a relatively balanced housing market, neither too tight nor too soft.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This suits businesses that don't rely heavily on professional services, such as tech or finance.

Kansas City's biggest strength is its fast pay growth, making it an attractive location for workers. However, its biggest risk is the shrinking labor force, which may make it harder for employers to find new hires.
