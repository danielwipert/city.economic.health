# San Antonio-New Braunfels

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 36.2th percentile | August 2026**

---

The San Antonio-New Braunfels metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 36.2th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low labor demand and high cost of living affordability. The city's labor demand composite score of 3.99 and unemployment rate of 4.80% are key metrics defining its current economic character, with the latter indicating a relatively loose labor market. Specifically, the labor demand is characterized by employment growth of +0.85% YoY and weekly hours deviation of -1.731% from its own trend.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate of +0.85% YoY and weekly hours deviation of -1.731% from its own trend signal a contraction in labor demand, as hours are running below trend despite job growth. This combination suggests that the job market is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The labor demand composite score of 3.99, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate of 4.80% indicates a labor market with some slack, rather than being tight. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city may find it relatively easier to staff, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The unemployment rate's percentile rank of 12th highlights the challenges of a loose labor market.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate of +2.20% is below average, ranking at the 30th percentile. This moderate wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, albeit not rapidly. The wage growth rate suggests a relatively flat cost environment for businesses.

**Cost of Living**
The city's cost of living, with a PSF of $172/sqft and earnings of $32.47/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.30, is relatively affordable, ranking in the top tier at the 88th percentile. The fact that PSF is falling by -4.4% YoY further enhances affordability. This means that the city has a talent attraction advantage, as workers can be drawn without needing significant wage premiums to offset high living costs.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of +0.23% YoY, indicating a slowly expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the hiring capacity in the city is not constrained by a shrinking labor pool, providing a slight advantage for businesses looking to expand.

**Building Permits**
The year-over-year change in residential building permits is +5.83%, indicating an expansion in housing supply. This growth in permits signals that developer confidence is present, and future housing supply is likely to improve, which should help maintain or improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce.

**Days on Market**
The current median days on market is 65 days, with a YoY direction of +0.0%, indicating a stable market. For a worker relocating to this city, the housing market is neither extremely competitive nor overly accessible, providing a relatively balanced environment for those looking to move.

**Office Economy**
The share of jobs in professional and office sectors, with a composite score of 3.22, ranks above average at the 76th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy roles but may be less ideal for those dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

The San Antonio-New Braunfels metro area offers businesses a unique combination of affordability and a growing labor force, albeit with moderate wage growth and a loose labor market. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand, which may signal underlying economic challenges that could impact business expansion and growth prospects.
