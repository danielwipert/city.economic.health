# Jacksonville

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 30.7th percentile | November 2026**

---

Jacksonville has a C- overall grade, with 30.7 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by weaker hiring, with only 0.3% employment growth over the past year. This slow growth is a major concern.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is 0.3% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means people looking for work may struggle to find jobs.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 4.5%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for businesses to hire, but also indicates weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is 1.0% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers have lower labor costs, but workers have less bargaining power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 5.8 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with a price of $212 per square foot and hourly pay of $36.45, making it less affordable than most metros. This makes it harder to attract and keep workers, especially with prices only falling 1.4% over the past year.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce grew -0.1% over the past year, near the middle of most metros. This means employers face a relatively stable hiring environment.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 10.8% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This signals a potential future housing supply squeeze and rising prices.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 66 days to sell, with sales getting 13.2% faster over the past year. This indicates a healthier housing market, making it easier for people to move here.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is about average compared to other metros. This suits businesses that need a mix of professional and other services.

The city's biggest strength is its relatively healthy housing market, with homes selling faster. However, its biggest risk is weaker hiring and slower pay growth, making it harder to attract and retain workers.
