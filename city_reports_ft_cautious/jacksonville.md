# Jacksonville

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 30.6th percentile | September 2026**

---

The city of Jacksonville has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 30.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 3.86, and its high unemployment rate of 4.90%, which ranks in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

**Labor Demand**
Jacksonville's employment growth rate is a modest +0.06% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.520% from the city's own 12-month trend. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The low labor demand composite score of 3.86, ranking in the 20th percentile, further supports this assessment.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Jacksonville is 4.90%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This high unemployment rate indicates a slack market, making it easier for businesses to hire workers. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand, which could impact businesses that rely on local spending.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate in Jacksonville is +1.34%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This slow wage growth rate suggests a relatively flat cost environment for employers, but it also means that workers have weak bargaining power. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but workers may not have the purchasing power to drive local economic growth.

**Cost of Living**
Jacksonville has a cost of living score ranking in the 55th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $214/sqft to $37.45/hr, or 5.71. The fact that PSF is falling -2.3% year-over-year relative to wages is a key driver of this near-median affordability score. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which could be a talent attraction advantage for businesses without needing to offer significant wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Jacksonville is shrinking at a rate of -0.29% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 50th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply implies a structural headwind for hiring, making it more challenging for businesses to find and recruit workers.

**Building Permits**
The number of residential building permits in Jacksonville is increasing by +8.24% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 54th percentile. This expansion in housing supply signals that developer confidence is growing, and future affordability and workforce accommodation may improve. However, the current pace of growth is not exceptionally strong, so businesses should not expect significant relief from affordability pressures in the short term.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Jacksonville is currently 65 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -12.2%. This relatively fast market, ranking in the 78th percentile, means that homes are selling quickly, which could make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. Businesses may need to offer relocation assistance or other incentives to attract talent to the area.

**Office Economy**
Jacksonville's professional and office worker share ranks near the median at the 58th percentile, with a composite score of 2.80. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool, making the city suited for businesses that require specialized office or professional roles, but it may not be the best fit for companies that need a highly specialized or abundant tech/finance/consulting workforce.

The city of Jacksonville offers businesses a relatively affordable cost of living and a slack labor market, making it easier to hire workers. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the low labor demand and slow wage growth, which could limit the city's ability to attract and retain top talent. Businesses should carefully consider these factors when evaluating Jacksonville as a potential location for expansion or relocation.
