# Jacksonville

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 30.7th percentile | November 2026**

Jacksonville has an overall grade of C-, scoring 30.7 out of 100 across 50 US metros, with its economy defined by slower job growth and higher unemployment compared to other cities. This slower hiring and reduced hours suggest that the labor demand in Jacksonville is weaker than in most cities, scoring 30 out of 100.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Jacksonville is 4.5%, which is higher than in most metros, scoring 20 out of 100, making it relatively easier for employers to hire but also indicating weaker local consumer demand. With a higher unemployment rate, businesses may find it less challenging to staff their operations, but the local economy may suffer from reduced consumer spending.

This slow wage growth means that workers' paychecks are not increasing as quickly as in other cities, which could impact their purchasing power and overall quality of life.

The cost of living in Jacksonville is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $212 and hourly pay of $36.45, scoring 33 out of 100, making it less affordable than most metros, although housing prices are falling by 1.4% over the past year. This decrease in housing prices is a positive sign, but the city's affordability is still a weakness compared to other metros.

**Labor Force Growth**
The labor force in Jacksonville is shrinking by 0.1% over the past year, scoring 52 out of 100, which is about average for the 50 metros, indicating a relatively stable but not expanding workforce. This slow growth in the labor force means that hiring may become more challenging in the future, as the pool of available workers is not increasing.

New home approvals in Jacksonville have decreased by 10.8% over the past year, scoring 30 out of 100, which signals a weaker home-building market and potential future housing supply constraints. This decline in building permits suggests that the city's housing market may become less affordable and less attractive to workers and businesses in the future.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Jacksonville are taking 66 days to sell, with a 13.2% decrease in days on market over the past year, scoring 78 out of 100, indicating a healthier housing market than in most metros. This relatively fast pace of home sales suggests a strong demand for housing in the city, making it easier for sellers to find buyers and for the market to absorb new inventory.

**Office Economy**
Jacksonville's office-based workforce is about average compared to other metros, scoring 56 out of 100, which suits businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. The city's office economy is neither a major strength nor a weakness, making it a decent location for companies in these industries, but not necessarily a hub for innovation or entrepreneurship.

Jacksonville's biggest strength is its relatively healthy housing market, with homes selling quickly, while its biggest risk is its slower job growth and higher unemployment, which could impact the local economy and make it less attractive to businesses and workers. Overall, the city's economy is facing challenges, but its housing market is a bright spot that could help drive future growth and development.
