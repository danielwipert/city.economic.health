# Jacksonville

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 31.6th percentile | October 2026**

---

The city of Jacksonville has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 31.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 3.86, and its high unemployment rate of 4.80%, which ranks in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

**Labor Demand**
Jacksonville's employment growth rate is a mere 0.06% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.520% from its own 12-month baseline, indicating a lack of genuine demand expansion. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than a survivor squeeze, as hours are not increasing to absorb eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 3.86 is below average, ranking at the 20th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Jacksonville is 4.80%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile, indicating a market with some slack. This means that businesses may find it relatively easier to hire workers, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The unemployment rate suggests that the market is not tight, giving employers some leverage in hiring and wage negotiations.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate in Jacksonville is 1.34%, which is relatively slow and ranks in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This stagnant wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power is also not increasing significantly. The slow wage growth may attract businesses looking for a flat cost environment, but it also indicates weak bargaining power for workers.

**Cost of Living**
Jacksonville has a cost of living ratio of $213/sqft to $37.45/hr, with a PSF falling -1.4% year-over-year, resulting in a near-median affordability score ranking at the 46th percentile. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The falling PSF is a key driver of this affordability, making it easier for businesses to attract and retain workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Jacksonville is contracting at a rate of -0.56% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of workers is decreasing. Businesses may face challenges in finding qualified workers, and may need to invest in training or recruitment programs to attract talent.

**Building Permits**
The number of residential building permits in Jacksonville is increasing by 8.24% year-over-year, indicating an expansion of housing supply. This growth in permits suggests that developer confidence is rising, and future housing supply is likely to increase, improving affordability and workforce accommodation. The increasing permits are a positive sign for businesses looking to relocate or expand, as it indicates a growing and more affordable housing market.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market in Jacksonville is 66 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -12.0%, resulting in an above-average ranking at the 68th percentile. This means that homes are selling relatively quickly, making it a competitive market for workers relocating to the city. However, the decreasing days on market also suggests a hot market, which may make it challenging for workers to find affordable housing.

**Office Economy**
Jacksonville's professional and office worker share is 2.76, ranking near the median at the 57th percentile, indicating a moderate depth of talent pool. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a highly specialized or deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics-dominant companies. However, it may not be the best fit for tech, finance, or consulting businesses that require a highly skilled and specialized workforce.

The city of Jacksonville offers businesses a relatively affordable cost of living and a slowing housing market, but its low labor demand and high unemployment rate pose significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor pool, which may limit the city's ability to support business growth and expansion. Despite these challenges, Jacksonville's growing housing supply and moderate talent pool make it an attractive location for certain types of businesses.
