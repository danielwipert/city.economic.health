# Jacksonville

**Grade: C (Very Poor) | 37.0th percentile | August 2026**

---

Jacksonville has an overall grade of C, ranking 37th out of 50 US metros, with a composite score that reflects its mixed economic performance. The city's labor demand and wage growth metrics, at 18th and 6th percentiles respectively, most define its current economic character, with employment growth at +0.36% and weekly hours deviation at -0.794%. These numbers signal a weak labor market with potential survivor squeeze, where remaining workers may be absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

**Labor Demand**
Jacksonville's employment growth rate is +0.36%, and its weekly hours deviation is -0.794%, indicating a contraction in labor demand. This combination signals a lack of genuine demand expansion, as hours are below trend despite modest job growth. The labor demand composite score of 4.01 is in the bottom tier, at the 18th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Jacksonville is 4.80%, which is in the bottom tier, at the 18th percentile. This indicates a relatively slack labor market, making it easier for businesses to hire, but also suggesting weaker local consumer demand. With this level of unemployment, businesses may face less wage pressure compared to tighter labor markets.

**Wage Growth**
Wage growth in Jacksonville is +0.39% year-over-year, which is in the bottom tier, at the 6th percentile. This stagnant wage growth implies a flat cost environment for employers, but also weak bargaining power for workers. As a result, worker purchasing power may not be increasing significantly, which could impact local consumer demand.

**Cost of Living**
Jacksonville has a cost of living score that ranks at the 76th percentile, with a PSF of $216/sqft, which is decreasing by -3.1% year-over-year, relative to average hourly earnings of $36.85. This makes the city more affordable compared to its peers, which could be a talent attraction advantage without requiring wage premiums. The decreasing PSF is a key driver of this affordability.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Jacksonville is growing at a rate of +0.78% year-over-year, which is above average, at the 68th percentile. This expanding labor force supply implies a positive trend for hiring capacity, as more workers are available to fill job openings. This growth could support business expansion and relocation decisions.

**Building Permits**
Building permits in Jacksonville are increasing by +15.03% year-over-year, which is above average, at the 70th percentile. This expansion in housing supply signals improving affordability and accommodation for the workforce in the future. As housing supply increases, it may alleviate some of the pressure on the local real estate market.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market in Jacksonville is 63 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -12.5%, which ranks at the 70th percentile. This relatively fast market means homes are selling quickly, which could make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the decreasing trend may indicate a normalization of the market.

**Office Economy**
Jacksonville's professional and office worker share is at the 56th percentile, indicating a near-median depth of talent pool. This makes the city suited for businesses that require a moderate level of specialized knowledge workers, but it may not be the best fit for tech, finance, or consulting firms that demand a deeper talent pool. The city's economy is more diverse, with a mix of industries beyond just office sectors.

In conclusion, Jacksonville offers a mixed bag for businesses, with a relatively affordable cost of living and expanding labor force, but also a weak labor market and stagnant wage growth. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited depth of professional talent, which may impact the ability to attract and retain specialized workers, particularly in knowledge-intensive industries.
