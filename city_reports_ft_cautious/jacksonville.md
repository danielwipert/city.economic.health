# Jacksonville

**Grade: C+ (Poor) | 39.1th percentile | August 2026**

---

The city of Jacksonville has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 39.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 4.80% and its stagnant wage growth of -0.68% year-over-year, indicating a tight labor market with weak wage pressure. These metrics suggest that while businesses may face challenges in hiring, they may also benefit from a relatively stable labor cost environment.

**Labor Demand**
Jacksonville's employment growth rate is 0.33% year-over-year, and its weekly hours deviation is -0.372% below its own trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 4.39, which ranks below average at the 32nd percentile. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion or survivor squeeze. The below-average labor demand suggests that businesses may not face intense competition for workers.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Jacksonville is 4.80%, ranking it at the 18th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is relatively small. As a result, businesses may need to offer competitive wages or benefits to attract top talent.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in Jacksonville is -0.68%, ranking it at the 2nd percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth environment means that employer labor costs are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power is also not increasing. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but may also not benefit from a highly motivated workforce.

**Cost of Living**
Jacksonville has a cost of living score ranking it at the 69th percentile, with a PSF of $216/sqft and average hourly earnings of $36.43, resulting in a ratio of 5.93. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The affordability of Jacksonville means that businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Jacksonville is growing at a rate of 0.78% year-over-year, ranking it above average at the 68th percentile. This expanding labor force supply implies that businesses have a growing pool of potential workers to hire from, making it easier to find talent. As a result, businesses may face less competition for workers and have more opportunities to attract top talent.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Jacksonville is increasing by 15.03% year-over-year, ranking it above average at the 70th percentile. This expansion in housing supply suggests that the city is becoming more accommodating to a growing workforce, which may lead to improved affordability and reduced pressure on the housing market. As a result, businesses may find it easier to attract and retain workers who can afford to live in the area.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market in Jacksonville is 63 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 12.5%, ranking it above average at the 70th percentile. This indicates a relatively fast-moving market, making it accessible for workers relocating to the city. As a result, businesses may find it easier to attract talent from other areas, as workers can quickly find and purchase or rent homes.

**Office Economy**
Jacksonville's professional and office worker share ranks at the 54th percentile, indicating a near-median depth of talent pool. This suggests that the city is suited for businesses that require a moderate level of specialized knowledge workers, but may not be the best fit for businesses that require a highly specialized or deep talent pool, such as tech or finance companies.

The city of Jacksonville offers businesses a relatively affordable and accessible environment, with a growing labor force and expanding housing supply. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses is the tight labor market and stagnant wage growth, which may limit the availability of skilled workers and create challenges for hiring and retention. As a result, businesses should carefully consider their hiring strategies and compensation packages to attract and retain top talent in this market.
