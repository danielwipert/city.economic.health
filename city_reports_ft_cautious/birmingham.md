# Birmingham

**Grade: B- (Below Average) | 45.4th percentile | September 2026**

---

Birmingham, with an overall grade of B- and a composite score ranking it 45.4th percentile out of 50 US metros, is characterized by its near-median labor demand and cost of living, but notably weak wage growth at +0.57% year-over-year. The city's economic character is most defined by its sluggish wage growth and declining labor force, which contracted by -1.10% year-over-year. These metrics suggest that while Birmingham has some positive trends, it also faces challenges in terms of labor market vitality.

**Labor Demand**
Birmingham's employment growth rate of +0.65% year-over-year, combined with a +0.140% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit a modest one. This combination indicates that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in slightly more hours than usual, suggesting a degree of labor market tightening. However, the near-median composite score of 5.35 suggests that this demand is not exceptionally strong.

**Unemployment**
The city's unemployment rate of 3.50% is near the median, indicating a labor market with some slack but not excessive weakness. This rate suggests that businesses may face moderate competition for talent, but it is not an extremely tight market. As a result, hiring may be manageable, but wage pressure is not extreme.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of +0.57%, Birmingham's labor market is experiencing stagnant wage growth, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile. This slow wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are not rising quickly, but it also means that workers have limited bargaining power and purchasing power is not increasing rapidly.

**Cost of Living**
Birmingham's cost of living, with a PSF of $159/sqft and average hourly earnings of $32.79, resulting in a ratio of 4.85, is near the median in terms of affordability, ranking at the 49th percentile. This suggests that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which could be an advantage for attracting talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Birmingham is contracting at a rate of -1.10% year-over-year, indicating that the supply of workers is shrinking. This contraction implies that businesses may face structural headwinds in hiring and expanding their workforce, as the pool of available workers is decreasing.

**Building Permits**
The city saw a +39.50% year-over-year increase in building permits, which is a top-tier performance, ranking at the 86th percentile. This surge in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which could improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce in the future.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Birmingham are currently sitting on the market for a median of 59 days, with a year-over-year increase of +1.7%. This indicates a moderate pace of home sales, neither extremely fast nor slow. For workers relocating to the city, this suggests a relatively accessible housing market, but not one that is overly competitive.

**Office Economy**
With an office and professional worker share ranking at the 46th percentile, Birmingham has a moderately deep talent pool in these sectors. This makes the city suited for businesses that require a mix of professional and office skills, but it may not be the best fit for companies requiring extremely specialized or abundant tech, finance, or consulting talent.

In conclusion, Birmingham offers a relatively affordable cost of living and an expanding housing supply, which could attract businesses looking for a cost-effective location. However, the city's slow wage growth and contracting labor force are significant risks that decision-makers should consider, as they may limit the city's ability to support long-term business expansion and talent attraction.
