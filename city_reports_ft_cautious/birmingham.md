# Birmingham

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 34.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

Birmingham has an overall grade of C-, scoring 34.3 out of 100 across 50 US metros, with its economy defined by slower hiring and weaker wage growth, including a mere 1.2% increase in employment over the past year. The city's labor market is further challenged by a high unemployment rate of 3.5%, which is higher than most metros, scoring 36 out of 100.

**Labor Demand**
Birmingham is adding jobs, albeit slowly, with a 1.2% increase in employment over the past year, and workers' hours are steady, with no change from their 12-month average, earning a score of 68 out of 100. This indicates a relatively strong labor demand, with the city hiring more workers than most metros, but the slow pace of job growth may limit opportunities for workers.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Birmingham is 3.5%, which is higher than most metros, scoring 36 out of 100, making it relatively easier for employers to hire, but also indicating weaker local consumer demand. With a higher unemployment rate than most cities, Birmingham has a larger pool of potential workers, but this also suggests that the local economy may not be as robust as others.

**Wage Growth**
Wages in Birmingham have actually decreased by 0.4% over the past year, earning a score of 2 out of 100, which means that employers may face lower labor costs, but workers may see reduced purchasing power. This slow wage growth is a weakness for the city, as it may make it harder to attract and retain top talent, especially compared to other metros with faster-rising wages.

**Cost of Living**
The cost of living in Birmingham is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $157 and hourly pay of $32.33, earning a score of 29 out of 100, and it's getting worse, with housing prices falling by 0.6% over the past year, but still outpacing wage growth. This makes it less affordable for local workers to live in the city, which could be a disadvantage for businesses looking to attract talent, as they may need to offer higher wages to compensate for the high cost of living.

**Labor Force Growth**
The labor force in Birmingham is shrinking, with a 0.7% decrease over the past year, earning a score of 34 out of 100, which means that the pool of potential workers is getting smaller, making it harder for businesses to find the talent they need. This slow labor force growth is a weakness for the city, as it may limit the availability of skilled workers and make it harder for businesses to expand.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals in Birmingham have decreased by 23.8% over the past year, earning a score of 10 out of 100, which signals that the city's housing supply may not be keeping up with demand, potentially leading to higher prices and reduced affordability. This decline in building permits is a weakness for the city, as it may limit the availability of housing options for workers and make it harder for businesses to attract talent.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Birmingham are taking 66 days to sell, which is getting slower, with a 10% increase over the past year, earning a score of 8 out of 100, indicating a softer housing market. This slower pace of home sales is a weakness for the city, as it may indicate that the housing market is cooling, and buyers are becoming more cautious, which could impact the local economy.

**Office Economy**
Birmingham's office-based workforce is about average, with a score of 52 out of 100, which means that the city has a moderate-sized pool of tech, finance, and professional services workers, making it suitable for businesses in these sectors. This average score indicates that Birmingham is not a hub for office-based industries, but it still has a sizable workforce in these areas, which could support businesses looking to establish or expand operations in the city.

Birmingham's biggest strength is its relatively strong labor demand, with the city hiring more workers than most metros, but its biggest risk is its slow wage growth, which may make it harder to attract and retain top talent, and limit the city's ability to compete with other metros for businesses and workers. Overall, Birmingham's economy is facing challenges, including a high unemployment rate, slow wage growth, and a shrinking labor force, which may impact its ability to attract businesses and talent in the future.
