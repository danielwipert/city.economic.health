# Birmingham

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 34.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

Birmingham has a C- overall grade. The city's economy is defined by slow job growth, with a 1.2% increase in employment over the past year.

**Labor Demand**
The city added jobs over the past year. Workers' hours are steady, indicating a stable job market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.5%. This makes it easier for employers to hire, but also signals weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay rose -0.4% over the past year. This means employers' labor costs are flat, but workers' paychecks are not growing.

**Cost of Living**
Housing is less affordable, with a price of $157 per square foot and $32.33 hourly pay. Prices are falling slightly, but the city remains expensive.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking at a rate of -0.7% per year. This makes hiring more challenging for employers.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 23.8% over the past year. This signals a weaker future housing supply and potential affordability issues.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 66 days to sell, up 10% from last year. This indicates a softening housing market, making it harder for sellers.

**Office Economy**
The city has an average-sized office-based workforce. This suits businesses like tech, finance, and professional services, but is not a standout.

Birmingham's biggest strength is its stable job market, with steady hours and slow job growth. Its biggest risk is a shrinking workforce and weak housing market, making it harder for employers to attract and retain talent.
