# Birmingham

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 34.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

Birmingham has a C- overall grade, with 34.3 out of 100. The city's economy is defined by slower hiring and lower pay growth, with employment growing 1.2% over the past year. This growth is stronger than most metros.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 1.2% more jobs over the past year, with weekly hours unchanged. This means people looking for work have a decent chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.5%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for businesses to hire, but also signals weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Pay grew -0.4% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers face flat labor costs, but workers have weak bargaining power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 4.9 hours of pay to buy one square foot of home, with prices at $157/sqft and pay at $32.33/hr. This makes the city less affordable than most metros, requiring wage premiums to attract workers.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 0.7% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers face a structural headwind when trying to hire.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 23.8% over the past year. This signals a future supply squeeze, putting upward pressure on prices and making housing less affordable.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 66 days to sell, up 10% from last year. This indicates a weakening housing market, making it harder for people to move here.

**Office Economy**
The city has an average share of office-based jobs, near the middle of the 50 metros. This suits businesses that need a general workforce, but not those requiring specialized talent.

Birmingham's biggest strength is its stronger hiring, but its biggest risk is the slow pay growth, which can lead to a brain drain and weaker consumer demand. The city's high cost of living and shrinking workforce also pose significant challenges for businesses and workers alike.
