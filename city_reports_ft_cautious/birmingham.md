# Birmingham

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 34.3th percentile | November 2026**

---

Birmingham earns a C- overall grade, with 34.3 out of 100 points. The city's economy is defined by slow hiring and weak wage growth, with employment growing 1.2% over the past year. This modest growth is a concern for businesses and workers.

**Labor Demand**
The city added 1.2% more jobs over the past year, better than most metros. This means people looking for work have a relatively strong chance of finding a job.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.5%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for businesses to hire, but also indicates weaker local consumer demand.

**Wage Growth**
Wages fell 0.4% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers face flat labor costs, but workers have limited bargaining power.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 4.9 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, at $157/sqft and $32.33/hr pay, less affordable than most metros. This makes it harder to attract and keep workers, as the cost of living is relatively high.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce shrank 0.7% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers face a shrinking labor pool, making hiring more challenging.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals fell 23.8% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This signals a potential supply squeeze, which could drive up housing prices and make the city less attractive.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 66 days to sell, up 10% from last year, a weaker housing market. This indicates a softening market, making it harder for people to sell their homes and relocate.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is about average, near the middle of the 50 metros. This suits businesses that need a mix of professional and non-professional services, but may not be ideal for those seeking a deep tech or finance talent pool.

The city's biggest strength is its relatively strong labor demand, with employment growing faster than most metros. However, its biggest risk is the slow wage growth, which may limit workers' purchasing power and make it harder for businesses to attract and retain talent.
