# Birmingham

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 48.2th percentile | October 2026**

---

Birmingham, with an overall grade of C+ and a composite score ranking it 48.2th percentile out of 50 US metros, is characterized by its above-average labor demand and top-tier building permit growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.94, driven by a 0.92% employment growth rate and a 0.349% weekly hours deviation above its own trend, signals genuine demand expansion. Additionally, the 36.73% year-over-year increase in building permits highlights a significant expansion in housing supply.

**Labor Demand**
Birmingham's employment growth rate of 0.92% and weekly hours deviation of 0.349% above its own trend indicate a genuine demand expansion, suggesting that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours than usual. This combination signals a strong labor market where businesses can find workers with increasing hours, indicating a healthy economy. The labor demand composite score of 5.94 further reinforces this, ranking above average at the 64th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Birmingham stands at 3.40%, placing it near the median at the 50th percentile. This rate suggests a balanced labor market with neither too much slack nor too much tightness. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate implies a manageable hiring process without excessive wage pressure or undue difficulty in finding qualified candidates.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 0.03%, Birmingham experiences stagnant wage growth, ranking at the bottom tier of the 2nd percentile. This slow wage growth environment means labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also indicates weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. This could affect the attractiveness of the city for talent seeking better compensation.

**Cost of Living**
Birmingham's cost of living, with a PSF of $157/sqft and average hourly earnings of $32.71/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.80, places it near the median at the 43rd percentile. The city is neither exceptionally affordable nor overly expensive compared to its peers. However, the 1.3% year-over-year decrease in PSF relative to wages slightly improves its affordability. This moderate cost of living means that businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to attract talent, but it does not provide a substantial talent attraction advantage either.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Birmingham is contracting at a rate of -1.30% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This negative growth rate, ranking near the median at the 46th percentile, suggests a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses may face challenges in finding new talent as the labor force contracts.

**Building Permits**
The city experienced a 36.73% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, signaling a significant expansion in housing supply and ranking at the top tier of the 92nd percentile. This rapid growth in permits suggests that developer confidence is high, and future housing supply is expected to increase, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Birmingham currently sit on the market for 61 days, with a 5.2% year-over-year increase. This increase in days on market, ranking below average at the 31st percentile, indicates a slightly slower market. For workers relocating to this city, the market is becoming more accessible, as homes are not selling as quickly as they were, providing more time for decision-making.

**Office Economy**
Birmingham's professional and office worker share, with a composite score of 2.46, places it near the median at the 48th percentile. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool suited for businesses requiring professional and office skills, such as tech, finance, and consulting. However, it is less suited for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

In conclusion, Birmingham offers a balanced labor market with genuine demand expansion and a rapidly expanding housing supply, which could attract businesses looking for a stable environment with improving affordability. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the stagnant wage growth, which could limit the city's appeal to top talent and affect local purchasing power, potentially impacting businesses reliant on a highly skilled and well-compensated workforce.
