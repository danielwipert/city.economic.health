# Birmingham

**Grade: C+ (Fair) | 48.3th percentile | October 2026**

---

Birmingham, with an overall grade of C+ and a composite score ranking it 48.3th percentile out of 50 US metros, is characterized by its above-average labor demand and top-tier building permit growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.94, driven by a +0.92% employment growth rate and +0.349% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, signals genuine demand expansion. Additionally, its building permits growth of +36.73% year-over-year highlights a strong developer confidence in the market.

**Labor Demand**
Birmingham's employment growth rate of +0.92% and weekly hours deviation of +0.349% from its own trend indicate a genuine demand expansion, suggesting that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours than usual. This combination signals a healthy labor market where businesses can find workers with increasing hours, indicating a strong demand for labor. The labor demand composite score of 5.94 further reinforces this, ranking above average at the 64th percentile.

**Unemployment**
The city's unemployment rate of 3.40% places it near the median, ranking at the 50th percentile. This rate suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, this also implies that local consumer demand might not be as strong as in areas with lower unemployment rates.

**Wage Growth**
With a year-over-year wage growth rate of +0.03%, Birmingham's wage growth is stagnant, ranking at the bottom tier of the 2nd percentile. This slow wage growth environment means that labor costs for employers are not increasing rapidly, but it also signifies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power.

**Cost of Living**
Birmingham's cost of living, with a PSF of $157/sqft and an earnings ratio of $32.71/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.80, ranks near the median at the 43rd percentile. The city's PSF is decreasing by -1.3% year-over-year, which is a key driver of its affordability. This affordability makes the city more attractive for talent without necessitating significant wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Birmingham is contracting at a rate of -1.30% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 46th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is shrinking.

**Building Permits**
The city's residential building permits have increased by +36.73% year-over-year, placing it in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This significant growth in building permits signals an expansion in future housing supply, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Birmingham currently sit on the market for a median of 61 days, with a year-over-year increase of +5.2%. This increase in days on market, ranking below average at the 32nd percentile, suggests a slightly slower market, which could be more accessible for relocating workers.

**Office Economy**
Birmingham's professional and office worker share, with a composite score of 2.46, ranks near the median at the 48th percentile. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool suited for businesses requiring professional and office skills, though it may not be as competitive as cities with higher rankings in this metric.

In conclusion, Birmingham offers a mix of above-average labor demand and top-tier building permit growth, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to expand. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the stagnant wage growth, which, while keeping labor costs low, may also limit worker purchasing power and attract fewer skilled workers due to the lack of wage growth opportunities.
