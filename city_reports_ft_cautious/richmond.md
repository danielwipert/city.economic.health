# Richmond

**Grade: D (Emergency) | 25.1th percentile | November 2026**

---

Richmond has an overall grade of D, with a score of 25.1 out of 100, indicating significant economic challenges. The city's economy is currently defined by a decline in job growth, with employment shrinking by 1.0% over the past year, and a relatively high unemployment rate of 3.8%, which is higher than most metros.

**Labor Demand**
The city is losing jobs, with employment down 1.0% over the past year, and workers' hours are also falling, by 0.1%, indicating a weak job market. This means that the city is not creating new opportunities for workers, and existing employees may be shouldering a heavier workload.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Richmond is 3.8%, which is higher than most metros, making it relatively easier for employers to hire, but also indicating weaker local consumer demand. With a score of 34 out of 100, the city's unemployment rate is a weakness, suggesting that many workers are still seeking employment.

**Wage Growth**
Wages in Richmond rose by 1.6% over the past year, which is slower than most metros, with a score of 16 out of 100, indicating that employers may not face significant pressure to increase pay. However, this slow wage growth also means that workers may not see significant increases in their paychecks.

**Cost of Living**
The cost of living in Richmond is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $226 and hourly pay of $35.71, and it is getting worse, with housing prices rising 0.4% over the past year. With a score of 18 out of 100, the city's cost of living is a weakness, making it less affordable for local workers compared to other metros.

**Labor Force Growth**
The pool of workers in Richmond is shrinking, with a decline of 2.2% over the past year, which is slower than most metros, making it a challenge for employers to find new talent. This decline in labor force growth is a weakness, with a score of 6 out of 100, indicating a structural headwind for hiring.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals in Richmond surged by 130.8% over the past year, which is stronger than most metros, signaling a significant increase in future housing supply. With a score of 96 out of 100, this is a strength for the city, indicating developer confidence and potential improvements in affordability.

**Days on Market**
Homes in Richmond are selling relatively quickly, with a median of 44 days on market, but this is getting slower, with a decline of 2.2% over the past year. With a score of 16 out of 100, the city's housing market is a weakness, indicating a softer market where buyers may be pulling back.

**Office Economy**
Richmond's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros, with a score of 22 out of 100, making it less suited for businesses that rely on professional and technical services. This weakness suggests that the city may not be the best fit for companies in the tech, finance, or consulting sectors.

The bottom line is that Richmond's biggest strength is its surge in new home approvals, which could lead to increased housing supply and improved affordability, but its biggest risk is its declining labor force and weak job market, which could make it challenging for employers to find and retain talent. Overall, the city's economic challenges, including high unemployment and slow wage growth, make it a less attractive location for businesses and workers alike.
