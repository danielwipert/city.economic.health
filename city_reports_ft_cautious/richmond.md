# Richmond

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 31.4th percentile | August 2026**

---

The Richmond metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 31.4th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its tight unemployment market, which ranks near the median at 56th percentile. Specifically, the labor demand composite score of 3.05 and unemployment rate of 3.80% are key metrics that shape the city's economic landscape.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate in Richmond is -0.72% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.245%. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a decline in job growth, and the slight increase in hours worked may be due to a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This suggests that businesses may face challenges in expanding their workforce in this market.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Richmond is 3.80%, which is near the median, ranking at the 56th percentile. This indicates that the market has some slack, but it is not overly loose, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire new talent. As a result, businesses may need to offer competitive wages to attract workers in this market.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth in Richmond is +2.59%, which is below average, ranking at the 36th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, but not rapidly. This may impact businesses' decisions on staffing and compensation packages.

**Cost of Living**
Richmond has a cost of living score that ranks at the 20th percentile, with a PSF of $229/sqft and average hourly earnings of $35.37, resulting in a ratio of 6.47. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is increasing by 1.8% year-over-year further exacerbates the affordability issue.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Richmond is contracting at a rate of -0.80% year-over-year, indicating that the workforce supply is shrinking. This decline in labor force growth poses a structural headwind for hiring, making it more challenging for businesses to find and recruit new talent in this market.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Richmond is increasing by 8.22% year-over-year, which is above average, ranking at the 60th percentile. This expansion in housing supply is a positive sign for future affordability and workforce accommodation, suggesting that the city may become more attractive to workers and businesses in the long run.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market in Richmond is currently 40 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -7.0%. This rapid pace of home sales indicates a competitive market, making it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing. The low percentile rank of 2nd percentile further emphasizes the competitive nature of the market.

**Office Economy**
Richmond's professional and office worker share is below average, ranking at the 26th percentile, with a composite score of 1.70. This suggests that the city has a relatively shallow talent pool in professional and office sectors, making it less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms.

In conclusion, Richmond offers a mixed bag for businesses, with a tightening labor market and moderate wage growth, but also a relatively expensive cost of living and a competitive housing market. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand and shrinking workforce supply, which may limit businesses' ability to expand and hire new talent in this market.
