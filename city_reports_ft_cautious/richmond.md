# Richmond

**Grade: C- (Critical) | 30.9th percentile | September 2026**

---

The Richmond metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 30.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand composite score of 3.24, which ranks in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, and its unemployment rate of 3.80%, which is near the median at the 54th percentile. These metrics suggest a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

**Labor Demand**
The employment growth rate in Richmond is -0.76% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.415% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a decline in job growth and hours worked. This suggests a lack of genuine demand expansion, which could be a concern for businesses looking to hire and expand.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Richmond is 3.80%, which is near the median at the 54th percentile. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, but not excessively so. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate means that there may be a sufficient pool of candidates, but wage pressure may not be as intense as in tighter labor markets.

**Wage Growth**
The year-over-year wage growth rate in Richmond is +2.49%, which ranks below average at the 32nd percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. However, worker purchasing power may not be increasing as quickly as in other cities with faster wage growth.

**Cost of Living**
Richmond has a cost of living score that ranks below average at the 24th percentile, with a PSF of $228/sqft and average hourly earnings of $35.92/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.35. This suggests that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. The fact that PSF is increasing by 1.8% year-over-year further exacerbates the affordability concern. This may make it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

**Labor Force Growth**
The civilian labor force in Richmond is contracting at a rate of -0.94% year-over-year, which ranks below average at the 32nd percentile. This decline in labor force supply suggests that the city may face structural headwinds in hiring and expanding its workforce. Businesses may need to consider strategies to attract talent from other areas or invest in workforce development programs.

**Building Permits**
The number of building permits in Richmond is increasing by 10.33% year-over-year, which ranks above average at the 62nd percentile. This expansion in housing supply suggests that the city may be becoming more affordable and accommodating for its workforce. However, the current pace of growth may not be sufficient to meet demand, and businesses should monitor this metric closely.

**Days on Market**
The median days on market for homes in Richmond is 44 days, with a year-over-year decline of -4.3%. This suggests that the housing market is relatively competitive, and homes are selling quickly. For a worker relocating to this city, this may mean that they need to act fast to secure housing, which could be a challenge.

**Office Economy**
Richmond's office economy is below average, with a percentile rank of 22. This suggests that the city has a relatively shallow talent pool in professional and office sectors. The city may be better suited for businesses that are not heavily reliant on specialized office talent, such as industrial or logistics companies. However, businesses in the tech, finance, or consulting sectors may find it challenging to attract the necessary talent.

The Richmond metro area offers a mixed bag for businesses, with some challenges in labor demand, cost of living, and labor force growth. However, the city's expanding housing supply and moderate unemployment rate may provide some opportunities for businesses looking to relocate or expand. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's relatively expensive cost of living, which may require wage premiums to attract talent.
