# Richmond

**Grade: D (Emergency) | 25.1th percentile | November 2026**

---

Richmond has an overall grade of D, with a score of 25.1 out of 100, indicating significant economic challenges. The city is losing jobs, with a 1.0% decline in employment over the past year, and workers' hours are also falling, by 0.1%. This means the job market is weak, with fewer opportunities for workers and less demand for labor.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate in Richmond is 3.8%, which is higher than most metros, making it relatively easier for employers to hire, but also indicating weaker local consumer demand. With a score of 34 out of 100, the city's unemployment rate is a weakness, suggesting that many workers are still looking for jobs.

This slow wage growth, with a score of 16 out of 100, is a weakness, indicating that workers have limited bargaining power.

With a score of 18 out of 100, the city's cost of living is a weakness, making it less attractive to workers and requiring employers to offer higher wages to compensate.

The pool of workers in Richmond is shrinking, with a decline of 2.2% over the past year, which makes it harder for employers to find new hires and suggests a structural headwind for the city's economy. This slow labor force growth, with a score of 6 out of 100, is a significant weakness, indicating that the city's workforce is not expanding.

This strong growth in building permits, with a score of 96 out of 100, is a major strength, suggesting that the city's housing market will become more affordable and attractive to workers.

Homes in Richmond are selling relatively quickly, taking 44 days to go under contract, but this is getting slower, with a decline of 2.2% over the past year, which is a weakness, indicating a softer housing market. With a score of 16 out of 100, the city's days on market is a weakness, suggesting that buyers are becoming less eager to purchase homes.

**Office Economy**
Richmond's office-based workforce, including tech, finance, and professional services, is smaller than most metros, with a score of 22 out of 100, making it less suited for businesses that rely on a deep pool of specialized talent. This smaller office economy is a weakness, indicating that the city may not be the best fit for companies that require a large number of skilled workers.

The bottom line is that Richmond's biggest strength is its surge in new home approvals, which will increase housing supply and make the city more attractive to workers, but its biggest risk is its declining labor force and slow wage growth, which will make it harder for employers to find and retain workers. Overall, the city's economic challenges, including high unemployment and a shrinking workforce, make it a less competitive location for businesses and workers alike.
