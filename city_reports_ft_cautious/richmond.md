# Richmond

**Grade: D (Emergency) | 25.1th percentile | November 2026**

---

The city of Richmond has an overall grade of D, with a score of 25.1 out of 100. This is largely due to its weak labor demand, with employment growth at -1.0% over the past year. The city's unemployment rate is also higher than most metros, at 3.8%.

**Labor Demand**
The city's job growth is -1.0% over the past year, weaker than most metros. This means people looking for work may struggle to find jobs, and employers may not need to hire as many new staff.

**Unemployment**
The unemployment rate is 3.8%, higher than most metros. This makes it easier for employers to find staff, but also means local consumer demand may be weaker.

**Wage Growth**
Pay growth is +1.6% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers' labor costs are not rising quickly, but workers' paychecks are not growing much either.

**Cost of Living**
It takes 6.3 hours of local pay to buy one square foot of home, at $226/sqft and $35.71/hr pay, making it less affordable than most metros. This makes it harder to attract and keep workers, and the situation is not improving.

**Labor Force Growth**
The workforce is shrinking by -2.2% over the past year, slower than most metros. This means employers may struggle to find new staff, as the labor pool is getting smaller.

**Building Permits**
New home approvals are up 130.8% over the past year, stronger than most metros. This signals that future housing supply will increase, which could improve affordability and attract more workers.

**Days on Market**
Homes take 44 days to sell, down 2.2% from last year, selling faster than most metros. However, this is still a relatively weak housing market, making it harder for people to move here.

**Office Economy**
The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros. This makes it less suitable for businesses that rely on professional and tech services.

The city's biggest strength is its rapid growth in new home approvals, which could improve housing affordability. However, its biggest risk is its weak labor demand, which may make it hard for businesses to grow and for workers to find jobs.
