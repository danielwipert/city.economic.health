Economic Analysis

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 28.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 4.11, and its high cost of living, with a PSF to wages ratio of 6.79. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to hire and retain talent.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 0.47% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.851% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The low labor demand composite score of 4.11 further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 4.20%, which ranks in the 70th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. The tight market also implies that workers may have more bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 1.83%, which is below average. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace. Workers in the city may see some increase in their purchasing power, but it may not be sufficient to keep pace with the high cost of living.

Cost of Living

Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood has a cost of living score that ranks in the 8th percentile, indicating that it is an expensive city relative to its peers. The PSF to wages ratio of 6.79, with a PSF of $221/sqft and average hourly earnings of $32.56, further reinforces this assessment. This means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is contracting at a rate of -8.76% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This decline in labor force growth poses a significant structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may struggle to find qualified workers to fill open positions.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is declining at a rate of -3.73% year-over-year. This decrease in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability challenges and make it more difficult for workers to relocate to the city.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 37 days, with a year-over-year decline of -5.1%. This relatively fast pace of home sales may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing, as the market is competitive.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood ranks in the 12th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool. This suggests that the city may be less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies, and more suited for industries with fewer specialized roles.

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area offers businesses a relatively tight labor market with moderate wage growth, but it also poses significant challenges, including a high cost of living and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited ability to attract and retain talent due to its expensive cost of living and tightening housing supply.
