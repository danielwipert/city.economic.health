Economic Analysis

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it at the 26.5th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand composite score of 4.11, ranking it in the 20th percentile, and its high unemployment rate of 4.20%, which actually indicates a relatively tight labor market, ranking it in the 70th percentile. These metrics suggest a mixed economic environment with both positive and negative signals.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is +0.47% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.851% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the modest job growth is accompanied by a decrease in working hours, indicating that the existing workforce is not being fully utilized. This suggests that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 4.20%, which ranks in the 70th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that there is less slack in the market, making it harder for businesses to hire new employees, and potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. As a result, businesses may need to offer higher salaries to attract top talent in this market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is +1.83%, which is below average, ranking in the 24th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace than in many other cities. While this may be beneficial for workers' purchasing power, it also means that employers may need to budget for increasing labor costs over time.

Cost of Living

Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood has a cost of living score that ranks in the 8th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $221/sqft to $32.56/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.79. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, making it less attractive for talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that the PSF is rising 4.2% year-over-year further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is shrinking at a rate of -8.76% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier. This contraction in labor force supply poses a significant structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is dwindling. Businesses may need to consider this constraint when planning their hiring strategies in this market.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is decreasing by -15.25% year-over-year, indicating a tightening of the housing supply. This decline in permits suggests that the city's future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing units is not keeping pace with demand.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is currently 37 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -5.1%. This relatively fast pace of home sales indicates a competitive market, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find affordable housing options. The low percentile rank of 8th percentile for days on market further underscores the competitive nature of the housing market.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood ranks in the 12th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, and may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

In conclusion, Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood offers a mixed economic environment with both positive and negative signals, posing challenges for businesses looking to locate or expand in the area. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor force supply, which may limit the city's ability to support business growth and expansion.
