Economic Analysis

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 26.1th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low labor demand and stagnant wage growth. The labor demand composite score of 3.86, combining a +0.12% employment growth rate and a -0.611% weekly hours deviation from trend, signals a contraction in genuine demand. This, coupled with a wage growth rate of +1.21%, suggests a challenging economic environment.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +0.12% and weekly hours deviation of -0.611% from trend indicate a lack of genuine demand expansion, suggesting that the city is experiencing a contraction in labor demand. This combination signals that the labor market is not growing robustly, with hours worked below the city's own trend. The labor demand composite score of 3.86, ranking in the 22nd percentile, further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.70% ranks in the 62nd percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market with some slack. This means that while it may not be extremely difficult to hire, there is still some competition for talent, and businesses may face moderate wage pressure. The relatively low unemployment rate suggests that workers have some bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +1.21% is in the bottom tier, ranking 12th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth environment means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. As a result, businesses may not face significant wage pressure, but workers may not have strong disposable incomes.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $214/sqft to $32.90/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.50, and a percentile rank of 14th, the city is relatively expensive. The fact that PSF is rising 2.4% YoY further exacerbates the affordability issue. This means that the city is at a talent attraction disadvantage, as workers may require wage premiums to afford the cost of living, making it less competitive for businesses looking to attract talent without additional compensation.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -8.53% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier. This significant decline in labor force supply indicates a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is shrinking. Businesses looking to expand or staff new operations may find it challenging due to this contraction.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -31.15%, indicating a sharp decline in new housing supply. This signals a potential future affordability crisis and suggests that the city's housing market may become even less accommodating for workers, potentially exacerbating talent attraction and retention challenges for businesses.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 43 days, with a year-over-year increase of 4.9%, ranking in the 74th percentile. This indicates a slower market, which can be more accessible for workers relocating to the city, as they have more time to find suitable housing. However, this also suggests a less competitive housing market, which could be a sign of weaker demand.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share composite score of 0.80, ranking in the 6th percentile, the city has a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This makes it less suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, or HQ sectors that rely on deep knowledge-economy talent, but it may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce requirements, such as industrial or logistics.

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area presents a challenging economic environment for businesses, with low labor demand, stagnant wage growth, and a shrinking labor force being significant concerns. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the city's limited and contracting labor pool, which could severely hinder hiring and expansion plans. Overall, businesses should carefully weigh these factors when considering the Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood area for location decisions.
