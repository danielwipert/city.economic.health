Economic Analysis

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 29.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low labor demand and stagnant wage growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 4.59, combining a +0.68% employment growth rate and a -0.658% weekly hours deviation from trend, signals a contraction in genuine demand expansion. This, coupled with a wage growth rate of +1.41%, which ranks in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile, defines the city's current economic character.

Labor Demand

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an employment growth rate of +0.68% and a weekly hours deviation of -0.658% from its own trend, indicating a lack of genuine demand expansion. This combination suggests that while there is some job growth, the hours worked are below the city's own trend, which may signal a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 4.59 is below average, ranking at the 30th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 4.20%, which is near the median, ranking at the 50th percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced market with neither significant slack nor tightness. For a business trying to hire in this city, the implication is that while it may not be overly difficult to find talent, there is also not a large pool of unemployed workers to draw from, suggesting moderate hiring conditions.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is +1.41%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile. This stagnant wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. For businesses, this could mean a relatively flat cost environment, but it may also indicate a less attractive market for top talent seeking better compensation.

Cost of Living

Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood has a cost of living score that ranks in the bottom tier at the 15th percentile, with a PSF of $215/sqft and an average hourly earnings of $32.58/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.60. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, especially since the PSF is rising at a rate of +1.9% YoY. The high cost of living means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums, making it less competitive in the labor market.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is contracting at a rate of -7.20% year-over-year, ranking at the bottom tier (0th percentile). This significant decline in the labor force supply implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is shrinking. Businesses looking to expand or establish operations in this city may face challenges in finding the necessary talent due to this contraction.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is -5.72%, indicating a tightening in housing supply. This decline suggests that developer confidence may be waning, and the future housing supply is not expanding as rapidly as needed, which could lead to affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 42 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.7%, ranking in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This slower market means that homes are sitting longer before being sold, which could be beneficial for workers relocating to the city as it indicates a more accessible and less competitive housing market.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is relatively low, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile. This suggests that the city's talent pool is not as deep in professional and office skills, making it less suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, or HQ sectors that require a high concentration of such talent. However, it may be more suitable for industries with different skill requirements.

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area offers a business environment with moderate unemployment and relatively flat labor costs, but it is constrained by low labor demand, stagnant wage growth, and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker considering this location is the significant decline in the labor force, which poses a structural challenge for hiring and talent acquisition, potentially limiting the city's attractiveness for businesses reliant on a growing and skilled workforce.
