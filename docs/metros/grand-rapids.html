Economic Analysis

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 31.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand composite score of 4.11, ranking it in the 20th percentile, and its high cost of living ratio of 6.66, which places it in the 8th percentile for affordability. These metrics suggest a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 0.47% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.851% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The low labor demand composite score of 4.11 further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 4.30%, ranking it in the 68th percentile, which means the market has some slack. This implies that businesses may have an easier time hiring in this city, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The relatively low unemployment rate suggests that the city is not experiencing extreme labor market tightness.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 1.83%, which is below average and ranks it in the 24th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies that employer labor costs are rising, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, but not rapidly. This may be a concern for businesses looking to keep labor costs under control.

Cost of Living

Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood has a cost of living ratio of 6.66, with a PSF of $217/sqft and average hourly earnings of $32.56/hr, ranking it in the 8th percentile for affordability. This means that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which may make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The rising PSF, with a 2.8% year-over-year increase, further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is contracting at a rate of -7.49% year-over-year, ranking it in the 0th percentile. This significant decline in labor force supply implies that businesses may face structural headwinds when trying to hire and expand their workforce in the city.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is -3.73%, ranking it near the median. This decline in building permits suggests that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 38 days, with a 2.7% year-over-year increase, ranking it in the 70th percentile. This means that homes are sitting on the market for a relatively longer period, making it a more accessible market for workers relocating to the city. However, this may also indicate a slower market with less competitive pressure.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is relatively low, ranking it in the 12th percentile. This suggests that the city has a shallower talent pool for businesses requiring specialized office or professional skills, making it less suited for tech, finance, or consulting companies. However, it may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area offers businesses a relatively slow-growing labor market with moderate wage growth, but its high cost of living and declining labor force supply pose significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited labor force growth, which may hinder businesses' ability to expand and hire in the area.
