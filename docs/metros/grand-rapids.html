Economic Analysis

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 21.1th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its labor demand and cost of living metrics, which are both below average. The labor demand composite score is 3.43, placing it in the 26th percentile, while the cost of living ratio is 6.85, ranking in the 6th percentile. These metrics suggest a challenging economic environment, with the labor demand score indicating a contraction and the cost of living being relatively expensive.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is -0.62% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.775% from the city's own 12-month baseline, signaling a contraction in labor demand. This combination indicates a lack of genuine demand expansion, with hours below trend during a period of job losses, suggesting a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This scenario points to a difficult hiring environment for businesses looking to expand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 4.40%, placing it in the 36th percentile, which indicates a degree of slack in the labor market. While this is not extremely high, it suggests that the market is not overly tight, making it somewhat easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter labor markets. However, the practical implication for a business trying to hire here is that while there may be available workers, the overall economic conditions might not attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is +2.67%, which is below average, ranking in the 34th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies that labor costs for employers are not rising too quickly, but it also means that worker purchasing power is not increasing rapidly. For businesses, this could mean a relatively stable labor cost environment, but it might also indicate weaker bargaining power for workers.

Cost of Living

Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood has a cost of living ratio of 6.85, with PSF at $224/sqft and hourly earnings at $32.68/hr, and this ratio is increasing as PSF rises 6.2% year-over-year. Ranking in the 6th percentile for affordability, this city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. This means that attracting talent without offering wage premiums could be challenging, as the high cost of living might deter potential employees.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is contracting at a rate of -6.22% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This significant contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. For businesses looking to expand or establish operations, this could pose a significant challenge in finding and hiring the necessary talent.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -21.72%, indicating a sharp decline in housing supply expansion. This signals a potential supply squeeze in the future, which could lead to decreased affordability and make it harder for the workforce to find accommodation, ultimately affecting the city's ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 33 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -8.3%, indicating a relatively fast-moving market. However, ranking in the 2nd percentile, this actually signifies a very competitive market where homes sell quickly, which could make it tough for relocating workers to find housing. This fast pace could be a challenge for businesses trying to attract talent from other areas.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors is 0.80, ranking in the 10th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these areas. This suggests that Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but might be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs.

The bottom line for a business considering Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is that while it may offer some advantages in terms of labor costs, the significant challenges in labor force growth, housing supply, and cost of living pose substantial risks. The single biggest constraint a decision-maker should factor in is the shrinking labor pool and its implications for hiring and talent attraction, which could outweigh any potential benefits of locating in this metro area.
