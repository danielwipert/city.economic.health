Economic Analysis

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 20.3th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score reflecting significant economic challenges. The city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand composite score of 3.84, which is in the 20th percentile, and its extremely low labor force growth rate of -6.22% YoY. These metrics signal a struggling job market with limited workforce expansion.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is -0.33% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating -0.923% from the city's own 12-month baseline, indicating a contraction in labor demand. This combination signals a genuine demand contraction, rather than a survivor squeeze, as both jobs and hours are declining. The labor demand composite score of 3.84, in the 20th percentile, further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 4.40%, which is in the 36th percentile, indicating a moderate level of slack in the labor market. While this is not extremely high, it suggests that the market is not particularly tight, making it somewhat easier for businesses to hire. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is +2.03%, which is in the 30th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not extremely rapidly, and worker purchasing power is increasing, albeit slowly. The wage growth is not stagnant, but it is also not fast enough to drive significant economic expansion.

Cost of Living

Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood has a cost of living score in the 8th percentile, with a PSF of $224/sqft and average hourly earnings of $32.46/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.90. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive, especially since PSF is rising 6.2% YoY. The low percentile rank suggests that the city is less affordable than most of its peers, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is contracting at a rate of -6.22% YoY, which is in the 0th percentile, indicating a severe structural headwind for hiring. This decline in labor force supply will make it increasingly difficult for businesses to find and hire qualified workers, limiting their capacity for growth.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is declining by -15.25% YoY, which is in the 22nd percentile, signaling a tightening of the housing supply. This reduction in new housing construction will likely lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for available housing, making it more challenging for workers to relocate to the area.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 33 days, with a YoY decline of -8.3%, indicating a relatively fast-paced market. However, the 2nd percentile rank suggests that homes are selling quickly, making it competitive for relocating workers to find suitable housing. This fast market may pose challenges for businesses trying to attract talent from other areas.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 0.74, which is in the 10th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics companies, and is less suitable for tech, finance, or consulting firms that rely on specialized office workers.

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area offers a relatively low-cost business environment, but its struggling job market, limited workforce expansion, and decreasing affordability pose significant risks for businesses considering relocation. The single biggest constraint for decision-makers is the city's extremely low labor force growth rate, which will limit the availability of qualified workers and hinder business growth.
