Economic Analysis

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 29.6th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score largely defined by its weak labor demand and stagnant wage growth, specifically a labor demand composite score of 4.59 and wage growth of +1.41% YoY. The city's labor market is characterized by slow employment growth and declining weekly hours, signaling a lack of genuine demand expansion. With a labor force growth rate of -7.20% YoY, the city's workforce supply is contracting.

Labor Demand

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an employment growth rate of +0.68% YoY, but weekly hours are deviating -0.658% from the city's own 12-month baseline, indicating a lack of genuine demand expansion. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than a survivor squeeze, as hours are not increasing to absorb eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 4.59 is below average, ranking 30th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 4.20%, near the median of the 50 US metros, indicating a relatively balanced labor market with neither significant tightness nor slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate means that hiring may not be overly challenging, but wage pressure is not entirely absent either. The market's near-median unemployment rate suggests a stable, though not highly competitive, environment for recruitment.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is +1.41%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth implies a flat cost environment for employers, but it also means weak bargaining power for workers, potentially affecting their purchasing power. For businesses, the slow wage growth could be seen as a cost advantage, but it may also reflect a lack of economic dynamism.

Cost of Living

Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood has a cost of living score that ranks in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile, with a PSF of $215/sqft and average hourly earnings of $32.58/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.60. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, as the PSF is rising +1.9% YoY. The low affordability score means that attracting talent without offering wage premiums could be challenging, as the cost of living is high relative to earnings.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is contracting at a rate of -7.20% YoY, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This significant decline in labor force growth poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses looking to expand or establish operations in this city may face challenges in finding sufficient skilled workers due to the contracting labor force.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is -5.72%, indicating that housing supply is tightening. This decline in building permits signals a potential future affordability crisis and suggests that the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce may be compromised. As housing supply fails to keep pace with demand, affordability and attractiveness for relocating workers may suffer.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 42 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.7%, ranking in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This slower market means that homes are sitting longer before being sold, which could be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as they have more time to find a home and the market is less competitive.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is relatively low, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile, indicating a less deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, or HQ decisions. This city is better suited for businesses that do not require a large pool of specialized office workers, such as industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area offers a business environment with relatively slow wage growth and a contracting labor force, posing significant challenges for hiring and expansion. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker considering this location is the shrinking labor pool, which could severely limit the city's ability to support business growth and attract necessary talent.
