Economic Analysis

The Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area has an overall grade of D, with a score of 26.1 out of 100, indicating significant economic challenges. One of the most defining features of this city's economy is its slow job growth, with employment increasing by only 0.1% over the past year, and weekly hours decreasing by 0.6%, suggesting a weak labor demand.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a very slow rate, with a mere 0.1% increase over the past year, and workers are actually putting in fewer hours, with a 0.6% decrease, indicating a lack of genuine demand for labor. This means that the job market is not strong, and employers may not be confident in their ability to find workers or may not need to hire as many people.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is 3.7%, which is lower than many other metros, making it a strength, with a score of 62 out of 100, indicating that it is easier for workers to find jobs, but harder for employers to hire. This low unemployment rate suggests that the labor market is relatively tight, which can drive up wages and make it more challenging for businesses to attract and retain talent.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood have risen by only 1.2% over the past year, which is slower than many other metros, with a score of 12 out of 100, indicating a weakness, as this slow wage growth can make it harder for workers to afford the cost of living. This means that employers may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but workers may not see much improvement in their purchasing power.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $214 and hourly pay of $32.90, and it is getting worse, with a 2.4% increase in price per square foot over the past year, resulting in a score of 14 out of 100, indicating a weakness. This means that housing is becoming less affordable for local workers, which can make it harder for businesses to attract and retain talent.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is shrinking, with a decline of 8.5% over the past year, resulting in a score of 0 out of 100, indicating a significant weakness, as this reduction in the workforce can make it even harder for businesses to find the workers they need. This decline in the labor force can create a structural headwind for hiring and economic growth.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

The number of new home building permits in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood has decreased by 31.1% over the past year, resulting in a score of 8 out of 100, indicating a weakness, as this decline in new home construction can lead to a shortage of housing supply and make it even harder for workers to find affordable housing. This decrease in building permits suggests that the city's housing market may not be able to keep up with demand, which can drive up prices and make the city less attractive to workers and businesses.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood are selling relatively quickly, with a median of 43 days on the market, and this is getting slightly slower, with a 4.9% increase over the past year, resulting in a score of 74 out of 100, indicating a strength, as this suggests a relatively healthy housing market. This means that the housing market is not too tight, and buyers have a reasonable amount of time to find a home, which can make the city more attractive to workers and businesses.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The office-based workforce in Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood is relatively small, with a score of 6 out of 100, indicating a weakness, as this suggests that the city may not have a deep talent pool in professional and technical services. This means that businesses that rely on a strong office-based workforce, such as tech, finance, and consulting firms, may not find the city as attractive as other locations with a more developed office economy.

The biggest strength of the Grand Rapids-Wyoming-Kentwood metro area is its relatively low unemployment rate, which can make it easier for workers to find jobs, but the biggest risk is its slow labor force growth, which can create a structural headwind for hiring and economic growth. Overall, the city's economic challenges, including slow job growth, high cost of living, and declining labor force, make it a less attractive location for businesses and workers, and it will need to address these issues to improve its economic competitiveness.
