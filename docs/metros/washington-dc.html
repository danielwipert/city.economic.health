Economic Analysis

The Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 47.5th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its above-average wage growth of 5.31% year-over-year. The combination of a 4.00% unemployment rate and strong wage growth suggests a complex labor market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is -2.15% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.969% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a decline in job creation and hours worked. This suggests a lack of genuine demand expansion in the labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is 4.00%, which ranks at the 60th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight labor market also implies upward pressure on wages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is 5.31%, which is above average and ranks at the 76th percentile. This fast wage growth rate implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing purchasing power for workers. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs, while workers may enjoy improved living standards.

Cost of Living

Washington-Arlington-Alexandria has a cost of living score that ranks at the 76th percentile, with a PSF of $298/sqft and average hourly earnings of $45.17/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.60. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, with a PSF that is falling by 2.3% year-over-year. This affordability advantage can attract talent without requiring wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is contracting at a rate of -2.20% year-over-year, which ranks at the 6th percentile. This decline in labor force supply implies that the city may face structural headwinds in hiring, as the pool of available workers is shrinking. Businesses may need to adapt their hiring strategies to accommodate this declining labor force.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is increasing by 40.37% year-over-year, which ranks at the 90th percentile. This rapid expansion in housing supply suggests that the city is experiencing a surge in developer confidence, which may lead to improved affordability and accommodation options for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is 43 days, with a year-over-year increase of 10.3%, which ranks at the 90th percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are taking longer to sell, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, this may also signal a normalization of the market after a period of rapid growth.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria ranks at the 32nd percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool compared to other cities. This suggests that the city may be less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies, but may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles.

The Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metro area offers a unique combination of above-average wage growth and relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for businesses that can adapt to its tight labor market and declining labor force supply. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses in this city is the low labor demand, which may limit the availability of skilled workers and require creative hiring strategies to overcome.
