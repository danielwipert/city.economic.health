Economic Analysis

The Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 45.0th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its weak labor demand, with a labor demand composite score of 1.50, and its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.31% in hourly earnings. These metrics suggest a complex economic environment with both challenges and opportunities.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is -2.15% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.969% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a decline in job creation and hours worked. This suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or hire new talent.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is 4.10%, ranking at the 52nd percentile, which indicates a near-median level of unemployment. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire new employees. However, the relatively low unemployment rate also means that businesses may face some competition for top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is 5.31%, ranking at the 76th percentile, indicating fast wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but worker purchasing power is also increasing. Businesses should be prepared for higher labor costs, but may also benefit from a more affluent local consumer base.

Cost of Living

Washington-Arlington-Alexandria has a cost of living score ranking at the 71st percentile, with a PSF of $305/sqft and average hourly earnings of $45.17/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.75. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The fact that PSF is falling by 1.9% year-over-year further supports this assessment. This affordability advantage can be a talent attraction strategy for businesses, as they may not need to offer wage premiums to compensate for high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is contracting at a rate of -1.70% year-over-year, ranking at the 10th percentile. This indicates that the labor force supply is shrinking, which may create structural headwinds for hiring and business expansion. Businesses should be prepared to compete aggressively for talent in this environment.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is increasing by 53.15% year-over-year, ranking at the 90th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is expanding, which may improve affordability and accommodation options for the workforce in the future. This is a positive signal for businesses considering relocation or expansion, as it may reduce the cost of attracting and retaining talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is 39 days, with a year-over-year increase of 2.6%. This ranks at the 74th percentile, indicating a relatively slow market. For workers relocating to this city, this means that they may have a more accessible and less competitive housing market, which can be an advantage for businesses looking to attract talent.

Office Economy

Washington-Arlington-Alexandria has an office economy score ranking at the 32nd percentile, indicating a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, it may be more suitable for businesses with industrial or logistics-dominated economies.

The Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metro area offers businesses a complex economic environment with both challenges and opportunities. While the city's strong wage growth and relatively affordable cost of living are attractive features, the weak labor demand and shrinking labor force supply are significant risks that decision-makers should factor in. Overall, businesses should carefully weigh these factors when considering relocation or expansion in this city.
