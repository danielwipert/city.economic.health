Economic Analysis

The Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 50.4th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its high wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.85%. The combination of these metrics suggests a complex economic environment, with both challenges and opportunities for businesses.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is -1.96% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.606% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a decline in job creation and hours worked. This suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or hire new workers.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is 3.80%, which ranks in the 78th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring new workers, as the pool of available labor is relatively small. As a result, businesses may need to offer competitive wages to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is 5.85%, which is in the top tier of US metros. This rapid wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising quickly, but it also means that workers have strong purchasing power. This could be beneficial for businesses that rely on a highly skilled and highly paid workforce.

Cost of Living

Washington-Arlington-Alexandria has a cost of living score that ranks in the 67th percentile, with a PSF of $301/sqft and average hourly earnings of $45.93/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.55. This suggests that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which could be a talent attraction advantage for businesses. The fact that PSF is falling by 2.3% year-over-year also contributes to the city's affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is contracting at a rate of -3.61% year-over-year, which is in the bottom tier of US metros. This decline in labor force supply suggests that businesses may face challenges in finding new workers, as the pool of available labor is shrinking. This could be a structural headwind for hiring and expansion plans.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is increasing by 27.36% year-over-year, which is in the top tier of US metros. This expansion in housing supply suggests that the city is likely to experience improving affordability and a more accommodating environment for workers in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria is 43 days, with a year-over-year increase of 13.2%, ranking in the 94th percentile. This suggests a relatively slow market, which could be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as they may have more time to find a home. However, this could also indicate a weakening demand for housing.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Washington-Arlington-Alexandria ranks in the 32nd percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool compared to other US metros. This suggests that the city may be less suited for businesses that require a deep pool of specialized office talent, such as tech or finance companies.

The Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metro area offers businesses a unique combination of high wage growth and relatively low cost of living, but it also poses significant challenges, particularly in terms of labor demand and labor force growth. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses in this city is the declining labor force supply, which could limit hiring and expansion plans.
