Economic Analysis

The Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 57.0th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 86th percentile, and its highly affordable cost of living, ranked at the 92nd percentile. The labor demand is driven by a 1.26% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 0.463% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend.

Labor Demand

The combination of a 1.26% employment growth rate and a 0.463% increase in weekly hours above trend signals genuine demand expansion in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area. This indicates that the city is experiencing a period of job growth, with hours worked also increasing, suggesting a strong and vibrant labor market. The labor demand composite score of 6.53 further reinforces this assessment, ranking the city at the 86th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington stands at 4.70%, which ranks at the 14th percentile, indicating a relatively high level of unemployment compared to other cities. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it easier for businesses to hire workers without facing significant wage pressure. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand compared to cities with lower unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is 3.44%, ranking near the median at the 50th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but at a manageable pace. At the same time, workers are experiencing a modest increase in their purchasing power, which can contribute to a stable and growing local economy.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $201/sqft to $38.32/hr, and a year-over-year decrease of 1.9% in PSF, Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington ranks at the 92nd percentile in terms of affordability. This makes the city an attractive location for talent, as the cost of living is relatively low compared to other metros, reducing the need for wage premiums to attract and retain workers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is growing at a rate of 0.73% year-over-year, which ranks above average at the 66th percentile. This expansion in the labor force supply suggests that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to find the talent they need to expand or establish operations.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -13.44%, indicating a contraction in housing supply. This decline in building permits suggests that the city may face future affordability challenges and potential workforce accommodation issues, as the supply of new housing units is not keeping pace with demand.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington is 54 days, with a year-over-year increase of 1.9%. This relatively moderate pace of home sales, ranking near the median at the 48th percentile, suggests that the housing market is neither extremely competitive nor overly accessible for relocating workers.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share ranked at the 92nd percentile, Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington has a deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. This makes the city an attractive location for companies seeking specialized knowledge-economy workers, but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

The Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, affordable cost of living, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain types of businesses. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the potential future affordability challenges and workforce accommodation issues arising from the decline in residential building permits, which could impact the city's ability to attract and retain talent over the long term.
