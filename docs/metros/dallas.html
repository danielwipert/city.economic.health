Economic Analysis

The Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro area has earned an overall grade of B+, with a score of 63.3 out of 100, indicating it is above average compared to the 50 metros. What most defines this city's economy right now is its strong labor demand, with employment growth of 1.0% over the past year and workers putting in 0.7% more hours, earning it a score of 76 out of 100.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.0% over the past year, and workers are putting in 0.7% more hours, indicating a strong demand for labor. This means the job market is healthy, with employers hiring and workers seeing more hours, making it a good time for businesses to expand.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate is 4.1%, which is about average for the 50 metros, earning a score of 44 out of 100. This rate makes it neither particularly easy nor hard for employers to hire, as there are still workers available, but the labor market is not overly loose.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose 4.7% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, earning a score of 72 out of 100. This means employers are seeing rising labor costs, but workers are benefiting from higher paychecks, which can boost consumer spending and the local economy.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

Housing is relatively affordable in Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington, with a price per square foot of $197 and hourly pay of $37.92, earning a score of 84 out of 100. The cost of living is actually getting more affordable, as housing prices have fallen 2.5% over the past year, making it easier for workers to afford homes.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers is growing at a rate of 0.6% over the past year, earning a score of 68 out of 100. This means the labor force is expanding, making it easier for employers to find new hires and for businesses to grow.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals have fallen 8.4% over the past year, earning a score of 36 out of 100. This decline in building permits signals that the future housing supply may be limited, which could lead to higher housing costs and make it harder for workers to afford homes.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

A typical home takes 64 days to sell, which is unchanged from last year, earning a score of 30 out of 100. This indicates a weaker housing market, as homes are not selling quickly, and buyers may be pulling back, making it a less desirable market for sellers.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a large office-based workforce, with a score of 92 out of 100, indicating it is better than most metros. This makes Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington a good fit for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors, which require a deep pool of skilled workers.

The bottom line is that Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington's biggest strength is its strong labor demand and growing workforce, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to expand. However, its biggest risk is the decline in building permits, which could lead to a limited housing supply and higher costs, making it harder for workers to afford homes and for businesses to attract talent.
