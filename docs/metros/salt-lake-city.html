Economic Analysis

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking in the 77.6th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the 88th percentile, and its deep professional talent pool, ranking in the 96th percentile for office and professional worker share. The labor demand is driven by a 2.66% year-over-year employment growth rate and a -0.609% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend.

Labor Demand

The combination of a 2.66% employment growth rate and a -0.609% deviation in weekly hours signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are not significantly above trend despite job growth. This indicates that the labor market is absorbing new jobs without overburdening existing workers. The labor demand composite score of 7.41 further supports this interpretation, suggesting a healthy and growing job market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Salt Lake City-Murray is 3.20%, ranking in the 80th percentile, which indicates a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight market also implies upward pressure on wages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate is 4.91%, ranking in the 74th percentile, which is above average. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but workers are also experiencing increased purchasing power. The moderate to fast wage growth rate indicates a healthy labor market with opportunities for workers to negotiate better compensation.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of 6.54, based on $260 per square foot and $39.78 per hour, Salt Lake City-Murray ranks in the 63rd percentile for affordability. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The affordability is a talent attraction advantage for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of 1.18% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This growth suggests that businesses will have a larger pool of potential workers to hire from, making it easier to find and attract talent. The expanding labor force is a positive sign for hiring capacity.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is 25.87%, indicating a significant expansion in housing supply. This growth in permits suggests that developer confidence is high, and future housing supply will increase, leading to improved affordability and a more attractive environment for workers. The expanding housing supply is a positive signal for future workforce accommodation.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 63 days, with a year-over-year increase of 6.8%. This indicates a relatively slow market, ranking in the 20th percentile, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. The slower market gives buyers more time to find suitable housing, reducing the pressure on relocating workers.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share ranking in the 96th percentile, Salt Lake City-Murray has a deep and talented professional pool. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require specialized knowledge-economy talent. The city's strong office economy is a significant advantage for businesses looking to establish or expand operations.

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, a deep professional talent pool, and relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential for upward pressure on wages due to the tight labor market, which may increase labor costs for employers.
