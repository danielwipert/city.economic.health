Economic Analysis

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 70.1th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is largely defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a composite score in the 96th percentile, and its strong office economy, also in the 96th percentile, with 4.32 out of 5 in terms of professional and office worker share. The labor demand is driven by a 3.20% year-over-year employment growth rate and a -0.796% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend.

Labor Demand

The combination of a 3.20% employment growth rate and a -0.796% deviation in weekly hours signals genuine demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs while hours worked are slightly below trend, indicating efficient labor utilization. This suggests that the labor market is experiencing real growth rather than just absorbing existing workload. The labor demand composite score of 7.70 further reinforces this positive outlook.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Salt Lake City-Murray stands at 3.60%, placing it in the top tier with an 80th percentile rank. This indicates a relatively tight labor market with some slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire but not excessively so. For a business trying to hire here, this means they may face some competition for talent but can still find qualified candidates without extreme wage pressure.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate is +4.60%, ranking in the 60th percentile as above average. This moderate to fast wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are rising, which is good for worker purchasing power. However, it also suggests that businesses may need to adjust their compensation packages to attract and retain talent in this market.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living percentile rank of 53th, Salt Lake City-Murray is near the median in terms of affordability, where the price per square foot ($265) compared to hourly earnings ($39.49) gives a ratio of 6.71. This means the city is neither exceptionally affordable nor overly expensive compared to its peers. For talent attraction, this neutral position suggests that businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums solely for the cost of living, but it does not provide a strong advantage either.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Salt Lake City-Murray is growing at a rate of +0.96% year-over-year, ranking in the 72nd percentile. This positive growth indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a favorable condition for hiring capacity. Businesses looking to expand or relocate can expect a gradually increasing pool of potential employees.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -21.89%, placing it in the bottom tier with a 16th percentile rank. This significant decline suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This trend signals a potential risk for businesses reliant on a readily available and affordable housing supply for their employees.

Days on Market

The current median days on market stands at 54 days, with a year-over-year increase of +3.9%. This indicates a slightly slower market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city as it provides them with more time to find suitable housing. However, this also suggests a normalization of the market rather than a rapid seller's market, which could impact housing affordability and availability.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share composite of 4.32 out of 5, ranking in the 96th percentile, Salt Lake City-Murray has a deep and talented pool of professional and office workers. This makes the city highly suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion, a moderate unemployment rate, and above-average wage growth, making it an attractive location for companies, especially those in professional and office sectors. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply due to the sharp decline in building permits, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.
