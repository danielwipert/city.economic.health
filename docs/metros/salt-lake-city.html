Economic Analysis

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 71.7th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and a deep professional talent pool being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, scoring in the 90th percentile for both labor demand composite and office/professional worker share. The labor demand composite score of 7.39, driven by a 2.49% employment growth rate and a -0.398% weekly hours deviation, signals genuine demand expansion. This combination of metrics suggests a city with a strong and growing economy.

Labor Demand

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has seen a 2.49% employment growth rate year-over-year, combined with a -0.398% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion. This signals that the city is adding jobs and that the existing workforce is not being overburdened, as hours are not significantly above trend. The labor demand composite score of 7.39 further reinforces this interpretation, placing the city in the top tier for labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Salt Lake City-Murray stands at 3.70%, which corresponds to an above-average percentile rank of 74th. This rate indicates a relatively tight labor market, suggesting that businesses may face challenges in finding available talent and could experience upward pressure on wages. However, it also implies a stable and committed workforce.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Salt Lake City-Murray is +4.49%, ranking in the 64th percentile. This moderate to fast wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but it also means that workers have increasing purchasing power. This balance is crucial for businesses considering relocation, as it affects both operational costs and the local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $262/sqft to $39.26/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.67, Salt Lake City-Murray ranks in the 39th percentile for affordability, indicating that the city is less affordable compared to its peers. This relatively low affordability score means that attracting talent might require wage premiums to compensate for the higher cost of living, potentially increasing labor costs for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Salt Lake City-Murray has grown by +0.35% year-over-year, placing it in the 72nd percentile. This positive growth rate indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a favorable condition for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a growing pool of potential employees.

Building Permits

The city has seen a +28.20% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking in the 84th percentile. This significant increase signals that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce, making it easier for businesses to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

Homes in Salt Lake City-Murray currently sit on the market for a median of 58 days, with a 0% year-over-year change. This stable and relatively fast market suggests that relocating workers may face a competitive environment when searching for housing, which could be a consideration for businesses in terms of relocation packages or support.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share composite score of 4.32, ranking in the 92nd percentile, Salt Lake City-Murray boasts a deep and talented professional pool. This makes the city highly suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

In conclusion, Salt Lake City-Murray offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion, a growing and skilled workforce, and an expanding housing supply. However, the relatively low affordability and tight labor market, indicated by the 39th percentile cost of living rank and 3.70% unemployment rate, respectively, pose significant challenges that decision-makers must carefully consider, particularly in terms of wage premiums and talent attraction strategies.
