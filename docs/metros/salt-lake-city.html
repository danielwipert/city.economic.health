Economic Analysis

Salt Lake City-Murray has earned an overall grade of A+, with a score of 77.6 out of 100, making it an excellent location for businesses, with a strong job market defined by 2.7% employment growth over the past year. This growth, combined with a low unemployment rate, indicates a thriving economy with a high demand for labor.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 2.7% over the past year, with workers' hours slightly decreasing by 0.6%, indicating a strong hiring environment with a high score of 88, better than most of the 50 metros. This means that the job market is robust, with employers actively hiring and a low risk of job losses, making it an attractive location for businesses.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Salt Lake City-Murray is 3.2%, which corresponds to a score of 80, indicating that it is lower than in most of the 50 metros, making it harder for employers to hire but also a sign of a strong economy. With a low unemployment rate, employers may face wage pressure, but it also indicates a talented and dedicated workforce.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Hourly earnings in Salt Lake City-Murray have risen by 4.9% over the past year, resulting in a score of 74, which is faster than in most of the 50 metros, meaning that employers can expect rising labor costs, but workers will see an increase in their paychecks. This wage growth is a strength, as it attracts and retains top talent, but it also means that businesses may need to adjust their budgets to accommodate higher labor costs.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Salt Lake City-Murray is relatively affordable, with a score of 63, indicating that it is more affordable than most of the 50 metros, and housing costs $260 per square foot, compared to an hourly pay of $39.78, although housing prices are rising slightly. This affordability makes the city an attractive location for workers and businesses alike, as it provides a good balance between wages and living expenses.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in Salt Lake City-Murray is growing at a rate of 1.2% over the past year, corresponding to a score of 86, which is faster than in most of the 50 metros, indicating a expanding pool of workers and a positive outlook for hiring. This growth in the labor force provides businesses with a steady supply of skilled workers, making it easier to find and hire top talent.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

The number of new home building permits in Salt Lake City-Murray has increased by 25.9% over the past year, resulting in a score of 84, which is stronger than in most of the 50 metros, signaling a growing housing supply and improving affordability. This increase in building permits is a strength, as it will help to meet the demand for housing and prevent a supply squeeze, making the city more attractive to workers and businesses.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Salt Lake City-Murray are taking 63 days to sell, which is getting slower by 6.8% over the past year, corresponding to a score of 20, indicating a weaker housing market than in most of the 50 metros. This slower pace of home sales may indicate a softening housing market, which could make it more challenging for workers to relocate to the area, but it also provides buyers with more time to find a home.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

Salt Lake City-Murray has a large office-based workforce, with a score of 96, indicating that it is larger than in most of the 50 metros, making it an ideal location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This strong office economy provides a deep talent pool and a suitable environment for businesses that require specialized knowledge and skills.

The city's biggest strength is its strong job market, with high employment growth and low unemployment, making it an attractive location for businesses and workers alike. However, the slower pace of home sales and rising housing prices may pose a risk to the city's affordability and attractiveness to relocating workers, making it essential for businesses to consider these factors when making location decisions.
