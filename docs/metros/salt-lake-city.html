Economic Analysis

Salt Lake City-Murray has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 71.6th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and a deep professional talent pool being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, driven by a 90th percentile labor demand composite score and a 92nd percentile office economy share. The city's labor market is characterized by a 2.49% year-over-year employment growth rate and a -0.398% weekly hours deviation from its own trend. These numbers signal a genuine demand expansion, with jobs being added and hours running slightly below trend, indicating a healthy and sustainable labor market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 2.49% and weekly hours deviation of -0.398% combine to signal a genuine demand expansion, with the city adding jobs at a rate above the national average and hours worked being slightly below trend. This indicates that the labor market is growing, but not at an unsustainable pace. The labor demand composite score of 7.39 puts the city in the top tier, at the 90th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.70% is relatively low, ranking in the 74th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with some slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, this means that it may be moderately challenging to find qualified candidates, and wage pressure may be present. However, the market is not extremely tight, and hiring is still feasible.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of 4.49% is above average, ranking in the 64th percentile, indicating moderate to fast wage growth. This implies that employer labor costs are rising, but worker purchasing power is also increasing. As a result, businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, but they can also expect a more affluent local consumer base.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of 6.67, based on $262 per square foot and $39.26 per hour, Salt Lake City-Murray ranks in the 39th percentile, indicating that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. This means that the city may not have a significant talent attraction advantage without offering wage premiums to offset the higher cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of 0.35% year-over-year, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating a slowly expanding labor pool. This implies that the hiring capacity is increasing, but at a moderate pace, and businesses may need to be patient when looking to fill open positions.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is 28.20%, ranking in the 84th percentile, indicating a significant expansion in housing supply. This signals that future affordability and workforce accommodation are likely to improve, making it easier for businesses to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 58 days, with a 0% year-over-year change, ranking in the 35th percentile. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, where homes are selling quickly, making it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the stable days on market suggests that the market is not becoming more competitive.

Office Economy

With an office economy share of 4.32, ranking in the 92nd percentile, Salt Lake City-Murray has a deep and specialized professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. However, it may be less suited for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses, which may require a different type of workforce.

In conclusion, Salt Lake City-Murray offers businesses a strong labor market, a deep professional talent pool, and an expanding housing supply, making it an attractive location for companies looking to grow and attract top talent. However, the relatively high cost of living and tight labor market may require businesses to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract and retain workers, which is the single biggest risk or constraint that decision-makers should factor in.
