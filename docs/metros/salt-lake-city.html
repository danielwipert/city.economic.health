Economic Analysis

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 66.0th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong labor demand and deep professional talent pool, as evidenced by a 90th percentile ranking in labor demand and a 96th percentile ranking in office economy. The city's labor demand composite score of 6.80, combining a 2.12% year-over-year employment growth rate and a -0.467% deviation in weekly hours, suggests genuine demand expansion. With an unemployment rate of 3.40%, the market is relatively tight, which has implications for hiring and wage pressure.

Labor Demand

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has seen a 2.12% year-over-year employment growth rate, coupled with a -0.467% deviation in weekly hours, indicating that while jobs are being added, hours are slightly below trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, rather than a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. The strong labor demand suggests a competitive market for workers.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Salt Lake City-Murray stands at 3.40%, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This tightness implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as well as upward pressure on wages. For a company trying to hire in this market, being competitive on salary and benefits will be crucial.

Wage Growth

Year-over-year wage growth in the Salt Lake City-Murray metro area is 4.07%, placing it near the median in terms of wage growth among comparable cities. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, and worker purchasing power is increasing, albeit not dramatically. This balance could support a stable business environment.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living percentile rank of 43, Salt Lake City-Murray is near the median in terms of affordability, where the price per square foot is $266 and the average hourly earnings are $38.85, resulting in a ratio of 6.85. This indicates that the city is neither exceptionally affordable nor overly expensive compared to its peers. For talent attraction, this means that businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums solely to offset the cost of living, potentially easing the burden on labor costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Salt Lake City-Murray has grown by -0.06% year-over-year, ranking in the 64th percentile. This slight contraction in labor force supply suggests that the pool of potential workers is not expanding, which could pose a structural headwind for hiring and business growth. Companies may need to be more competitive or look to alternative labor sources.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -4.56%, indicating that housing supply is not expanding as quickly as demand, potentially leading to a supply squeeze. This trend signals that future affordability and the ability to accommodate a growing workforce could be at risk, making it essential for businesses to consider the long-term implications of housing availability on their operations and employee attractiveness.

Days on Market

Homes in Salt Lake City-Murray are currently sitting on the market for a median of 50 days, with a year-over-year increase of 4.2%. This suggests a slightly slower market, which could be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as it may indicate a more accessible and less competitive housing market. However, this also needs to be considered in the context of the overall job market and housing supply trends.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share ranked in the 96th percentile, Salt Lake City-Murray boasts a deep and talented pool of professional workers, making it highly suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. This city is less suited for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant, given its strong orientation towards knowledge-economy jobs.

In conclusion, Salt Lake City-Murray offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion and a deep professional talent pool, which are significant advantages for companies looking to establish or expand operations. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the potential for a tightening housing supply, which could impact future affordability and workforce accommodation, necessitating careful planning and consideration of long-term housing strategies for employees.
