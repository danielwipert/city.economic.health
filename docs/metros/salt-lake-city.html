Economic Analysis

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area boasts an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking in the 77.3th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 7.70, and its strong office economy, ranking in the 96th percentile. The combination of a 3.20% employment growth rate and a -0.796% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend suggests genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has seen a 3.20% employment growth rate year-over-year, paired with a -0.796% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a surge in job creation, with hours worked remaining relatively stable. The labor demand composite score of 7.70 further reinforces this notion, ranking in the top tier at the 96th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Salt Lake City-Murray stands at 3.60%, ranking in the top tier at the 80th percentile. This tight labor market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is relatively small. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive wages to attract top talent in this market.

Wage Growth

The city has experienced a 4.60% year-over-year wage growth rate, ranking above average at the 60th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a manageable pace. Meanwhile, workers are seeing an increase in their purchasing power, which can have a positive impact on local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of 6.71, based on $265 per square foot and $39.49 per hour, Salt Lake City-Murray ranks near the median at the 53rd percentile. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The fact that PSF is rising by 1.1% year-over-year, however, may impact future affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Salt Lake City-Murray has grown by 0.96% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 72nd percentile. This expansion in labor force supply suggests that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to hire and expand their operations.

Building Permits

The city has seen a 40.08% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking in the top tier at the 88th percentile. This surge in permits indicates that housing supply is expanding, which can lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market currently stands at 54 days, with a 3.9% year-over-year increase. This suggests that the housing market is normalizing, becoming slightly more accessible for relocating workers. However, the relatively fast pace of sales still indicates a competitive market.

Office Economy

Salt Lake City-Murray boasts a deep professional talent pool, ranking in the top tier at the 96th percentile. This makes the city an ideal location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require specialized knowledge-economy workers. Conversely, the city may be less suited for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, a deep professional talent pool, and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the tight labor market and rising wages may pose challenges for companies looking to hire and expand in this location, making it essential for decision-makers to factor in these constraints when considering Salt Lake City-Murray as a potential business location.
