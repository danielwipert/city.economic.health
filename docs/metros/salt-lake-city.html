Economic Analysis

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 71.8th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and a deep professional talent pool being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, scoring in the 90th percentile for both labor demand composite and office/professional worker share. The labor demand composite score of 7.39, driven by a 2.49% employment growth rate and a -0.398% weekly hours deviation, signals genuine demand expansion. This combination of metrics suggests a city with a strong and growing economy.

Labor Demand

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has seen a 2.49% employment growth rate year-over-year, combined with a -0.398% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion. This signals that the city is adding jobs and that the existing workforce is not being overburdened, as hours are not significantly above trend. The labor demand composite score of 7.39 further reinforces this interpretation, placing the city in the top tier.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Salt Lake City-Murray stands at 3.70%, ranking in the 74th percentile, which indicates a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight market implies that wage pressure is likely to be upward.

Wage Growth

The city has experienced a 4.49% year-over-year wage growth, ranking in the 64th percentile, which is above average but not exceptionally high. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a manageable rate, and worker purchasing power is increasing. However, it may not be sufficient to outpace cost of living increases significantly.

Cost of Living

Salt Lake City-Murray has a cost of living score that places it in the 40th percentile, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $262/sqft to $39.26/hr, or 6.67. This indicates that the city is less affordable than many of its peers, as the cost of living is relatively high compared to wages. The slight increase in PSF year-over-year further suggests that affordability is not improving. This could make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Salt Lake City-Murray has grown by 0.35% year-over-year, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating a slow but positive expansion of the workforce supply. This slow growth suggests that while hiring capacity is not severely constrained, it may not be as robust as in cities with higher labor force growth rates.

Building Permits

The city has seen a 28.20% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, placing it in the 84th percentile. This significant rise in permits signals that housing supply is expected to expand, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. This trend is positive for businesses looking to relocate or expand, as it suggests a more favorable future housing market.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Salt Lake City-Murray is 58 days, with a 0% year-over-year change, ranking in the 36th percentile. This relatively fast market means that homes are selling quickly, which can make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the stable year-over-year trend suggests that the market is not becoming significantly more competitive.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share composite score of 4.31, ranking in the 92nd percentile, Salt Lake City-Murray has a very deep talent pool in professional and office sectors. This makes the city highly suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions, but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain types of businesses. However, the relatively high cost of living and tight labor market, which may lead to wage pressure, are significant factors that decision-makers should consider when evaluating this location.
