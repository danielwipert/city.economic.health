Economic Analysis

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking in the 75.4th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a composite score in the 96th percentile, and its strong office economy, also ranking in the 96th percentile. The labor demand is driven by a 3.20% year-over-year employment growth rate and a -0.796% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend.

Labor Demand

The combination of a 3.20% employment growth rate and a -0.796% deviation in weekly hours signals genuine demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs while hours worked are slightly below trend. This indicates that the labor market is experiencing real growth, rather than just absorbing existing workload. The labor demand composite score of 7.70 further supports this interpretation, placing the city in the top tier.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Salt Lake City-Murray is 3.50%, ranking in the 72nd percentile, which indicates a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight market also suggests that workers may have more bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Salt Lake City-Murray is 4.60%, ranking in the 60th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that employer labor costs are rising, but not excessively so, and that workers are experiencing some increase in purchasing power. However, the wage growth is not exceptionally high, which may help keep labor costs in check.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living percentile rank of 55th, Salt Lake City-Murray is near the median in terms of affordability, with a PSF of $263/sqft and an hourly earnings rate of $39.49, resulting in a ratio of 6.66. This means that the city is neither exceptionally affordable nor expensive, and businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to attract talent. However, the slight increase in PSF of 0.8% year-over-year may indicate some upward pressure on costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Salt Lake City-Murray is growing at a rate of 1.13% year-over-year, ranking in the 86th percentile, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This growth suggests that businesses will have a larger pool of potential workers to draw from, making it easier to hire and staff operations.

Building Permits

The city has seen a 40.08% year-over-year increase in building permits, ranking in the 88th percentile, indicating a significant expansion in housing supply. This growth suggests that the city is likely to experience improving affordability and a more accommodating environment for workers, as the increased supply of housing will help meet demand.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 57 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 1.7%, ranking in the 22nd percentile. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, where homes are selling quickly, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the decrease in days on market suggests that the market may be normalizing slightly.

Office Economy

With an office economy percentile rank of 96th, Salt Lake City-Murray has a deep and talented pool of professional workers, making it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. However, the city may be less suited for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors, which may require a different type of workforce.

The Salt Lake City-Murray metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, a deep professional talent pool, and a growing workforce supply, making it an attractive location for a variety of industries. However, the relatively tight labor market and moderate wage growth may pose challenges for businesses looking to hire and staff operations, and decision-makers should factor in the potential need for competitive wages and benefits to attract top talent.
