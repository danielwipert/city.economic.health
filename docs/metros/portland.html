Economic Analysis

The Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 36.0th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score largely defined by its weak labor demand and high wage growth. The labor demand composite score of 1.50, combining a -2.07% employment growth rate and a -0.245% weekly hours deviation from trend, signals a contraction in labor demand. This, coupled with a strong wage growth rate of 5.82%, suggests a complex economic environment.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of -2.07% and weekly hours deviation of -0.245% from the city's own trend indicate a contraction in labor demand, signaling that the city is experiencing a period of job losses and reduced working hours. This combination suggests a lack of genuine demand expansion, instead pointing towards a potential survivor squeeze where remaining workers may absorb the workload of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 1.50 falls into the bottom tier, at the 0th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 5.00% places the city below the average, ranking at the 24th percentile. This rate indicates that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, the practical implication for a business trying to hire in this city is that while there may be a pool of available workers, the overall labor market conditions are not highly favorable.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 5.82%, the city experiences above-average wage increases, ranking at the 76th percentile. This fast wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers but also indicates good worker purchasing power. Businesses should factor in the potential for increased labor expenses when considering this location.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro, with a PSF of $306/sqft and an earnings ratio of $42.08/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.27, is considered above average in terms of affordability, ranking at the 74th percentile. This means the city is relatively more affordable compared to its peers, especially since the PSF is falling by 2.2% YoY. This affordability could serve as a talent attraction advantage, as workers may not require significant wage premiums to maintain their standard of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro metro area is contracting at a rate of -0.81% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This negative growth rate suggests a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses should consider this constraint when planning for future workforce needs.

Building Permits

The city has seen a 2.48% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, which is near the median, ranking at the 56th percentile. This increase signals that housing supply is expanding, which could lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

Homes in the Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro area are currently sitting on the market for a median of 57 days, with a year-over-year increase of 3.6%. This indicates a slightly slower market compared to the previous year. For a worker relocating to this city, the market is becoming more accessible, as homes are not selling as quickly as they were, providing more time for decision-making.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors is 0.68, ranking at the 8th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these areas. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations, and may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs.

The Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro metro area offers a complex mix of economic conditions, with high wage growth and relative affordability being notable advantages. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for a business considering this location is the weak labor demand and contracting labor force, which could significantly impact hiring capacity and business expansion plans.
