Economic Analysis

The Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 35.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 1.50, indicating a struggling job market. The city's economic character is most defined by its negative employment growth rate of -2.81% and high wage growth rate of +6.37%. These metrics suggest a complex economic environment, with both challenges and opportunities for businesses.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro is -2.81%, and weekly hours are deviating -0.489% from the city's own 12-month baseline, signaling a contraction in labor demand. This combination indicates a lack of genuine demand expansion, with the city experiencing a decline in job creation. The negative hours deviation suggests that the remaining workforce is not absorbing additional hours, further emphasizing the labor market's weakness.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro is 4.70%, ranking in the 24th percentile, indicating a below-average but still relatively tight labor market. This level of unemployment suggests that while there is some slack in the market, hiring may still be challenging, and businesses may face upward pressure on wages. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive compensation packages to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro is +6.37%, ranking in the 80th percentile, indicating fast and rising labor costs for employers. This strong wage growth is good for worker purchasing power, as employees will have more disposable income to spend on goods and services. However, it also means that businesses will need to factor in higher labor costs when making location decisions.

Cost of Living

Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro has a cost of living score with a PSF of $309/sqft, which is decreasing by -1.9% YoY, and an earnings ratio of $41.92/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.37, ranking in the 57th percentile. This suggests that the city is near the median in terms of affordability, making it neither particularly attractive nor unattractive for talent without wage premiums. The decreasing PSF is a positive sign, but the overall affordability is still average.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro is growing at a rate of -1.14% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor pool. This negative growth rate suggests that the workforce supply is shrinking, creating a structural headwind for hiring and potentially limiting the city's ability to support business expansion.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro is increasing by +7.12% year-over-year, indicating an expansion of future housing supply. This growth in permits suggests that developer confidence is rising, and the city's housing market may become more affordable in the future, making it easier for workers to relocate and for businesses to attract talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro is 51 days, with a year-over-year increase of +4.1%. This indicates a relatively slow market, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, the rising DOM may also signal a normalization of the market, rather than a demand erosion, as the city's job market is still struggling.

Office Economy

Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro has an office economy score ranking in the 8th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The Portland-Vancouver-Hillsboro metro area offers a complex economic environment, with both challenges and opportunities for businesses. While the city's high wage growth and expanding housing supply are positive signs, the struggling job market and shrinking labor pool pose significant risks for businesses looking to locate or expand in the area. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited labor market, which may limit the ability to attract and retain top talent.
