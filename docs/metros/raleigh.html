Economic Analysis

Raleigh-Cary has earned an overall grade of A, with a score of 71.0 out of 100, making it a very good location for businesses, driven in part by its strong labor demand, with employment growth of 2.3% over the past year. This growth, combined with a low unemployment rate of 2.7%, suggests a thriving job market with a high demand for workers.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 2.3% over the past year, with workers' hours also rising by 1.0%, indicating a strong demand for labor and a healthy job market. This means that businesses in Raleigh-Cary are likely to face a competitive hiring environment, but also have access to a growing and engaged workforce.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

With an unemployment rate of 2.7%, Raleigh-Cary has a very low level of joblessness, making it harder for employers to hire and potentially driving up wages. This low unemployment rate, which scores 94 out of 100, indicates that the city has a tight labor market, where workers have more bargaining power.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Over the past year, hourly earnings in Raleigh-Cary have risen by 3.2%, which is slower than many other metros, with a score of 34 out of 100. This slower wage growth means that employers may face relatively stable labor costs, but workers may not see significant increases in their paychecks.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Raleigh-Cary is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $213 and hourly pay of $38.12, and it's getting even more affordable as housing prices have fallen by 3.2% over the past year, earning a score of 80 out of 100. This makes the city an attractive location for workers and businesses looking for a reasonable cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in Raleigh-Cary is growing at a rate of 0.9% over the past year, which is faster than many other metros, with a score of 80 out of 100. This growing labor force provides a positive outlook for businesses looking to hire and expand in the area.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in Raleigh-Cary have decreased by 22.7% over the past year, which is a weaker trend than many other metros, with a score of 12 out of 100. This decline in building permits may signal a future shortage of housing supply, which could impact the city's ability to attract and retain workers.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Raleigh-Cary are taking 64 days to sell, which is getting slower, with a 6.7% increase over the past year, earning a score of 18 out of 100. This slower pace of home sales indicates a softer housing market, which may make it easier for buyers to find a home, but could also be a sign of a weakening market.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

Raleigh-Cary has a large and thriving office-based workforce, with a score of 98 out of 100, making it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This strong office economy provides a deep talent pool for businesses looking to establish or expand their operations in the city.

Raleigh-Cary's biggest strength is its strong labor demand and low unemployment rate, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to hire and grow. However, the city's biggest risk is its slower wage growth and declining building permits, which could impact its ability to attract and retain workers and provide affordable housing options.
