Economic Analysis

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking in the 76.6th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its exceptionally strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 98th percentile, driven by a 2.07% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.137% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend. Additionally, its low unemployment rate of 3.10% and high office economy percentile rank of 98th also play significant roles in shaping its economic landscape.

Labor Demand

The Raleigh-Cary metro area exhibits a robust labor demand, with a 2.07% year-over-year employment growth rate and weekly hours 1.137% above its own trend, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a significant increase in job creation, with workers putting in more hours, suggesting a thriving economy. The labor demand composite score of 8.33 further reinforces this assessment, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Raleigh-Cary stands at 3.10%, which is relatively low, indicating a tight labor market. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. With an unemployment rate percentile rank of 94th, the market has little slack, making it a competitive environment for employers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Raleigh-Cary is 3.43%, which is near the median, suggesting moderate wage increases. This rate of wage growth implies that employer labor costs are rising, but at a manageable pace, while workers are experiencing an increase in purchasing power. However, the 48th percentile rank in wage growth indicates that the city is not an outlier in terms of wage pressure, with many other metros experiencing similar or higher rates of growth.

Cost of Living

Raleigh-Cary has a cost of living percentile rank of 74th, indicating that it is more affordable than many of its peer cities, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $217/sqft to $37.64/hr, or 5.77. The fact that PSF is falling by 2.2% year-over-year relative to wages further enhances the city's affordability. This affordability advantage can be a significant talent attraction factor, as businesses can offer competitive wages without needing large premiums to offset high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Raleigh-Cary is growing at a year-over-year rate of 0.37%, which, while positive, indicates a relatively slow expansion of the workforce supply. This slow growth suggests that hiring capacity may be somewhat constrained, as the pool of potential workers is not increasing rapidly. However, the 58th percentile rank in labor force growth indicates that the city is not uniquely challenged in this regard, with many metros experiencing similar or slower growth rates.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Raleigh-Cary is +4.15%, indicating a moderate expansion of housing supply. This increase in permits suggests that developer confidence is present, and the future housing supply is likely to improve, which can help maintain or improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. The 54th percentile rank in building permits growth, however, indicates that the city is not leading in terms of housing supply expansion.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Raleigh-Cary is 54 days, with a year-over-year increase of 3.9%, placing it at the 62nd percentile. This increase in days on market, combined with the absolute level, suggests a slightly slower and more accessible market for relocating workers, providing them with more time to find suitable housing. However, the market remains relatively competitive, and relocating workers may still face challenges in finding homes quickly.

Office Economy

Raleigh-Cary boasts a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy percentile rank of 98th, indicating a strong presence of jobs in professional and office sectors. This makes the city highly suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions, where access to specialized knowledge workers is crucial. Conversely, it may be less ideal for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant, which require different types of workforce skills.

The Raleigh-Cary metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, low unemployment, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain types of industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the potential for hiring challenges due to the tight labor market, which may necessitate offering competitive wages to attract and retain talent.
