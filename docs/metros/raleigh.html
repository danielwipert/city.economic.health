Economic Analysis

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 70.8th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and low unemployment rate defining its current economic character, at 98th and 94th percentiles, respectively. The labor demand composite score of 8.63, driven by a 2.23% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.207% deviation in weekly hours above trend, signals a genuine demand expansion. This combination of metrics suggests a thriving job market.

Labor Demand

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has seen a 2.23% year-over-year employment growth rate, coupled with a 1.207% deviation in weekly hours above trend, indicating a strong labor demand. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, where jobs are being added and hours are increasing, suggesting a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 8.63 further reinforces this notion, placing the city in the top tier.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Raleigh-Cary stands at 3.10%, which is in the top tier, indicating a tight labor market. This low unemployment rate implies that it may be challenging for businesses to hire, as the pool of available workers is limited, and wage pressure may be higher. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Raleigh-Cary is 3.16%, which is below average, ranking in the 38th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, albeit not as rapidly as in other cities. This may impact businesses' decisions on staffing and compensation.

Cost of Living

Raleigh-Cary has a cost of living score that places it in the 81st percentile, with a PSF of $213/sqft and average hourly earnings of $38.60/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.52. The fact that PSF is decreasing by 3.2% year-over-year makes the city more affordable. This affordability advantage can be a significant talent attraction factor, as workers may not require wage premiums to maintain their standard of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Raleigh-Cary has contracted by 1.63% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This negative growth rate implies that the supply of workers is decreasing, which may pose a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion. Companies may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to attract workers from other areas.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Raleigh-Cary has decreased by 5.68% year-over-year, suggesting that housing supply is tightening. This decline in building permits may lead to a supply squeeze, potentially impacting future affordability and workforce accommodation. As a result, businesses may face challenges in attracting and retaining workers due to limited housing options.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Raleigh-Cary is 59 days, with no year-over-year change. This relatively fast pace indicates a competitive market, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the stable days on market suggests that the market is not overheating, and workers may still find suitable housing options.

Office Economy

Raleigh-Cary has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking in the 98th percentile. This suggests that the city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized workers. However, it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Raleigh-Cary metro area offers businesses a strong labor market, low unemployment, and an affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for companies looking to expand or relocate. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor pool, which may pose challenges for hiring and business growth, and the tightening housing supply, which could impact workforce accommodation and affordability.
