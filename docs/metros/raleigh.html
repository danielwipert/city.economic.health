Economic Analysis

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 75.1th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is largely defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a composite score in the 98th percentile, and its strong office economy, also in the 98th percentile, with 4.48 out of 5 score in professional/office worker share. The labor demand is driven by a 2.07% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.137% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend.

Labor Demand

The Raleigh-Cary metro area is experiencing genuine demand expansion, with a 2.07% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.137% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend. This combination signals a strong and growing job market, where hours are increasing alongside employment, indicating that businesses are not only hiring but also utilizing their workforce more intensely. The labor demand composite score of 8.33 places it in the top tier, at the 98th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Raleigh-Cary stands at 3.20%, which is in the top tier, ranking at the 84th percentile. This indicates a relatively tight labor market with less slack, making it harder for businesses to hire due to increased competition for available workers. As a result, businesses may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Raleigh-Cary is 3.43%, placing it near the median at the 48th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but not at an alarming rate, while workers benefit from a modest increase in purchasing power. However, the relatively moderate pace of wage growth may not significantly erode profit margins for businesses.

Cost of Living

Raleigh-Cary has a cost of living score that ranks in the 74th percentile, indicating it is more affordable than many of its peer cities, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $215/sqft to $37.64/hr, which is 5.71. The fact that PSF is falling by 2.3% year-over-year relative to wages enhances the city's affordability. This makes it an attractive location for talent, as businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Raleigh-Cary is growing at a rate of 0.48% year-over-year, which is above average, ranking at the 68th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, albeit slowly, providing a slightly increasing pool of potential hires for businesses. However, the growth rate is not exceptionally high, which may limit the rapid expansion of businesses reliant on a rapidly growing labor force.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is 4.15%, placing it near the median at the 54th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is slowly expanding, which could improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce over time. However, the moderate pace of permit issuance may not rapidly alleviate any existing housing supply constraints.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Raleigh-Cary is 59 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.3%, ranking near the median at the 52nd percentile. This indicates a moderately competitive market that is becoming slightly less heated over time. For workers relocating to this city, the market is neither extremely competitive nor very accessible, offering a relatively balanced environment for homebuyers.

Office Economy

Raleigh-Cary boasts a deep professional talent pool, with its office economy ranking in the 98th percentile. This makes the city highly suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ operations that require specialized knowledge workers. Conversely, it may be less ideal for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant, which require different skill sets.

The Raleigh-Cary metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, a deep professional talent pool, and a relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for a variety of industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the relatively tight labor market, which may lead to increased competition for talent and upward pressure on wages, potentially affecting business operational costs and profitability.
