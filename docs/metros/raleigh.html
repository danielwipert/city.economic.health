Economic Analysis

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 75.1th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a composite score in the 98th percentile, and its strong office economy, also in the 98th percentile, with 4.48 out of 5 score for professional and office worker share. The labor demand is driven by a 2.07% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.137% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend.

Labor Demand

The Raleigh-Cary metro area exhibits a strong labor demand, with a 2.07% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.137% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a significant increase in job creation and hours worked, suggesting a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 8.33 places it in the top tier, with a 98th percentile rank.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Raleigh-Cary is 3.20%, which is relatively low and indicates a tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in finding and hiring qualified workers, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. With an 84th percentile rank, the city's low unemployment rate suggests a strong economy with limited slack in the labor market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Raleigh-Cary is 3.43%, which is near the median compared to other US metros. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but at a manageable pace. Workers in the area are also experiencing an increase in purchasing power, which can have a positive impact on local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Raleigh-Cary has a cost of living score that places it in the 74th percentile, indicating that it is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The city's PSF of $215/sqft is decreasing at a rate of -2.3% year-over-year, relative to average hourly earnings of $37.64/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.71. This affordability makes it an attractive location for talent, as businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Raleigh-Cary is growing at a rate of 0.48% year-over-year, which is above average compared to other US metros. This expansion in labor force supply suggests that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to find and hire talent. With a 68th percentile rank, the city's labor force growth is a positive indicator for hiring capacity.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Raleigh-Cary is increasing at a rate of 4.15% year-over-year, which is near the median compared to other US metros. This expansion in housing supply suggests that the city is experiencing developer confidence and investment in new residential construction, which can help improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Raleigh-Cary is 59 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.3%. This suggests that the housing market is normalizing, with homes taking slightly longer to sell. With a 52nd percentile rank, the city's days on market is near the median, indicating a relatively balanced market that is neither extremely competitive nor extremely slow.

Office Economy

Raleigh-Cary has a deep professional talent pool, with an office and professional worker share score of 4.48 out of 5, placing it in the 98th percentile. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. The city is less suited for industries that require a large workforce in industrial or logistics roles.

The Raleigh-Cary metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, a deep professional talent pool, and relatively affordable cost of living. However, the city's tight labor market and moderate wage growth rate may pose challenges for businesses looking to hire and retain talent, making it essential for decision-makers to factor in these constraints when considering a location in Raleigh-Cary.
