Economic Analysis

The Raleigh-Cary metro area boasts an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 80.7th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is largely defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a composite score of 8.84, and its strong office economy, with a professional worker share of 4.54. The labor demand is driven by a 2.17% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.930% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend.

Labor Demand

The Raleigh-Cary metro area exhibits a strong labor demand, with a 2.17% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.930% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a surge in job creation, with workers putting in more hours to meet the growing demand. The labor demand composite score of 8.84 further reinforces this trend, placing the city in the top tier of labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Raleigh-Cary stands at 3.00%, which is relatively low and indicates a tight labor market. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the available workforce is scarce, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. With an unemployment rate percentile rank of 88th, the city's labor market is characterized by a high level of employment, making it a competitive environment for businesses looking to hire.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Raleigh-Cary is 4.25%, which is moderate and indicates a stable labor cost environment. While this wage growth is not exceptionally high, it still suggests that workers have some bargaining power, and businesses may need to adjust their compensation packages accordingly. The 58th percentile rank for wage growth indicates that the city's wage growth is near the median, neither exceptionally high nor low.

Cost of Living

Raleigh-Cary has a cost of living percentile rank of 84th, indicating that it is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The city's PSF of $218/sqft, which is decreasing by 2.2% year-over-year, combined with an average hourly earnings of $38.42, results in a ratio of 5.67, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. This affordability advantage can be a major draw for businesses looking to attract and retain employees.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Raleigh-Cary is growing at a rate of 0.58% year-over-year, indicating a moderate expansion of the workforce supply. This growth suggests that the city's labor pool is increasing, providing businesses with a slightly larger talent pool to draw from. However, the 78th percentile rank for labor force growth indicates that the city's workforce expansion is not exceptionally rapid.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Raleigh-Cary has increased by 15.33% year-over-year, signaling an expansion of the city's housing supply. This growth in building permits suggests that the city is likely to experience improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for its workforce in the future. The 76th percentile rank for building permits indicates that the city's housing supply is growing at a rate above the national average.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Raleigh-Cary is 50 days, with a year-over-year change of 0.0%, indicating a relatively fast-paced market. However, the 30th percentile rank for days on market suggests that the city's housing market is actually relatively competitive, making it challenging for relocating workers to find homes. This fast-paced market may require businesses to provide relocation assistance or adjust their hiring timelines accordingly.

Office Economy

Raleigh-Cary has a deep professional talent pool, with a percentile rank of 98th, indicating that it is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. The city's strong office economy, with a professional worker share of 4.54, makes it an attractive location for businesses that require specialized knowledge workers. However, the city may be less suitable for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

The Raleigh-Cary metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, a deep professional talent pool, and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the city's tight labor market and competitive housing market may pose challenges for businesses looking to hire and relocate workers, making it essential for decision-makers to factor in these constraints when considering Raleigh-Cary as a potential location.
