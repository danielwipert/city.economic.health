Economic Analysis

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 70.7th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and a deep professional talent pool being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, scoring 98th percentile in both employment growth and office economy. The labor demand composite score is 8.63, indicating a top-tier performance. This suggests that the city is experiencing genuine demand expansion, with employment growth of 2.23% and weekly hours 1.207% above trend.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 2.23% and hours deviation of 1.207% above trend signal genuine demand expansion, indicating that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a sign of a strong and growing economy. This combination suggests that businesses are hiring and expanding, creating new opportunities for workers. The labor demand composite score of 8.63 reinforces this assessment, placing the city in the top tier.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.10% is relatively low, ranking in the 94th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This means that businesses may face challenges in finding and hiring qualified workers, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate also suggests that workers have more bargaining power, which could drive up labor costs.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of 3.16% is below average, ranking in the 38th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace than in other cities. While this may be beneficial for workers' purchasing power, it also means that businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages.

Cost of Living

The city's cost of living, with a PSF of $213/sqft and hourly earnings of $38.60, resulting in a ratio of 5.52, is relatively affordable, ranking in the 82nd percentile. This means that the city offers a talent attraction advantage, as workers can afford to live and work in the area without requiring significant wage premiums. The fact that PSF is falling by 3.2% YoY further enhances the city's affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -1.63% year-over-year, ranking in the 36th percentile, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This suggests that the city may face structural headwinds in hiring, as the supply of workers is decreasing. Businesses may need to consider strategies to attract workers from other areas or invest in training and development programs.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -5.68%, ranking in the 36th percentile, indicating a tightening of housing supply. This suggests that the city may face future affordability and workforce accommodation challenges, as the supply of housing is not keeping pace with demand. This could lead to increased competition for housing and higher costs for workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market is 59 days, with a year-over-year change of 0.0%, ranking in the 35th percentile. This suggests that the housing market is relatively balanced, neither extremely competitive nor extremely accessible. Workers relocating to the city may face a moderate level of competition for housing, but the market is not overly challenging.

Office Economy

The city's professional talent pool is deep, ranking in the 98th percentile, with a share of 4.54 out of 5. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high level of specialized knowledge and skills. However, the city may be less suited for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

The Raleigh-Cary metro area offers businesses a strong labor demand and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for companies in the tech and finance sectors. However, the city's shrinking labor pool and tightening housing supply pose significant risks and constraints that decision-makers should factor in, potentially requiring strategies to attract workers from other areas or invest in training and development programs. Overall, the city's strengths make it a competitive location, but its weaknesses require careful consideration and planning.
