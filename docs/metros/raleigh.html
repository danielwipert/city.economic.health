Economic Analysis

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 79.8th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is largely defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a composite score of 8.33, and its low unemployment rate of 3.10%, which signals a tight labor market. The combination of a 2.07% employment growth rate and a 1.137% weekly hours deviation from trend also underscores the city's strong labor demand.

Labor Demand

The Raleigh-Cary metro area has seen a 2.07% employment growth rate and a 1.137% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a surge in job creation, with hours worked above the trend line, suggesting that businesses are actively seeking to hire and expand their operations. The labor demand composite score of 8.33 further reinforces this notion, placing the city in the top tier of labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Raleigh-Cary stands at 3.10%, which is a top-tier percentile score of 94th, indicating a very tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in finding available talent, and wage pressure is likely to be higher. As a result, companies looking to hire in this market may need to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract top candidates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Raleigh-Cary is 3.43%, which places it near the median at the 48th percentile. While this rate is not exceptionally high, it still indicates moderate wage growth, which can lead to rising labor costs for employers. However, this also translates to increased purchasing power for workers, making the city an attractive location for businesses that value a skilled and relatively affluent workforce.

Cost of Living

Raleigh-Cary has a cost of living percentile rank of 74th, with a PSF of $217/sqft and average hourly earnings of $37.64/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.77. Although this ratio is above average, the city's cost of living is still relatively affordable, particularly given the 2.2% year-over-year decline in PSF. This makes Raleigh-Cary an attractive location for talent, as businesses can offer competitive salaries without needing to provide excessive wage premiums to offset high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Raleigh-Cary has grown by 0.37% year-over-year, which is near the median at the 58th percentile. While this growth rate is not exceptionally high, it still indicates that the labor force is expanding, albeit slowly. This modest growth suggests that businesses may face some challenges in finding new talent, but the overall labor pool is still increasing.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Raleigh-Cary has increased by 31.25% year-over-year, placing it in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This significant growth in building permits signals that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate the city's growing workforce. As a result, businesses can expect a more stable and affordable housing market for their employees.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Raleigh-Cary is currently 54 days, with a year-over-year increase of 3.9%. This places the city at the 62nd percentile, indicating a relatively balanced market. While homes are still selling fairly quickly, the rising days on market suggests a healthy normalization of the housing market, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find suitable housing.

Office Economy

Raleigh-Cary has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy percentile rank of 98th. This makes the city an ideal location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. However, the city may be less suited for industries that rely heavily on industrial or logistics workers.

The Raleigh-Cary metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, low unemployment, and a relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for companies looking to expand or relocate. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the potential for wage pressure and challenges in finding available talent in a tight labor market, which may require businesses to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract top candidates.
