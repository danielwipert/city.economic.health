Economic Analysis

Raleigh-Cary earns an overall grade of A, with a score of 71.0 out of 100, making it better than most of the 50 metros. The city's economy is defined by its strong job market, with employment growth of 2.27% over the past year, and a low unemployment rate of 2.70%, indicating a tight labor market.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 2.27% over the past year, with workers' hours rising by 1.0%, indicating a strong demand for labor. This means that the job market is healthy, with employers looking to hire and workers putting in more hours, making it a good time for businesses to expand.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 2.70% is very low, making it hard for employers to hire, as there are fewer people looking for work. With a score of 94, Raleigh-Cary has a tighter labor market than most of the 50 metros, which can drive up wages and make it challenging for businesses to find the talent they need.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 3.23% over the past year, which is slower than many other metros, with a score of 34, indicating that wage pressure is relatively low. This means that employers may not face significant cost increases, but workers may not see their paychecks grow as quickly as they would in other cities.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

Housing in Raleigh-Cary is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $213 and hourly pay of $38.12, earning a score of 80, which is better than most of the 50 metros. The cost of living is getting more affordable, as housing prices have fallen by 3.2% over the past year, making it easier for workers to find affordable housing.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers is growing at a rate of 0.92% over the past year, which is a relatively slow pace, but still indicates an expanding workforce, with a score of 80. This means that businesses may have a slightly easier time finding talent, but the growth rate is not as strong as in other metros.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals have fallen by 22.66% over the past year, which is a significant decline, earning a score of 12, indicating a potential future housing supply shortage. This signals that the city's housing market may become less affordable in the future, making it harder for workers to find homes and for businesses to attract talent.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes are taking 64 days to sell, which is getting slower, with a 6.7% increase over the past year, earning a score of 18, indicating a softening housing market. This is a weakness, as a slower pace of home sales can indicate a less healthy housing market, making it harder for workers to relocate and for businesses to attract talent.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

Raleigh-Cary has a large and growing office-based workforce, with a score of 98, making it better than most of the 50 metros, and suiting businesses in tech, finance, and professional services. The city's strong office economy makes it an attractive location for businesses looking to establish or expand their operations in these sectors.

The bottom line is that Raleigh-Cary's biggest strength is its strong job market, with low unemployment and growing employment, making it an attractive location for businesses. However, the city's biggest risk is its potential future housing supply shortage, driven by a decline in new home approvals, which could make it harder for workers to find affordable housing and for businesses to attract talent.
