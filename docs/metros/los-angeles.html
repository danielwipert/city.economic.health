Economic Analysis

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area has an overall grade of B, with a score of 55.3 out of 100, indicating it is an average city for business, with its strongest aspect being its labor demand, which is stronger than most metros, with a score of 86 out of 100. This is driven by employment growth of 1.8% over the past year, with workers putting in 0.3% more hours, indicating a genuine demand for labor.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.8% over the past year, with workers also putting in 0.3% more hours, which means the job market is strong and employers are looking for more workers. This is a positive sign for the local economy, as it indicates that businesses are expanding and need more labor to meet demand, with a score of 86 out of 100, better than most of the 50 metros.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in the city is 4.4%, which is lower than most metros, with a score of 64 out of 100, making it a strength, as it indicates a tight labor market where employers may face challenges in finding workers. This low unemployment rate means that employers may need to offer competitive wages to attract and retain talent, as the labor market is relatively tight.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages in the city rose by 3.6% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 46 out of 100, indicating that employers' labor costs are rising, but not as quickly as in some other cities. This moderate wage growth means that workers are seeing some increase in their paychecks, but it may not be enough to keep up with the cost of living in the area.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in the city is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $642 and hourly pay of $41.84, and it is getting worse, as the price per square foot has fallen by 3.0% over the past year, but the score of 4 out of 100 indicates it is less affordable than most metros. This means that workers may struggle to afford housing and other living expenses, making it a challenge for employers to attract and retain talent.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in the city is shrinking, with a decline of 1.3% over the past year, which is slower than most metros, with a score of 24 out of 100, indicating a weakness, as it means that the pool of available workers is decreasing. This decline in the labor force makes it even more challenging for employers to find the workers they need, as the talent pool is shrinking.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

The number of new home building permits in the city increased by 27.3% over the past year, with a score of 88 out of 100, indicating a strength, as it signals that the housing supply is expanding, which could help to address affordability issues. This increase in building permits is a positive sign for the city's housing market, as it suggests that more homes will be available in the future, which could help to reduce prices and make housing more affordable.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in the city are taking 57 days to sell, which is relatively stable, with no change over the past year, but the score of 30 out of 100 indicates a weaker housing market than most metros. This stability in the days on market suggests that the housing market is not too tight, but it is also not overly soft, as buyers have a reasonable amount of time to find a home.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros, with a score of 30 out of 100, indicating a weakness, as it means that the city may not have as deep a talent pool for tech, finance, and professional services companies. This smaller office workforce makes the city less suited for businesses that rely on these types of workers, such as tech startups or financial firms.

The city's biggest strength is its labor demand, with a strong job market and growing employment, which makes it an attractive location for businesses looking to expand. However, its biggest risk is its high cost of living, which makes it challenging for workers to afford housing and other living expenses, and could make it difficult for employers to attract and retain talent.
