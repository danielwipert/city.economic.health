Economic Analysis

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 47.3th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand, with a composite score of 6.18 ranking it in the 76th percentile, and its cost of living, which is in the bottom tier with a 4th percentile rank due to a high PSF to wages ratio of $652/sqft to $41.61/hr. The labor demand and cost of living metrics provide a mixed picture of the city's economic health.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 1.83% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.681% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as jobs are being added and hours are running above trend, indicating a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 6.18 reinforces this signal, ranking the city in the 76th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 5.00%, ranking it in the 48th percentile, indicating a near-median level of unemployment. This suggests that the market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter labor markets. However, this also implies that local consumer demand may be weaker due to the availability of labor.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 3.48%, ranking it in the 52nd percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate of wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a pace that is not excessively high. Meanwhile, workers in the area are experiencing an increase in purchasing power, which can benefit local businesses through increased consumer spending.

Cost of Living

Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim has a cost of living score ranking it in the 4th percentile, indicating that it is one of the more expensive cities relative to peers, with a PSF to wages ratio of $652/sqft to $41.61/hr. However, the PSF is falling by 2.1% year-over-year, which is a key driver of affordability. Despite this, the city's high cost of living means that businesses may need to offer wage premiums to attract talent, as the city's affordability is a significant disadvantage.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is contracting at a rate of -2.18% year-over-year, ranking it in the 8th percentile. This indicates that the labor force supply is shrinking, presenting a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion in the area. Businesses looking to grow their workforce may face challenges due to the declining labor pool.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is increasing by 17.69% year-over-year, ranking it in the 66th percentile. This signals that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate workforce growth in the future. The increase in building permits is a positive indicator for businesses considering relocation or expansion, as it suggests that the city is investing in its housing infrastructure.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 53 days, with a year-over-year change of 0.0%, ranking it in the 28th percentile. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, which can make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. The low percentile rank suggests that the market is competitive, and businesses may need to provide relocation assistance to help employees navigate the housing market.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim ranks in the 54th percentile, indicating a near-median depth of professional talent pool. This suggests that the city is suited for a variety of businesses, including those in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors, but may not have a significant advantage in these areas. The city's office economy is neither particularly strong nor weak, making it a viable option for businesses that do not require a highly specialized workforce.

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area offers businesses a mixed economic environment, with strong labor demand and moderate wage growth, but also a high cost of living and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering this location is the high cost of living, which may require wage premiums to attract and retain talent, potentially offsetting the benefits of the city's strong labor market.
