Economic Analysis

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 47.3th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand, with a composite score of 6.18 ranking it at the 76th percentile, and its cost of living, which is in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile due to a high PSF to wages ratio of $652/sqft to $41.61/hr. The labor demand and cost of living metrics signal a complex economic environment, with both positive and negative trends.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 1.83% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating from the trend by -0.681%. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs and hours are running above trend, indicating a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 6.18 supports this interpretation, ranking the city at the 76th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 5.00%, ranking it at the 48th percentile, indicating a near-median level of unemployment. This suggests that the market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to cities with tighter labor markets. However, the unemployment rate is not low enough to indicate a significant surplus of workers, and businesses may still face some competition for talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 3.48%, ranking it at the 52nd percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate of wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, and worker purchasing power is increasing. As a result, businesses may need to factor in moderate wage increases when planning for labor costs.

Cost of Living

Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim has a cost of living percentile rank of 4, indicating that it is one of the more expensive cities in the US, with a PSF to wages ratio of $652/sqft to $41.61/hr. However, the PSF is falling by 2.1% year-over-year, which may help improve affordability in the future. The high cost of living makes it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums, as the city's expensive housing market may deter some workers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is shrinking by 2.18% year-over-year, ranking it at the 8th percentile. This contraction in the labor force supply indicates a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may need to consider this trend when planning for future hiring needs.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is increasing by 17.69% year-over-year, ranking it at the 66th percentile. This expansion in housing supply suggests that the city is taking steps to address its affordability challenges, which may improve the prospects for workforce accommodation in the future. As a result, businesses may find it easier to attract and retain talent in the long term.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 53 days, with no year-over-year change. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, ranking it at the 28th percentile. For workers relocating to this city, the fast-paced market may make it challenging to find and secure housing, potentially affecting their decision to move.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 2.66, ranking it at the 54th percentile. This suggests that the city has a moderately deep talent pool in these sectors, making it suitable for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting industries. However, the city may be less suited for businesses that require a large workforce in industrial or logistics sectors.

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area offers businesses a complex economic environment with both opportunities and challenges. While the city has a strong labor demand and moderate wage growth, its high cost of living and shrinking labor force supply pose significant risks for businesses looking to attract and retain talent. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's expensive housing market, which may require businesses to offer wage premiums to compete for talent.
