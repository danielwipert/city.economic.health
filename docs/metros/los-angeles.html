Economic Analysis

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area has an overall grade of B, with a score of 55.3 out of 100, indicating it is an average city for business and economic growth, with employment growth being a notable strength, at 1.8% over the past year. This growth, combined with a low unemployment rate, defines the city's economy right now, with 86 out of 100 score in labor demand, indicating stronger hiring than most metros.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.8% over the past year, with workers' hours also rising by 0.3%, indicating a strong job market with genuine demand for labor. This means that employers are likely to face a competitive hiring environment, but also have opportunities to attract top talent in a growing market.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in the city is 4.4%, which is lower than most metros, with a score of 64 out of 100, making it a strength for the city. This low unemployment rate means that employers may face challenges in finding available workers, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages in the city rose by 3.6% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 46 out of 100. This moderate wage growth means that employers can expect stable labor costs, but workers may not see significant increases in their paychecks.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in the city is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $642 and hourly pay of $41.84, resulting in a score of 4 out of 100, indicating it is less affordable than most metros. The price per square foot has actually fallen by 3.0% over the past year, but the city remains one of the more expensive places to live, making it challenging for workers to afford housing.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in the city is shrinking at a rate of -1.3% over the past year, with a score of 24 out of 100, indicating slower workforce growth than most metros. This decline in the labor force means that employers may face challenges in finding available workers, and may need to consider recruiting from outside the city or investing in training and development programs.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in the city have increased by 27.3% over the past year, with a score of 88 out of 100, indicating stronger home-building than most metros. This surge in building permits signals that the city's housing supply is likely to increase in the future, which could help improve affordability and attract more workers to the area.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

The typical home in the city takes 57 days to sell, with no change over the past year, resulting in a score of 30 out of 100, indicating a weaker housing market than most metros. This relatively slow pace of home sales suggests that the housing market may be softening, making it easier for buyers to find homes, but potentially challenging for sellers.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros, with a score of 30 out of 100, indicating a weaker presence of tech, finance, and professional services. This means that the city may be more suited to businesses that are not heavily reliant on these sectors, such as manufacturing or logistics companies.

The city's biggest strength is its strong labor demand, with a score of 86 out of 100, indicating a competitive and growing job market. However, its biggest risk is its high cost of living, with a score of 4 out of 100, making it challenging for workers to afford housing and potentially limiting the city's ability to attract and retain top talent.
