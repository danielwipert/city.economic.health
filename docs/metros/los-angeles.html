Economic Analysis

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area has an overall grade of B, with a score of 55.3 out of 100, indicating it is average compared to other metros. What most defines this city's economy right now is its strong job growth, with employment increasing by 1.83% over the past year, and workers putting in 0.3% more hours, indicating a genuine demand for labor.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.83% over the past year, with workers also putting in 0.3% more hours, which means the job market is strong and employers are looking for more workers. This is a positive sign for the local economy, as it indicates that businesses are expanding and need more labor to meet demand, with a score of 86, better than most of the 50 metros.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate is 4.40%, which is relatively low, making it somewhat challenging for employers to hire new workers, with a score of 64, above average. This low unemployment rate means that workers have more bargaining power, and employers may need to offer higher wages to attract and retain talent.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 3.57% over the past year, which is a moderate increase, meaning that employers' labor costs are rising, but workers' paychecks are not increasing as quickly as in some other metros, with a score of 46, near median. This moderate wage growth may make it easier for employers to manage their labor costs, but it also means that workers may not have as much purchasing power as they would in cities with faster wage growth.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

Housing in Los Angeles is relatively expensive, with a price of $642 per square foot, compared to an hourly pay of $41.84, and the cost of living is getting worse, as the price per square foot has fallen by 3.0% over the past year, but is still high, with a score of 4, indicating it is one of the least affordable metros. This high cost of living may make it difficult for workers to afford housing, and employers may need to offer higher wages to compensate for the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers is shrinking, with a decline of 1.27% over the past year, which means that employers may face challenges in finding new workers, with a score of 24, below average. This decline in the labor force may lead to a shortage of skilled workers, making it harder for businesses to expand and grow.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals increased by 27.28% over the past year, which signals that the future housing supply is likely to expand, and affordability may improve, with a score of 88, better than most of the 50 metros. This increase in building permits is a positive sign for the local economy, as it indicates that developers are confident in the market and are building more homes to meet demand.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

A typical home takes 57 days to sell, which is relatively slow, and the market is not changing much, with a 0.0% change over the past year, with a score of 30, below average. This slow pace of home sales may indicate a softening housing market, where buyers have more time to consider their options, but it also means that sellers may have to wait longer to close a deal.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is relatively small, with a score of 30, below average, which means that it may not be the best fit for businesses that rely heavily on tech, finance, or professional services. This smaller office economy may make it more challenging for businesses in these sectors to find the skilled workers they need, and they may need to look elsewhere to expand their operations.

The bottom line is that Los Angeles' biggest strength is its strong job growth, with a score of 86, better than most of the 50 metros, but its biggest risk is its high cost of living, with a score of 4, indicating it is one of the least affordable metros. This combination of strong job growth and high cost of living may make it challenging for employers to attract and retain workers, and for workers to afford the high cost of living in the area.
