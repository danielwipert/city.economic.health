Economic Analysis

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 47.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand, with a composite score of 6.18 ranking it at the 76th percentile, and its cost of living, which is ranked at the 6th percentile due to a high PSF to wages ratio of $659/sqft to $41.61/hr. The labor demand and cost of living metrics provide a mixed picture of the city's economic health.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 1.83% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.681% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, as jobs are being added and hours are running above trend, indicating a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 6.18 confirms this, ranking the city at the 76th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 5.00%, ranking it at the 42nd percentile. This rate indicates a near-median level of slack in the labor market, making it neither extremely tight nor very loose. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate means that finding talent may be manageable, but not extremely easy or difficult.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 3.48%, ranking it at the 52nd percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not extremely quickly. At the same time, worker purchasing power is increasing, which can have positive effects on local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim has a cost of living ranked at the 6th percentile, indicating that it is one of the more expensive cities in the US. The PSF to wages ratio of $659/sqft to $41.61/hr is high, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums. However, the fact that PSF is falling -1.9% year-over-year may provide some relief in the future.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is shrinking at a rate of -2.24% year-over-year, ranking it at the 4th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a significant structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is increasing by 17.69% year-over-year, ranking it at the 66th percentile. This expansion in housing supply is a positive sign for future affordability and workforce accommodation, as it may help to alleviate some of the pressure on the housing market.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is 52 days, with a year-over-year increase of 2.0%. This indicates a near-median level of market competitiveness, making it neither extremely fast nor slow for workers to find housing. However, the rising days on market may signal a healthy normalization of the market.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim is ranked at the 54th percentile, indicating a moderate depth of talent pool. This city is suited for businesses that require a mix of professional and non-professional workers, but may not be the best fit for those requiring a highly specialized or deep knowledge-economy talent pool.

The Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim metro area offers a mixed picture for businesses, with a strong labor demand and moderate wage growth, but also a high cost of living and shrinking labor force supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the challenging labor market conditions, particularly the shrinking labor force supply, which may make it difficult to find and retain talent.
