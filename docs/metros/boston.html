Economic Analysis

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of C, with a score of 43.4 out of 100, indicating it is performing poorly compared to other metros. One of the key challenges facing this city's economy is its labor demand, with employment growth at -0.04% over the past year and weekly hours unchanged, earning a score of 32 out of 100, which is below average.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is losing jobs, with a decline of 0.04% over the past year, and workers' hours are not increasing, indicating a weak job market. This means that employers may not be looking to hire as many new employees, and workers may not be seeing as many opportunities for overtime or extra hours.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.90%, which is relatively high, earning a score of 12 out of 100, indicating it is easier for employers to hire but may also mean weaker local consumer demand. This higher unemployment rate suggests that there are more people looking for work, which could be beneficial for employers looking to fill positions.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages in the city have risen by 3.26% over the past year, which is a relatively slow pace, earning a score of 38 out of 100, indicating that labor costs for employers are not rising too quickly. This slow wage growth may mean that workers are not seeing significant increases in their paychecks, which could impact their purchasing power.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $454 and hourly pay of $46.20, earning a score of 31 out of 100, indicating it is less affordable than many other metros. However, the price per square foot has fallen by 2.6% over the past year, which may make housing slightly more affordable for local workers.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in the city is growing at a rate of 2.87% over the past year, earning a score of 98 out of 100, indicating a rapidly expanding workforce. This growth in the labor force is a positive sign for employers, as it means there are more potential workers available to fill positions.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

The number of new home building permits in Boston-Cambridge-Newton has increased by 39.61% over the past year, earning a score of 90 out of 100, indicating a significant expansion in future housing supply. This increase in building permits suggests that developers are confident in the city's housing market and are investing in new construction.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in the city are taking an average of 38 days to sell, with the days on market increasing by 2.7% over the past year, earning a score of 62 out of 100, indicating a relatively healthy housing market. This moderate pace of home sales suggests that the housing market is not too tight, giving buyers a reasonable amount of time to find a home.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is near the median compared to other metros, earning a score of 58 out of 100, indicating a moderate-sized talent pool for tech, finance, and professional services. This suggests that Boston-Cambridge-Newton may be a good fit for businesses in these sectors, but may not have the same level of specialization as some other cities.

The biggest strength of the Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area is its rapidly growing labor force, which is expanding at a rate of 2.87% over the past year, making it an attractive location for employers looking to hire. However, the city's biggest risk is its high cost of living, with a price per square foot of $454 and hourly pay of $46.20, which may make it difficult for workers to afford housing and for employers to attract and retain talent.
