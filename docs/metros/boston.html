Economic Analysis

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 44.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its labor force growth and office economy, with a labor force growth rate of 2.87% YoY and an office/professional worker share ranking in the 62nd percentile. The combination of a strong labor force expansion and a deep professional talent pool makes this city an attractive location for businesses seeking skilled workers.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is -0.44% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating -0.650% from the city's own 12-month baseline, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 3.68, which ranks in the 34th percentile. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a slowdown in job growth and a decrease in working hours. This suggests that businesses may face less competition for workers, but also a less dynamic labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.90%, ranking in the 24th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring workers, as the pool of available candidates is smaller, and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. The tight labor market also suggests that workers may have more bargaining power, which could lead to higher labor costs for businesses.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.53%, ranking in the 42nd percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This means that employer labor costs are rising, but at a moderate pace, and workers are experiencing an increase in purchasing power. However, the moderate wage growth may not be sufficient to keep pace with the cost of living in the city, which could impact the attractiveness of the location for workers.

Cost of Living

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a cost of living score ranking in the 17th percentile, with a PSF of $486/sqft and an average hourly earnings of $45.89/hr, resulting in a ratio of 10.59. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The high cost of living may offset the benefits of the city's strong labor force growth and professional talent pool.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is growing at a rate of 2.87% YoY, ranking in the 98th percentile, indicating a rapid expansion of the workforce. This means that businesses have access to a growing pool of potential workers, making it easier to hire and staff their operations. The strong labor force growth also suggests that the city is likely to have a diverse and dynamic labor market.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is increasing by 5.64% YoY, ranking in the 59th percentile, indicating a moderate expansion of the housing supply. This suggests that the city is experiencing a gradual increase in housing availability, which could help to improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. However, the moderate pace of growth may not be sufficient to keep pace with the demand for housing, which could lead to ongoing affordability challenges.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 37 days, with a year-over-year increase of 19.4%, ranking in the 98th percentile. This indicates that the housing market is slowing down, making it more accessible for workers to relocate to the city. The slower market may give workers more time to find a home, reducing the stress and competition associated with a hot market.

Office Economy

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has an office/professional worker share ranking in the 62nd percentile, indicating a deep talent pool of professional and office workers. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of skilled workers. However, the city may be less suitable for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors, which require a different type of workforce.

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a strong labor force growth and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for businesses seeking skilled workers. However, the city's high cost of living and relatively tight labor market may pose challenges for businesses trying to attract and retain talent, and the moderate wage growth may not be sufficient to keep pace with the cost of living, making it the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in.
