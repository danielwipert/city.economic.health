Economic Analysis

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 45.4th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor force growth, with a 2.87% year-over-year increase, and its high cost of living, with a PSF to earnings ratio of 9.99. The labor demand composite score of 4.13, ranking in the 26th percentile, also plays a significant role in shaping the city's economic landscape.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is -0.12% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.351% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The negative employment growth rate and below-trend hours suggest that the city's labor market is experiencing a slowdown.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.90%, ranking in the 32nd percentile. This indicates a moderate level of slack in the labor market, making it neither extremely tight nor very loose. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate means that there is a pool of available workers, but it may not be as large as in cities with higher unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is +2.93%, ranking in the 32nd percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not extremely quickly. As a result, worker purchasing power is increasing, but not at a rapid pace, which may have implications for local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a cost of living percentile rank of 20th, indicating that it is relatively expensive compared to its peers. The PSF to earnings ratio of 9.99, with PSF at $458/sqft and decreasing by 1.5% year-over-year, suggests that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The high cost of living may be a challenge for businesses looking to relocate or expand in this city.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is growing at a rate of 2.87% year-over-year, ranking in the 98th percentile. This rapid expansion of the labor force supply means that businesses in this city have a growing pool of potential workers to draw from, making it easier to hire and staff new positions.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Boston-Cambridge-Newton has increased by 72.27% year-over-year, ranking in the 94th percentile. This significant expansion of housing supply suggests that the city is experiencing an increase in developer confidence and a future improvement in affordability. As a result, the city may become more attractive to relocating workers and businesses in the long term.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is currently 51 days, with a year-over-year increase of 13.3%. This ranks in the 88th percentile, indicating a relatively slow market. For a worker relocating to this city, the slower market means that they may have more time to find a home and negotiate a better price, making the city more accessible to newcomers.

Office Economy

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has an office economy percentile rank of 56th, indicating a near-median depth of professional talent pool. This suggests that the city is suited for a variety of businesses, but may not have the same level of specialization as cities with a deeper talent pool. The city is likely well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors, but may not be the best fit for industries that require a highly specialized workforce.

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a growing labor force and expanding housing supply, making it an attractive location for companies looking to relocate or expand. However, the city's high cost of living and moderate wage growth rate may pose challenges for businesses trying to attract and retain talent, and the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the potential need to offer wage premiums to compete in this market.
