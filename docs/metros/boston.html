Economic Analysis

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 44.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor force growth, with a year-over-year increase of 2.87%, and its high cost of living, with a PSF to earnings ratio of 10.45. The labor demand composite score of 3.73, ranking in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile, also plays a significant role in shaping the city's economic landscape.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 0.27% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.951% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the small increase in employment is accompanied by a decrease in hours worked. This suggests that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion, but rather a potential survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.90%, ranking in the below-average tier at the 34th percentile. This indicates a relatively tight labor market with some slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire in this city. As a result, businesses may face some wage pressure when trying to attract and retain talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 2.89%, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. At the same time, workers in the city are experiencing a modest increase in purchasing power.

Cost of Living

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a cost of living percentile rank of 16, indicating that it is an expensive city relative to its peers. The PSF to earnings ratio of 10.45, with PSF at $474/sqft and earnings at $45.37/hr, highlights the challenge of attracting talent without offering wage premiums. However, the fact that PSF is falling by 1.0% year-over-year is a positive sign for affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is growing at a rate of 2.87% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile. This rapid expansion of the labor force supply suggests that businesses have a large and growing pool of potential workers to draw from, making it easier to hire and staff operations.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is increasing by 87.24% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 94th percentile. This surge in building permits indicates that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodation options for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 46 days, with a year-over-year increase of 17.9%. This rising days on market, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile, suggests a slowing market that is becoming more accessible for relocating workers. However, the relatively fast pace of sales still indicates a competitive market.

Office Economy

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a deep professional talent pool, ranking in the near-median tier at the 58th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that require a high concentration of office workers. However, it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a growing labor force and expanding housing supply, making it an attractive location for companies looking to hire and staff operations. However, the city's high cost of living and moderate wage growth rate pose significant challenges for businesses trying to attract and retain talent without offering substantial wage premiums, which is the single biggest risk or constraint that decision-makers should factor in when considering this location.
