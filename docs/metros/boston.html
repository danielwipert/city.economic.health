Economic Analysis

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it at the 43.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor force growth, with a year-over-year increase of 2.87%, and its high cost of living, with a PSF to wages ratio of 10.68. The labor demand composite score of 3.82, ranking in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile, also plays a significant role in shaping the city's economic landscape.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 0.39% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -1.026% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the modest job growth is accompanied by a decrease in working hours, indicating a potential survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. This scenario suggests that the labor market is not experiencing genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.90%, ranking below average at the 24th percentile. This indicates a relatively tight labor market with some slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire in this city. As a result, companies may face some wage pressure when competing for talent, but it is not as intense as in cities with even lower unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.21%, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but not at an alarming rate. At the same time, workers in the city are experiencing a modest increase in purchasing power, which can contribute to local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a cost of living percentile rank of 14th, indicating that it is an expensive city relative to its peers. The PSF to wages ratio of 10.68, with PSF at $486/sqft and wages at $45.50/hr, highlights the challenge of attracting talent without offering wage premiums. This can be a significant disadvantage for businesses looking to relocate or expand in the city, as they may need to compensate employees for the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is growing at a rate of 2.87% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile. This rapid expansion of the labor force supply suggests that the city has a strong hiring capacity, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to grow their workforce. The increasing labor pool can provide a competitive advantage for companies operating in the city.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Boston-Cambridge-Newton has increased by 67.46% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This significant rise in building permits indicates that the housing supply is expanding, which can lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce. As a result, the city may become more attractive to relocating workers and businesses in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is currently 37 days, with a year-over-year increase of 19.4%. This relatively fast pace of home sales, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile, suggests that the housing market is competitive, but still accessible for relocating workers. The rising days on market may indicate a healthy normalization of the market, rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is ranked near the median at the 52nd percentile. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool, making the city suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors, but less ideal for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces. The city's office economy is neither exceptionally strong nor weak, offering a balanced environment for various types of businesses.

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a rapidly growing labor force and an expanding housing supply, which can lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for workers. However, the city's high cost of living and moderate wage growth rate pose significant challenges for companies looking to attract and retain talent without offering substantial wage premiums, making it essential for decision-makers to carefully weigh these factors when considering a location in this city.
