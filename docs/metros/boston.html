Economic Analysis

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 44.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its labor force growth rate of +2.87% YoY and its cost of living, with a PSF to wages ratio of 10.59, ranking it at the 16th percentile for affordability. The combination of a shrinking labor demand and high cost of living presents a unique challenge for businesses considering this location.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is -0.44% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating -0.650% from the city's own 12-month baseline, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 3.68, which ranks at the 34th percentile. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a slowdown in job growth and a decrease in working hours. This scenario suggests that businesses may face a more competitive environment for talent, but also potentially weaker consumer demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.90%, ranking at the 24th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. The tight labor market also implies that workers may have more bargaining power, potentially driving up labor costs.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is +3.53%, ranking at the 42nd percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate of wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a moderate pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing. As a result, businesses may need to balance their labor costs with the potential benefits of a more skilled and productive workforce.

Cost of Living

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a cost of living ranking at the 16th percentile, with a PSF to wages ratio of 10.59, and PSF decreasing by -0.2% YoY. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The high cost of living may deter some workers from relocating to the area, potentially limiting the pool of available talent.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is growing at a rate of +2.87% YoY, ranking at the 98th percentile, indicating a rapidly expanding workforce supply. This growth in the labor force suggests that businesses will have access to a increasing pool of potential workers, making it easier to hire and staff their operations. The expanding labor force also implies that the city is likely to have a more dynamic and diverse workforce.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is increasing by +5.64% YoY, ranking at the 60th percentile, indicating an expansion in housing supply. This growth in building permits suggests that the city is likely to experience an increase in available housing, potentially improving affordability and making it easier for workers to relocate to the area. The expanding housing supply may also help to mitigate the high cost of living, making the city more attractive to businesses and workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 37 days, with a YoY increase of +19.4%, ranking at the 98th percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are taking longer to sell, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. The slower market may also give workers more negotiating power, potentially leading to better deals on housing.

Office Economy

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a professional and office worker share ranking at the 62nd percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. The city's strong office economy makes it an attractive location for businesses in these sectors, offering access to a highly skilled and specialized workforce. However, the city may be less suited for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a highly skilled workforce and a growing labor force, but also presents challenges such as a high cost of living and a tight labor market. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering this location is the potential difficulty in attracting and retaining talent due to the high cost of living, which may require wage premiums to remain competitive.
