Economic Analysis

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 45.3th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor force growth of +2.87% YoY and its high building permits growth of +67.46% YoY, indicating a expanding workforce and housing supply. However, its labor demand composite score of 3.82 ranks in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile, suggesting a mismatch between job growth and hours worked.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is +0.39% YoY, while weekly hours are -1.026% below trend, signaling a contraction in labor demand rather than genuine demand expansion. This combination suggests that despite some job growth, the overall labor market is not as strong as it could be, with hours worked below the 12-month baseline. This may indicate a degree of survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.90%, ranking in the below-average tier at the 34th percentile, indicating a moderate level of slack in the labor market. While this is not an extremely tight market, it still presents some challenges for businesses trying to hire, as they may face competition for available workers. However, it is not as severe as in cities with even lower unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is +3.21%, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile, indicating a moderate pace of wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not extremely rapidly, while workers are seeing some improvement in their purchasing power. However, this pace of wage growth is not exceptionally high, which may limit the attractiveness of the city to businesses seeking to minimize labor costs.

Cost of Living

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a cost of living score ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile, with a PSF of $474/sqft and average hourly earnings of $45.50/hr, resulting in a ratio of 10.42. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which may make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is falling by -1.0% YoY is a positive sign, but the overall affordability score remains low.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is growing at a rate of +2.87% YoY, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile, indicating a rapidly expanding workforce. This suggests that the city has a strong supply of potential workers, which can support business growth and hiring capacity. This is a significant advantage for businesses looking to expand their operations in the area.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is growing at a rate of +67.46% YoY, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile, indicating a significant expansion of housing supply. This suggests that the city is likely to see improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for workers in the future. This is a positive sign for businesses concerned about the ability of their employees to find housing.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is currently 46 days, with a year-over-year increase of +17.9%, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile. This indicates a slowing market, which may make it more accessible for workers relocating to the city to find housing. However, the absolute level of days on market is still relatively low, suggesting that the market remains competitive.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 2.78, ranking near the median at the 52nd percentile, indicating a moderate depth of talent pool. This suggests that the city is suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors, but may not be as attractive for industries with more specialized or industrial workforce requirements. The city's office economy is neither exceptionally strong nor weak, making it a viable option for a range of businesses.

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a rapidly expanding workforce and housing supply, but also presents challenges due to its relatively high cost of living and moderate labor market. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential difficulty in attracting talent without offering wage premiums, due to the city's expensive cost of living. However, the city's strong labor force growth and expanding housing supply make it an attractive option for businesses looking to grow and expand their operations.
