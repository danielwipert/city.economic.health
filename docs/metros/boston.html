Economic Analysis

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 43.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its exceptionally high labor force growth rate of +2.87% YoY. These metrics signal a complex economic environment with both challenges and opportunities.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is +0.27% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating -0.951% from the city's own 12-month baseline, indicating a labor demand composite score of 3.73, which falls into the bottom tier. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, suggesting that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. Instead, it may be facing a period of slow job growth and reduced working hours.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is 3.90%, ranking it below average at the 34th percentile. This indicates a moderate level of slack in the labor market, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, this also means that local consumer demand may be weaker due to the higher unemployment rate.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is +2.89%, placing it near the median at the 40th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. At the same time, workers are experiencing some improvement in their purchasing power, which can contribute to local economic stability.

Cost of Living

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has a cost of living score in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile, with a PSF of $474/sqft and average hourly earnings of $45.37/hr, resulting in a ratio of 10.45. The PSF has decreased by -1.0% YoY, which is a positive sign for affordability. However, the city remains relatively expensive compared to its peers, which could make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is growing at a rate of +2.87% YoY, ranking it in the top tier at the 98th percentile. This significant expansion in labor force supply indicates that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, which can support business hiring needs and overall economic growth.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Boston-Cambridge-Newton has increased by +67.46% YoY, placing it in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This substantial rise in building permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which can lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Boston-Cambridge-Newton is currently 46 days, with a year-over-year increase of +17.9%, ranking it in the top tier at the 98th percentile. This indicates a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing.

Office Economy

Boston-Cambridge-Newton has an office economy percentile rank of 58th, indicating a moderately deep talent pool in professional and office sectors. This makes the city suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions, but less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

The Boston-Cambridge-Newton metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a growing labor force and expanding housing supply, which can support economic growth. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand and relatively high cost of living, which may require wage premiums to attract and retain talent.
