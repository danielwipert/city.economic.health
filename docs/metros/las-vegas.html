Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has earned an overall grade of B, with a score of 59.8 out of 100, indicating it is an average city for business and economic growth. What most defines this city's economy right now is its strong labor demand, with employment growth of 1.73% over the past year and workers' hours rising by 1.1%, earning it a top-tier score of 94.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.73% over the past year, with workers' hours also increasing by 1.1%, indicating a strong and growing job market. This means that businesses are hiring and workers are putting in more hours, signaling genuine demand for labor and a healthy economy.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 4.80%, which is near the median, earning it a score of 48 out of 100. This rate makes it neither extremely easy nor difficult for employers to hire, as there is a moderate pool of available workers.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 3.65% over the past year, which is near the median, earning it a score of 50 out of 100. This moderate wage growth means that employers' labor costs are increasing, but not excessively, while workers are seeing some improvement in their paychecks.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is near the median, with a price per square foot of $258 and hourly pay of $32.90, earning it a score of 47 out of 100. The fact that the price per square foot is falling by 2.6% over the past year makes housing slightly more affordable for local workers.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is shrinking by 0.59% over the past year, earning it a score of 40 out of 100. This decline in the labor force makes it a challenge for businesses to find new hires, as the available workforce is decreasing.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals have decreased by 4.90% over the past year, earning it a score of 46 out of 100. This decline in building permits signals that the future housing supply may be limited, which could lead to increased competition for available homes and higher prices.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

A typical home in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas takes 59 days to sell, with the days on market increasing by 1.7% over the past year, earning it a score of 46 out of 100. This indicates a relatively balanced housing market, but one that is slightly softening, as homes are taking a bit longer to sell.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is significant, earning it a top-tier score of 88 out of 100, indicating a deep talent pool in tech, finance, and professional services. This makes Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas an attractive location for businesses in these sectors, such as tech and finance companies, as well as corporate headquarters.

The bottom line is that Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas's biggest strength is its strong labor demand, with a top-tier score of 94, indicating a healthy and growing job market. However, its biggest risk is the decline in labor force growth, which could make it challenging for businesses to find new hires and limit the city's economic growth potential.
