Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking at the 57.6th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 96th percentile, driven by a 2.93% employment growth rate and a 0.171% weekly hours deviation above its own trend. The combination of strong employment growth and above-trend hours worked signals genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area is experiencing a significant labor demand expansion, with a 2.93% employment growth rate and a 0.171% weekly hours deviation above its own trend. This combination indicates that the city is adding jobs at a rapid pace and workers are putting in more hours than usual, signaling a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 8.57 ranks in the top tier at the 96th percentile, confirming the city's robust labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas stands at 5.20%, ranking below average at the 36th percentile. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire workers compared to tighter markets. However, the unemployment rate is not so low that it would drive up wages significantly, providing a moderate environment for hiring.

Wage Growth

The city's wage growth is moderate, with a year-over-year increase of 4.53% in hourly earnings, ranking near the median at the 58th percentile. This rate of wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, while workers are seeing a decent increase in their purchasing power. The moderate wage growth suggests a balanced labor market where neither employers nor workers have excessive bargaining power.

Cost of Living

Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas has a cost of living that is above average, with a ratio of $262 per square foot to $33.03 per hour, but it ranks in the 61st percentile in terms of affordability. The fact that the PSF is falling by 2.2% year-over-year relative to wages is a key driver of its above-average affordability score. This means the city has a talent attraction advantage without needing significant wage premiums, as the cost of living, although not the lowest, is manageable compared to peer cities.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is growing at a rate of 0.22% year-over-year, which is near the median at the 50th percentile. This slow but positive growth indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, albeit gradually, providing a modest increase in the hiring capacity for businesses.

Building Permits

The city has seen a significant decline in residential building permits, with a year-over-year change of -38.98%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 0th percentile. This sharp decrease signals that the housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 57 days, with a year-over-year increase of 5.6%. This indicates a moderate pace of home sales, neither extremely fast nor slow, suggesting a relatively accessible market for workers relocating to the city. The near-median percentile rank of 56th percentile confirms that the housing market is not overly competitive, providing a balanced environment for both buyers and sellers.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is significant, ranking in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This deep talent pool makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

In conclusion, Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion, moderate wage growth, and an above-average but manageable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the sharp decline in building permits, which signals potential future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce, making it crucial to monitor and plan for housing supply and affordability in the long term.
