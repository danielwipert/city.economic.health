Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 69.1th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and a deep office economy talent pool defining its current economic character. The labor demand composite score of 7.44, driven by a 1.46% employment growth rate and a 1.098% weekly hours deviation above trend, signals genuine demand expansion. This combination of metrics suggests a city with a robust job market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 1.46% and weekly hours deviation of 1.098% above trend indicate a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a combination that suggests a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 7.44, ranking in the 92nd percentile, further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 4.80% ranks in the 56th percentile, indicating a near-median level of slack in the labor market. This means that while the market is not extremely tight, it is still relatively competitive, and businesses may face some challenges in hiring. However, the unemployment rate is not high enough to indicate a significant surplus of available workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of 4.21% ranks in the 60th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a manageable rate, and worker purchasing power is increasing. While this may put some upward pressure on wages, it is not excessively high.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Las Vegas, with a PSF of $259/sqft and an earnings ratio of 7.77, ranks in the 51st percentile, indicating a near-median level of affordability. The fact that PSF is falling by 3.0% year-over-year relative to wages suggests that the city is becoming more affordable, which can be a talent attraction advantage without requiring wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of -0.21% year-over-year, ranking in the 62nd percentile, indicating a slight contraction in the labor force supply. This suggests that the hiring capacity may be somewhat constrained, although the negative growth rate is relatively modest.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is 28.53%, ranking in the 86th percentile, indicating a significant expansion in housing supply. This suggests that developer confidence is high, and future housing supply is likely to increase, which can improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 58 days, with a year-over-year increase of 1.8%, ranking in the 47th percentile. This suggests that the housing market is relatively balanced, neither extremely competitive nor excessively slow, making it accessible for relocating workers.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers is 3.86, ranking in the 88th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses that require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers, but may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area offers businesses a strong labor market, a deep office economy talent pool, and an expanding housing supply, making it an attractive location for companies looking to grow. However, the slight contraction in labor force supply and moderate wage growth rate are factors that decision-makers should consider when evaluating this city as a potential location.
