Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 69.2th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and a thriving office economy being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, driven by a 92th percentile labor demand composite score and an 88th percentile office/professional worker share. The city's labor market is characterized by a 1.46% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.098% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, with jobs being added and hours running above trend.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 1.46% and weekly hours deviation of 1.098% above trend indicate a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This signals that the city is experiencing an increase in job opportunities and workers are putting in more hours, which is a positive indicator for businesses looking to expand or relocate. The labor demand composite score of 7.44, ranking in the 92th percentile, further reinforces this trend.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 4.80%, ranking in the 56th percentile, indicating a near-median level of slack in the labor market. This means that while the market is not extremely tight, it is still relatively competitive, and businesses may face some challenges in finding the right talent. However, the current unemployment rate suggests that there is still a pool of available workers for businesses to tap into.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in the city is 4.21%, ranking in the 60th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This means that employer labor costs are rising, but at a manageable rate, and workers are experiencing an increase in purchasing power. The moderate wage growth suggests that businesses can expect to pay slightly higher wages to attract and retain talent.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is near median, with a percentile rank of 51, and a price-to-salary ratio of $259/sqft to $33.35/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.77. The fact that the PSF is falling by 3.0% year-over-year is a key driver of the city's affordability. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to relocate without having to offer significant wage premiums to compensate for high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the city is growing at a rate of -0.21% year-over-year, ranking in the 62nd percentile, indicating a slight contraction in the labor force supply. This means that the pool of available workers is shrinking, which could pose a structural headwind for hiring in the future. However, the relatively slow rate of contraction suggests that businesses can still find talent, albeit with some effort.

Building Permits

The city is experiencing a 28.53% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking in the 86th percentile, indicating a significant expansion in housing supply. This suggests that developer confidence is high, and the future housing supply is expected to increase, which could lead to improved affordability and a more attractive environment for relocating workers.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 58 days, with a year-over-year increase of 1.8%, ranking in the 48th percentile. This indicates a relatively competitive market, but one that is still accessible for relocating workers. The slow increase in days on market suggests that the market is normalizing, making it easier for workers to find homes.

Office Economy

The city has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share of 3.86, ranking in the 88th percentile. This makes it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. However, it may be less suited for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area offers businesses a strong labor market, a thriving office economy, and a relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for relocation or expansion. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the slight contraction in the labor force supply, which could pose challenges for hiring in the future.
