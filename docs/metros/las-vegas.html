Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking at the 56.1th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 94th percentile, and its high office economy share, ranking at the 88th percentile. The labor demand is driven by a 2.11% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 0.196% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend.

Labor Demand

The combination of a 2.11% employment growth rate and a 0.196% increase in weekly hours signals genuine demand expansion in the Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area. This indicates that jobs are being added and hours are running above trend, suggesting a healthy and growing labor market. The top-tier composite score of 94th percentile reinforces this interpretation, pointing to a strong labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas stands at 5.30%, ranking at the 14th percentile, which indicates a relatively loose labor market. This suggests that there is some slack in the market, making it easier for businesses to hire workers without facing intense competition. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand compared to tighter labor markets.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in the metro area is 5.01%, ranking at the 64th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, while workers are experiencing an increase in their purchasing power. The moderate pace of wage growth may help balance the needs of both employers and employees.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $264/sqft to $33.11/hr, ranking at the 51st percentile, Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is near the median in terms of affordability. The fact that PSF is falling by 1.9% year-over-year relative to wages is a positive sign for affordability. This means that the city offers a relatively neutral environment for talent attraction, neither requiring significant wage premiums to compensate for high costs nor offering a substantial advantage due to low costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the metro area is growing at a rate of 1.33% year-over-year, ranking at the 82nd percentile, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the labor pool is increasing, which is a favorable condition for hiring and business expansion. It implies that businesses can expect a growing talent pool from which to recruit.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -54.44%, ranking at the 0th percentile, which signals a sharp contraction in housing supply. This decline in building permits suggests that the future housing supply is tightening, which could lead to affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This is a significant concern for businesses considering relocation or expansion in the area.

Days on Market

The current median days on market stands at 55 days, with a 10.0% year-over-year increase, ranking at the 64th percentile. This indicates a moderately competitive market that is becoming slightly less competitive over time. For workers relocating to this city, the market is relatively accessible, but the rising days on market could signal a normalization towards a more balanced market.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share ranking at the 88th percentile, Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas has a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses in these sectors but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area offers businesses a strong labor demand and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the sharp decline in building permits, which threatens future affordability and workforce accommodation, potentially undermining the city's otherwise favorable economic conditions.
