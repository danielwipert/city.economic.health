Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking at the 59.6th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the top tier at the 92nd percentile, and its moderate cost of living, ranked at the 45th percentile. The labor demand is driven by a combination of employment growth and hours worked above trend.

Labor Demand

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has seen employment growth of 1.42% year-over-year, combined with weekly hours 0.733% above its own 12-month trend. This signals genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours worked are increasing, indicating a strong and growing labor market. The labor demand composite score of 7.02 is in the top tier, at the 92nd percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 5.40%, which ranks at the 30th percentile, indicating a market with some slack. This means that while it may not be as challenging to hire as in tighter markets, there is still a notable pool of potential employees available. For a business trying to hire, this suggests a relatively balanced environment, neither extremely competitive nor overly loose.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in the metro area is 3.95%, ranking near the median at the 54th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, and worker purchasing power is increasing at a steady pace. This balance is likely to support both business operations and local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $261/sqft to $32.65/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.99, and a percentile rank of 45, Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is near the median in terms of affordability. The fact that PSF is falling by 1.9% year-over-year relative to wages indicates a trend towards greater affordability. This means the city has a moderate talent attraction advantage without needing significant wage premiums to compensate for high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the metro area has grown by 0.98% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 84th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a growing pool of potential employees.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -9.84%, ranking below average at the 36th percentile. This decline suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

Homes in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas are currently sitting on the market for a median of 58 days, with a year-over-year increase of 3.6%. This indicates a moderately competitive market that is becoming slightly less heated, which could make it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors, combined with its growth trend, places Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This suggests a deep talent pool suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ operations, making it an attractive location for knowledge-economy businesses.

In conclusion, Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas offers a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion, moderate wage growth, and a relatively balanced unemployment rate, making it an attractive location for businesses. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, signaled by the decline in building permits, which could impact future affordability and workforce accommodation.
