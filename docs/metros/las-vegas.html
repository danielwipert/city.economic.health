Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 59.8th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven primarily by its strong labor demand and deep office economy. The city's labor demand composite score of 7.82, which combines a 1.73% employment growth rate and a 1.097% weekly hours deviation above trend, signals genuine demand expansion. This is further supported by its top-tier percentile ranks in both employment growth and weekly hours deviation, at 94th percentile for both metrics.

Labor Demand

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has a labor demand composite score of 7.82, driven by a 1.73% employment growth rate and a 1.097% weekly hours deviation above trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a sign of a strong and growing economy. The top-tier percentile ranks in both employment growth and weekly hours deviation, at 94th percentile for both metrics, further support this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 4.80%, ranking in the 48th percentile, indicating a near-median level of slack in the labor market. This rate suggests that while the market is not extremely tight, it is also not overly loose, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire. However, the relatively moderate unemployment rate implies that businesses may face some competition for talent, but not to the extent that it significantly drives up wages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 3.65%, ranking in the 50th percentile, indicating a moderate pace of wage increases. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not at an alarming rate, while workers are seeing their purchasing power increase. The moderate wage growth implies a relatively stable labor cost environment, which can be beneficial for businesses looking to manage their expenses.

Cost of Living

Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas has a cost of living score that ranks in the 47th percentile, with a PSF of $258/sqft and an average hourly wage of $32.90, resulting in a ratio of 7.84. The city's cost of living is near median, but the 2.6% year-over-year decrease in PSF suggests that it is becoming more affordable. This affordability can be a talent attraction advantage, as workers may not require significant wage premiums to maintain their standard of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is growing at a rate of -0.59% year-over-year, ranking in the 40th percentile, indicating a contracting labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of available workers is shrinking. Businesses may need to consider strategies to attract talent from other areas or invest in workforce development programs to address the shrinking labor pool.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas has decreased by 4.90% year-over-year, ranking in the 46th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This decrease suggests that the city's housing market may become less affordable in the future, potentially affecting workforce attraction and accommodation. The decline in building permits may lead to increased competition for housing, driving up costs and making it more challenging for businesses to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 59 days, with a year-over-year increase of 1.7%, ranking in the 46th percentile. This increase indicates a slightly slower market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as they may have more time to find a suitable home. However, the relatively moderate days on market suggests that the housing market is still relatively competitive.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 3.86, ranking in the 88th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses that require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers, but may not be as suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area offers businesses a strong labor demand and a deep office economy, making it an attractive location for companies in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering this location is the contracting labor pool, which may lead to challenges in attracting and retaining talent, and the potential for a tightening housing supply, which could drive up costs and affect workforce accommodation.
