Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 69.0th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong labor demand and office economy. The city's labor demand composite score of 7.44, which combines a 1.46% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.098% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, signals genuine demand expansion. This, coupled with its top-tier office economy, defines the city's current economic character.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 1.46% and weekly hours deviation of 1.098% above trend indicate a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 7.44, ranking in the 92nd percentile, further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 4.80% places the city near the median, ranking in the 56th percentile. This rate suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, it also implies that local consumer demand may not be as strong as in areas with lower unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of 4.21% is above average, ranking in the 60th percentile. This moderate to fast wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, which can be a challenge for businesses but also indicates good worker purchasing power. As wages grow, workers have more disposable income, potentially benefiting local businesses.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $259/sqft to $33.35/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.77, and a percentile rank of 50th, the city is near the median in terms of affordability. The fact that PSF is falling by 3.0% year-over-year relative to wages is a key driver of its affordability. This means the city offers a relatively neutral environment for talent attraction, neither requiring significant wage premiums to compensate for high costs nor offering a substantial advantage in terms of affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of -0.21% year-over-year, which, despite being a slight contraction, ranks in the 62nd percentile. This slight decline in labor force growth suggests that the supply of workers is not expanding as quickly as in other areas, potentially presenting a structural headwind for hiring in the future.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is +28.53%, ranking in the 86th percentile, indicating a significant expansion in housing supply. This surge in residential building permits signals developer confidence in the market and suggests that future housing supply will increase, potentially improving affordability and accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

Homes are currently sitting on the market for 58 days, with a year-over-year increase of 1.8%. This moderate days on market, ranking in the 48th percentile, suggests a relatively balanced market that is neither extremely competitive nor overly accessible for relocating workers. The slight increase in days on market may indicate a healthy normalization of the housing market.

Office Economy

The city's office economy, with a composite score of 3.84 and ranking in the 88th percentile, indicates a deep talent pool suited for professional, tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses in these sectors but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion and a deep professional talent pool, making it particularly attractive for office and professional services sectors. However, the slight contraction in labor force growth presents a risk that decision-makers should factor in, as it may impact future hiring capacity and require strategic workforce planning.
