Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 56.1th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its top-tier labor demand, with a composite score of 8.57, and its strong labor force growth, at +1.33% year-over-year. The combination of these metrics signals a city with genuine demand expansion and a growing workforce.

Labor Demand

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has seen employment growth of +2.93% year-over-year, combined with a +0.171% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This indicates a strong and growing labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas stands at 5.30%, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This suggests a relatively loose labor market, with some slack, making it easier for businesses to hire. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand compared to tighter labor markets.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in the area has been +4.53% year-over-year, placing it near the median at the 58th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, and worker purchasing power is increasing at a steady pace. This balance could support both business operations and worker well-being.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $264/sqft to $33.03/hr, and a year-over-year decrease of -1.9% in PSF, Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas ranks near the median in terms of affordability, at the 55th percentile. This means the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which could be a talent attraction advantage without necessitating significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas has grown by +1.33% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the city has a structural advantage for hiring, as the labor pool is increasing.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits has been -38.98%, signaling a sharp contraction in housing supply expansion. This decline suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand.

Days on Market

Homes in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas currently sit on the market for a median of 55 days, with a year-over-year increase of +10.0%. This indicates a slightly slower market, which could make it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing, compared to faster-moving markets.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is significant, ranking it in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This deep talent pool makes the city well-suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ operations, but less ideal for industries requiring large industrial or logistics workforces.

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand and growing workforce supply, making it an attractive location for expansion. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the sharp decline in building permits, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.
