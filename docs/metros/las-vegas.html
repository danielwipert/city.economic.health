Economic Analysis

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking at the 59.6th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the top tier at the 92nd percentile, and its moderate cost of living, ranked near the median at the 45th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $261/sqft to $32.65/hr. The labor demand is driven by a combination of employment growth and hours worked above trend.

Labor Demand

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area has an employment growth rate of +1.42% year-over-year and weekly hours deviation of +0.733% from its own trend, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a real increase in labor demand, with more jobs being added and existing workers putting in more hours. The top-tier composite score of 7.02 further reinforces this trend.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 5.40%, ranking below average at the 30th percentile. This indicates that the labor market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, the unemployment rate is not so low that it would drive up wages significantly due to intense competition for workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is +3.95%, placing it near the median at the 54th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing but not at an alarming rate. At the same time, workers are experiencing some improvement in their purchasing power, which can contribute to local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living percentile rank of 45th, Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is considered near the median in terms of affordability, with a price-to-salary ratio of $261/sqft to $32.65/hr. The fact that the PSF is decreasing by -1.9% year-over-year relative to wages makes the city more attractive for talent without necessitating significant wage premiums. This affordability can be a talent attraction advantage.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is growing at a rate of +0.98% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 84th percentile. This expansion in labor force supply indicates that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, which can support hiring needs and business expansion.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -9.84%, ranking below average at the 36th percentile. This decline suggests that the housing supply is not expanding as quickly as demand, potentially leading to future affordability issues and constraints on workforce accommodation.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas is 58 days, with a year-over-year increase of +3.6%. This indicates a slightly slower market, which can be more accessible for relocating workers, giving them more time to find suitable housing without facing extreme competition.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas ranks in the top tier at the 86th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ operations. This makes the city an attractive location for knowledge-economy businesses but less ideal for those requiring large industrial or logistics workforces.

The Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion and a moderate cost of living, making it attractive for talent without excessive wage pressure. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the decline in building permits, which may lead to future housing affordability issues and impact the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce.
