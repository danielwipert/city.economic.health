Economic Analysis

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking at the 61.7th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the 78th percentile, and its labor force growth, which is expanding at a rate of 1.42% year-over-year. The combination of these metrics suggests a city with a growing workforce and increasing job opportunities.

Labor Demand

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area has seen employment growth of 0.74% year-over-year and weekly hours deviation of 0.922% above its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing an increase in job creation and workers are putting in more hours, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand or relocate. The labor demand composite score of 6.26 further reinforces this trend, ranking in the 78th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is 4.20%, which is relatively low and indicates a tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in finding and hiring qualified workers, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract top talent. With an unemployment rate ranking in the 72nd percentile, the market has some slack, but not enough to make hiring significantly easier.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is 1.51%, which is relatively slow and ranks in the 18th percentile. This slow wage growth may help keep labor costs under control for employers, but it also means that workers may have limited bargaining power and purchasing power. As a result, businesses may not need to worry about rapidly rising labor costs, but may still need to offer competitive wages to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has a cost of living score ranking in the 47th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $228/sqft to $32.62/hr, or 6.99. The fact that the PSF is falling by 0.9% year-over-year is a positive sign for affordability. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which can be a talent attraction advantage for businesses, as they may not need to offer significant wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is growing at a rate of 1.42% year-over-year, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire and expand. This expanding labor force supply means that businesses will have a larger pool of potential workers to draw from, making it easier to find and hire qualified talent. With a labor force growth rate ranking in the 88th percentile, the city is well-positioned to support business expansion.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has increased by 2.10% year-over-year, which is a relatively modest growth rate ranking in the 50th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is expanding, but not rapidly, which may help to keep affordability relatively stable. As a result, businesses can expect that their workers will have a relatively stable and affordable housing market, which can be an attractive feature for talent relocation.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is currently 48 days, with a year-over-year increase of 9.1%. This ranks in the 84th percentile, indicating a slower market. For workers relocating to the city, this means that they may have a more accessible and less competitive housing market, which can make it easier to find and purchase a home.

Office Economy

Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has a professional and office worker share of 2.52, which ranks in the 50th percentile. This suggests that the city has a relatively deep talent pool in professional and office sectors, making it well-suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. However, it may not be as well-suited for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area offers businesses a growing workforce, increasing job opportunities, and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses is the tight labor market, which may make it challenging to find and hire qualified workers. Despite this, the city's strong labor demand and expanding labor force supply make it an attractive location for businesses looking to expand or relocate.
