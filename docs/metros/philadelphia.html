Economic Analysis

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area has an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking at the 61.7th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score at the 78th percentile, and its moderate cost of living, ranked at the 47th percentile. The labor demand is driven by a combination of employment growth and hours worked above trend, signaling genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The city's employment growth rate is 0.74% year-over-year, and weekly hours are 0.922% above the 12-month baseline, indicating a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a positive sign for businesses looking to expand. The labor demand composite score of 6.26 further reinforces this trend, ranking above average at the 78th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is 4.20%, ranking above average at the 72nd percentile, which means the market has some slack but is still relatively tight. This rate implies that while it may not be extremely difficult to hire, businesses may still face some competition for top talent, and wage pressure could be a concern. However, the relatively low unemployment rate suggests that the local consumer demand is likely to be stable.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in the city is 1.51%, which is relatively slow, ranking at the 18th percentile. This slow wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also means that worker purchasing power is not increasing quickly. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but workers may not have as much disposable income to spend.

Cost of Living

Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has a cost of living ranked at the 47th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $228/sqft to $32.62/hr, or 6.99. The city's cost of living is near the median, making it neither extremely affordable nor expensive. The fact that the PSF is falling by 0.9% year-over-year is a positive sign for affordability. This means that the city can attract talent without necessarily requiring significant wage premiums, as the cost of living is relatively manageable.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the city is growing at a rate of 1.42% year-over-year, which is a top-tier growth rate, ranking at the 88th percentile. This expansion in the labor force supply implies that businesses have a growing pool of potential workers to hire from, making it easier to find talent. The increasing labor force also suggests that the city is likely to continue attracting new workers and businesses.

Building Permits

The city has seen a 2.10% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking near the median at the 50th percentile. This moderate growth in permits suggests that the housing supply is slowly expanding, which could help improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. However, the relatively modest growth rate may not be enough to keep up with demand, potentially leading to some affordability challenges in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is 48 days, with a 9.1% year-over-year increase, ranking at the 84th percentile. This means that homes are sitting on the market for a relatively long time, indicating a slower market that is more accessible to relocating workers. The rising days on market suggests that the housing market is normalizing, making it easier for workers to find and purchase homes.

Office Economy

The city has an office economy ranked at the 50th percentile, with a share of professional and office workers at 2.52 out of 5. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool, suitable for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors, but not exceptionally strong. The city is well-suited for businesses that require a mix of professional and non-professional workers, but may not be the best fit for those requiring extremely specialized or high-end talent.

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion, a moderate cost of living, and a growing labor force. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses in this city is the potential for affordability challenges in the future, driven by modest growth in housing supply. Overall, the city presents a compelling opportunity for businesses looking to expand or relocate, with a relatively stable and growing economy.
