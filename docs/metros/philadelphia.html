Economic Analysis

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking it at the 66.1th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the top tier at the 84th percentile, and its labor force growth, which is expanding at a rate of +1.23% YoY, ranking in the top tier at the 88th percentile. These metrics signal a city with genuine job growth and an increasing workforce supply.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is +0.83% YoY, combined with a +1.300% deviation in weekly hours above its own 12-month trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing real job growth, with hours worked increasing above the trend, suggesting that businesses are hiring and workers are taking on more hours. This is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand or relocate to the area.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is 4.40%, which ranks at the 76th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. With an unemployment rate below the national average, businesses may face challenges in finding available workers, which could lead to upward pressure on wages. However, this also suggests a skilled and employed workforce, which can be beneficial for businesses looking for talented workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is +2.14%, which ranks below average at the 26th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but at a slower pace than in many other cities. While this may help keep costs under control for businesses, it also means that workers may not have as much bargaining power or purchasing power as they would in cities with faster wage growth.

Cost of Living

Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has a cost of living score that ranks at the 42nd percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $228/sqft to $32.59/hr, or approximately 7.00. The fact that the PSF is falling by -0.9% YoY relative to wages is a key driver of its near-median affordability score. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which can be a talent attraction advantage for businesses, as they may not need to offer wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is growing at a rate of +1.23% YoY, which ranks in the top tier at the 88th percentile. This expanding workforce supply is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it indicates a growing pool of potential workers. This can help mitigate the challenges of a tight labor market and provide businesses with the talent they need to grow and expand.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has increased by +16.37% YoY, which ranks above average at the 65th percentile. This increase in building permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which can help improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. This is a positive sign for businesses, as it indicates that the city is investing in its infrastructure and preparing for future growth.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is currently 49 days, with a year-over-year increase of +8.9%. This ranks at the 78th percentile, indicating a relatively slow market. For workers relocating to the city, this means that they may have more time to find a home and negotiate a price, making the city more accessible to newcomers.

Office Economy

Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has an office economy score that ranks at the 59th percentile, with a share of professional and office workers at 2.82 out of 5. This suggests a deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. While the city may not be as dominant in these sectors as some of its peers, it still offers a significant advantage for businesses looking to tap into a knowledgeable and skilled workforce.

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, expanding workforce supply, and relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the moderate wage growth, which may limit the bargaining power and purchasing power of workers, potentially affecting the local consumer demand and the overall attractiveness of the city for certain types of businesses.
