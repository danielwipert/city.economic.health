Economic Analysis

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area has earned an overall grade of A, with a score of 69.8 out of 100, indicating it is a very good location for businesses, with a strong labor market and growing workforce. One of the key defining features of this city's economy is its low unemployment rate of 3.6%, which is lower than most of the 50 metros, and its employment growth of 0.5% over the past year, indicating a strong demand for labor.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 0.5% over the past year, and workers' hours are rising by 1.0%, indicating a strong labor market with genuine demand for workers. This means that businesses are likely to face competition for talent, but it also suggests a healthy and growing economy with opportunities for workers.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 3.6% makes it harder for employers to hire, as there are fewer workers available, but it also indicates a strong and stable labor market. With a score of 92 out of 100, Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has lower unemployment than most of the 50 metros, making it a challenging but desirable location for businesses.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 3.5% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 42 out of 100. This means that employers can expect moderate labor cost increases, while workers will see some growth in their paychecks, but not as high as in some other metros.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is about average, with a score of 59 out of 100, and a price per square foot of $227, compared to hourly pay of $32.96, which is relatively stable. The fact that the price per square foot is falling by 1.3% over the past year suggests that housing is becoming more affordable for local workers.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers is growing at a rate of 1.0% over the past year, which is faster than most of the 50 metros, with a score of 84 out of 100. This means that businesses can expect a steady supply of new workers, making it easier to hire and expand their operations.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals increased by 11.5% over the past year, with a score of 68 out of 100, indicating a strong and growing housing market. This suggests that the city is likely to have a more affordable and abundant housing supply in the future, which will attract more workers and businesses.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes are taking 48 days to sell, which is getting slower by 11.6% over the past year, but still indicates a healthy housing market, with a score of 88 out of 100. This means that buyers have a relatively good selection of homes, and sellers can still expect to sell their properties fairly quickly, making it a balanced and stable market.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a larger office-based workforce than most of the 50 metros, with a score of 62 out of 100, making it a suitable location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This suggests that Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has a deep talent pool and a strong knowledge economy, which will attract businesses that require specialized skills and expertise.

The bottom line is that Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington's biggest strength is its strong and growing labor market, with low unemployment and a steady supply of new workers, making it an attractive location for businesses. However, its biggest risk is the potential for rising labor costs and competition for talent, which could make it challenging for businesses to hire and retain workers, especially in the office and professional services sectors.
