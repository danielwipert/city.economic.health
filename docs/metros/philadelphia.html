Economic Analysis

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area has earned an overall grade of A, with a score of 69.8 out of 100, indicating it is a very good location for businesses, with a strong labor market and growing workforce. One of the key defining features of this city's economy is its low unemployment rate of 3.6%, which is lower than most metros, and its employment growth of 0.5% over the past year, indicating a strong demand for labor.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 0.5% over the past year, and workers are putting in 1.0% more hours than their 12-month average, indicating a strong labor market with genuine demand for workers. This means that businesses are likely to face competition for talent, but also have a large pool of skilled workers to draw from, with a score of 66 out of 100, indicating stronger hiring than most metros.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 3.6% is a strength, with a score of 92 out of 100, indicating lower unemployment than most metros, making it harder for employers to hire but also indicating a tight labor market. This low unemployment rate means that employers may need to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract top talent, but it also suggests a strong and stable local economy.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages in the city have risen by 3.5% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 42 out of 100, indicating that employers' labor costs are rising, but not as quickly as in some other cities. This moderate wage growth means that workers are seeing some increase in their paychecks, but employers are not facing extreme pressure to raise wages, making it a relatively stable labor market.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Philadelphia is about average, with a score of 59 out of 100, and a price per square foot of $227, compared to an hourly pay of $32.96, and the price per square foot has actually fallen by 1.3% over the past year, making it more affordable for local workers. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to other metros, which could be a talent attraction advantage, especially since housing costs are not rising as quickly as wages.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in the city is growing at a rate of 1.0% over the past year, with a score of 84 out of 100, indicating faster workforce growth than most metros, which is a strength for businesses looking to hire. This growing pool of workers means that employers have a larger talent pool to draw from, and the city is likely to have a strong and diverse workforce in the future.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

The number of new home building permits has risen by 11.5% over the past year, with a score of 68 out of 100, indicating stronger home-building than most metros, which signals that the city is likely to have a growing housing supply in the future. This increase in building permits suggests that the city is investing in its infrastructure and is likely to have a more affordable and available housing stock, making it more attractive to workers and businesses.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

The typical home in the city takes 48 days to sell, and this number has risen by 11.6% over the past year, with a score of 88 out of 100, indicating a healthier housing market than most metros. This relatively fast pace of home sales, combined with the increase in days on market, suggests that the housing market is balanced and healthy, with plenty of options for buyers and sellers, and is not too tight or too soft.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a relatively large office-based workforce, with a score of 62 out of 100, indicating a larger office workforce than most metros, which makes it well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This means that the city has a deep talent pool of skilled workers in these industries, and is likely to be an attractive location for companies looking to establish or expand their operations in these sectors.

The bottom line is that Philadelphia's biggest strength is its strong and growing labor market, with low unemployment and a diverse workforce, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to hire and grow. However, the city's biggest risk is the potential for rising labor costs and competition for talent, which could make it challenging for employers to attract and retain top workers, but overall, the city's economy is well-positioned for growth and development.
