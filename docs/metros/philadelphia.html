Economic Analysis

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking in the 65.9th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the top tier at the 84th percentile, and its above-average labor force growth rate of 1.23% YoY. The combination of these metrics signals a city with genuine demand expansion and a growing workforce.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is 0.83% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating 1.300% above the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as jobs are being added and hours are running above trend. The labor demand composite score of 6.76 further supports this conclusion, indicating a strong and growing labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is 4.40%, ranking in the 76th percentile, which indicates a relatively tight market with some slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, this means that while it may be slightly more challenging to find talent, it is still possible to attract workers without excessive wage pressure.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is 2.14%, which is below average, ranking in the 26th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, but not rapidly.

Cost of Living

Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington has a cost of living score ranking in the 41st percentile, with a PSF of $228/sqft and average hourly earnings of $32.59/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.00. This indicates that the city is near the median in terms of affordability, neither particularly expensive nor cheap. The fact that PSF is falling by 0.9% YoY is a positive sign for affordability, making it easier for businesses to attract talent without wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is growing at a rate of 1.23% YoY, which is in the top tier, ranking in the 88th percentile. This expanding labor force supply implies that businesses have a growing pool of potential workers to hire from, making it easier to find talent.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is increasing by 16.37% YoY, which is above average, ranking in the 64th percentile. This expansion of housing supply signals that future affordability and workforce accommodation are likely to improve, making it easier for workers to relocate to the city.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is 49 days, with a YoY increase of 8.9%, ranking in the 78th percentile. This indicates a relatively competitive market, but still accessible for workers relocating to the city, as homes are not selling extremely quickly.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington is above average, ranking in the 60th percentile. This indicates a deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, making it an attractive location for knowledge-economy companies.

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with growing demand and an expanding workforce, making it an attractive location for companies looking to hire and grow. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the moderate wage growth rate, which may lead to rising labor costs over time, potentially impacting business profitability.
