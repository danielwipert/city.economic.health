Economic Analysis

The Philadelphia-Camden-Wilmington metro area has earned an overall grade of A, with a score of 69.8 out of 100, indicating it is a very good location for businesses, better than most of the 50 metros. What most defines this city's economy right now is its strong labor market, with employment growth of 0.49% over the past year and a low unemployment rate of 3.60%, which is better than 92% of the metros.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 0.49% over the past year, and workers' hours are rising by 1.0%, indicating a genuine demand for labor. This means that the job market is healthy, with employers looking to hire and workers putting in more hours, earning a score of 66, which is above average.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 3.60% is very low, making it hard for employers to hire, as there are fewer available workers, and earning a score of 92, which is top tier. This low unemployment rate also means that employers may face upward pressure on wages, as workers have more bargaining power.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 3.53% over the past year, which is near the median, earning a score of 42, indicating that wage growth is not exceptionally strong. This moderate wage growth means that employers' labor costs are rising, but not too quickly, while workers' paychecks are increasing, but not dramatically.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Philadelphia is near the median, with a price per square foot of $227 and hourly pay of $32.96, earning a score of 59, which is near median. The fact that the price per square foot is falling by 1.3% over the past year makes housing slightly more affordable for local workers compared to other metros.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers is growing at a rate of 1.03% over the past year, which is top tier, earning a score of 84, indicating a strong and expanding workforce. This growth in the labor force makes it easier for employers to hire, as there are more available workers, and it also signals a healthy and dynamic economy.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals increased by 11.53% over the past year, earning a score of 68, which is above average, indicating a growing housing supply. This increase in building permits signals that developers are confident in the market, and it may help to improve affordability and attract more workers to the area.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

A typical home takes 48 days to sell, and this number is increasing by 11.6% over the past year, earning a score of 88, which is top tier, indicating a healthy housing market. This increase in days on market is a good sign, as it means that buyers have more time to find a home, and it is not too tight, making it easier for relocating workers to find a place to live.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is above average, earning a score of 62, indicating a deep talent pool in tech, finance, and professional services. This makes Philadelphia a suitable location for businesses in these sectors, such as tech, finance, and consulting, which require a highly skilled and educated workforce.

The bottom line is that Philadelphia's biggest strength is its strong labor market, with low unemployment and growing labor force, making it an attractive location for businesses. However, its biggest risk is the potential for upward pressure on wages, as the low unemployment rate gives workers more bargaining power, which could increase labor costs for employers.
