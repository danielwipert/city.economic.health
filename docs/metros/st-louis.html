Economic Analysis

St. Louis earns an overall grade of A, ranking in the 69.2th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and highly affordable cost of living being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, at 78th and 98th percentiles, respectively. The city's labor demand composite score of 6.44 and cost of living ratio of 4.28, with PSF at $163/sqft and hourly earnings at $38.11, signal a unique combination of job growth and affordability. With these metrics, St. Louis presents an attractive environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in St. Louis is +0.68% year-over-year, combined with a +1.193% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a real increase in labor demand, rather than just a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 6.44 further supports this interpretation, ranking in the 78th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in St. Louis is 3.90%, which is above average, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market with some slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, the practical implication is that while it may be slightly harder to find candidates than in cities with higher unemployment rates, the market is not excessively tight, allowing for manageable hiring processes. This balance suggests a stable environment for workforce planning.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in St. Louis is +3.51%, which is near the median, ranking in the 48th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that employer labor costs are increasing, but not at an alarming rate, while worker purchasing power is also growing, albeit not exceptionally fast. The moderate wage growth environment means that businesses can expect stable labor costs without significant pressure to increase wages dramatically.

Cost of Living

St. Louis is highly affordable, with a cost of living percentile rank of 98th, driven by a PSF of $163/sqft that is decreasing by -1.8% year-over-year, compared to hourly earnings of $38.11, resulting in a ratio of 4.28. This high affordability ranking means that the city has a significant talent attraction advantage, as workers can enjoy a high standard of living without requiring wage premiums to compensate for expensive housing.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in St. Louis is growing at a rate of -1.33% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor supply. This negative growth rate implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is shrinking, which could lead to increased competition for talent and potentially higher wages over time.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in St. Louis has increased by +18.07% year-over-year, signaling an expansion in housing supply. This increase in building permits suggests that developer confidence is high, and the future housing supply is expected to improve, which should help maintain or improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in St. Louis is 50 days, with an 8.7% year-over-year increase, indicating a slightly slower market. For a worker relocating to this city, the increasing days on market suggest a more accessible and less competitive housing market, making it easier to find and purchase a home.

Office Economy

St. Louis has a professional and office worker share that ranks in the 54th percentile, indicating a moderately deep talent pool in these sectors. This city is best suited for businesses that can thrive in a mixed economy with a notable presence of professional and office roles, but it may not be the ideal location for businesses requiring an exceptionally deep pool of specialized tech, finance, or consulting talent.

In conclusion, St. Louis offers businesses a unique blend of strong labor demand, high affordability, and moderate wage growth, making it an attractive location for expansion or relocation. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the contracting labor force, which could lead to future hiring challenges and increased labor costs, necessitating strategic workforce planning and talent attraction strategies.
