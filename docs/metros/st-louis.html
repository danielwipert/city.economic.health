Economic Analysis

St. Louis has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 69.2th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and highly affordable cost of living being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, scoring 78th and 98th percentiles respectively. The city's labor demand composite score of 6.44 and cost of living ratio of 4.28, with PSF at $163/sqft and hourly earnings at $38.11, signal a favorable business environment. With these metrics, St. Louis presents a unique blend of economic growth and affordability.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in St. Louis is +0.68% year-over-year, combined with a +1.193% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion as both jobs and hours worked are increasing. This combination signals a strong labor market where businesses are adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 6.44 further reinforces this positive outlook.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in St. Louis stands at 3.90%, which is above average, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market with some slack. For businesses trying to hire in this market, the implication is that while it may be slightly challenging to find talent, it is not excessively competitive, and wage pressure, although present, is manageable. This balance suggests a stable environment for recruitment.

Wage Growth

St. Louis experiences a year-over-year wage growth of +3.51%, placing it near the median at the 48th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing but at a pace that does not overly strain budgets. For workers, this translates into modestly increasing purchasing power, supporting local consumer demand without excessively inflating costs for businesses.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of 4.28, where PSF is $163/sqft and hourly earnings are $38.11, and considering the PSF has decreased by -1.8% year-over-year, St. Louis ranks in the 98th percentile for affordability. This high ranking means the city is very affordable relative to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without the need for significant wage premiums to offset living costs. The decreasing PSF relative to wages is a key driver of this affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in St. Louis has contracted by -1.33% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing, which could challenge businesses looking to expand their workforce in the area. Managing recruitment and retention strategies will be crucial in this environment.

Building Permits

St. Louis has seen an increase of +18.07% in residential building permits year-over-year, signaling an expansion in housing supply. This growth in permits suggests that developer confidence is strong, and the future housing supply is expected to improve, which should help maintain or improve affordability and support the attraction and accommodation of the workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in St. Louis is 50 days, with an increase of +8.7% year-over-year, ranking in the 74th percentile. This indicates a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it a more accessible and less competitive environment for workers relocating to the city. The rising DOM suggests a healthy normalization of the housing market.

Office Economy

St. Louis has a professional and office worker share that places it in the 51st percentile, indicating a moderately deep talent pool suited for businesses requiring specialized roles, though it may not be as robust as in cities with a higher concentration of tech, finance, or consulting sectors. This city is best suited for businesses that can thrive in a mixed economy with a balance of industrial, logistics, and professional services, and less suited for those requiring a highly specialized knowledge-economy workforce.

In conclusion, St. Louis offers businesses a unique combination of a strong labor demand, highly affordable cost of living, and a moderately growing economy, making it an attractive location for investment and expansion. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the shrinking labor force, which could pose challenges for hiring and workforce expansion plans, necessitating strategic recruitment and retention initiatives to thrive in this market.
