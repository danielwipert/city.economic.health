Economic Analysis

St. Louis has earned an overall grade of A+, with a score of 73.7 out of 100, making it an excellent choice for businesses and workers alike. The city's economy is currently defined by its strong labor demand, with employment growth of 1.0% over the past year and workers putting in 1.0% more hours, indicating a genuine demand for labor.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.0% over the past year, with workers also putting in 1.0% more hours, which means the job market is strong and employers are looking to hire. This is a strength for the city, with a score of 82 out of 100, indicating that St. Louis is doing better than most metros in terms of labor demand.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in St. Louis is 3.4%, which is lower than most metros, making it a strength with a score of 78 out of 100. This low unemployment rate means that it may be harder for employers to hire, as there are fewer workers available, but it also indicates a strong and competitive job market.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages in St. Louis have risen by 4.3% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, with a score of 62 out of 100. This means that employers may face rising labor costs, but workers will see an increase in their paychecks, giving them more purchasing power.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in St. Louis is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $163 and hourly pay of $38.21, and it's getting even more affordable as housing prices have fallen by 1.8% over the past year. This is a significant strength for the city, with a score of 98 out of 100, making it more attractive to workers and businesses looking for a affordable place to live and operate.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in St. Louis has shrunk by 0.1% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 54 out of 100. This means that the pool of workers is not growing, which may make it slightly more challenging for employers to find new hires.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

The number of new home approvals in St. Louis has increased by 7.4% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 58 out of 100. This indicates that developers are confident in the city's housing market, and the future supply of homes is likely to increase, making it more affordable for workers to live in the area.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in St. Louis are taking 51 days to sell, which is getting slower by 13.3% over the past year, but this is still a healthy housing market, with a score of 84 out of 100. This means that buyers have a reasonable amount of time to find a home, and the market is not too tight, making it easier for workers to relocate to the area.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The office-based workforce in St. Louis is smaller than most metros, with a score of 38 out of 100, which means that the city may not be the best fit for businesses in the tech, finance, or professional services sectors. However, this also means that the city may have opportunities for growth and development in these areas, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to expand.

The city's biggest strength is its affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for workers and businesses alike. However, the city's relatively small office-based workforce is a weakness, which may make it less competitive for certain types of businesses, but also presents opportunities for growth and development in these areas.
