Economic Analysis

St. Louis earns an overall grade of A, ranking in the 69.1th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and highly affordable cost of living being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, scoring 78th and 98th percentiles, respectively. The city's labor demand composite score of 6.44 and cost of living ratio of $163/sqft to $38.11/hr, with a PSF decline of 1.8% YoY, signal a favorable business environment. With these metrics, St. Louis presents a unique blend of economic growth and affordability.

Labor Demand

St. Louis exhibits a labor demand composite score of 6.44, driven by employment growth of 0.68% YoY and weekly hours 1.193% above its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a sign of a healthy and growing economy. The above-average scores in both employment growth and hours worked suggest a strong labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in St. Louis stands at 3.90%, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. However, it also suggests a skilled and engaged workforce, which can be beneficial for companies looking to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in St. Louis is moderate, with a year-over-year increase of 3.51%, placing it near the median at the 48th percentile. This rate of wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively, while workers are experiencing a modest increase in purchasing power. As a result, businesses can expect to see some upward pressure on labor costs, but it is manageable.

Cost of Living

St. Louis is highly affordable, with a cost of living ratio of $163/sqft to $38.11/hr and a PSF decline of 1.8% YoY, earning it a 98th percentile rank. This high affordability score means that the city offers a significant talent attraction advantage without requiring wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to relocate or expand. The declining PSF relative to wages is a key driver of this affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in St. Louis is contracting at a rate of -1.33% YoY, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is shrinking. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to attract talent from other areas or invest in workforce development programs.

Building Permits

Residential building permits in St. Louis are increasing by 18.07% YoY, ranking in the 66th percentile, indicating an expansion in housing supply. This growth in permits suggests that developer confidence is high, and future housing supply is likely to improve, which should help maintain affordability and support workforce accommodation. As a result, the city is likely to remain an attractive location for workers and businesses alike.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in St. Louis is 50 days, with an 8.7% YoY increase, ranking in the 74th percentile. This slower market means that relocating workers may find it easier to find and purchase a home, as properties are not being snapped up as quickly as in other cities. However, this also suggests a slightly more buyer-friendly market, which could impact housing prices and affordability.

Office Economy

St. Louis has a professional and office worker share of 2.64, ranking near the median at the 54th percentile, indicating a moderately deep talent pool. This city is well-suited for businesses that require a mix of professional and office skills, but may not be the best fit for those seeking a highly specialized or dominant tech/finance talent pool. As a result, companies in industries like consulting, finance, or headquarters operations may find St. Louis an attractive location.

In conclusion, St. Louis offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, highly affordable cost of living, and moderate wage growth, making it an attractive location for relocation or expansion. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the contracting labor force supply, which may pose challenges for hiring and talent attraction in the long term.
