Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C-, with a score of 35.6 out of 100, indicating it performs worse than most of the 50 metros. The city's economy is currently defined by its sluggish job market, with employment growth at -0.23% over the past year, and a labor force that is shrinking by 1.37% annually.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is losing jobs, with a decline of 0.23% over the past year, and workers' hours are also falling by 0.1%, indicating a weak job market. This means that the city is not creating new opportunities for workers, and existing employees may be shouldering a heavier workload after job cuts.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate is 3.80%, which is near the median, making it neither extremely easy nor hard for employers to hire. With a score of 42, the city's unemployment rate is not a major advantage or disadvantage compared to other metros.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 4.48% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, with a score of 66, indicating that employers' labor costs are increasing, and workers' paychecks are getting bigger. This rapid wage growth may attract workers to the area, but it also poses a challenge for businesses looking to keep costs under control.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

Housing in the area is relatively expensive, with a price of $222 per square foot, and hourly pay at $34.92, resulting in a score of 26, indicating that the cost of living is higher than in most metros. The price per square foot has increased by 2.3% over the past year, making it even more challenging for local workers to afford housing.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers is shrinking by 1.37% annually, with a score of 18, indicating that the city is experiencing a significant decline in its labor force, making it harder for businesses to find new employees. This decline poses a structural challenge for hiring and may limit the city's economic growth.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals have decreased by 17.54% over the past year, with a score of 18, signaling that the city's future housing supply is likely to be constrained, which may exacerbate affordability issues. This decline in building permits suggests that the city's housing market may become even more competitive, making it harder for workers to find affordable housing.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

A typical home takes 45 days to sell, with the time to sell increasing by 2.3% over the past year, resulting in a score of 56, indicating a relatively healthy housing market. Although the housing market is not extremely tight, the slight increase in days on market may give buyers a bit more time to find a home, which could be beneficial for relocating workers.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is smaller than in most metros, with a score of 24, indicating that it may not be the best fit for businesses in the tech, finance, or professional services sectors. The city's economy is more suited for industries that do not rely heavily on a large pool of office-based workers, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area's biggest strength is its rapid wage growth, which can attract workers and drive economic activity. However, the city's biggest risk is its shrinking labor force and constrained housing supply, which may limit its ability to attract new businesses and talent, ultimately hindering its economic growth.
