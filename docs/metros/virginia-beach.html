Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 35.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its below-average labor demand, with a composite score of 3.98, and its above-average wage growth of 4.48% year-over-year. The labor demand and wage growth metrics suggest a complex economic environment, with a mix of job market contraction and rising labor costs.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in this metro area is -0.23% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.074% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the job market is not expanding. The negative employment growth rate and below-trend hours suggest that businesses may not be adding new jobs at a pace that keeps up with national trends.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 3.80%, which ranks near the median at the 42nd percentile. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire workers. However, the relatively low unemployment rate also means that wage pressure may be moderate, as workers have some bargaining power in the job market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in this metro area is 4.48%, which is above average and ranks at the 66th percentile. This fast wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, which could be a challenge for businesses looking to keep costs under control. On the other hand, workers in this metro area are experiencing increasing purchasing power, which could be beneficial for local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in this metro area is below average, with a percentile rank of 26th, and a PSF-to-earnings ratio of $222/sqft to $34.92/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.36. However, the PSF is increasing by 2.3% year-over-year, which may erode affordability over time. The relatively low affordability score means that this city may not have a significant talent attraction advantage without offering wage premiums to offset the cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in this metro area is contracting at a rate of -1.37% year-over-year, which ranks at the 18th percentile. This decline in labor force supply suggests that the hiring capacity for businesses may be limited, making it challenging to find and recruit new workers. The shrinking labor pool could be a structural headwind for businesses looking to expand in this area.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in this metro area is decreasing by 17.54% year-over-year, which ranks at the 18th percentile. This sharp decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which could lead to affordability challenges and make it harder for workers to relocate to this area. The decrease in permits may signal a future supply squeeze, making it essential for businesses to consider the potential impact on workforce accommodation.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in this area is 45 days, with a year-over-year increase of 2.3%. This relatively moderate days on market suggests that the housing market is not extremely competitive, making it accessible for workers relocating to this city. However, the rising days on market may indicate a normalization of the market, which could be beneficial for buyers.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in this metro area is below average, with a percentile rank of 24th. This suggests that the talent pool for tech, finance, and consulting businesses may not be as deep as in other cities. The city's economy is likely more suited for industries that are not heavily reliant on specialized office workers, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area offers a mix of above-average wage growth and below-average labor demand, making it a complex location for businesses to navigate. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor pool, which could limit hiring capacity and make it challenging for businesses to expand in this area. Overall, businesses should carefully consider the trade-offs between labor costs, talent availability, and affordability when evaluating this location.
