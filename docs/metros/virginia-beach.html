Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it at the 42.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its weak labor demand, with a labor demand composite score ranking in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, and its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.91% in hourly earnings. The combination of these metrics suggests a complex economic environment with both opportunities and challenges for businesses.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in this metro area is -0.55% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.523%. This combination signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, rather than genuine demand expansion. This metric ranks in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, indicating a challenging labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 3.70%, which is near the median and ranks at the 54th percentile. This suggests a relatively balanced labor market with some slack, making it easier for businesses to hire workers without excessive wage pressure. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand compared to tighter labor markets.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in this metro area is 5.91%, which is above average and ranks at the 78th percentile. This indicates fast wage growth, which can lead to rising labor costs for employers but also increases worker purchasing power. Businesses should factor in the potential for higher labor costs when considering this location.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in this metro area is below average, with a percentile rank of 37, and a PSF to earnings ratio of $224/sqft to $35.28/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.35. However, the PSF is increasing by 2.3% year-over-year, which may erode affordability over time. This relatively low cost of living can be a talent attraction advantage, as workers may not require wage premiums to afford the local cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in this metro area is contracting at a rate of -1.31% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 22nd percentile. This indicates a shrinking labor pool, which can create a structural headwind for hiring and may limit the city's ability to support business growth.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in this metro area has increased by 106.92% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 96th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is expanding, which can improve affordability and support workforce growth in the future. This is a positive signal for businesses considering relocation, as it may ease housing constraints for their workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in this metro area is 37 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 2.6%. This ranks in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile, indicating a relatively fast-paced market. For workers relocating to this city, this may mean a competitive and potentially challenging housing market, although the rapid sales pace can also indicate a desirable location.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in this metro area is relatively low, ranking in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile. This suggests a less deep talent pool for businesses requiring specialized office or professional skills, making it less suited for tech, finance, or consulting businesses. However, it may be more suitable for industries with different skill requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth and expanding housing supply, but it also poses significant challenges due to its weak labor demand and contracting labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the potential difficulty in hiring and retaining workers due to the shrinking labor pool, which may limit the city's ability to support long-term business growth.
