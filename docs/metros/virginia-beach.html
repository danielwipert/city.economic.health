Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 42.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its weak labor demand, with a labor demand composite score in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, and strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.54% in hourly earnings, ranking it in the top tier at the 80th percentile. These two metrics suggest a complex economic environment with both challenges and opportunities.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in this metro area is -0.68% year-over-year, and weekly hours are only 0.075% above the 12-month trend, indicating a contraction in labor demand rather than genuine expansion. This combination signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 3.27 is in the bottom tier, confirming the weak labor market conditions.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 4.00%, which is near the median, ranking it at the 50th percentile. This suggests a relatively balanced labor market with neither significant tightness nor slack. For a business trying to hire in this area, the moderate unemployment rate implies a manageable but not overly competitive hiring environment.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in this metro area is 5.54%, which is relatively fast and ranks in the top tier at the 80th percentile. This rapid wage growth implies increasing labor costs for employers but also stronger purchasing power for workers. Businesses should factor in the potential for rising labor costs when considering this location.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $224/sqft to $35.13/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.38, and a percentile rank of 41, this city is near the median in terms of affordability. The PSF is increasing by 2.8% year-over-year, which does not help affordability. This moderate affordability means that businesses may not have a significant talent attraction advantage without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in this metro area is shrinking by -1.05% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor supply. This negative growth rate, ranking in the 22nd percentile, suggests a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is decreasing.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits has increased by 77.17% year-over-year, which is a very strong signal of developer confidence and an expanding future housing supply. This top-tier growth, ranking at the 92nd percentile, suggests that affordability may improve, and the city may become more attractive for workforce accommodation.

Days on Market

Homes in this metro area are currently sitting on the market for a median of 39 days, with no year-over-year change. This relatively fast market, ranking in the 26th percentile, implies that relocating workers may face a competitive housing market, which could be a challenge for businesses trying to attract talent.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in this metro area is below average, ranking in the 26th percentile. This suggests a less deep talent pool for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy workers, making it less suited for tech, finance, or consulting headquarters decisions but potentially more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

In conclusion, the Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area offers a complex economic environment with strong wage growth but weak labor demand and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for a business considering this location is the challenging labor market conditions, which could impact hiring capacity and labor costs. Despite these challenges, the area's strong building permit growth suggests potential for future improvements in affordability and workforce accommodation.
