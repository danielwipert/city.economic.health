Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 41.3th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its labor demand and labor force growth metrics, which both rank in the bottom tier at 14th and 12th percentiles, respectively. The labor demand composite score of 3.36 and labor force growth rate of -2.61% YoY are key indicators of this city's current economic character. These numbers suggest a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk is -0.55% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.174%, indicating a contraction in labor demand rather than genuine demand expansion. This combination signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 3.36 reinforces this interpretation, ranking in the bottom tier at 14th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk is 3.70%, which corresponds to an above-average percentile rank of 66th. This suggests a relatively tight labor market with some slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire. The practical implication is that businesses may face some competition for talent, but it's not an extremely tight market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk is +5.51%, ranking above average at 78th percentile. This indicates fast wage growth, which implies rising labor costs for employers but strong purchasing power for workers. Businesses should factor in the potential for increasing labor costs when considering relocation or expansion.

Cost of Living

Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk has a cost of living percentile rank of 53rd, with a PSF of $223/sqft and average hourly earnings of $35.71, resulting in a ratio of 6.24. This suggests a near-median affordability level, neither extremely high nor low. The city's affordability is not a significant advantage or disadvantage for talent attraction, as the cost of living is relatively average compared to peers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk is contracting at a rate of -2.61% YoY, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses should be cautious when considering expansion or relocation, as the shrinking labor force may limit their ability to find and hire talent.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk is -10.45%, indicating a tightening of housing supply. This decline in building permits signals a potential supply squeeze, which could lead to decreased affordability and make it more challenging for workers to relocate to the area.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk is 44 days, with a YoY change of +0.0%. This suggests a relatively stable market, neither extremely competitive nor accessible. However, the below-average percentile rank of 36th implies a slightly slower market, which could be beneficial for workers relocating to the area.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk is below average, ranking at the 24th percentile. This indicates a relatively shallow talent pool for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy workers, making the city less suited for tech, finance, or consulting businesses. However, it may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area offers businesses a relatively tight labor market with fast wage growth, but it also presents significant challenges, including a shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the limited availability of skilled workers and the potential for decreased affordability, which could hinder a business's ability to attract and retain talent.
