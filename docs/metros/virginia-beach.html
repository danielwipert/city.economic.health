Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 41.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a labor demand composite score of 3.01 ranking it in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, and its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.91% in hourly earnings. The combination of these metrics suggests a complex economic environment.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in this metro area is -0.55% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.523%. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a decline in job creation and hours worked, which may lead to a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 4.00%, ranking it near the median at the 50th percentile. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to cities with tighter labor markets. However, this also implies that local consumer demand may be weaker due to the availability of labor.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in this metro area is 5.91%, ranking it above average at the 78th percentile. This indicates fast wage growth, which will lead to rising labor costs for employers but also increase worker purchasing power. Businesses should factor in the potential for higher labor costs when considering this location.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in this metro area is below average, with a percentile rank of 37th, and a price-to-salary ratio of $224/sqft to $35.28/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.35. However, the PSF is increasing by 2.8% year-over-year, which may erode affordability over time. This relatively low cost of living could be a talent attraction advantage, as workers may not require wage premiums to afford the cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in this metro area is shrinking, with a year-over-year growth rate of -1.05%, ranking it below average at the 22nd percentile. This contraction in the labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, making it challenging for businesses to find and recruit talent.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in this metro area has increased by 106.92% year-over-year, ranking it in the top tier at the 96th percentile. This significant increase in building permits signals an expansion in future housing supply, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in this metro area is 39 days, with no year-over-year change. This relatively fast market may make it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing, potentially affecting a business's ability to attract and retain talent.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in this metro area is relatively low, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile. This suggests a less deep talent pool for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy workers, making it less suited for tech, finance, consulting, or HQ decisions, but potentially more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area offers a complex economic environment with strong wage growth but low labor demand and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for a business considering this location is the challenge of finding and recruiting talent due to the contracting labor force supply, which may outweigh the benefits of relatively low costs and expanding housing supply.
