Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 41.2th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low labor demand and labor force growth. The labor demand composite score of 3.36, combining a -0.55% employment growth rate and a +0.174% weekly hours deviation, and the -2.61% labor force growth rate are the two metrics that most define this city's current economic character, signaling a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate. With a labor demand score in the bottom tier, at the 14th percentile, this city's economic landscape is marked by contraction.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of -0.55% and weekly hours deviation of +0.174% indicate a scenario where hours are slightly above trend during a period of job losses, suggesting a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand rather than genuine demand expansion. The low labor demand composite score of 3.36 further emphasizes the challenges in this market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.70% places this city above average, at the 66th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market with some slack. For a business trying to hire in this market, the implication is that while it may be slightly easier to find talent than in extremely tight markets, there is still enough competition to potentially drive up wages. However, the relatively low unemployment rate does not fully offset the concerns raised by other metrics.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +5.51% is above average, ranking at the 78th percentile, indicating fast wage growth. This implies rising labor costs for employers but also good worker purchasing power, which can be beneficial for consumer demand. The fast wage growth is a positive aspect of this city's economy, suggesting a strong labor market in terms of compensation.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $223/sqft to $35.71/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.24, and a percentile rank of 52, this city is near the median in terms of affordability. The PSF is increasing at a rate of +2.8% YoY, which does not help affordability. This means that while the city is not extremely expensive, it does not offer a significant talent attraction advantage without wage premiums, as its affordability is average compared to peers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -2.61% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, making it challenging for businesses to find and recruit talent. The implication for hiring capacity is negative, suggesting that businesses may face difficulties in expanding their workforce.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -10.45%, indicating a tightening in housing supply. This signals potential future affordability issues and challenges for workforce accommodation, as a lack of new housing developments can lead to increased competition for existing homes and higher prices. The decrease in building permits is a concern for future workforce attraction and retention.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 44 days, with a year-over-year change of +0.0%, indicating a stable but relatively slow market. For a worker relocating to this city, the housing market is neither extremely competitive nor very accessible, offering a moderate environment for home buying. The stable days on market suggest a balanced market, but the context of other economic indicators suggests caution.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share composite score placing it at the 24th percentile, this city has a below-average depth of professional talent pool. This makes it less suited for businesses requiring a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, consulting, or HQ decisions, but potentially more suitable for industries with different workforce requirements.

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area presents a mixed bag for businesses considering relocation or expansion, with fast wage growth and a relatively tight labor market being positives, but labor demand contraction, shrinking labor force, and tightening housing supply being significant negatives. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the challenging labor market conditions, including low labor demand and a contracting labor force, which could hinder business growth and expansion plans. Overall, while there are some positive aspects, the economic character of this city is defined by its labor market challenges.
