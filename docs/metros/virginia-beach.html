Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C-, with a score of 35.6 out of 100, indicating it is weaker than most of the 50 metros, with employment growth being a particular concern, at -0.2% over the past year. This weakness in job growth, combined with a score of 28 out of 100, suggests that the city is struggling to add new jobs at a pace that keeps up with most other metros.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is losing jobs, with employment growth at -0.2% over the past year, and workers' hours are also falling, by -0.1%, indicating a weak job market with fewer opportunities. This means that the city is not creating new jobs at a rate that can absorb the existing workforce, making it a challenging environment for job seekers.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate is 3.8%, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 42 out of 100, indicating that it is neither particularly easy nor hard for employers to hire. This average unemployment rate suggests that while there are some available workers, the labor market is not overly loose, and employers may still face some competition for top talent.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 4.5% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, with a score of 66 out of 100, indicating a strength for workers but a potential cost pressure for employers. This faster wage growth means that workers are seeing meaningful increases in their paychecks, but it also signals that employers may need to budget for higher labor costs.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

Housing in the area is relatively expensive, with a price of $222 per square foot and hourly pay of $34.92, and it is getting worse, with a score of 26 out of 100, indicating that the city is less affordable than most metros. This means that workers may need to allocate a larger portion of their income towards housing, making it a challenge for employers to attract and retain talent.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers is shrinking, with labor force growth at -1.4% over the past year, and a score of 18 out of 100, indicating a weakness that makes it harder for employers to find new hires. This decline in the labor force suggests that the city is experiencing a brain drain or a lack of in-migration, which can limit the availability of skilled workers.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals fell by 17.5% over the past year, with a score of 18 out of 100, indicating a weakness that signals a potential future housing shortage. This decline in building permits suggests that the city's housing supply is not keeping up with demand, which can lead to higher housing costs and reduced affordability.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes are taking 45 days to sell, which is about average, with a score of 56 out of 100, and the market is getting slightly slower, with a 2.3% increase in days on market. This suggests a relatively balanced housing market, but the slight increase in days on market may indicate a softening of demand or an increase in supply.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros, with a score of 24 out of 100, indicating a weakness that makes it less suitable for businesses that rely on a deep pool of professional talent. This smaller office workforce suggests that the city may not be the best fit for companies in the tech, finance, or consulting sectors that require a large pool of skilled workers.

The biggest strength of the Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area is its faster wage growth, which is a positive for workers and can help attract talent, but the biggest risk is its weak labor demand, which can make it challenging for employers to find new hires and for the city to grow its economy. Overall, the city's economy is facing significant challenges, and addressing its labor demand and affordability issues will be crucial to improving its overall grade and attractiveness to businesses and workers.
