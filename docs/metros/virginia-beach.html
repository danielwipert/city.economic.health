Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it at the 42.3th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its weak labor demand, with a labor demand composite score in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, and strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.54% in hourly earnings, ranking it in the top tier at the 80th percentile. These metrics suggest a complex economic environment with both challenges and opportunities.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in this metro area is -0.68% year-over-year, and weekly hours are running 0.075% above the city's own 12-month trend. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the small increase in hours worked is not enough to offset the decline in employment growth, indicating a potential survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 3.90%, which places it near the median at the 46th percentile. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets, but still presents some challenges in finding the right talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in this metro area is 5.54%, which is a strong increase ranking it in the top tier at the 80th percentile. This implies rising labor costs for employers but also good news for worker purchasing power, as higher wages can support local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $224/sqft to $35.13/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.38, and a percentile rank of 37th, this city is below average in terms of affordability. This means that while it may not require significant wage premiums to attract talent, it does not have a strong talent attraction advantage due to its cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in this metro area is shrinking at a rate of -0.91% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor pool. This presents a structural headwind for hiring, as a shrinking workforce limits the supply of potential employees.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is +77.17%, which is a significant increase ranking it in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This signals that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and support workforce accommodation in the future.

Days on Market

Homes in this metro area are currently sitting on the market for 40 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.4%. This suggests a relatively fast-paced market, which can make it competitive and challenging for relocating workers to find housing.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in this metro area is below average, with a percentile rank of 26th. This indicates a less deep talent pool for businesses requiring specialized professional or office skills, making it less suited for tech, finance, consulting, or HQ decisions that rely heavily on such talent.

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area offers a complex mix of strong wage growth and weak labor demand, suggesting both opportunities for businesses that can navigate its challenges and risks, particularly in terms of hiring and talent attraction. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the shrinking labor force, which poses a significant challenge for businesses looking to expand or establish operations in this area.
