Economic Analysis

The Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area has an overall grade of C-, with a score of 35.6 out of 100, indicating it is weaker than most of the 50 metros, with employment growth being a significant concern, at -0.2% over the past year. This weakness is reflected in its labor demand, with a score of 28, indicating that the city is adding fewer jobs than most metros, and workers' hours are also falling, by -0.1% compared to the same period last year.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is losing jobs, with employment growth at -0.2% over the past year, and workers' hours are also falling, which means the job market is weaker than most metros. This weakness in labor demand, with a score of 28, indicates that the city is not creating as many new job opportunities as other metros, making it a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in the city is 3.8%, which corresponds to a score of 42, indicating that it is about average for the 50 metros, making it neither particularly easy nor hard for employers to hire. With an unemployment rate around the national average, employers may face some competition for talent, but it's not as intense as in cities with much lower unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 4.5% over the past year, which is a strength, with a score of 66, indicating that wage growth is faster than in most metros. This rapid wage growth means that employers may face rising labor costs, but it's also good news for workers, who will see their paychecks increase, making the city more attractive for talent.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

Housing in the city is relatively expensive, with a price per square foot of $222 and hourly pay of $34.92, corresponding to a score of 26, indicating that the city is less affordable than most metros, and it's getting worse, with prices rising by 2.3% over the past year. This high cost of living may make it difficult for businesses to attract and retain talent, as workers may be deterred by the expensive housing market.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in the city is shrinking, with labor force growth at -1.4% over the past year, corresponding to a score of 18, indicating that the city has slower workforce growth than most metros. This decline in the labor force makes it a challenging environment for businesses looking to hire, as the talent pool is not expanding, and employers may face difficulties finding the workers they need.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals fell by 17.5% over the past year, corresponding to a score of 18, indicating that the city has weaker home-building activity than most metros. This decline in building permits suggests that the city's housing supply may not be keeping pace with demand, which could lead to higher housing costs and make it even more difficult for workers to afford homes.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in the city are selling relatively quickly, with a median of 45 days on the market, and this is getting slightly slower, with a 2.3% increase over the past year, corresponding to a score of 56, indicating that the housing market is about average for the 50 metros. This relatively healthy housing market, with a score of 56, indicates that buyers have a reasonable amount of time to find a home, and sellers can expect to sell their properties without too much delay.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is smaller than in most metros, with a score of 24, indicating that the city may not be as well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, or professional services sectors. This smaller office workforce means that businesses looking to establish or expand their operations in these sectors may find it more challenging to attract the specialized talent they need.

The biggest strength of the Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk metro area is its rapid wage growth, which makes it an attractive location for workers, but its biggest risk is its weak labor demand, which makes it challenging for businesses to find the talent they need and may deter investment and expansion. Overall, the city's economy is facing significant challenges, and addressing its labor demand and affordability issues will be crucial to improving its competitiveness and attractiveness to businesses and workers.
