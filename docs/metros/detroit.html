Economic Analysis

The Detroit-Warren-Dearborn metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 29.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 3.96 ranking it in the 22th percentile, and its high unemployment rate of 5.50%, which ranks it in the 2th percentile. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is -0.24% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.219% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not adding jobs at a significant rate and hours are not increasing, which may lead to a surplus of workers. This scenario does not suggest genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is 5.50%, ranking it in the 2th percentile, which means the market has significant slack. This implies that businesses may find it easier to hire workers, but the local consumer demand may be weaker due to the higher unemployment rate. Companies looking to staff up may find a larger pool of available workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is +1.06%, ranking it in the 14th percentile, which indicates stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power is also not increasing significantly. Employers may benefit from a flat cost environment, but workers may have limited bargaining power.

Cost of Living

Detroit-Warren-Dearborn has a cost of living score ranking it in the 31th percentile, with a PSF of $186/sqft and an hourly earnings rate of $36.54, resulting in a ratio of 5.09. This means the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. The fact that the PSF is not falling year-over-year suggests that affordability is not improving. This may require businesses to offer wage premiums to attract talent, as the city's affordability is not a significant advantage.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is growing at a rate of +2.21% year-over-year, ranking it in the 94th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding rapidly, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire. The growing labor force can accommodate the needs of expanding companies.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn has decreased by -10.99% year-over-year, ranking it in the 28th percentile. This decline suggests that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This could be a concern for businesses relying on a mobile workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is 38 days, with an 8.6% year-over-year increase, ranking it in the 84th percentile. This means the market is slowing down, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, this slowing market may also indicate weakening demand.

Office Economy

Detroit-Warren-Dearborn has an office economy score ranking it in the 38th percentile, with a share of professional and office workers indicating a moderately deep talent pool. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a highly specialized or knowledge-economy dominant workforce, such as industrial or logistics companies. It may not be the ideal location for tech, finance, or consulting firms.

The Detroit-Warren-Dearborn metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a growing labor force and a slowing housing market, which may provide opportunities for relocation or expansion. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand and high unemployment rate, which may signal a challenging economic environment and require careful consideration of the local market's strengths and weaknesses.
