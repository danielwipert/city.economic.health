Economic Analysis

The Detroit-Warren-Dearborn metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 27.4th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 6.00% and its below-average labor demand composite score of 4.43, which signals a mix of slow employment growth and decreased weekly hours. These metrics, particularly the unemployment rate and labor demand, set the tone for the city's economic landscape.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is +0.16% year-over-year, combined with a -0.098% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, indicating a labor market that is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. This combination suggests that while there is some job growth, the decrease in hours worked per week may indicate a degree of survivor squeeze, where remaining workers absorb the workload of eliminated roles. This scenario does not strongly support rapid business expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn stands at 6.00%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile, which signifies a tight labor market with considerable slack. For a business trying to hire in this market, the low unemployment rate implies that it may be challenging to find suitable candidates, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages to attract and retain talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is +0.60%, placing it in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth environment suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also means that worker purchasing power is not increasing significantly, which could impact local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Detroit-Warren-Dearborn has a cost of living score that ranks it in the 26th percentile, with a PSF of $186/sqft and average hourly earnings of $36.68, resulting in a ratio of 5.07. This indicates that the city is less affordable than many of its peers, which could make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is increasing by 1.6% year-over-year further reduces the city's affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is growing at a rate of +0.69% year-over-year, ranking it above average at the 74th percentile. This expansion in the labor force supply is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it indicates a growing pool of potential workers.

Building Permits

The city experienced a -18.71% year-over-year change in residential building permits, placing it in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile. This significant decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

Homes in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn are currently sitting on the market for a median of 40 days, with a year-over-year increase of 5.3%, ranking it in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This slower market means that workers relocating to the city may find it easier to find and purchase a home, as the market is less competitive.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn ranks near the median at the 40th percentile, indicating a moderate depth of professional talent. This makes the city more suited for businesses that do not require a deeply specialized knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics operations, rather than tech, finance, or consulting firms.

The Detroit-Warren-Dearborn metro area offers a mix of challenges and opportunities for businesses, with its tight labor market and slow wage growth being significant factors. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker to consider is the city's tightening housing supply, signaled by the sharp decline in building permits, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in attracting and retaining talent.
