Economic Analysis

The Detroit-Warren-Dearborn metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it at the 28.5th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 3.96, and its high unemployment rate of 5.40%, which ranks in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. The combination of these metrics suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is -0.24% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.219% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. Instead, it may be experiencing a period of job losses, which could lead to a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is 5.40%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. This indicates a slack labor market with a significant amount of unemployment, making it easier for businesses to staff their operations. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand, which could impact businesses that rely on local spending.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is +1.06%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This suggests stagnant wage growth, which means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly. However, this also implies weak bargaining power for workers, which could impact their purchasing power and overall economic well-being.

Cost of Living

Detroit-Warren-Dearborn has a cost of living score that ranks at the 29th percentile, with a PSF of $186/sqft and average hourly earnings of $36.54/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.09. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. The fact that PSF is rising by +0.5% year-over-year further exacerbates the affordability issue, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is growing at a rate of +2.68% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 94th percentile. This suggests that the labor force supply is expanding rapidly, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire. This growth in labor force supply could provide a competitive advantage for companies looking to tap into a growing pool of workers.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is decreasing by -10.99% year-over-year, ranking below average. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn is 39 days, with a year-over-year increase of +2.6%. This ranks in the above-average tier at the 74th percentile, indicating a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold. For workers relocating to this city, this could be a competitive advantage, as they may have more time to find a suitable home.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Detroit-Warren-Dearborn ranks at the 38th percentile, with a composite score of 2.10. This suggests a relatively shallow talent pool in professional and office sectors, making the city less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms.

The Detroit-Warren-Dearborn metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a growing labor force and a relatively slow housing market, which could provide a competitive advantage for companies looking to relocate or expand. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand and high unemployment rate, which could impact local consumer demand and make it challenging to attract and retain top talent.
