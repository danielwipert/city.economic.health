Economic Analysis

Cleveland, with an overall grade of B- and a composite score ranking it 50.8th percentile among 50 US metros, is characterized by its near-median labor demand and above-average unemployment rate, with 3.40% of the labor force unemployed. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.27 and unemployment rate are the two metrics that most define its current economic character, with the labor force growth rate of +1.80% YoY being a notable bright spot. Specifically, the combination of +0.57% employment growth YoY and +0.170% weekly hours deviation from trend suggests a genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

Cleveland's employment growth rate of +0.57% YoY and weekly hours deviation of +0.170% from its own trend signal a genuine demand expansion, indicating that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours than usual. This combination suggests that the labor market is experiencing genuine growth, rather than just a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 5.27, ranking near the median at 48th percentile, further supports this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Cleveland is 3.40%, ranking above average at the 60th percentile, which means the market has some slack. This rate implies that it may be slightly easier for businesses to hire in Cleveland compared to other cities, as there is a larger pool of available workers. However, this also means that local consumer demand may be somewhat weaker due to the relatively higher unemployment.

Wage Growth

Cleveland's year-over-year wage growth rate of +0.88% is in the bottom tier, ranking at the 10th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth environment means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also signifies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. For businesses, this could mean a flat cost environment, but it may also indicate a less dynamic local economy.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $143/sqft to $34.26/hr, ranking near the median at the 50th percentile, Cleveland is neither particularly affordable nor expensive relative to its peers. The PSF has increased by 13.5% YoY, which does not help affordability. This neutral affordability ranking means that Cleveland does not have a significant talent attraction advantage due to low costs, and businesses may need to consider wage premiums to attract top talent.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Cleveland is growing at a rate of +1.80% YoY, which is in the top tier, ranking at the 94th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding rapidly, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a growing pool of potential employees. This rapid labor force growth is a significant advantage for companies seeking to expand their operations in the city.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Cleveland is -0.61%, ranking near the median at the 49th percentile, indicating a slight tightening of housing supply. This decrease in building permits suggests that the future housing supply may not keep pace with demand, potentially leading to affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Cleveland is 55 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.8%, ranking above average at the 64th percentile. This means that homes are sitting on the market for a relatively longer period, indicating a slower market that is more accessible to relocating workers. However, this also signals a less competitive housing market, which could affect the attractiveness of the city to potential residents.

Office Economy

Cleveland's professional and office worker share, with a composite score of 2.82, ranks near the median at the 59th percentile, indicating a moderately deep talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that Cleveland is suited for businesses that require a mix of professional and industrial skills, but it may not be the best fit for companies that solely rely on a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance firms.

In conclusion, Cleveland offers businesses a unique combination of a growing labor force and a relatively slack labor market, which could facilitate hiring and expansion. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's stagnant wage growth and potential future affordability issues due to tightening housing supply, which could impact both labor costs and the attractiveness of the city to relocating workers.
