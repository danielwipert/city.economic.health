Economic Analysis

Cleveland earns an overall grade of B-, with a score of 52.6 out of 100, indicating it ranks below average among the 50 metros. The city's economy is currently defined by its slow wage growth, with hourly earnings rising only 1.8% over the past year, and a labor force that is growing faster than most metros, at a rate of 1.8% over the past year.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

Cleveland is adding jobs at a rate of 0.6% over the past year, with workers' hours also rising by 0.6%, indicating a moderate level of labor demand. This means the job market is relatively stable, with some growth, but not at a pace that would put significant pressure on wages or labor availability.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Cleveland is 3.4%, which is about average for the 50 metros, making it neither particularly easy nor hard for employers to hire. With a score of 54 out of 100, the city's unemployment rate suggests a balanced labor market, where employers may not face significant challenges in finding workers, but workers also may not have abundant job opportunities.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Hourly earnings in Cleveland rose by 1.8% over the past year, which is slower than most metros, with a score of 20 out of 100. This slow wage growth means that employers may face relatively stable labor costs, but workers may not see significant increases in their paychecks, potentially affecting their purchasing power.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Cleveland is about average, with a price per square foot of $143 and hourly pay of $34.42, resulting in a score of 50 out of 100. However, the price per square foot has increased by 13.5% over the past year, making housing less affordable for local workers compared to other metros.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in Cleveland is growing at a rate of 1.8% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, with a score of 92 out of 100. This rapid growth in the workforce means that employers may find it easier to hire, as the pool of available workers is expanding.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in Cleveland have decreased by 6.8% over the past year, with a score of 42 out of 100, indicating a relatively stable but not particularly strong housing market. This decline in building permits may signal that the future housing supply could be limited, potentially affecting affordability and workforce attraction.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Cleveland are taking 55 days to sell, with a 7.8% increase in days on market over the past year, resulting in a score of 60 out of 100. This indicates a relatively healthy housing market, where buyers have a moderate amount of time to find a home, and sellers can still expect to sell their properties within a reasonable timeframe.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

Cleveland has a larger office-based workforce than most metros, with a score of 62 out of 100, making it suitable for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This strong office economy suggests that the city has a deep talent pool in these areas, which could attract businesses looking to establish or expand their operations.

The bottom line is that Cleveland's biggest strength is its rapidly growing labor force, which can provide a competitive advantage for employers looking to hire. However, the city's biggest risk is its slow wage growth, which may affect workers' purchasing power and the overall attractiveness of the city to businesses and talent.
