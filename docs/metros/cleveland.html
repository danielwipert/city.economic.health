Economic Analysis

Cleveland earns an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 50.7th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its near-median labor demand and low wage growth, with a labor demand composite score of 5.27 and wage growth of +0.88% year-over-year. These metrics suggest a mixed economic environment, with employment growth and hours worked near the national median, but wage pressures lagging behind.

Labor Demand

Cleveland's employment growth rate is +0.57% year-over-year, combined with a +0.170% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month trend. This signals a genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. The labor demand composite score of 5.27, ranking near the median at 48th percentile, indicates a moderate level of job market activity.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Cleveland is 3.40%, ranking above average at the 60th percentile. This indicates a relatively tight labor market, with some slack but generally favorable conditions for workers. For a business trying to hire in Cleveland, this means that hiring may be more challenging and wage pressure could be higher due to the lower unemployment rate.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Cleveland is +0.88% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile. This stagnant wage growth environment means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power is also not increasing significantly. As a result, businesses may face less pressure to increase wages, but workers may have limited disposable income to drive local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Cleveland's cost of living is near the median, with a percentile rank of 50th, and a price-to-salary ratio of $143/sqft to $34.26/hr, or 4.17. However, the PSF has increased by 13.5% year-over-year, indicating a less affordable environment. This suggests that the city may not have a significant talent attraction advantage without offering wage premiums to offset the rising cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Cleveland is growing at a rate of +1.80% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 94th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding rapidly, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire in the city. The growing labor force provides a favorable environment for recruitment and talent acquisition.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Cleveland has decreased by -0.61% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 48th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is not expanding rapidly, which could lead to future affordability concerns and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact of a tightening housing market on their ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Cleveland is 55 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.8%, ranking above average at the 63rd percentile. This indicates a relatively slow market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city as they have more time to find a home. However, this also suggests that the housing market may be normalizing after a period of rapid growth.

Office Economy

Cleveland's professional and office worker share is 2.86, ranking above average at the 60th percentile. This indicates a deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. However, the city may be less suitable for industries with limited office-based roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Cleveland offers a mixed economic environment with a growing labor force and a relatively tight labor market, but also stagnant wage growth and a rising cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering Cleveland as a location is the potential for future affordability concerns and housing market tightening, which could impact their ability to attract and retain talent.
