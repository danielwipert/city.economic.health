Economic Analysis

The Milwaukee-Waukesha metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 39.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 3.95, and its stagnant wage growth, at +0.52% year-over-year. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Milwaukee-Waukesha is -0.25% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -1.588% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not adding jobs at a significant rate and existing workers are seeing reduced hours. This scenario suggests a lack of genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Milwaukee-Waukesha is 3.80%, ranking it 64th percentile, which means the market has some slack. This rate implies that while it may not be extremely difficult to hire, there is still a pool of available workers, and businesses may not face intense wage pressure. However, the relatively low unemployment rate compared to other metrics suggests a nuanced labor market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Milwaukee-Waukesha is +0.52%, placing it in the bottom tier at the 6th percentile. This stagnant wage growth indicates that labor costs for employers are not rising significantly, but it also means workers have weak bargaining power and limited purchasing power. This could affect the overall attractiveness of the city for talent.

Cost of Living

Milwaukee-Waukesha has a cost of living score that ranks it in the bottom tier at the 6th percentile, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $227/sqft to $35.76/hr, or 6.35. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which could make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The 2.2% year-over-year increase in PSF further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Milwaukee-Waukesha is growing at a rate of +0.85% year-over-year, placing it in the top tier at the 80th percentile. This expansion in labor force supply is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it indicates a growing pool of potential workers. However, the city's other labor market challenges may still pose hurdles.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Milwaukee-Waukesha has increased by +13.52% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 58th percentile. This growth in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which could improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. However, the pace of growth is not exceptionally high, which may not fully address the city's affordability challenges.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Milwaukee-Waukesha is 36 days, with a year-over-year increase of +12.5%, placing it in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This indicates a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold, which could make it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, this also signals a less competitive market, which may have implications for the local economy.

Office Economy

Milwaukee-Waukesha has an office economy score that ranks it below average at the 30th percentile, with a share of professional and office workers indicating a less deep talent pool. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring a high concentration of tech, finance, or consulting talent but may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs.

The Milwaukee-Waukesha metro area offers businesses a growing labor force and an expanding housing supply, but it is constrained by low labor demand, stagnant wage growth, and a relatively high cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the city's challenging labor market conditions, which could make it difficult to attract and retain talent without significant wage premiums.
