Economic Analysis

Oklahoma City has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 45.3th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its below-average labor demand, with a composite score of 4.39, and its above-average wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.07%. These metrics suggest a mixed economic environment, with some positive trends in wage growth but challenges in labor demand.

Labor Demand

Oklahoma City's employment growth rate is 0.15% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.117% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The below-average labor demand composite score of 4.39 further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Oklahoma City is 4.20%, which is below average, ranking at the 24th percentile. This suggests a relatively tight labor market, making it harder for businesses to hire and potentially leading to upward wage pressure. Companies looking to staff up in this city may face challenges in finding available talent.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Oklahoma City is 5.07% year-over-year, which is above average, ranking at the 68th percentile. This indicates a relatively fast pace of wage growth, which can lead to rising labor costs for employers but also increases worker purchasing power. Businesses should factor in the potential for higher labor costs when considering this location.

Cost of Living

Oklahoma City has a cost of living score that ranks at the 80th percentile, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $172/sqft to $33.58/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.12. This suggests that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The high affordability score means that Oklahoma City has a talent attraction advantage, as workers can maintain a decent standard of living without requiring significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Oklahoma City is growing at a rate of -0.04% year-over-year, which is near the median. This indicates a slightly contracting labor pool, presenting a structural headwind for hiring. Businesses may need to consider strategies to attract talent from outside the city or invest in workforce development programs.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Oklahoma City has decreased by 6.14% year-over-year. This decline suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability challenges and make it harder for the workforce to find accommodation. This trend may negatively impact the city's ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Oklahoma City is 52 days, with a year-over-year increase of 6.1%. This ranks at the 80th percentile, indicating a slower market. For workers relocating to this city, the slower market means that they have more time to find a home, making the city more accessible for relocation.

Office Economy

Oklahoma City's professional and office worker share is ranked at the 2nd percentile, with a composite score of 0.36. This suggests a relatively shallow talent pool in professional and office sectors, making the city less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, it may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

In conclusion, Oklahoma City offers a mixed economic environment with above-average wage growth but below-average labor demand. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering this location is the tightening housing supply, which could lead to future affordability challenges and impact talent attraction and retention. Despite this, the city's relative affordability and slower housing market may still make it an attractive option for certain types of businesses.
