Economic Analysis

Oklahoma City has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 46.7th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its near-median labor demand composite score of 5.17 and its below-average labor force growth rate of -1.65% YoY. These metrics suggest a mixed economic environment, with some indicators pointing to stability and others to potential challenges.

Labor Demand

Oklahoma City's employment growth rate is +0.74% YoY, while weekly hours are -0.163% below trend, resulting in a near-median labor demand composite score. This combination signals a moderate expansion of jobs, but with hours worked slightly below trend, indicating some slack in the labor market. The city is not experiencing a strong genuine demand expansion, but rather a gradual growth with some room for additional hiring.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Oklahoma City is 3.90%, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This rate indicates a relatively balanced labor market, neither extremely tight nor overly slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate means that finding talent may not be excessively difficult, but it also may not be particularly easy, with some wage pressure possible.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Oklahoma City is +4.20% YoY, which is near the median at the 58th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not extremely rapidly. Workers in the city are experiencing an increase in purchasing power, which can contribute to local consumer demand, benefiting businesses that rely on a strong local economy.

Cost of Living

Oklahoma City has a cost of living score ranking it near the median at the 56th percentile, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $171/sqft to $34.33/hr, or 4.98. The city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which can be a talent attraction advantage without needing significant wage premiums. This affordability can help businesses in attracting and retaining workers without incurring high labor costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Oklahoma City is shrinking at a rate of -1.65% YoY, ranking below average at the 34th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may face challenges in finding the talent they need, potentially leading to increased competition for workers and upward pressure on wages.

Building Permits

Residential building permits in Oklahoma City are increasing by +14.19% YoY, ranking above average at the 61st percentile. This expansion in housing supply is a positive sign for future affordability and workforce accommodation, indicating that the city is likely to remain attractive to workers and businesses alike. The growing housing supply can help mitigate potential labor market constraints.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Oklahoma City is 57 days, with a +9.6% YoY increase, ranking near the median at the 58th percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced housing market, neither extremely competitive nor overly slow. For workers relocating to the city, the housing market is accessible, but not excessively fast-paced, allowing for a reasonable timeframe to find suitable housing.

Office Economy

Oklahoma City's professional and office worker share ranks in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. The city is less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Oklahoma City offers a mixed economic environment with both positive and negative factors for businesses to consider. The city's relatively affordable cost of living and expanding housing supply are advantages, but the shrinking labor force and limited professional talent pool pose significant risks. Decision-makers should carefully weigh these factors, particularly the labor market constraints, when evaluating Oklahoma City as a potential business location.
