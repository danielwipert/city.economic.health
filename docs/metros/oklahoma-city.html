Economic Analysis

Oklahoma City has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 44.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its high wage growth rate of 6.51% year-over-year and its low unemployment rate of 4.20%, which suggests a tight labor market. Additionally, the cost of living in Oklahoma City is relatively affordable, with a PSF to wages ratio of 5.05, ranking it in the top tier at the 94th percentile.

Labor Demand

Oklahoma City's employment growth rate is -0.54% year-over-year, and its weekly hours deviation from trend is +0.000%, indicating a labor demand composite score of 3.54, which ranks below average at the 30th percentile. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion or survivor squeeze. The lack of job growth and stagnant hours worked suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand their workforce.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Oklahoma City is 4.20%, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city may face significant competition for talent, leading to upward pressure on wages. As a result, companies may need to offer higher salaries or more attractive benefits to attract and retain employees.

Wage Growth

Oklahoma City's year-over-year wage growth rate is 6.51%, ranking it in the top tier at the 82nd percentile, indicating fast wage growth. This rapid increase in wages suggests that employer labor costs are rising, while worker purchasing power is also increasing. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs when considering relocation or expansion in this city.

Cost of Living

Oklahoma City's cost of living is relatively affordable, with a PSF to wages ratio of 5.05, ranking it in the top tier at the 94th percentile. The city's PSF is $172/sqft, which has decreased by 0.6% year-over-year, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. This affordability advantage can be a major draw for businesses looking to relocate or expand in the city.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Oklahoma City is contracting at a rate of -1.07% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This decline in labor force growth suggests that hiring capacity may be constrained, making it challenging for businesses to find the talent they need to expand or maintain operations.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Oklahoma City has decreased by 6.33% year-over-year, ranking it near the median at the 40th percentile. This decline in permits suggests that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for workforce accommodation in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Oklahoma City is 51 days, with a year-over-year increase of 13.3%, ranking it in the top tier at the 80th percentile. This slower market means that workers relocating to the city may have a more accessible and less competitive housing market, making it easier for them to find a home.

Office Economy

Oklahoma City's professional talent pool is relatively shallow, ranking in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, and more suited for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Oklahoma City offers businesses a unique combination of high wage growth, affordable cost of living, and a relatively slow housing market. However, the city's tight labor market, contracting labor force, and limited professional talent pool pose significant risks and constraints for businesses considering relocation or expansion in this city, making it essential for decision-makers to carefully weigh these factors when making their decision.
