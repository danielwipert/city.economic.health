Economic Analysis

Cincinnati earns an overall grade of B+, scoring 60.0 out of 100 across 50 US metros, with its strong wage growth and affordable cost of living being major defining characteristics, including an 8.9% rise in hourly earnings over the past year. The city's economy is marked by a mix of strengths and weaknesses, with a score of 98 out of 100 for wage growth and 92 out of 100 for cost of living, indicating faster pay growth and more affordable housing than most metros.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

Cincinnati added jobs at a rate of 0.4% over the past year, which is slower than most metros, with a score of 38 out of 100, indicating weaker hiring than 62% of the 50 metros. Workers' hours are also slightly falling, by 0.1%, which means the job market is not as strong as it could be, with remaining workers not taking on significantly more hours.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The city's unemployment rate is 3.6%, which is lower than most metros, scoring 74 out of 100, making it harder for employers to hire as the labor market is relatively tight. This low unemployment rate means that employers may face more competition for workers and potentially higher wages, as a score of 74 out of 100 indicates better than 74% of the 50 metros.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Hourly earnings in Cincinnati rose by 8.9% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, with a score of 98 out of 100, indicating stronger wage growth than 98% of the 50 metros. This rapid pay growth means that employers may face rising labor costs, but workers will see more money in their paychecks, as the city's wage growth outpaces most other metros.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Cincinnati is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $189 and hourly pay of $36.19, scoring 92 out of 100, which is more affordable than most metros. The price per square foot is also falling by 0.5% over the past year, making it an attractive location for workers and businesses, as the city's affordability is better than 92% of the 50 metros.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in Cincinnati is shrinking by 1.2% over the past year, which is slower than most metros, with a score of 26 out of 100, indicating weaker workforce growth than 74% of the 50 metros. This decline in the labor force means that employers may face challenges in finding new workers, as the city's labor force growth lags behind most other metros.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in Cincinnati fell by 20.2% over the past year, which is weaker than most metros, scoring 14 out of 100, indicating slower home-building than 86% of the 50 metros. This decline in building permits signals that the city's housing supply may not be keeping up with demand, potentially leading to higher housing costs in the future, as the city's building permit growth is weaker than most metros.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Cincinnati are taking 44 days to sell, which is getting slower by 15.8% over the past year, but still scores 98 out of 100, indicating a healthier housing market than most metros. This slower pace of home sales is actually a sign of a healthier market, as it gives buyers more time to find a home and indicates a more balanced market, with the city's days on market being better than 98% of the 50 metros.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

Cincinnati's office-based workforce is smaller than most metros, scoring 36 out of 100, which means it may not be the best fit for businesses that rely heavily on tech, finance, or professional services. The city's economy is more suited to industries that don't require a large pool of office workers, as the city's office economy is smaller than 64% of the 50 metros.

The bottom line is that Cincinnati's biggest strength is its rapid wage growth, which is attracting workers and driving economic activity, with an 8.9% rise in hourly earnings over the past year. However, the city's biggest risk is its slow labor force growth, which could limit its ability to fill new jobs and sustain economic expansion, as the city's labor force is shrinking by 1.2% over the past year.
