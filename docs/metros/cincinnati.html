Economic Analysis

Cincinnati earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 73.7th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its exceptionally low unemployment rate of 3.90% and strong wage growth of +7.22% year-over-year, indicating a tight labor market with rising labor costs. These metrics suggest a competitive environment for businesses looking to hire and retain top talent.

Labor Demand

Cincinnati's employment growth rate stands at +0.97% year-over-year, while weekly hours are -0.372% below trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 5.29, near the median. This combination signals a moderate expansion of genuine demand, as the city is adding jobs, albeit with hours slightly below trend. The labor market is growing, but not at an accelerated pace.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Cincinnati is 3.90%, placing it in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This indicates a very tight labor market with little slack, making it challenging for businesses to hire new employees. As a result, companies may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain talent in this competitive environment.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Cincinnati is +7.22% year-over-year, ranking it in the top tier at the 96th percentile. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers, which can be a challenge for businesses. However, it also translates to increased purchasing power for workers, potentially boosting local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Cincinnati's cost of living is relatively affordable, with a PSF of $192/sqft and hourly earnings of $35.56, resulting in a ratio of 5.40. The city's cost of living percentile rank is 90th, indicating it is more affordable than most peers. This affordability advantage can attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Cincinnati is growing at a rate of +0.14% year-over-year, near the median. This slow growth indicates a slightly expanding workforce supply, which can support hiring needs, but may not be sufficient to meet rapid demand growth.

Building Permits

Residential building permits in Cincinnati are increasing by +24.76% year-over-year, above the average. This rise in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which can improve affordability and support workforce growth in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Cincinnati is 40 days, with an 8.1% year-over-year increase. This indicates a relatively slow market, ranked in the top tier at the 88th percentile. For workers relocating to Cincinnati, this means a more accessible and less competitive housing market.

Office Economy

Cincinnati's professional and office worker share is near the median, ranking at the 44th percentile. This suggests a moderate depth of talent pool, making it suitable for a variety of businesses, but potentially less ideal for those requiring a highly specialized or deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies.

In conclusion, Cincinnati offers businesses a unique combination of low unemployment, strong wage growth, and affordability, making it an attractive location for talent attraction and retention. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential for rising labor costs due to the tight labor market and rapid wage growth, which may impact business profitability and competitiveness.
