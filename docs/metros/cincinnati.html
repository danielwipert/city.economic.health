Economic Analysis

Cincinnati earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking at the 75.0th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its exceptionally low unemployment rate of 3.70% and strong wage growth of +7.22% year-over-year, indicating a tight labor market with rising labor costs. These metrics suggest that Cincinnati is an attractive location for businesses seeking a skilled workforce, but may also pose challenges for hiring and talent retention.

Labor Demand

Cincinnati's employment growth rate is +0.97% year-over-year, while weekly hours are -0.372% below trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 5.29, ranking near the median at the 48th percentile. This combination signals a moderate expansion of genuine demand, as the city is adding jobs, albeit with slightly below-trend hours. This suggests that businesses can expect a steady, but not explosive, growth in labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Cincinnati is 3.70%, ranking at the 94th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market with minimal slack. This means that businesses may face significant challenges in hiring and retaining talent, as the pool of available workers is limited, leading to upward pressure on wages. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract and retain employees.

Wage Growth

Cincinnati's year-over-year wage growth is +7.22%, ranking at the 96th percentile, indicating fast and rising labor costs for employers. This strong wage growth is beneficial for worker purchasing power, as employees can expect higher earnings and increased disposable income. However, this also means that businesses will need to budget for higher labor costs, potentially impacting their bottom line.

Cost of Living

Cincinnati's cost of living is relatively affordable, with a PSF of $191/sqft and a ratio of 5.37 to average hourly earnings of $35.56, ranking at the 88th percentile. This means that the city offers a talent attraction advantage, as workers can enjoy a relatively low cost of living without requiring significant wage premiums. The fact that PSF is falling by -0.5% year-over-year further enhances the city's affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Cincinnati is growing at a rate of -0.21% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 54th percentile, indicating a slightly contracting labor pool. This suggests that the city's workforce supply is not expanding rapidly, which may pose a structural headwind for hiring and business growth. Companies may need to invest in talent development and retention strategies to mitigate this risk.

Building Permits

Cincinnati's residential building permits are increasing by +24.76% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 76th percentile, indicating an expansion of future housing supply. This growth in building permits suggests that the city's housing market is likely to become more affordable, which will improve the attractiveness of the city for relocating workers and support workforce accommodation.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Cincinnati is 42 days, with a year-over-year increase of +13.5%, ranking at the 98th percentile, indicating a slower and more buyer-friendly market. This means that workers relocating to the city may find it relatively accessible to purchase or rent a home, as the market is not extremely competitive. However, this also suggests that the city's housing market may be normalizing, rather than experiencing rapid growth.

Office Economy

Cincinnati's professional and office worker share is 2.28, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile, indicating a moderate depth of talent pool. This suggests that the city is suited for a variety of businesses, but may not be the best fit for companies requiring a highly specialized or deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance firms.

In conclusion, Cincinnati offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market, strong wage growth, and relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for companies seeking a skilled workforce. However, the city's slightly contracting labor pool and moderate talent pool depth pose risks that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating Cincinnati as a potential business location.
