Economic Analysis

Cincinnati earns an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking in the 67.0th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its exceptionally low unemployment rate of 3.60% and strong wage growth of +8.11% year-over-year, indicating a tight labor market with rising labor costs. These metrics suggest that Cincinnati is an attractive location for businesses seeking a skilled workforce, but may pose challenges for hiring and talent retention.

Labor Demand

Cincinnati's employment growth rate is +0.31% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from trend by +0.000%, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 4.73, which ranks below average in the 32nd percentile. This combination signals a moderate expansion of genuine demand, as hours are not significantly above trend despite job growth. However, the low labor demand score suggests that the city's job market may not be as robust as its low unemployment rate would imply.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Cincinnati is 3.60%, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile, indicating a very tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in Cincinnati may face significant challenges in finding available workers, and may need to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract talent. The low unemployment rate also suggests that workers in Cincinnati have strong bargaining power.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Cincinnati is +8.11% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile, indicating fast-rising labor costs for employers. This strong wage growth is good for worker purchasing power, as employees are seeing significant increases in their earnings. However, it may pose challenges for businesses seeking to control labor costs, as they may need to adjust their pricing or operating models to accommodate rising wages.

Cost of Living

Cincinnati has a cost of living score that ranks in the top tier at the 94th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $190/sqft to $36.31/hr, or 5.23. The city's PSF is also falling by -1.0% year-over-year, making it more affordable relative to wages. This means that Cincinnati has a talent attraction advantage, as workers can afford a high quality of life without requiring significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Cincinnati is shrinking by -1.35% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 42nd percentile. This contraction in labor supply may pose a structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may struggle to find available workers to fill open positions. The shrinking labor force also suggests that Cincinnati's workforce may not be as dynamic or adaptable as other cities.

Building Permits

Residential building permits in Cincinnati are falling by -1.83% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 46th percentile. This decline in permits suggests that housing supply may be tightening, which could lead to future affordability challenges and make it harder for workers to relocate to the city. The falling permits also signal that developer confidence may be waning, which could have implications for the city's long-term economic growth.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Cincinnati is 44 days, with a year-over-year increase of +12.8%, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This means that homes are sitting on the market for longer, making it a more accessible and less competitive market for workers relocating to the city. The rising days on market also suggests that the city's housing market may be normalizing, with less pressure on prices and more opportunities for buyers.

Office Economy

Cincinnati's professional and office worker share is 2.30, ranking near the median at the 42nd percentile. This suggests that the city has a moderate-sized talent pool of specialized workers, making it suitable for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors. However, the city may not be as well-suited for businesses requiring a large pool of industrial or logistics workers.

In conclusion, Cincinnati offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market, strong wage growth, and a high quality of life, making it an attractive location for companies seeking a skilled workforce. However, the city's shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply pose significant risks and constraints that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating Cincinnati as a potential business location.
