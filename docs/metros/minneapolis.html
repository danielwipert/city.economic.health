Economic Analysis

The Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 35.4th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score reflecting a mix of mediocre labor demand and cost of living metrics. The city's economic character is most defined by its stagnant wage growth, at -0.14% year-over-year, and its below-average labor force growth, at -1.32% year-over-year. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to attract and retain talent.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is 1.36% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.894% from the city's own 12-month baseline, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 5.31, near the median. This combination signals a modest expansion in job growth, but with hours worked below trend, it may indicate some level of survivor squeeze. The labor market is not demonstrating strong, genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is 4.40%, ranking below average at the 26th percentile. This indicates a relatively tight labor market with some slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire. As a result, businesses may face some wage pressure when trying to attract talent in this market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is -0.14%, ranking at the bottom tier. This stagnant wage growth signals flat labor costs for employers but weak bargaining power for workers. The slow wage growth may limit worker purchasing power, potentially affecting local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington has a cost of living ratio of $208/sqft to $40.22/hr, with a percentile rank of 43, indicating a near-median affordability level. The city's PSF is falling -1.9% year-over-year, which is a key driver of its affordability. This means the city has a moderate talent attraction advantage without requiring significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is contracting at -1.32% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to compensate for the declining labor force.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is +31.92%, ranking in the top tier. This significant increase in permits signals an expansion in future housing supply, which should improve affordability and accommodate workforce growth. The rising permits suggest developer confidence in the local market.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is 39 days, with a year-over-year change of +0.0%, ranking below average at the 28th percentile. This indicates a relatively competitive market for relocating workers, as homes are selling quickly. However, the stable days on market may signal a healthy normalization in the housing market.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is below average, ranking at the 24th percentile. This suggests a relatively shallow talent pool for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy workers, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. The city may be better suited for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington metro area offers businesses a mix of moderate affordability and expanding housing supply, but its stagnant wage growth and contracting labor force pose significant challenges for talent attraction and retention. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited labor force growth, which may require businesses to adapt their recruitment strategies to succeed in this market.
