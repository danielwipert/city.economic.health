Economic Analysis

The Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington metro area has an overall grade of C-, with a score of 37.1 out of 100, indicating it is struggling compared to other major US cities. One of the key challenges facing this city is its relatively high unemployment rate of 4.5%, which is a weakness compared to most other metros, and slower-than-average wage growth of 0.7% over the past year.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.1% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, but workers' hours are actually falling by 0.8%. This means that while the job market is not shrinking, it is not particularly strong either, with workers not seeing a significant increase in their hours.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 4.5% makes it easier for employers to hire, as there are more workers available, but it also means that workers may not have as much bargaining power when it comes to wages. This is a weakness for the city, as a lower unemployment rate would indicate a tighter labor market and more competition for workers.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages in the city rose by 0.7% over the past year, which is slower than most other metros, meaning that employers' labor costs are not rising as quickly, but workers' paychecks are not increasing as much either. This slow wage growth is a weakness for the city, as it can make it harder to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in the city is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $204 and hourly pay of $40.79, and it is getting even more affordable as housing prices fell by 2.9% over the past year. This is a strength for the city, as it makes it easier for workers to afford housing and other living expenses, which can be a major draw for businesses looking to relocate.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in the city is shrinking by 0.7% over the past year, which is slower than most other metros, making it harder for businesses to find the workers they need. This slow labor force growth is a weakness for the city, as it can limit its ability to support business expansion and economic growth.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

The number of new home approvals in the city rose by 10.9% over the past year, which is stronger than most other metros, indicating that developers are confident in the city's housing market and are building more homes to meet demand. This is a strength for the city, as it suggests that the housing supply will increase, making it easier for workers to find affordable housing.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in the city are taking 43 days to sell, which is getting slightly longer as the days on market rose by 4.9% over the past year, but this is still a relatively healthy housing market. This is a strength for the city, as it indicates that the housing market is balanced, with neither buyers nor sellers having too much power, making it easier for workers to buy or sell homes.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is smaller than most other metros, with a score of 38 out of 100, which means it may not be the best fit for businesses that rely heavily on tech, finance, or professional services. This is a weakness for the city, as it may limit its ability to attract businesses in these sectors, which are often major drivers of economic growth.

The city's biggest strength is its relatively affordable cost of living, which makes it an attractive location for businesses looking to relocate, but its biggest risk is its slow labor force growth, which can limit its ability to support business expansion and economic growth. Overall, the city needs to address its labor market challenges in order to attract more businesses and drive economic growth.
