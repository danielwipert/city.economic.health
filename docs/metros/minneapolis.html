Economic Analysis

The Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking at the 37.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 4.50% and its slow wage growth of +0.68% year-over-year, indicating a tight labor market with limited room for wage negotiation. The labor demand composite score of 5.05, near the median, further underscores the mixed signals in this market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +1.12% and weekly hours deviation of -0.822% suggest a scenario where job growth is present but hours worked are slightly below trend, indicating a moderate expansion rather than a strong demand surge. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit not a robust one. The labor market is adding jobs, but the slight decrease in hours worked per week may indicate some level of efficiency or productivity gains.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 4.50% is notably low, ranking in the bottom tier at the 6th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This tightness means that businesses may face significant challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is small, leading to potential wage pressure. For a business trying to hire in this market, the low unemployment rate implies a competitive environment where attracting and retaining talent may require premium offers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +0.68% is slow, ranking in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, suggesting stagnant labor costs but also limited bargaining power for workers. This slow wage growth implies a flat cost environment for employers, which can be beneficial for controlling labor expenses but may also reflect weak worker purchasing power. For businesses, this means labor costs are not escalating rapidly, but worker morale and retention might be challenged by the lack of significant wage increases.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $204/sqft (which has decreased by 2.9% year-over-year) to $40.79/hr, ranking in the 74th percentile, Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is more affordable than most of its peers. This affordability is a significant talent attraction advantage, as workers can maintain a higher standard of living without necessitating wage premiums from employers. The decreasing PSF relative to wages further enhances the city's attractiveness for both businesses and workers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force has contracted by -0.73% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. For businesses looking to expand or establish operations, this shrinking labor force poses a significant challenge in finding and hiring the necessary talent.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is +10.95%, signaling an expansion in housing supply. This increase in permits suggests that developer confidence is strong, and future housing supply is likely to improve, which should help with affordability and workforce accommodation. As housing supply expands, the city may become more attractive for workers considering relocation, potentially easing some of the hiring challenges faced by businesses.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 43 days, with a year-over-year increase of +4.9%, ranking in the 74th percentile. This indicates a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, in the context of a strong job market, this could also signal a healthy normalization rather than demand erosion.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share composite score ranking in the 38th percentile, the professional talent pool in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is not as deep as in other metros. This city is less suited for businesses requiring a large pool of specialized, office-based talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs.

The Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington metro offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market with slow wage growth and an affordable cost of living, which can be attractive for controlling costs while maintaining worker purchasing power. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor force, which poses significant challenges for hiring and talent acquisition, potentially outweighing the benefits of locating in this metro.
