Economic Analysis

The Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 39.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 5.57. This city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 4.70% and stagnant wage growth of +0.27% year-over-year. The combination of these metrics suggests a tight labor market with limited room for wage growth.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is +1.39% year-over-year, while weekly hours are -0.671% below trend. This combination signals a moderate expansion in job numbers, but with hours worked below the 12-month baseline, indicating some degree of survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 5.57 is near the median, suggesting a balanced but not particularly strong labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is 4.70%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile. This indicates a tight labor market with limited slack, making it harder for businesses to hire new employees. As a result, businesses may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is +0.27%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This stagnant wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies weak bargaining power for workers and limited growth in worker purchasing power. This may impact the overall attractiveness of the city for businesses looking to relocate or expand.

Cost of Living

Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington has a cost of living score of 63rd percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $206/sqft to $40.75/hr, indicating that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The fact that PSF is falling by -2.4% year-over-year relative to wages further supports this affordability. This makes the city more attractive for talent attraction without requiring significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is contracting at a rate of -1.77% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 28th percentile. This decline in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is shrinking. Businesses may need to adapt their hiring strategies to compensate for this trend.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is increasing by +18.45% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 70th percentile. This expansion in housing supply suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may improve, as more housing options become available to support a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is 42 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.7%, ranking in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This indicates a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, this trend may also signal a normalization of the market rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington is ranked near the median at the 40th percentile, indicating a moderate depth of talent pool in these sectors. This city is best suited for businesses that can thrive in a mixed economy with a balance of industrial, logistics, and office sectors, but may not be the top choice for businesses requiring a deep pool of specialized tech or finance talent.

The Minneapolis-St. Paul-Bloomington metro area offers a relatively affordable cost of living and a moderate labor market, but the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses is the tight labor market with limited room for wage growth, posing challenges for hiring and talent retention. Despite this, the city's expanding housing supply and slowing housing market may provide opportunities for businesses looking to relocate or expand in a more accessible and affordable environment.
