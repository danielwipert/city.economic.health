Economic Analysis

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking 51.2th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average wage growth and highly affordable cost of living. The city's economic character is defined by a wage growth rate of 5.33% year-over-year and a cost of living ratio of $233/sqft to $37.12/hr, which is in the top tier of affordability at the 82nd percentile. This unique combination signals a city with strong labor market dynamics and attractive living costs.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 0.56% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of +0.240% from its own trend, indicating a genuine demand expansion. This combination suggests that the city is experiencing a modest increase in jobs and hours worked, signaling a healthy labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.32 places the city near the median, indicating a balanced labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 4.80%, which is in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This low unemployment rate means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is limited, and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. The tight market also suggests that workers have significant bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 5.33%, which is above average at the 78th percentile. This fast wage growth rate implies that employer labor costs are rising, but it also means that workers have increasing purchasing power. The strong wage growth is a double-edged sword, benefiting workers but potentially increasing costs for businesses.

Cost of Living

Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a cost of living ratio of $233/sqft to $37.12/hr, with the price per square foot falling by 5.7% year-over-year, making it highly affordable at the 82nd percentile. This affordability is a significant talent attraction advantage, as workers can maintain a high standard of living without requiring large wage premiums. The falling PSF relative to wages is a key driver of this high affordability score.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is shrinking at a rate of -0.50% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor pool. This decline in labor force growth poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may need to adapt their hiring strategies to attract workers from other areas or invest in training and development programs.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is increasing by 3.19% year-over-year, indicating an expansion of housing supply. This growth in building permits suggests that developer confidence is rising, and future housing supply is likely to improve, which should help maintain affordability and accommodate a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 74 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 3.9%, indicating a relatively fast-paced market. This fast market means that relocating workers may face competition for housing, and businesses may need to offer relocation assistance or flexible housing options to attract talent.

Office Economy

Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a deep professional talent pool, ranking in the top tier at the 82nd percentile, making it well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. The city's strong office economy is a significant advantage for businesses requiring specialized knowledge workers, but it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth, affordable living costs, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for businesses in the knowledge economy. However, the city's tight labor market and declining labor force growth pose significant risks and constraints that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating this location for their business operations.
