Economic Analysis

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 54.8th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, with a composite score of 5.71, and its top-tier cost of living, with a percentile rank of 84th. The labor demand is driven by a combination of employment growth and hours worked above trend, while the cost of living is influenced by a PSF to earnings ratio of 6.22, with PSF decreasing by 6.1% year-over-year.

Labor Demand

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an employment growth rate of 0.92% year-over-year and a weekly hours deviation of 0.120% above its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. The labor demand composite score of 5.71 is above average, ranking at the 60th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 4.70%, ranking at the 16th percentile, indicating a relatively loose labor market. This means that there is some slack in the market, making it easier for businesses to hire workers. However, the relatively high unemployment rate also suggests weaker local consumer demand.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 5.13%, ranking above average at the 72nd percentile. This indicates fast wage growth, which will lead to rising labor costs for employers. On the other hand, workers will benefit from increased purchasing power, making the city more attractive to talent.

Cost of Living

Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a cost of living percentile rank of 84th, indicating that it is more affordable relative to its peers. The PSF to earnings ratio of 6.22, with PSF decreasing by 6.1% year-over-year, makes the city an attractive destination for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. This is a major advantage for businesses looking to relocate or expand in the area.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is growing at a rate of 0.34% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 70th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire. The growing labor force will provide a larger pool of potential workers, making it easier to find talent.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has decreased by 10.10% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 30th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The decrease in building permits is a concern for businesses that rely on a steady supply of housing for their workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 76 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 3.8%. This indicates a relatively fast-moving market, making it competitive for workers relocating to the city. The decrease in days on market suggests that the housing market is still active, but the relatively low days on market may make it challenging for workers to find housing.

Office Economy

Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy percentile rank of 82nd. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. However, the city may not be as suitable for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors, which require a different type of workforce.

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area offers businesses a unique combination of above-average labor demand and a highly affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This could offset some of the advantages of locating in the area, and businesses should carefully consider this factor when making their decision.
