Economic Analysis

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 52.5th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven significantly by its above-average labor demand and top-tier cost of living. The city's economic character is most defined by its 60th percentile labor demand composite score, which combines a +0.92% employment growth rate and a +0.120% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, and its 84th percentile cost of living score, indicating a highly affordable environment. This affordability is underscored by a PSF of $233/sqft, which has decreased by 5.7% year-over-year, paired with an average hourly wage of $37.29, resulting in a ratio of 6.25.

Labor Demand

The Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area exhibits a labor demand composite score of 5.71, placing it in the 60th percentile, driven by a +0.92% employment growth rate and a +0.120% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours worked are increasing. The positive trend in both employment and hours worked suggests a strong and growing labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater stands at 4.80%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with significant competition for workers. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. The low unemployment rate suggests that the local labor market has little slack, making it harder for companies to find and recruit talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in the Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater metro area is +5.13%, ranking in the 72nd percentile, which is considered above average. This fast wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers but also indicates strong worker purchasing power. As wages grow rapidly, businesses may need to adjust their compensation packages to attract and retain talent, while workers benefit from increased disposable income.

Cost of Living

Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has a cost of living score in the 84th percentile, making it one of the more affordable cities relative to its peers. The decreasing PSF of $233/sqft, down 5.7% year-over-year, compared to average hourly earnings of $37.29, results in a favorable ratio of 6.25. This high affordability score means that the city has a significant talent attraction advantage, as workers can maintain a high standard of living without requiring substantial wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater has contracted by -0.50% year-over-year, placing it near the median at the 44th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply suggests that the hiring capacity may be constrained, posing a structural challenge for businesses looking to expand their workforce. The shrinking labor pool could limit the city's ability to support rapid business growth.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is +3.19%, ranking near the median at the 52nd percentile. This moderate increase in building permits indicates that the housing supply is slowly expanding, which should help improve affordability and accommodate workforce growth over time. However, the pace of expansion may not be sufficient to keep up with demand, potentially leading to future affordability challenges.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater is 74 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -3.9%, placing it in the 76th percentile. This relatively fast market, with homes selling quickly, may pose challenges for relocating workers, as they may face competition and pressure to make rapid purchasing decisions. The fast-paced housing market could be a consideration for businesses relocating employees to the area.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater places it in the top tier at the 82nd percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy workers but may be less ideal for those primarily involved in industrial or logistics sectors.

In conclusion, Tampa-St. Petersburg-Clearwater offers businesses a unique combination of above-average labor demand, fast wage growth, and a highly affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for talent and business expansion. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tight labor market and the potential for hiring challenges due to the low unemployment rate and contracting labor force supply, which could limit the city's ability to support rapid business growth and require strategic workforce planning.
