Economic Analysis

Memphis has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking it at the 60.2th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 6.63%, and its highly affordable cost of living, with a PSF to earnings ratio of 4.67, ranking it in the top tier at the 98th percentile. These two metrics suggest a city with a competitive labor market and attractive living conditions.

Labor Demand

Memphis has experienced employment growth of 0.06% year-over-year, combined with a 1.422% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a healthy and growing labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.81 ranks in the 70th percentile, further supporting the notion of a strong labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Memphis stands at 4.70%, ranking it near the median at the 40th percentile. This rate suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to cities with tighter labor markets. However, this also implies that local consumer demand may be somewhat weaker due to the availability of labor.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 6.63%, Memphis is experiencing fast wage growth, ranking it in the top tier at the 90th percentile. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers but also indicates strong worker purchasing power, which can be beneficial for local businesses relying on consumer spending.

Cost of Living

Memphis is highly affordable, with a cost of living ranking in the top tier at the 98th percentile. The city's PSF to earnings ratio is 4.67, with PSF at $153/sqft and earnings at $32.74/hr, and this ratio is becoming even more favorable due to a 4.4% year-over-year decrease in PSF. This affordability makes Memphis an attractive location for talent without the need for significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Memphis has contracted by 0.92% year-over-year, ranking it below average at the 34th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may face challenges in finding sufficient skilled workers to meet their needs.

Building Permits

Memphis has seen a 5.95% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking it near the median at the 56th percentile. This expansion in housing supply suggests that developer confidence is present, and future affordability and workforce accommodation are likely to improve, making the city more attractive for relocating workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Memphis is 68 days, with a year-over-year increase of 6.2%, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market where homes do not stay on the market for long, which can make it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing.

Office Economy

Memphis has a relatively shallow professional talent pool, ranking in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more appropriate for industries with different skill requirements, like logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Memphis offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth, highly affordable living conditions, and a relatively slack labor market, making it an attractive location for certain types of industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the contracting labor force supply, which may pose challenges in finding and retaining skilled workers, potentially limiting the city's long-term growth potential.
