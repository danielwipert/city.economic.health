Economic Analysis

Memphis earns an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking at the 57.9th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its top-tier wage growth of 6.63% year-over-year and its highly affordable cost of living, with a PSF to wages ratio of 4.70, ranking in the 98th percentile. These metrics suggest a city with strong labor market dynamics and attractive living costs.

Labor Demand

Memphis exhibits a labor demand composite score of 5.81, ranking in the 70th percentile, driven by employment growth of 0.06% year-over-year and weekly hours 1.422% above its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. The labor market is thus characterized by a genuine increase in labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Memphis stands at 4.80%, ranking in the 36th percentile, indicating a market with some slack. This implies that businesses may find it somewhat easier to hire in Memphis compared to tighter labor markets, although wage pressure may still be present. The relatively higher unemployment rate suggests that the city's labor market is not as competitive as others.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 6.63%, Memphis ranks in the 90th percentile, indicating fast-rising wages. This rapid wage growth implies increasing labor costs for employers but also enhances worker purchasing power, contributing to a stronger local economy. Businesses should factor in the potential for higher labor costs when considering Memphis as a location.

Cost of Living

Memphis offers a highly affordable cost of living, with a PSF to wages ratio of 4.70 and a percentile rank of 98, driven by a PSF of $154/sqft that is falling by 6.1% year-over-year relative to wages of $32.74/hr. This affordability advantage is a significant talent attraction factor, as businesses can offer competitive wages without needing large premiums to offset high living costs. The city's affordability is a major draw for workers and businesses alike.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Memphis is contracting at a rate of -1.37% year-over-year, ranking in the 18th percentile. This decline in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may face challenges in finding sufficient skilled workers to meet their needs. The shrinking labor pool is a concern for companies looking to expand or establish operations in Memphis.

Building Permits

Memphis has seen a year-over-year increase in residential building permits of 5.95%, ranking near the median at the 56th percentile. This expansion in housing supply suggests that developer confidence is present, and future affordability may improve, supporting workforce accommodation and attraction. However, the moderate pace of permit growth may not fully keep up with demand, potentially limiting the improvement in affordability.

Days on Market

Homes in Memphis are currently sitting on the market for 66 days, with a year-over-year increase of 8.2%, ranking in the 10th percentile. This slower market means that relocating workers may find it more accessible to purchase or rent a home, as there is less competition and pressure to act quickly. The rising days on market indicate a normalization of the housing market, making it more favorable for buyers.

Office Economy

Memphis has a relatively shallow professional talent pool, ranking in the 2nd percentile with an office/professional worker share of 0.38. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more appropriate for industries with different skill requirements, like logistics or manufacturing. The city's economic profile is more aligned with traditional industries rather than office-intensive sectors.

In conclusion, Memphis offers businesses an attractive combination of affordable living costs and strong wage growth, making it a competitive location for talent attraction and retention. However, the city's shrinking labor force supply poses a significant risk and constraint for businesses looking to hire and expand, requiring careful consideration and planning to mitigate this challenge. Overall, Memphis presents a mixed economic profile that businesses must carefully weigh when making location decisions.
