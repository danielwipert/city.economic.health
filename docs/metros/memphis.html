Economic Analysis

Memphis has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 35.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its high cost of living affordability, ranking in the top tier with a score of 90th percentile. Specifically, the labor demand composite score of 2.11 and the cost of living ratio of $153/sqft to $33.22/hr, with a PSF decline of 2.5% YoY, are key metrics shaping the city's economic landscape.

Labor Demand

Memphis has an employment growth rate of -0.49% YoY and weekly hours 1.586% above its own trend, indicating a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion. The labor demand composite score of 2.11 is in the bottom tier, ranking 4th percentile, which further supports the notion of a struggling labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Memphis is 4.60%, ranking below average at the 34th percentile. This suggests that the market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter labor markets. However, the relatively high unemployment rate also implies weaker local consumer demand, which could impact businesses reliant on local spending.

Wage Growth

Memphis experiences a year-over-year wage growth of +4.52%, which is above average, ranking at the 68th percentile. This indicates fast wage growth, leading to rising labor costs for employers but also increasing worker purchasing power. Businesses should factor in the potential for higher labor costs when considering Memphis as a location.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living score in the top tier at the 90th percentile, Memphis is more affordable relative to its peers, given its PSF of $153/sqft and hourly earnings of $33.22, resulting in a ratio of 4.61. The 2.5% YoY decline in PSF further enhances affordability. This makes Memphis attractive for talent without necessitating significant wage premiums, offering a competitive advantage in talent attraction.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Memphis is shrinking at a rate of -2.77% YoY, indicating a contracting labor supply. This contraction poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses should consider this trend when assessing their hiring capacity in the city.

Building Permits

Memphis sees a year-over-year increase in building permits of +5.41%, ranking near the median at the 52nd percentile. This rise in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which could improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce in the future. However, the current pace is moderate, and its impact on future affordability and workforce attraction will depend on sustained growth.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Memphis is 72 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.5%. This ranks in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile, indicating a slower market where homes sit longer before being sold. For workers relocating to Memphis, this could mean a more accessible and less competitive housing market, potentially easing the relocation process.

Office Economy

Memphis has a relatively shallow professional talent pool, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. Instead, Memphis might be more accommodating to industries with fewer specialized roles, such as industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

In conclusion, Memphis offers businesses an affordable cost of living and a potentially accessible housing market, but it is constrained by low labor demand, a shrinking labor force, and a limited professional talent pool. The single biggest risk for a decision-maker considering Memphis is the challenge of finding and retaining skilled workers due to the city's contracting labor supply and shallow professional talent pool.
