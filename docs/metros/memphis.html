Economic Analysis

Memphis earns an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 57.1th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 6.63%, and its high cost of living affordability, with a percentile rank of 98th. These metrics suggest a city with a competitive labor market and attractive living costs.

Labor Demand

Memphis has an employment growth rate of 0.06% and weekly hours 1.422% above its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a moderate increase in job creation, with workers putting in more hours than usual, suggesting a healthy labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.81 ranks in the 70th percentile, further supporting this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Memphis is 4.80%, ranking in the 36th percentile, indicating a market with some slack. This means that businesses may find it relatively easier to hire workers, but may also face less upward pressure on wages. However, the unemployment rate is not extremely low, suggesting that there is still a pool of available workers.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 6.63%, Memphis is experiencing fast wage growth, ranking in the 90th percentile. This implies that employer labor costs are rising, but worker purchasing power is also increasing, which can be beneficial for businesses that rely on local consumer demand. The strong wage growth suggests a competitive labor market where workers have bargaining power.

Cost of Living

Memphis has a cost of living percentile rank of 98th, making it one of the most affordable cities relative to its peers. The city's PSF of $154/sqft is decreasing by 6.1% year-over-year, and the ratio of PSF to hourly earnings of $32.74/hr is 4.70, indicating a very affordable housing market. This means that businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Memphis is shrinking at a rate of -1.37% year-over-year, ranking in the 18th percentile. This contraction in labor supply implies that businesses may face structural headwinds when trying to hire, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. This could lead to increased competition for workers and upward pressure on wages.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Memphis is decreasing by 1.49% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 48th percentile. This suggests that the housing supply is not expanding rapidly, which could lead to future affordability concerns and make it more difficult for workers to find housing. However, the decrease is not extremely sharp, so the situation is not yet critical.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Memphis is 66 days, with a year-over-year increase of 8.2%, ranking in the 10th percentile. This indicates a relatively slow market, where homes are taking longer to sell, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, the slow market may also suggest some weakness in demand.

Office Economy

Memphis has a professional and office worker share of 0.38, ranking in the 2nd percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city is not well-suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies, but may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

In conclusion, Memphis offers businesses a competitive labor market with strong wage growth and attractive living costs, making it an appealing location for talent attraction. However, the city's shrinking labor force and slow housing market growth pose significant risks and constraints that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating Memphis as a potential business location.
