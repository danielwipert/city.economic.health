Economic Analysis

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 50.3th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 6.57, and its high wage growth rate of 7.80% year-over-year. The combination of these metrics suggests a city with a robust job market and rising labor costs.

Labor Demand

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an employment growth rate of 1.28% year-over-year and a weekly hours deviation of 0.481% above its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours are increasing, indicating a strong and growing labor market. The top-tier percentile rank of 80th for labor demand composite score reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 4.70%, which ranks at the 24th percentile, indicating a below-average performance. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, the unemployment rate is not low enough to indicate a significant surplus of workers, and businesses may still face some competition for talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 7.80%, which is a top-tier performance, ranking at the 94th percentile. This rapid wage growth indicates rising labor costs for employers, but it also means that workers have increasing purchasing power, which can benefit local businesses. The high wage growth rate suggests that businesses may need to adjust their compensation packages to remain competitive.

Cost of Living

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has a cost of living score that ranks at the 12th percentile, indicating that it is an expensive city relative to its peers. The PSF-to-earnings ratio is $583/sqft versus $43.46/hr, resulting in a ratio of 13.41. Although the PSF is falling by 3.6% year-over-year, the city's affordability is still a concern, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is shrinking at a rate of -0.76% year-over-year, which ranks at the 32nd percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may face challenges in finding qualified workers. The declining labor force growth rate may limit the city's ability to support rapid business expansion.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has decreased by 16.72% year-over-year, ranking at the 22nd percentile. This decline in permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The reduction in building permits is a concern for businesses that rely on a steady supply of housing for their employees.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 48 days, with a year-over-year increase of 2.1%, ranking at the 54th percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced market, neither extremely competitive nor extremely slow. For workers relocating to this city, the housing market is moderately accessible, but the rising days on market may signal a normalization of the market.

Office Economy

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has an office economy score that ranks at the 60th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool in professional and office sectors. This city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting industries, but may be less suitable for those in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors. The above-average office economy score suggests that businesses can find specialized talent in this city.

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with high wage growth, but it also poses challenges due to its expensive cost of living and tightening housing supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited labor force growth, which may hinder rapid business expansion and require businesses to offer competitive compensation packages to attract and retain talent.
