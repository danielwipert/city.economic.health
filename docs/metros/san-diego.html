Economic Analysis

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 38.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score defined largely by its high wage growth and low cost of living affordability. The city's economic character is most notably marked by a 94th percentile wage growth rate of +7.50% year-over-year and a cost of living ratio of $587/sqft to $44.28/hr, which places it in the 12th percentile for affordability. This combination suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to manage labor costs.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is +0.70% year-over-year, combined with a +0.152% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, indicating a near median labor demand composite score of 5.43. This signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit at a moderate pace. The combination of job growth and hours above trend suggests that the labor market is experiencing genuine demand, rather than a survivor squeeze.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 4.90%, placing it in the 30th percentile, which indicates a below-average labor market tightness. This means that while there is some slack in the market, it may still pose challenges for businesses trying to hire, particularly in certain sectors. The practical implication is that businesses may face moderate competition for talent, but not to the extent seen in tighter labor markets.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is +7.50%, ranking in the 94th percentile, which is significantly above the national average. This fast wage growth rate implies rising labor costs for employers, but also strong worker purchasing power. As a result, businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, but can also expect a skilled and affluent local consumer base.

Cost of Living

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has a cost of living ratio of $587/sqft to $44.28/hr, placing it in the 12th percentile for affordability, indicating that the city is relatively expensive. The PSF has fallen by -3.5% year-over-year, but this has not been enough to improve the city's affordability ranking. This means that businesses may need to offer wage premiums to attract talent, as the high cost of living could be a deterrent for some workers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has contracted by -2.06% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to attract talent from other areas or invest in training and development programs.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has fallen by -36.38% year-over-year, ranking in the 2nd percentile, which signals a significant tightening of the housing supply. This sharp decline in building permits suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of housing is not keeping pace with demand.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 46 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -6.1%, placing it in the 4th percentile. This indicates a highly competitive market, where homes are selling quickly. For a worker relocating to this city, this means that the housing market is challenging, with limited time to make a decision on a property.

Office Economy

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has an office economy percentile rank of 52, indicating a near median depth of professional talent pool. This suggests that the city is suited for a variety of businesses, but may not have the same level of specialization as other metros. The city is likely well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors, but may not be the best fit for industries requiring a highly specialized or unique talent pool.

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area offers businesses a highly skilled and affluent workforce, with strong wage growth and a competitive labor market. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's relatively high cost of living and tightening housing supply, which may require businesses to offer wage premiums and adapt their recruitment strategies to attract and retain talent.
