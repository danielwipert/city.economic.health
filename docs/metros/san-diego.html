Economic Analysis

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an overall grade of C with a composite score ranking it 41.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its high wage growth rate of 7.50% year-over-year and its low cost of living affordability score, ranking it in the 14th percentile. The combination of these two metrics suggests a city with rising labor costs but a challenging environment for attracting talent due to high costs.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 0.70% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating 0.152% above the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours are increasing, indicating a growing economy. However, the near-median percentile rank of 52nd suggests that this growth is not exceptionally strong compared to other metros.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 4.70%, ranking it in the 42nd percentile, which indicates a near-median level of slack in the labor market. This rate suggests that the market is not overly tight, but it is also not extremely slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire. The practical implication is that businesses may face some competition for talent, but it is still possible to find qualified workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 7.50%, ranking it in the 94th percentile, indicating fast and rising labor costs. This implies that employers will face increasing costs, but workers will have strong purchasing power, which can be beneficial for businesses relying on local consumer demand. The high wage growth rate suggests that businesses may need to adjust their compensation packages to remain competitive.

Cost of Living

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has a cost of living score ranking it in the 14th percentile, with a PSF of $594/sqft and average hourly earnings of $44.28/hr, resulting in a ratio of 13.41. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The -2.8% year-over-year change in PSF suggests that housing costs are decreasing, but the city's overall affordability remains low.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is contracting at a rate of -1.75% year-over-year, ranking it in the 12th percentile. This indicates that the labor force supply is shrinking, which can create a structural headwind for hiring and may limit the city's ability to support business growth. The implication is that businesses may face increasing competition for a decreasing pool of workers.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is -36.38%, ranking it in the 2nd percentile. This suggests that housing supply is tightening, which can lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for workforce accommodation. The sharp decline in building permits signals a potential supply squeeze, making it essential for businesses to consider the long-term implications for their workforce.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 45 days, with a 0.0% year-over-year change, ranking it in the 28th percentile. This indicates a moderately competitive market, neither extremely fast nor slow. For a worker relocating to this city, the market is relatively accessible, but the lack of change in days on market suggests a stable, rather than improving, environment.

Office Economy

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has an office economy score ranking it in the 52nd percentile, with a share of professional and office workers indicating a moderately deep talent pool. This city is best suited for businesses that can leverage its existing professional workforce, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may not be ideal for industries requiring a large, unskilled labor force.

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area offers businesses a unique combination of high wage growth and a challenging cost of living environment. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's shrinking labor force supply, which can limit business growth and create competition for talent. This factor should be carefully considered when evaluating the city as a potential location for business operations.
