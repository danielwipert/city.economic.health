Economic Analysis

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 44.5th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 6.72%, and its high cost of living, with a PSF to wages ratio of 13.69. The combination of these two metrics suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to attract and retain talent.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 0.51% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.482% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The labor demand composite score of 4.53 further reinforces this assessment, ranking below average at the 36th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 4.40%, ranking near the median at the 58th percentile. This suggests that the market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter labor markets. However, the unemployment rate is not low enough to indicate a significant surplus of available workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 6.72%, ranking at the 92nd percentile. This indicates fast wage growth, which will lead to rising labor costs for employers. On the other hand, workers in the city will benefit from increased purchasing power, making it an attractive location for talent.

Cost of Living

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has a cost of living percentile rank of 14, indicating that it is an expensive city relative to its peers. The PSF to wages ratio of 13.69, with PSF at $600/sqft and wages at $43.84/hr, further reinforces this assessment. This means that businesses may need to offer wage premiums to attract talent, as the high cost of living may deter some workers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is contracting at a rate of -1.30% year-over-year. This indicates that the labor force supply is shrinking, which will create a structural headwind for hiring in the city. Businesses may need to consider this constraint when making location decisions.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is -23.29%, ranking at the 12th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 45 days, with a year-over-year increase of 2.3%. This indicates a slightly slower market, which may make it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, the market is still relatively competitive, and businesses should consider this when making location decisions.

Office Economy

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has a professional and office worker share of 2.92, ranking above average at the 60th percentile. This indicates a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. However, the city may be less suited for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area offers businesses a strong and growing economy with a highly skilled workforce, but the high cost of living and tightening housing supply pose significant risks. The single biggest constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the need to offer wage premiums to attract and retain talent in this expensive city.
