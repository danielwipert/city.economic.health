Economic Analysis

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score of 50.3th percentile, ranking it below average among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 80th percentile, and its high wage growth, with a top-tier rate of 7.80% year-over-year. These metrics signal a city with a robust job market and rising labor costs.

Labor Demand

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an employment growth rate of 1.28% year-over-year and a weekly hours deviation of 0.481% above its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 6.57 further reinforces this assessment, placing the city in the top tier.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 4.70%, which is below average, ranking in the 24th percentile. This indicates a relatively tight labor market with some slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire. As a result, companies may face some wage pressure when trying to attract and retain talent in this market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 7.80%, which is a top-tier rate, indicating fast wage growth. This rapid increase in wages suggests that labor costs for employers will rise, while workers will enjoy greater purchasing power. The high wage growth rate also implies a competitive labor market where businesses must offer attractive compensation packages to attract and retain employees.

Cost of Living

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has a cost of living score that ranks in the 12th percentile, indicating that the city is relatively expensive. With a price per square foot of $583 and average hourly earnings of $43.46, the ratio of 13.41 is high, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that the price per square foot is falling by 3.6% year-over-year slightly mitigates this issue but does not significantly alter the city's overall affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is shrinking at a rate of -0.76% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor supply. This decline suggests that the city's workforce is not expanding, which could pose a structural headwind for hiring and business growth. Companies may need to consider strategies to attract talent from other areas or invest in workforce development programs.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has decreased by 16.72% year-over-year, signaling a tightening of the housing supply. This decline in permits suggests that the city's future affordability and ability to accommodate a growing workforce may be at risk, potentially exacerbating existing labor market challenges.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 48 days, with a year-over-year increase of 2.1%. This indicates a moderately competitive market that is becoming slightly less heated. For workers relocating to the city, this means that the housing market is still relatively accessible, but the trend suggests it may become more challenging to find a home in the near future.

Office Economy

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has an above-average share of professional and office workers, ranking in the 60th percentile. This suggests a deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. However, the city may be less suitable for industries that require a large workforce in industrial or logistics roles.

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area offers businesses a strong labor demand and high wage growth, indicating a robust and competitive economy. However, the city's relatively high cost of living and shrinking labor force supply pose significant risks that decision-makers must consider when evaluating this location for their business operations. The biggest constraint for businesses in this city will be managing labor costs and attracting talent in a tight and expensive market.
