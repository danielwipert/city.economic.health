Economic Analysis

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 37.5th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score defined largely by its low labor demand and high wage growth. The labor demand composite score of 4.53, combining a +0.51% employment growth rate and a -0.482% weekly hours deviation, signals a contraction in genuine demand expansion. This, coupled with a +6.72% year-over-year wage growth rate, suggests a complex economic environment.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +0.51% and weekly hours deviation of -0.482% indicate a lack of genuine demand expansion, as hours are not running above trend during job growth. This combination suggests a mild contraction in labor demand, rather than a survivor squeeze. The labor demand composite score of 4.53 is below average, ranking at the 36th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 4.70% places the market near the median, ranking at the 42nd percentile. This rate suggests a moderate level of slack in the labor market, making it neither extremely tight nor overly loose for businesses trying to hire. However, the practical implication is that hiring may not be as competitive as in markets with lower unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +6.72% is exceptionally high, ranking at the 92nd percentile. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers and increased purchasing power for workers. As a result, businesses may face higher costs, but workers will have more disposable income, potentially benefiting local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $594/sqft to $43.84/hr, or 13.55, and a -2.8% year-over-year change in PSF, the city ranks at the 12th percentile in terms of affordability. This low percentile rank indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which could hinder talent attraction without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force has contracted by -1.75% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This decline in labor force growth signals a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses may face challenges in finding and recruiting talent in this environment.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -37.09%, indicating a sharp decline in housing supply expansion. This significant decrease suggests a potential supply squeeze building ahead, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 45 days, with a 0% year-over-year change. This stable, relatively moderate days on market suggests a balanced market that is neither extremely competitive nor overly accessible for relocating workers. However, the lack of change may indicate a normalization of the market rather than a significant shift.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers is 2.92, ranking at the 60th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy roles but may be less ideal for those with primarily industrial or logistics-dominant operations.

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area offers businesses a unique combination of high wage growth and a deep professional talent pool, but the single biggest risk or constraint is the city's relatively high cost of living and shrinking labor force, which could hinder talent attraction and hiring capacity. Decision-makers should carefully weigh these factors when considering this location for their business operations. Overall, the city's economic character is complex, with both opportunities and challenges that need to be carefully navigated.
