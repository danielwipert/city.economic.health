Economic Analysis

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 38.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score defined largely by its high wage growth and low affordability. The city's economic character is most notably marked by a 94th percentile wage growth rate of +7.50% year-over-year and a cost of living ratio of $587/sqft to $44.28/hr, ranking in the 12th percentile for affordability. These metrics suggest a challenging environment for businesses looking to manage labor costs while attracting talent.

Labor Demand

The San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad metro area has an employment growth rate of +0.70% year-over-year and weekly hours deviation of +0.152% from its own trend, combining for a labor demand composite score in the 52nd percentile. This signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit at a moderate pace. The combination of job growth and hours above trend indicates that the labor market is experiencing genuine demand, rather than a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad stands at 4.90%, ranking in the 30th percentile, indicating a market with some slack but not overly loose. This rate suggests that while it may not be extremely difficult to hire, there is still competition for talent, and businesses may face some wage pressure. However, the market is not as tight as in other metros, providing a relatively more accessible hiring environment.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of +7.50%, San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad ranks in the 94th percentile, indicating fast and significant wage increases. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers but also strong purchasing power for workers. Businesses should anticipate higher compensation packages to attract and retain talent in this market.

Cost of Living

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has a cost of living ratio of $587/sqft to $44.28/hr, ranking in the 12th percentile for affordability, making it one of the more expensive cities relative to peers. The PSF has decreased by -3.5% year-over-year, but the city remains expensive. This low affordability score means that attracting talent without offering significant wage premiums will be challenging, as the high cost of living may deter potential employees.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has contracted by -2.06% year-over-year, ranking in the 22nd percentile. This negative growth rate indicates that the labor force supply is shrinking, posing a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion. Companies looking to grow their workforce may face challenges in finding sufficient talent.

Building Permits

Residential building permits in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad have decreased by -36.38% year-over-year, ranking in the 2nd percentile. This sharp decline signals a tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The decrease in building permits suggests that developer confidence is low, potentially exacerbating housing supply constraints.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad is 46 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -6.1%, ranking in the 4th percentile. This fast-paced market indicates that homes are selling quickly, making it competitive and potentially challenging for relocating workers to find housing. The low days on market suggests a hot housing market, which could be a barrier for talent relocation.

Office Economy

San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad has an office and professional worker share ranked in the 53rd percentile, indicating a moderately deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. However, it is less suited for industries requiring large industrial or logistics workforces. The city's professional talent pool is average compared to its peers, suggesting that businesses in knowledge-economy sectors may find sufficient talent, but those in other sectors may face more challenges.

In conclusion, San Diego-Chula Vista-Carlsbad offers a high-wage growth environment that can attract highly skilled talent, but it also presents significant challenges, including a very high cost of living and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk for businesses considering this location is the affordability and housing supply constraints, which could limit their ability to attract and retain talent without offering substantial compensation packages.
