Economic Analysis

The city of Jacksonville has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 31.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand composite score of 3.86, ranking it in the 20th percentile, and its high unemployment rate of 4.80%, ranking it in the 14th percentile. These metrics signal a challenging labor market with limited job growth and high unemployment.

Labor Demand

Jacksonville's employment growth rate is a modest +0.06% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.520% from its own trend. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The low labor demand composite score of 3.86 further reinforces this signal.

Unemployment

The city's unemployment rate stands at 4.80%, ranking it in the 14th percentile, indicating a labor market with significant slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in Jacksonville may find it easier to staff, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The high unemployment rate suggests that the market is not tight, giving employers more flexibility in hiring.

Wage Growth

Jacksonville's year-over-year wage growth rate is +1.34%, ranking it in the 14th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth means that employer labor costs are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power is also not increasing significantly. The low wage growth rate may make it challenging for businesses to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $213/sqft vs $37.45/hr, Jacksonville ranks in the 47th percentile, indicating a near-median affordability score. The city's PSF is falling -1.4% year-over-year, making it more affordable relative to wages. This means that Jacksonville has a talent attraction advantage, as workers can afford to live in the city without requiring significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The city's civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -0.56% year-over-year, ranking it in the 56th percentile. This means that the labor force supply is shrinking, creating a structural headwind for hiring. Businesses may face challenges in finding qualified workers as the labor pool contracts.

Building Permits

Jacksonville's residential building permits are increasing at a rate of +8.24% year-over-year, ranking it in the 54th percentile. This signals that housing supply is expanding, which may improve affordability and workforce accommodation in the future. The increase in building permits suggests that developers are confident in the city's growth prospects.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Jacksonville is 66 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -12.0%, ranking it in the 68th percentile. This indicates a relatively fast market, making it challenging for relocating workers to find homes. The low days on market suggests that the city's housing market is competitive.

Office Economy

Jacksonville's professional and office worker share ranks in the 58th percentile, indicating a near-median depth of talent pool. The city is suited for businesses that do not require a highly specialized knowledge-economy workforce, such as industrial or logistics-dominant companies. However, it may not be the best fit for tech, finance, or consulting firms that require a deep talent pool.

The city of Jacksonville offers businesses a relatively affordable cost of living and a large labor market with significant slack, making it easier to hire. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses is the city's low labor demand and stagnant wage growth, which may limit the availability of skilled workers and create challenges for talent attraction and retention. Overall, Jacksonville's economic character presents both opportunities and challenges for businesses considering location decisions.
