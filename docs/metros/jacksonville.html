Economic Analysis

The city of Jacksonville has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 31.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand composite score of 3.86, ranking it in the 20th percentile, and its high unemployment rate of 4.80%, ranking it in the 14th percentile. These metrics signal a challenging labor market with limited job growth and high unemployment.

Labor Demand

Jacksonville's employment growth rate is a modest +0.06% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.520% from its own 12-month trend. This combination signals a labor market with limited genuine demand expansion, as the slight job growth is not accompanied by an increase in working hours. Instead, it may indicate a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The city's unemployment rate stands at 4.80%, ranking it in the 14th percentile, indicating a labor market with significant slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in Jacksonville may find it relatively easier to staff, but may also face weaker local consumer demand due to the high number of unemployed individuals.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Jacksonville is +1.34% year-over-year, ranking it in the 14th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth means that employer labor costs are not rising rapidly, but it also translates to weak bargaining power for workers and limited growth in purchasing power.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $213/sqft to $37.45/hr, Jacksonville ranks in the 47th percentile in terms of affordability. This near-median ranking means that the city is neither extremely affordable nor expensive, but its cost of living is relatively stable. The fact that PSF is falling -1.4% year-over-year is a positive sign for affordability, making it easier for businesses to attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Jacksonville is contracting at a rate of -0.56% year-over-year, ranking it in the 56th percentile. This decline in labor force supply means that businesses may face structural headwinds when trying to hire, as the pool of available workers is shrinking.

Building Permits

Residential building permits in Jacksonville are increasing by +8.24% year-over-year, ranking it in the 54th percentile. This expansion in housing supply is a positive sign for future affordability and workforce accommodation, as it indicates that developers are confident in the city's growth prospects.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Jacksonville is 66 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -12.0%, ranking it in the 67th percentile. This relatively fast pace of home sales means that workers relocating to the city may face a competitive market, making it challenging to find and secure housing.

Office Economy

Jacksonville's professional and office worker share ranks in the 58th percentile, indicating a moderately deep talent pool. This makes the city suitable for businesses that require a mix of professional and non-professional roles, but it may not be the best fit for companies that require a highly specialized or knowledge-intensive workforce.

In conclusion, Jacksonville offers businesses a labor market with some challenges, including high unemployment and slow wage growth, but also some opportunities, such as a relatively stable cost of living and expanding housing supply. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering locating in Jacksonville is the shrinking labor force, which may limit their ability to hire and grow in the long term.
