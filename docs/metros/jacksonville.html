Economic Analysis

Jacksonville has an overall grade of C-, with a score of 30.7 out of 100, indicating it is struggling compared to other metros, with employment growth of only 0.30% over the past year. The city's economy is most defined by its slow job growth and low wage increases, with hourly earnings rising by just 0.97% over the past year.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

Jacksonville is adding jobs, but at a slow pace of 0.30% over the past year, and workers' hours are actually falling by 0.5%, indicating a weak labor market. This means that while there are some new jobs being created, the overall demand for labor is not strong, and employers may not be looking to hire as many new workers.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Jacksonville is 4.50%, which is higher than many other metros, making it relatively easier for employers to find workers. However, this also means that there may be less upward pressure on wages, as workers may have fewer job opportunities to choose from.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Hourly earnings in Jacksonville rose by just 0.97% over the past year, which is a very slow pace of wage growth, meaning that workers' paychecks are not increasing much. This slow wage growth also means that employers may not face significant pressure to raise wages, but it can also make it harder for workers to afford the cost of living in the city.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Jacksonville is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $212 and hourly pay of $36.45, although housing prices are falling by 1.4% over the past year, which may make it slightly more affordable. However, with a score of 33 out of 100, the city's cost of living is still less affordable than many other metros, which can make it harder to attract and retain workers.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in Jacksonville is shrinking by 0.12% over the past year, which means that the pool of available workers is actually getting smaller. This can make it harder for employers to find the workers they need, and may lead to a more competitive job market in the future.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in Jacksonville fell by 10.81% over the past year, which signals that the future housing supply may be limited, and could lead to higher housing prices and rents. This decline in building permits is a concern, as it may make it harder for workers to find affordable housing in the city.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Jacksonville are taking 66 days to sell, which is getting slower by 13.2% over the past year, but with a score of 78 out of 100, the housing market is still relatively healthy. This slower pace of home sales can give buyers more time to find a home, but it can also be a sign that the market is softening, and may lead to more homes being available for sale.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

Jacksonville has a relatively average-sized office-based workforce, with a score of 56 out of 100, which means it has a moderate number of jobs in tech, finance, and professional services. This makes the city a decent fit for businesses that need a mix of office and non-office workers, but it may not be the best fit for companies that require a large pool of highly specialized office workers.

Jacksonville's biggest strength is its relatively healthy housing market, with homes taking a reasonable amount of time to sell, but its biggest risk is its slow job growth and low wage increases, which can make it harder to attract and retain workers. Overall, the city's economy is facing significant challenges, and will need to see improvements in job growth and wage increases to become more competitive with other metros.
