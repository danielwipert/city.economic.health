Economic Analysis

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking at the 49.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.78%, and its extremely competitive housing market, with a median days on market of 37 days and a year-over-year decrease of 9.8%. The combination of these metrics suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to attract and retain talent.

Labor Demand

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has an employment growth rate of 0.56% and a weekly hours deviation of 0.469% above its own trend, indicating a near median labor demand composite score of 5.55. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, with jobs being added and hours running above trend. However, the relatively modest employment growth rate suggests that this demand expansion is not overly robust.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area is 4.60%, ranking at the 42nd percentile, indicating a near median level of unemployment. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, but is not overly loose, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire. The practical implication is that businesses may need to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area is 5.78%, ranking at the 84th percentile, indicating fast wage growth. This suggests that employer labor costs are rising rapidly, while worker purchasing power is increasing. As a result, businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, but can also expect a highly productive and skilled workforce.

Cost of Living

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has a cost of living ratio of $627/sqft to $50.39/hr, with a year-over-year decrease of 4.3% in PSF, ranking at the 16th percentile. This suggests that the city is relatively expensive, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The low affordability score is a significant constraint for businesses looking to relocate or expand in this area.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area is contracting at a rate of -2.31% year-over-year, ranking at the 16th percentile. This indicates that the labor force supply is shrinking, creating a structural headwind for hiring. Businesses may need to consider alternative locations or strategies to attract and retain talent.

Building Permits

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has seen a year-over-year increase of 118.02% in building permits, ranking at the 98th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is expanding rapidly, which may improve affordability and workforce accommodation in the future. However, the current housing market remains highly competitive, and it may take time for the increased supply to have a significant impact.

Days on Market

The median days on market in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area is 37 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 9.8%. This suggests that the housing market is highly competitive, making it challenging for relocating workers to find affordable housing. The low days on market also indicates a seller's market, which may drive up housing costs and reduce affordability.

Office Economy

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has a professional and office worker share of 2.36, ranking at the 46th percentile. This suggests that the city has a moderately deep talent pool, making it suitable for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors. However, the city may not be as well-suited for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area offers businesses a highly skilled and productive workforce, with strong wage growth and a rapidly expanding housing supply. However, the city's extremely high cost of living and competitive housing market pose significant challenges for talent attraction and retention, making it essential for businesses to carefully consider these factors when making location decisions. Ultimately, the biggest risk for businesses in this area is the potential for rising labor costs and reduced affordability, which could impact their bottom line and competitiveness.
