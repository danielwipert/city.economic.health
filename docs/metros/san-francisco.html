Economic Analysis

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 47.3th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.99%, and its low labor force growth, with a year-over-year decline of -1.59%. These metrics signal a tight labor market with rising labor costs.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 0.22% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.149% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a labor demand contraction, as hours are not running above trend despite modest job growth. This suggests that the labor market is not experiencing genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 4.20%, ranking it at the 64th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the low unemployment rate puts upward pressure on wages. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 5.99%, ranking it at the 86th percentile, indicating fast wage growth. This implies that labor costs for employers are rising, while worker purchasing power is increasing. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs when considering this location.

Cost of Living

San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont has a cost of living score ranking it at the 20th percentile, with a PSF of $629/sqft and average hourly earnings of $49.82, resulting in a ratio of 12.63. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive, which may make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is falling -4.8% year-over-year is a positive sign, but the overall affordability score remains low.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is declining -1.59% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor pool. This signals a structural headwind for hiring, as the shrinking labor force may limit the availability of skilled workers. Businesses may need to consider this constraint when planning their workforce expansion.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is increasing 32.14% year-over-year, indicating an expansion of housing supply. This suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may improve, as the increased supply of housing may help to moderate prices.

Days on Market

The median days on market in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 39 days, with a year-over-year decline of -13.3%. This indicates a highly competitive market, where homes are selling quickly. As a result, relocating workers may face challenges in finding affordable housing, which could impact their decision to move to this city.

Office Economy

San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont has an office economy score ranking it at the 52nd percentile, with a share of professional and office workers indicating a moderately deep talent pool. This city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors, but may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area offers businesses a highly skilled and productive workforce, with strong wage growth and a deep talent pool. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the low labor force growth, which may limit the availability of skilled workers and drive up labor costs.
