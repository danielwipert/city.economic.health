Economic Analysis

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking 52.3th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong labor demand and low unemployment rate, at 3.60% and 62nd percentile, respectively. The city's economic character is defined by its above-average employment growth of 0.28% and weekly hours deviation of 0.421%, indicating genuine demand expansion. With a wage growth rate of 6.51%, this city also exhibits strong labor cost pressures.

Labor Demand

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has an employment growth rate of 0.28% and a weekly hours deviation of 0.421%, signaling genuine demand expansion as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a real increase in labor demand, rather than just a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 5.11, at the 62nd percentile, further supports this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 3.60%, ranking at the 80th percentile, which means the market is tight with low unemployment. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as well as upward pressure on wages, making it essential to offer competitive compensation packages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate suggests that workers have more bargaining power, which can drive up labor costs.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 6.51%, placing it at the 82nd percentile, which is considered fast. This rapid wage growth implies that employer labor costs are rising quickly, while workers enjoy increased purchasing power. However, for businesses, this means a higher cost environment that needs to be factored into operational budgets and strategic planning.

Cost of Living

San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont has a cost of living percentile rank of 18th, indicating that it is an expensive city relative to its peers, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $649/sqft to $50.06/hr, or 12.96. Although the PSF is falling by 4.1% YoY, the city remains less affordable, which can hinder talent attraction without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living. This suggests that businesses may need to adjust their compensation strategies to attract and retain workers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is contracting at a rate of -1.81% year-over-year, ranking at the 10th percentile, which means the labor force supply is shrinking. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is decreasing, making it more challenging for businesses to find the talent they need. This trend can exacerbate the challenges posed by low unemployment and high wage growth.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is -11.22%, indicating that housing supply is tightening rather than expanding. This decline in building permits signals a potential supply squeeze in the future, which could further exacerbate affordability issues and make it more difficult for the workforce to find accommodation, ultimately affecting the city's attractiveness to businesses and workers alike.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 37 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 7.5%, placing it at the 6th percentile. This fast-paced market means that homes are selling quickly, which can make it competitive and challenging for relocating workers to find housing. The rapid turnover in the housing market underscores the need for businesses to consider the housing situation in their relocation and talent attraction strategies.

Office Economy

San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont has an office economy percentile rank of 60th, indicating a deep talent pool in professional and office sectors. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions that require a knowledgeable and specialized workforce. However, it may be less ideal for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant, which require different skill sets and infrastructures.

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area offers businesses a strong labor demand environment and a deep professional talent pool, but it also presents significant challenges, including a tight labor market, high and rising labor costs, and a highly competitive housing market. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's expensive cost of living and tightening housing supply, which can hinder talent attraction and retention without appropriate strategic adjustments.
