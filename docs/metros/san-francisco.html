Economic Analysis

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 47.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.99%, and its low labor force growth, with a year-over-year decline of -1.59%. These metrics signal a tight labor market with rising labor costs.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 0.22% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.149% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a labor demand contraction, as the small employment growth is accompanied by a decrease in hours worked, indicating a potential survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 4.20%, ranking it at the 64th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure, as the low unemployment rate gives workers more bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 5.99%, ranking it at the 86th percentile, indicating fast wage growth. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increases worker purchasing power, making the area attractive for workers but potentially costly for businesses.

Cost of Living

San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont has a cost of living score ranking it at the 20th percentile, with a PSF of $629/sqft and average hourly earnings of $49.82, resulting in a ratio of 12.63. The fact that PSF is falling -4.8% year-over-year is a key driver of the relatively low affordability score, indicating that the city is expensive relative to peers, which may require wage premiums to attract talent.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is declining -1.59% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor supply. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the shrinking labor pool limits the availability of workers for businesses.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is +19.88%, ranking it at the 80th percentile, indicating an expansion in housing supply. This increase in building permits signals improving affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market in San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont is 39 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -13.3%, ranking it at the 0th percentile. This extremely competitive market means that homes are selling very quickly, making it challenging for relocating workers to find housing, as the market favors sellers over buyers.

Office Economy

San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont has a professional and office worker share ranking it at the 52nd percentile, indicating a near-median depth of professional talent pool. This city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors but may not offer the same advantages for industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area offers businesses a strong, talented workforce with high wage growth, but also presents challenges such as a tight labor market, high cost of living, and extremely competitive housing market. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the low labor force growth, which may limit the availability of workers and drive up labor costs, making it essential to factor in strategies for attracting and retaining talent in this competitive environment.
