Economic Analysis

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 45.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 5.99%, and its extremely competitive housing market, with homes selling at a median of 38 days on market and a year-over-year decrease of 15.6% in days on market. The combination of these metrics suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to attract and retain talent.

Labor Demand

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has an employment growth rate of 0.22% and a weekly hours deviation of -0.149% from its own trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 4.46, which ranks 32nd percentile. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the small employment growth is accompanied by a decrease in weekly hours worked. This suggests that businesses may not face intense competition for workers, but the overall labor market is not expanding rapidly.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area is 4.40%, which ranks 56th percentile, indicating a near-median level of unemployment. This means that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire workers. However, the unemployment rate is not low enough to indicate a highly competitive market, and businesses may still face some challenges in attracting top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area is 5.99%, which ranks 86th percentile, indicating fast wage growth. This means that employer labor costs are rising rapidly, and workers have strong bargaining power. As a result, businesses may need to offer competitive salaries to attract and retain talent, but workers will also have more purchasing power, which can benefit local businesses.

Cost of Living

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has a cost of living score that ranks 18th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $619/sqft to $49.82/hr, resulting in a ratio of 12.42. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that the price per square foot is decreasing by 3.9% year-over-year may provide some relief, but the overall cost of living remains high.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area is contracting at a rate of -1.71% year-over-year, which ranks 16th percentile. This means that the labor force supply is shrinking, creating a structural headwind for hiring. Businesses may need to consider alternative locations or strategies to attract talent, as the local labor pool is not expanding.

Building Permits

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has seen a year-over-year increase of 32.14% in building permits, which ranks 82nd percentile. This indicates that housing supply is expanding, which may improve affordability and workforce accommodation in the future. However, the current competitive housing market may still pose challenges for relocating workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market in the San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area is 38 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 15.6%. This ranks 0th percentile, indicating an extremely competitive market. For workers relocating to this city, the housing market is highly competitive, making it challenging to find and secure a home.

Office Economy

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area has a professional and office worker share of 2.58, which ranks 52nd percentile. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool, making the city suitable for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors. However, the city may not be as well-suited for industries that require a large workforce or have limited specialized roles.

The San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth and a highly competitive housing market. However, the biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the challenging labor market, with a shrinking labor force and extremely competitive housing market, which may require businesses to offer wage premiums to attract and retain talent.
