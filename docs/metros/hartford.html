Economic Analysis

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 72.9th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is largely defined by its exceptionally low unemployment rate of 2.70% and its strong labor demand composite score of 6.34, which ranks in the 84th percentile. The combination of a high employment growth rate and low unemployment suggests a highly competitive job market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +1.88% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.593% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as jobs are being added and hours are running above trend, indicating a strong and growing labor market. The labor demand composite score of 6.34 further reinforces this assessment, ranking in the top tier at the 84th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 2.70%, which is extremely low and indicates a very tight labor market. This means that businesses may face significant challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is limited, and wage pressure is likely to be high. With an unemployment rate ranking in the 98th percentile, this market has very little slack.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +4.73%, which is above average and ranks in the 62nd percentile. This moderate to fast wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but worker purchasing power is also increasing. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs, but they can also expect a more affluent local consumer base.

Cost of Living

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has a cost of living score that ranks in the 35th percentile, with a PSF of $253/sqft and average hourly earnings of $39.99/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.33. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which may make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is increasing by 4.1% year-over-year further exacerbates this issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is growing at a rate of +2.77% year-over-year, which is a very positive sign for businesses looking to hire. This expanding labor force supply suggests that the city has a strong pipeline of potential workers, making it easier for companies to find the talent they need. With a labor force growth rate ranking in the 96th percentile, this city is well-positioned for future hiring needs.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is increasing by +18.05% year-over-year, which is a strong indicator of developer confidence and a growing housing supply. This expansion of housing stock is likely to improve affordability and make the city more attractive to relocating workers. With a building permits growth rate ranking in the 78th percentile, the city is poised for future growth and development.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 33 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.9%. This suggests a highly competitive and fast-paced housing market, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing. The low days on market ranking in the 4th percentile indicates a very hot market, which can be a constraint for businesses looking to attract talent from outside the area.

Office Economy

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has a professional and office worker share that ranks in the 40th percentile, with a composite score of 2.14. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool, making the city suitable for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors. However, it may not be the best fit for companies requiring a large pool of industrial or logistics workers.

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area offers businesses a highly competitive and growing labor market, with a strong pipeline of potential workers and a moderate to fast pace of wage growth. However, the city's low unemployment rate and relatively high cost of living may pose significant challenges for businesses looking to hire and attract talent, making it essential for decision-makers to carefully consider these factors when evaluating this location.
