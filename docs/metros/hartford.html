Economic Analysis

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 70.4th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is largely defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a composite score in the 88th percentile, and its extremely low unemployment rate of 2.70%, ranking in the 98th percentile. The combination of these two metrics signals a highly competitive job market with strong demand for labor.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +1.88% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by -0.321%, still indicating a strong labor demand. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs and hours are running above trend, albeit slightly below the baseline. This suggests that businesses are hiring and workers are in high demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 2.70%, ranking in the 98th percentile, indicating an extremely tight labor market. This means that there is very little slack in the market, making it challenging for businesses to hire new employees. As a result, businesses may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +5.21%, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating moderate to fast wage growth. This implies that labor costs for employers are rising, but worker purchasing power is also increasing. As a result, businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, but they can also expect a more affluent local consumer base.

Cost of Living

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has a cost of living percentile rank of 29th, indicating that it is relatively expensive compared to its peers, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $251/sqft to $39.67/hr, or 6.33. This means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering competitive salaries to attract and retain top talent.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is growing at a rate of +2.77% year-over-year, ranking in the 96th percentile, indicating a rapidly expanding labor force supply. This means that the city has a strong pipeline of potential employees, making it easier for businesses to hire and expand their operations.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has decreased by -7.56% year-over-year, ranking in the 38th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This signals that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact of a tightening housing market on their ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 36 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -5.3%, ranking in the 4th percentile. This indicates a highly competitive market where homes are selling quickly, making it challenging for relocating workers to find affordable housing. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering relocation assistance or other incentives to help attract talent to the area.

Office Economy

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has an office economy percentile rank of 34th, indicating a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This means that the city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep pool of specialized office workers, such as industrial or logistics companies. However, it may be less suitable for businesses that require a large number of professional or office workers, such as tech or finance companies.

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area offers businesses a highly competitive labor market with strong demand for labor, a growing labor force, and moderate to fast wage growth. However, the city's high cost of living and tightening housing supply may pose significant challenges for businesses looking to attract and retain top talent, making it essential for decision-makers to carefully consider these factors when evaluating the city as a potential location.
