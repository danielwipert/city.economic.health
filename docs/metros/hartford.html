Economic Analysis

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking it at the 67.1th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 86th percentile, and its extremely low unemployment rate of 2.70%, ranking it at the 98th percentile. The combination of these two metrics suggests a highly competitive job market with genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +1.88% year-over-year, accompanied by a +0.025% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, indicating that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a sign of a thriving economy. The labor demand composite score of 6.96 further reinforces this trend, ranking it at the 86th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 2.70%, which is extremely low and ranks it at the 98th percentile. This suggests a very tight labor market with little slack, making it challenging for businesses to hire new employees. As a result, businesses may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +5.30%, which is above average and ranks it at the 74th percentile. This indicates that wages are rising at a moderate to fast pace, which may lead to increased labor costs for employers. However, it also suggests that workers have strong bargaining power and increasing purchasing power, which can be beneficial for local businesses.

Cost of Living

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has a cost of living ratio of $250/sqft to $40.19/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.22, which ranks it at the 24th percentile in terms of affordability. This means that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which may make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is rising by 3.7% year-over-year further exacerbates this issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is growing at a rate of +2.77% year-over-year, which is a top-tier growth rate ranking it at the 96th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, providing businesses with a growing pool of potential employees to hire from. This trend is likely to continue, making it easier for companies to find the talent they need.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has decreased by -26.73% year-over-year, which is a significant decline ranking it at the 10th percentile. This suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which may lead to increased competition for available housing and upward pressure on prices. This trend may negatively impact future affordability and workforce accommodation in the area.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is currently 35 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.8%. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, although the decrease in days on market suggests that it may be slowing down slightly. For workers relocating to this city, the market may still be competitive, but the slight slowdown could make it more accessible.

Office Economy

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has a professional and office worker share of 1.90, ranking it at the 26th percentile. This suggests that the city has a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors, making it less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies. However, it may be more suitable for industries that are less dependent on specialized office skills.

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area offers businesses a highly competitive job market with strong labor demand and a growing workforce. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the relatively expensive cost of living, which may require wage premiums to attract talent, and the tightening housing supply, which may impact future affordability and workforce accommodation.
