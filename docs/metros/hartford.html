Economic Analysis

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking it at the 67.1th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 86th percentile, and its extremely low unemployment rate of 2.70%, ranking it at the 98th percentile. The combination of these two metrics suggests a highly competitive job market with genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +1.88% year-over-year, combined with a +0.025% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, signaling genuine demand expansion. This indicates that the city is experiencing an increase in jobs and hours worked, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand or relocate. The top-tier composite score of 86th percentile further reinforces this trend.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 2.70%, ranking it at the 98th percentile, indicating an extremely tight labor market. This means that there is very little slack in the market, making it challenging for businesses to hire new employees. As a result, businesses may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +5.30%, ranking it at the 74th percentile, indicating moderate to fast wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers may be rising, but workers will also have increased purchasing power. While this may be a challenge for businesses, it also indicates a strong and growing local economy.

Cost of Living

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has a cost of living score ranking it at the 24th percentile, indicating that it is relatively expensive compared to its peers. The city's PSF of $250/sqft and average hourly earnings of $40.19/hr result in a ratio of 6.22, which is below average. This means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is growing at a rate of +2.77% year-over-year, ranking it at the 96th percentile, indicating a rapidly expanding workforce supply. This is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests that there will be a growing pool of potential employees to draw from.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has decreased by -26.73% year-over-year, ranking it at the 10th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This suggests that the city may face future affordability and workforce accommodation challenges, which could impact its ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 35 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.8%, ranking it at the 12th percentile. This indicates a relatively fast-paced and competitive housing market, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find affordable housing.

Office Economy

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has an office economy score ranking it at the 26th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This suggests that the city may be better suited for businesses that are not heavily reliant on office or professional workers, such as industrial or logistics companies.

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area offers businesses a highly competitive job market with strong labor demand and low unemployment, but also presents challenges such as a tight labor market and relatively high cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering this location is the tightening housing supply, which could impact affordability and workforce accommodation in the future.
