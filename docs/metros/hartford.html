Economic Analysis

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 67.7th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and low unemployment rate being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, at 86th and 98th percentiles, respectively. The labor demand composite score of 6.96, driven by a +1.88% employment growth rate and +0.025% weekly hours deviation from trend, signals a genuine demand expansion. This combination of metrics suggests a city with a robust job market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +1.88% and weekly hours deviation of +0.025% from trend indicate a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 6.96, at the 86th percentile, further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 2.70% is extremely low, ranking in the 98th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is limited, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate also suggests that workers have strong bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +5.30% is above average, ranking in the 74th percentile, indicating moderate to fast wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but workers are also experiencing increased purchasing power. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs, but can also benefit from a more affluent local consumer base.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Hartford, with a PSF of $251/sqft and average hourly earnings of $40.19/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.25, ranks in the 33rd percentile, indicating that the city is relatively expensive. However, the fact that the PSF is increasing by 3.3% YoY suggests that affordability may continue to decline. This could make it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of +2.77% year-over-year, ranking in the 96th percentile, indicating a rapidly expanding workforce supply. This suggests that businesses will have access to a growing pool of potential workers, making it easier to hire and staff their operations. The strong labor force growth also bodes well for the city's long-term economic prospects.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -26.73%, ranking in the 10th percentile, indicating a sharp decline in housing supply. This suggests that the city may face a tightening housing market, which could lead to increased competition for housing and higher costs for workers. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact on their employees' affordability and quality of life.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 36 days, with a year-over-year decline of -5.3%, ranking in the 4th percentile, indicating a very competitive housing market. This suggests that workers relocating to the city may face challenges in finding and securing housing, as homes are selling quickly. The fast-paced market may require businesses to provide relocation assistance or other incentives to attract talent.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in the city is 1.90, ranking in the 26th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city may be better suited for businesses in industries such as manufacturing or logistics, rather than tech, finance, or consulting. However, the city's strong labor force growth and low unemployment rate may still make it an attractive location for businesses looking to establish a presence in the region.

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area offers businesses a strong labor market, low unemployment, and a growing workforce, making it an attractive location for companies looking to expand or establish a presence in the region. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's relatively expensive cost of living and tightening housing market, which may require businesses to offer wage premiums or relocation assistance to attract and retain talent.
