Economic Analysis

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking in the 69.7th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its exceptionally low unemployment rate of 2.70% and its strong labor demand composite score of 6.61, which ranks in the 88th percentile. The combination of a high employment growth rate and low unemployment suggests a highly competitive job market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +1.88% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.321% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are not excessively above trend despite strong job growth, indicating that the labor market is absorbing new jobs without overburdening existing workers. The labor demand composite score of 6.61 reinforces this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 2.70%, ranking in the 98th percentile, which means the job market is extremely tight. This tight market implies that businesses may face significant challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is limited, and wage pressure is likely to be high. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is +5.21%, ranking in the 72nd percentile. This moderate to fast wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, while worker purchasing power is increasing. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs, but they can also benefit from a more affluent local consumer base.

Cost of Living

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has a cost of living ratio of $253/sqft to $39.67/hr, with a percentile rank of 22, indicating that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. The PSF is rising 4.1% year-over-year, which further exacerbates the affordability issue. This means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is growing at a rate of +2.77% year-over-year, ranking in the 96th percentile. This rapid labor force growth indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire. The increasing labor pool provides a structural tailwind for hiring and talent acquisition.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has decreased by -7.56% year-over-year, ranking in the 38th percentile. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact on their employees' ability to find affordable housing.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 33 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.9%. This fast-paced market means that homes are selling quickly, making it challenging for relocating workers to find available housing. The low days on market percentile rank of 4 indicates a highly competitive housing market, which may require businesses to provide relocation assistance or other incentives to attract talent.

Office Economy

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has an office economy percentile rank of 34, indicating a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics companies. However, it may not be the ideal location for tech, finance, or consulting firms that rely on a specialized workforce.

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area offers businesses a highly competitive job market with strong labor demand and low unemployment. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the relatively expensive cost of living, which may require wage premiums to attract and retain talent. Despite this challenge, the city's expanding labor force and growing economy make it an attractive location for businesses looking to establish or expand their operations.
