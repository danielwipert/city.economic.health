Economic Analysis

The Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 70.4th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is largely defined by its exceptional labor demand, with a composite score in the top tier at 88th percentile, and its extremely low unemployment rate of 2.70%, ranking it in the top tier at 98th percentile. The combination of these two metrics signals a highly competitive job market with strong demand for labor.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 1.88% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by -0.321%, still indicating a top-tier labor demand composite score of 88th percentile. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs while hours worked are slightly below trend, suggesting that the growth is not solely due to existing workers taking on more hours. This is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand or establish operations in the area.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 2.70%, which is extremely low and ranks in the top tier at 98th percentile. This indicates a very tight labor market with little slack, making it challenging for businesses to hire new employees. As a result, businesses may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain top talent in this competitive job market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is 5.21%, which is above average and ranks in the 72nd percentile. This moderate to fast wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but it also means that workers have increasing purchasing power, which can be beneficial for local businesses that rely on consumer spending.

Cost of Living

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has a cost of living ratio of $251 per square foot to $39.67 per hour, resulting in a below-average affordability score of 29th percentile. This means that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which could make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. However, the fact that the PSF is rising by 3.3% year-over-year while wages are also growing may help to mitigate some of the affordability concerns.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is growing at a rate of 2.77% year-over-year, which is in the top tier at 96th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, providing a positive environment for businesses looking to hire and grow their operations. The increasing labor force can help to alleviate some of the pressure on wages and provide a more sustainable hiring environment.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has decreased by 7.56% year-over-year, ranking in the 38th percentile. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply may be tightening, which could lead to future affordability concerns and make it more challenging for workers to relocate to the area.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford is currently 36 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 5.3%. This rapid pace of home sales indicates a highly competitive and fast-paced market, making it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing. The low days on market percentile rank of 4th percentile suggests that the market is extremely hot, and buyers may need to act quickly to secure a property.

Office Economy

Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford has a professional and office worker share of 2.08, ranking in the 34th percentile. This suggests that the city has a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors, making it less suited for businesses that rely heavily on tech, finance, or consulting expertise. However, the city may be more attractive to businesses in other industries, such as logistics or manufacturing, that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool.

In conclusion, Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford offers businesses a highly competitive labor market with strong demand and low unemployment, but also presents challenges such as a tight labor market and rising labor costs. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses in this area is the potential for affordability concerns and housing supply constraints, which could impact their ability to attract and retain top talent.
