Economic Analysis

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking in the 28.2th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its weak labor demand and stagnant wage growth. The labor demand composite score of 2.73, combining a -0.59% employment growth rate and a +0.742% weekly hours deviation, signals a contraction in labor demand. This is further exacerbated by the low wage growth rate, which is among the lowest in the country.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -0.59% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating +0.742% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion or survivor squeeze. The low labor demand composite score of 2.73, ranking in the 2nd percentile, indicates a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand their workforce.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 3.40%, ranking in the 64th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. The tight labor market also puts upward pressure on wages, which could increase labor costs for employers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is +0.53%, ranking in the 6th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth means that labor costs for employers are not increasing rapidly, but it also implies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. The low wage growth rate may make it difficult for businesses to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a cost of living score ranking in the 10th percentile, with a PSF of $164/sqft and an average hourly earnings of $33.83/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.85. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive, which could make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The 5.1% year-over-year increase in PSF further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is contracting at a rate of -1.13% year-over-year, ranking in the 28th percentile. This decline in labor force supply means that businesses may face structural headwinds in hiring, as the pool of available workers is shrinking. The implications for hiring capacity are negative, making it more challenging for businesses to find the talent they need.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is increasing at a rate of +10.41% year-over-year, ranking in the 62nd percentile. This expansion in housing supply is a positive signal for future affordability and workforce accommodation, as it may help to alleviate some of the pressure on the housing market. However, the current cost of living issues may still pose a challenge.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 55 days, with a year-over-year increase of +10.0%, ranking in the 66th percentile. This indicates a relatively slow market, which may be more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, the rising days on market may also signal a normalization of the housing market, rather than a decline in demand.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 0.92, ranking in the 16th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics companies, and less suited for tech, finance, or consulting firms that rely on specialized office workers.

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area offers a relatively low-cost business environment, but the weak labor demand, stagnant wage growth, and expensive cost of living pose significant challenges for businesses looking to expand or relocate. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the limited availability of skilled workers, which may require businesses to offer higher wages or invest in talent development programs to attract and retain top talent.
