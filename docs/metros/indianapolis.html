Economic Analysis

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it at the 32.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its tight unemployment market, with a rate of 3.30% ranking it in the top tier. The combination of these metrics, particularly the low employment growth rate of -1.05% and the low wage growth rate of +0.58%, suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -1.05% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.471%. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion, as hours are slightly above trend despite job losses. This scenario may indicate a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 3.30%, ranking it in the top tier with an 88th percentile score. This tight market implies that there is little slack in the labor market, making it harder for businesses to hire and potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. For a business trying to hire in this city, this means that they may need to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is +0.58%, which is relatively stagnant. This slow wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also means that worker purchasing power is not increasing significantly. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but they may also not see a significant boost in consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a cost of living score in the bottom tier, with a 10th percentile rank, indicating that it is relatively expensive. The city's PSF is $165/sqft, with a ratio of 4.87 to average hourly earnings of $33.86, and is increasing by 4.4% year-over-year. This means that the city is less attractive for talent without wage premiums, as the high cost of living may offset any potential benefits of relocating to the area.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is contracting at a rate of -1.87% year-over-year. This decline in labor force supply implies that the city's hiring capacity is shrinking, making it a structural headwind for businesses looking to expand or relocate. As a result, businesses may need to invest in training and development programs to attract and retain top talent.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is decreasing by 3.35% year-over-year. This decline in permits suggests that the city's housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and make it more difficult for workers to relocate to the area. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering relocation assistance or other benefits to attract top talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 53 days, with a year-over-year increase of 12.8%. This relatively slow market, with a 76th percentile rank, means that homes are sitting on the market for longer periods, making it more accessible for workers to relocate to the area. However, this may also indicate a normalization of the market, rather than a sign of weak demand.

Office Economy

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a relatively shallow professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking it in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This means that the city is less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies, but may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area offers businesses a relatively tight labor market with low unemployment, but also presents challenges such as low labor demand, slow wage growth, and a relatively high cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker to factor in is the city's shrinking labor force supply, which may make it difficult to attract and retain top talent, and the tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability issues.
