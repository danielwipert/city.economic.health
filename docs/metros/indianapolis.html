Economic Analysis

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking in the 33.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 2.36, indicating a struggling job market. The two metrics that most define this city's current economic character are its negative employment growth rate of -1.05% and low wage growth of +0.58%. These numbers suggest a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -1.05%, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.471%, signaling a contraction in labor demand rather than genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 2.36, ranking in the 6th percentile, further emphasizes the weakness in the job market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 3.40%, ranking in the 88th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with low unemployment. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city will face significant competition for talent, leading to potential wage pressure. With such a low unemployment rate, companies may need to offer higher salaries to attract and retain employees.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is +0.58%, ranking in the 8th percentile, which is stagnant compared to other metros. This slow wage growth means that labor costs for employers will remain relatively flat, but workers will have weak bargaining power. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but workers may not have the purchasing power to drive local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a cost of living score ranking in the 12th percentile, with a PSF of $165/sqft and an earnings ratio of $33.86/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.87. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The 4.4% year-over-year increase in PSF further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is contracting at a rate of -0.99% year-over-year, ranking in the 28th percentile. This decline in labor force supply means that businesses may face structural headwinds when trying to hire, as the pool of available workers is shrinking. As a result, companies may need to invest in training and development programs to attract and retain talent.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has decreased by -3.15% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 48th percentile. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact on their employees' ability to find affordable housing.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 51 days, with an 8.5% year-over-year increase, ranking in the 80th percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are taking longer to sell, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, this trend may also signal a weakening demand for housing, which could have implications for the local economy.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is relatively low, ranking in the 14th percentile. This suggests that the city's talent pool is not as deep in specialized roles, making it less suited for tech, finance, or consulting businesses. However, the city may be more attractive to industries with less specialized labor requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood offers a relatively low-cost business environment, but its struggling job market, slow wage growth, and tightening housing supply pose significant risks for businesses looking to expand or relocate. The single biggest constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited labor force growth and shallow talent pool, which may hinder a company's ability to attract and retain skilled workers.
