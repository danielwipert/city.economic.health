Economic Analysis

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 31.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its tight unemployment market, with a rate of 3.40% ranking it in the top tier. The labor demand and unemployment metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to hire and expand.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -1.17% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.446%. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a decline in job creation and hours worked. The positive deviation in weekly hours suggests a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 3.40%, ranking it in the top tier with an 88th percentile score. This tight labor market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as there are fewer available workers, and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate also puts upward pressure on wages, making it a competitive market for employers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is +0.09%, ranking it in the bottom tier. This stagnant wage growth signals that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also means that workers have weak bargaining power and limited purchasing power. The slow wage growth may make it difficult for businesses to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a cost of living score in the bottom tier, with a 10th percentile rank, indicating that it is relatively expensive compared to its peers. The city's PSF is $165/sqft, with a ratio of 4.93 to average hourly earnings of $33.45/hr, and is increasing by 4.4% year-over-year. This makes it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is contracting at a rate of -0.99% year-over-year, ranking it below average with a 28th percentile score. This decline in labor force supply means that businesses may face structural headwinds in hiring, as the pool of available workers is shrinking. The contracting labor force may limit the city's ability to support business growth and expansion.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is decreasing by 3.15% year-over-year, ranking it near the median with a 48th percentile score. This decline in building permits signals that the housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability challenges and limitations on workforce accommodation. The decrease in permits may make it difficult for relocating workers to find affordable housing.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 51 days, with an 8.5% year-over-year increase, ranking it in the top tier with an 80th percentile score. This slower market means that workers relocating to the city may have an easier time finding a home, as properties are sitting on the market for longer periods. The increasing days on market may signal a normalization of the housing market, making it more accessible to buyers.

Office Economy

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a relatively shallow professional talent pool, ranking in the bottom tier with a 14th percentile score. The city's office economy is not well-suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, it may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area offers businesses a relatively low-cost environment, but its tight labor market and stagnant wage growth may pose challenges for hiring and expansion. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand and contracting labor force, which may limit the city's ability to support business growth and attract top talent.
