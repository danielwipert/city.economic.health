Economic Analysis

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 36.3th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score largely defined by its low labor demand and stagnant wage growth, specifically a -1.33% employment growth rate and -0.71% year-over-year wage growth. The combination of these metrics signals a challenging economic environment. The city's labor market is characterized by a low unemployment rate of 3.00%, but this is paired with negative employment growth and stagnant wages.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -1.33%, indicating a contraction in the number of jobs available, while the weekly hours deviation from the 12-month baseline is +0.298%, suggesting that remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, indicative of a survivor squeeze. This combination signals a lack of genuine demand expansion in the labor market. The labor demand composite score of 2.14 falls in the bottom tier, at the 4th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 3.00%, which is relatively low and indicates a tight labor market. This tight market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as there are fewer available workers, and may experience upward pressure on wages. The 92nd percentile rank for unemployment suggests that the market has very little slack.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -0.71%, indicating stagnant or even declining wages. This slow wage growth environment means that labor costs for employers are not increasing, but it also signifies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. The bottom-tier percentile rank for wage growth, at 0th percentile, underscores the challenges in this aspect of the labor market.

Cost of Living

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a cost of living score that places it in the bottom tier, at the 10th percentile, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $167/sqft to $32.76/hr, or 5.10. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, especially since PSF is rising by 5.0% year-over-year. The high cost of living, relative to wages, may hinder talent attraction without offering wage premiums to offset the expense.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is growing at a rate of -0.25% year-over-year, indicating a slight contraction in the labor force supply. This contraction suggests that the hiring capacity may be structurally constrained, making it challenging for businesses to find and recruit new talent. The near-median percentile rank of 54th percentile for labor force growth, however, suggests that this constraint is not as severe as in other metrics.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -0.22%, indicating a slight tightening in the housing supply. This decrease in building permits may signal future affordability challenges and potential difficulties in accommodating a growing workforce, as the supply of new housing is not expanding to meet demand. The near-median percentile rank of 52nd percentile for building permits suggests a moderate level of developer confidence.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 45 days, with a year-over-year increase of 9.8%. This increase, coupled with the top-tier percentile rank of 90th percentile, indicates a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold. For workers relocating to this city, this could mean a more accessible and less competitive housing market.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is relatively low, placing it in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile. This suggests that the city has a less deep talent pool in these areas, making it less suited for businesses that require a strong presence of tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations. The city may be more suited for industries that are less dependent on professional and office workers.

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area presents a mixed bag for businesses, with a tight labor market and low unemployment rate being offset by stagnant wage growth, a high cost of living, and a contracting labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker considering this location is the challenging labor market dynamics, which could hinder hiring and talent attraction efforts. Overall, businesses must carefully weigh these factors when deciding whether to locate in this metro area.
