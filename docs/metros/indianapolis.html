Economic Analysis

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it at the 32.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier at the 6th percentile, and its tight unemployment market, with a rate of 3.30% ranking it in the top tier at the 88th percentile. Specifically, the employment growth rate of -1.05% and weekly hours deviation of +0.471% signal a contraction in labor demand.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -1.05%, indicating a decline in job additions, while the weekly hours deviation is +0.471%, suggesting that remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, characteristic of a survivor squeeze. This combination signals a lack of genuine demand expansion in the labor market. The labor demand composite score of 2.36 ranks in the bottom tier at the 6th percentile, further emphasizing the weakness in labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 3.30%, ranking it in the top tier at the 88th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with low unemployment. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure due to the limited availability of workers. The low unemployment rate suggests that the local labor market has slack, but it is relatively minimal.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is +0.58%, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth implies a flat cost environment for employers but weak bargaining power for workers. The moderate wage growth rate suggests that worker purchasing power is not increasing significantly.

Cost of Living

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a cost of living ratio of $165/sqft to $33.86/hr, with a ratio of 4.87, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, indicating that the city is relatively expensive. The PSF is increasing by 4.4% year-over-year, further contributing to the high cost of living. This expense means that the city may require wage premiums to attract talent, as its affordability is lower than many of its peers.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is contracting at a rate of -1.87% year-over-year, ranking it below average at the 26th percentile. This decline in the labor force supply implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is shrinking. The negative growth rate suggests that businesses may face challenges in finding qualified workers.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is decreasing by 3.35% year-over-year, ranking it below average at the 38th percentile. This decline in building permits signals a potential tightening of the housing supply, which could lead to decreased affordability and make it more difficult for workers to relocate to the area. The decrease in permits suggests that developer confidence may be waning.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 53 days, with a year-over-year increase of 12.8%, ranking it above average at the 76th percentile. This increase in days on market suggests a slower, more buyer-friendly market, which could make it easier for relocating workers to find housing. However, the relatively high days on market may also indicate a decrease in demand.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is relatively low, with a composite score ranking it in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This suggests that the city may not have a deep talent pool for tech, finance, or consulting businesses, but could be more suited for industrial or logistics-dominant economies. The low ranking indicates that businesses requiring specialized office skills may need to look elsewhere.

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area offers a business a tight labor market with low unemployment, but this also means that hiring may be challenging due to wage pressure. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the low labor demand and contracting labor force, which could limit the availability of qualified workers and hinder business growth. Overall, the city's economic character is defined by its low labor demand and tight labor market, making it essential for businesses to carefully consider these factors when making location decisions.
