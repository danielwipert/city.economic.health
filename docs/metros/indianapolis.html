Economic Analysis

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it at the 41.5th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its tight unemployment market, with a rate of 3.00% ranking it in the top tier. The combination of these two metrics, particularly the low labor demand and stagnant wage growth, signals a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -1.06% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.398%. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion, as hours are not significantly above trend to indicate a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 2.42 ranks in the bottom tier, indicating a weak job market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 3.00%, ranking it in the top tier with a percentile score of 92. This tight labor market means that there is little slack, making it harder for businesses to hire and potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. For a business trying to hire in this market, it may be necessary to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is +0.36%, ranking it in the bottom tier with a percentile score of 6. This stagnant wage growth indicates that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also means that worker purchasing power is not increasing significantly. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but workers may not have the disposable income to drive local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a cost of living score ranking it in the bottom tier, with a percentile score of 8, indicating that it is relatively expensive. The PSF to earnings ratio is $167/sqft vs $32.82/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.09, and PSF is rising by 5.0% year-over-year. This means that the city is less affordable than many of its peers, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is growing at a rate of -0.25% year-over-year, ranking it near the median with a percentile score of 54. This indicates that the labor force supply is contracting, rather than expanding, which may create a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion. As a result, businesses may need to invest in training and development programs to attract and retain top talent.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is increasing by 19.68% year-over-year, ranking it in the top tier with a percentile score of 82. This expansion in housing supply signals that developer confidence is strong, and future affordability and workforce accommodation may improve. As a result, the city may become more attractive to relocating workers and businesses looking to expand.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 45 days, with a year-over-year increase of 9.8%, ranking it in the top tier with a percentile score of 90. This indicates a slower market, where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, this may also signal a normalization of the market, rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood ranks below average, with a percentile score of 20. This indicates that the city has a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors, making it less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech or finance companies. However, the city may be more suitable for businesses with industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area offers businesses a relatively low-cost environment, with a growing housing supply, but it also presents challenges, such as a tight labor market and stagnant wage growth. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the low labor demand, which may limit the city's ability to support business expansion and growth. As a result, businesses should carefully consider these factors when evaluating Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood as a potential location for relocation or expansion.
