Economic Analysis

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 34th out of 50 US metros, with a composite score largely defined by its low labor demand and stagnant wage growth, specifically a -1.33% employment growth rate and -0.71% year-over-year wage growth. The city's economic character is also marked by a tight labor market, with an unemployment rate of 3.40%, and a relatively expensive cost of living, with a PSF to earnings ratio of 5.07. These metrics suggest a challenging environment for businesses looking to hire and expand.

Labor Demand

The city's labor demand is characterized by a -1.33% employment growth rate and a +0.298% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, indicating a contraction in labor demand. This combination signals a lack of genuine demand expansion, with hours slightly above trend during a period of job losses, suggesting a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This metric ranks in the bottom tier, at the 4th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 3.40%, ranking in the top tier at the 86th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city will face significant competition for talent, leading to potential wage pressure and hiring challenges. The low unemployment rate suggests that workers have strong bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in this city is -0.71%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 0th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power is also not increasing. The stagnant wage growth may make it difficult for businesses to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is relatively expensive, with a PSF to earnings ratio of 5.07 and a percentile rank of 8, indicating that the city is less affordable compared to its peers. The PSF is $166/sqft, which has increased by 4.4% year-over-year, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums. This may put the city at a disadvantage in terms of talent attraction.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in this city is growing at a rate of +0.22% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 50th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is slowly expanding, providing a modest increase in hiring capacity for businesses. However, the slow growth rate may not be sufficient to meet the demands of rapidly expanding companies.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has decreased by -0.22% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 52nd percentile. This suggests that housing supply is not expanding rapidly, which may lead to future affordability and workforce accommodation challenges. The slow growth in building permits may indicate a tightening housing market.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in this city is 47 days, with a year-over-year increase of 4.4%, ranking in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. The rising days on market may suggest a normalization of the housing market.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is relatively low, ranking in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile. This suggests that the city has a less deep talent pool for tech, finance, and consulting businesses, making it less suited for companies that require specialized office workers. The city may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area offers businesses a tight labor market with low unemployment, but also presents challenges such as stagnant wage growth, a relatively expensive cost of living, and a slow expansion of the labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential difficulty in attracting and retaining top talent due to the city's low affordability and stagnant wage growth, which may require businesses to offer wage premiums to compete in the market.
