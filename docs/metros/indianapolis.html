Economic Analysis

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area has an overall grade of C-, ranking 32.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low labor demand and stagnant wage growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 2.36, combining a -1.05% employment growth rate and a +0.471% weekly hours deviation, signals a contraction in labor demand. This, coupled with a low wage growth rate of +0.58%, defines the city's current economic character.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is -1.05% year-over-year, while weekly hours are running +0.471% above the city's own 12-month trend. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion or survivor squeeze, indicating that the city is not adding jobs at a pace that would suggest strong labor market health. The labor demand composite score of 2.36 falls in the bottom tier, at the 6th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is 3.40%, which ranks in the top tier at the 88th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with low unemployment. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city may face challenges in finding available workers, leading to potential wage pressure. The low unemployment rate suggests that the labor market has slack, but not as much as other cities.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is +0.58%, which is relatively stagnant and falls in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile. This slow wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are not rising quickly, but it also means that workers have weak bargaining power and limited purchasing power growth. As a result, businesses may not face significant labor cost increases, but workers may not have the disposable income to drive local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood has a cost of living score that ranks in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile, with a PSF of $165/sqft and average hourly earnings of $33.86/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.87. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which could make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is rising +4.4% year-over-year relative to wages further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is shrinking at a rate of -0.99% year-over-year, which falls below average at the 28th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply implies that the hiring capacity for businesses in this city may be structurally constrained, making it harder to find and recruit new workers. The declining labor force growth rate is a significant headwind for businesses looking to expand their workforce.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is decreasing at a rate of -3.35% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 38th percentile. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The reduction in permits may signal a supply squeeze building ahead, potentially limiting the city's ability to attract and retain workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood is currently 51 days, with a year-over-year increase of +8.5%, ranking in the top tier at the 80th percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are sitting longer before being sold, which could make it more accessible for workers relocating to this city. However, in the context of a strong job market, a rising days on market could signal a healthy normalization, but given the weak job market, it may indicate demand erosion.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood ranks in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these areas. This city is less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce requirements.

The Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood metro area offers businesses a relatively low-wage and low-unemployment environment, but its slow labor force growth, tightening housing supply, and expensive cost of living relative to peers pose significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited labor market dynamism, characterized by low labor demand and stagnant wage growth, which may hinder businesses' ability to attract, retain, and grow their workforce.
