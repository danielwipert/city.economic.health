Economic Analysis

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 36.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand, with a composite score in the 68th percentile, and its high unemployment rate, which stands at 4.60% and ranks in the 12th percentile. These two metrics suggest a complex labor market with both positive and negative trends.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is +0.10% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating +1.564% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth, indicating that the labor market is experiencing an increase in workload without relying on survivor squeeze. This is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand or establish operations in the area.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 4.60%, ranking in the 12th percentile, which indicates a relatively loose labor market with some slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city may have an easier time finding candidates, but may also face weaker local consumer demand due to the higher unemployment rate. As a result, companies may need to consider the potential impact on their customer base and revenue streams.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is +2.15%, which ranks in the 28th percentile and indicates moderate wage growth. This means that employer labor costs are rising, but at a slower pace than in many other cities, and worker purchasing power is increasing, but not as quickly as in other areas. As a result, businesses may need to balance their labor costs with the need to attract and retain top talent in a competitive market.

Cost of Living

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a cost of living score in the 60th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $279/sqft to $43.51/hr, or 6.41. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, with a falling PSF value (-3.1% YoY) contributing to its high affordability score. This makes it an attractive location for talent, as workers can afford a higher quality of life without requiring significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is shrinking at a rate of -2.99% year-over-year, which ranks in the 6th percentile and indicates a contracting labor pool. This is a significant structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may struggle to find qualified candidates in a shrinking labor market. As a result, companies may need to consider investing in training and development programs to attract and retain top talent.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is declining at a rate of -19.62% year-over-year, which ranks in the 16th percentile and indicates a tightening housing supply. This signals that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as a lack of new housing developments could drive up prices and make it harder for workers to relocate to the area.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is currently 58 days, with a year-over-year decline of -3.3%. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, although the decline in days on market is slowing. For workers relocating to this city, the market is still relatively competitive, but the slowing pace of sales may make it slightly more accessible for buyers.

Office Economy

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking in the 67th percentile. This makes it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. However, it may be less suited for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area offers businesses a complex labor market with both opportunities and challenges, including a relatively affordable cost of living and a deep professional talent pool. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor force, which could make it difficult for companies to find and retain qualified candidates, and may require significant investments in training and development programs to attract and retain top talent.
