Economic Analysis

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area has an overall grade of C-, with a score of 34.7 out of 100, indicating it is struggling compared to other major US cities. One of the key challenges facing the city's economy is its labor market, where employment growth is only 0.25% over the past year, and unemployment is at 4.30%, making it harder for employers to find workers.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs, but at a slow pace of 0.25% over the past year, and workers are putting in more hours, with a 1.2% increase in weekly hours, indicating a moderate level of labor demand. This means that while there are some job opportunities available, the market is not particularly strong, with a score of 64 out of 100, indicating it is better than about 64% of the 50 metros.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 4.30% is relatively high, making it harder for employers to hire, as there are fewer workers available, and the city's score of 14 out of 100 indicates it is worse than most of the 50 metros. This high unemployment rate also means that employers may have to offer higher wages to attract workers, which can increase their costs.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages in the city have risen by 1.91% over the past year, which is a relatively slow pace, with a score of 22 out of 100, indicating it is worse than about 78% of the 50 metros. This slow wage growth means that workers may not have as much purchasing power, and employers may not face as much pressure to increase wages.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $277 and an hourly pay of $43.06, but it is getting slightly more affordable, with a 3.5% decrease in price per square foot over the past year, and a score of 57 out of 100. This means that while housing is still relatively expensive, it is becoming more affordable for local workers compared to other metros.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in the city is shrinking at a rate of 2.37% over the past year, which is a significant decline, with a score of 4 out of 100, indicating it is worse than almost all of the 50 metros. This shrinking labor pool makes it even harder for employers to find workers, and can lead to a shortage of skilled workers in the city.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

The number of new home building permits in the city has decreased by 18.55% over the past year, which is a significant drop, with a score of 16 out of 100, indicating it is worse than about 84% of the 50 metros. This decrease in building permits signals that the city's housing supply may not be keeping up with demand, which can lead to higher housing costs and make it harder for workers to afford homes.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in the city are taking an average of 60 days to sell, which is a relatively slow pace, and this time is decreasing by 4.8% over the past year, with a score of 30 out of 100, indicating it is worse than about 70% of the 50 metros. This slow pace of home sales indicates a softening housing market, which can make it harder for workers to relocate to the city.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a relatively large office-based workforce, with a score of 74 out of 100, indicating it is better than about 74% of the 50 metros, making it a good location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This large office-based workforce provides a deep pool of skilled workers for businesses to draw from, making it an attractive location for companies in these sectors.

The biggest strength of the Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area is its office economy, with a large and skilled workforce, making it a good location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. However, the city's biggest risk is its shrinking labor force and slow employment growth, which can make it harder for employers to find workers and lead to a shortage of skilled workers in the city.
