Economic Analysis

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 45.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, with a composite score of 5.42, and its below-average wage growth, at +1.94% year-over-year. The labor demand is driven by employment growth of +0.31% and weekly hours deviation of +0.687% from its own trend.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +0.31% and hours deviation of +0.687% signal genuine demand expansion, as both metrics are above average. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing an increase in jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.42 further supports this, ranking it above average at the 62nd percentile.

Unemployment

The actual unemployment rate is 3.90%, which is above average, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as there are fewer unemployed workers available. The practical implication is that companies may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent in this market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate is +1.94%, which is below average, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth means that employer labor costs are not increasing rapidly, but it also implies that worker purchasing power is not growing strongly. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but workers may not have as much disposable income to spend.

Cost of Living

The city has a cost of living percentile rank of 67th, indicating that it is more affordable relative to its peers. The PSF of $284/sqft is decreasing by -3.1% year-over-year, and the ratio of PSF to hourly earnings of $42.59/hr is 6.67. This affordability advantage means that businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The year-over-year growth rate of the civilian labor force is -2.15%, indicating that the labor force is contracting. This means that the supply of workers is shrinking, which can create a structural headwind for hiring. Businesses may need to consider this constraint when planning their workforce expansion.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -17.89%, indicating that housing supply is tightening. This decline in permits suggests that the future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 51 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -1.9%. This relatively fast market means that homes are selling quickly, which can make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the decrease in days on market suggests that the market may be normalizing slightly.

Office Economy

The city has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy percentile rank of 60th. This makes it well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. However, it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area offers businesses a relatively tight labor market with above-average labor demand, but also presents challenges such as stagnant wage growth and a contracting labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor force, which can limit hiring capacity and create structural headwinds for workforce expansion.
