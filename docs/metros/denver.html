Economic Analysis

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking 45.8th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, with a composite score of 5.42, and its below-average wage growth, at +1.94% year-over-year. The labor demand and wage growth metrics provide a nuanced view of the city's job market, with the former indicating genuine demand expansion and the latter suggesting moderate labor cost increases.

Labor Demand

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area has an employment growth rate of +0.31% year-over-year and a weekly hours deviation of +0.687% from its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a moderate increase in job creation, with hours worked above the trend line, suggesting that employers are increasing production to meet growing demand. The labor demand composite score of 5.42 ranks in the 62nd percentile, further supporting the notion of genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 3.90%, ranking in the 62nd percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This suggests that there is some slack in the market, but it may still be challenging for businesses to find qualified candidates, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. With an unemployment rate below the national average, businesses may face competition for top talent, making it essential to offer competitive compensation packages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is +1.94%, ranking in the 26th percentile, indicating moderate labor cost increases. This rate of wage growth suggests that employers may face rising labor costs, but at a slower pace than in many other cities. As a result, workers in the area may experience modest increases in purchasing power, but businesses may not face extreme pressure to raise wages to attract and retain talent.

Cost of Living

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a cost of living score ranking in the 67th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $284/sqft to $42.59/hr, equivalent to 6.67. This indicates that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, with a falling PSF value (-3.1% year-over-year) contributing to its high affordability score. As a result, businesses may find it easier to attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset the cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Denver-Aurora-Centennial has contracted by -2.15% year-over-year, ranking in the 6th percentile, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This decline in labor force growth poses a significant challenge for businesses looking to hire, as it may lead to a structural headwind in finding qualified candidates. As a result, companies may need to invest in training and development programs or consider relocating to areas with more abundant labor supplies.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Denver-Aurora-Centennial has decreased by -14.11% year-over-year, ranking in the 26th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This decline in building permits suggests that the city's housing market may become less affordable in the future, potentially making it more difficult for businesses to attract and retain talent. As a result, companies may need to consider the potential long-term implications of a tightening housing market on their workforce accommodation and recruitment strategies.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is currently 51 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -1.9%, ranking in the 18th percentile. This indicates a relatively fast-paced housing market, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find suitable housing. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering relocation assistance or flexible housing options to attract top talent.

Office Economy

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has an office economy score ranking in the 60th percentile, with a share of professional and office workers indicating a deep talent pool. This suggests that the city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which rely on specialized knowledge workers. However, the city may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area offers businesses a unique combination of above-average labor demand and relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for companies looking to expand or relocate. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's shrinking labor force, which may pose significant challenges for hiring and talent acquisition in the long term.
