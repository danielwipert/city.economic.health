Economic Analysis

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking 50.1th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its labor demand and unemployment metrics. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.29, combining a +0.60% employment growth rate and a +0.147% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, suggests a near median performance. The unemployment rate of 3.60% also stands out, indicating a relatively tight labor market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +0.60% and weekly hours deviation of +0.147% from its own trend suggest genuine demand expansion in the Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area. This combination signals that the city is experiencing job growth with hours running above trend, indicating a healthy labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.29, ranking near the median at the 58th percentile, reinforces this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.60% in Denver-Aurora-Centennial indicates a tight labor market, ranking above average at the 76th percentile. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with potential upward pressure on wages. For a company trying to hire in this city, the low unemployment rate means a more competitive hiring environment.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +2.01% in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is below average, ranking at the 28th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace than in many other cities. For workers, this means modest increases in purchasing power, but not as strong as in cities with faster wage growth.

Cost of Living

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a cost of living score that ranks above average at the 65th percentile, with a PSF of $286/sqft and average hourly earnings of $42.34/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.75. The fact that PSF is falling by -3.7% YoY relative to wages makes the city more affordable. This affordability advantage can attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, making it a competitive location for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is contracting at a rate of -2.58% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This decline in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is shrinking. Businesses may need to adapt their hiring strategies or consider relocation to areas with expanding labor forces.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is -14.11%, indicating a tightening of housing supply. This decline in permits suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand. This could lead to increased housing costs and reduced attractiveness for relocating workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 48 days, with a year-over-year increase of +6.7%. This increase, ranking above average at the 74th percentile, indicates a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold. For workers relocating to this city, the slower market could make it easier to find a home, but it also signals a less competitive housing market.

Office Economy

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a professional and office worker share that ranks near the median at the 56th percentile, with a composite score of 2.86. This suggests a moderately deep talent pool suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions, but not as strong as in cities with higher rankings. The city is less suited for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant.

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area offers businesses a relatively tight labor market with moderate wage growth, making it an attractive location for companies seeking to minimize labor costs while still accessing a skilled workforce. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the contracting labor force, which poses significant challenges for future hiring and talent acquisition, potentially outweighing the city's current advantages.
