Economic Analysis

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 34.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand, which is above average with a composite score of 5.89, and its labor force growth, which is contracting at a rate of -2.37% YoY. The combination of these metrics suggests a city with genuine demand expansion, but facing challenges in sustaining its workforce.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is +0.25% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating +1.245% from the city's own 12-month baseline, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that jobs are being added, and hours are running above trend, suggesting a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.89 further supports this, ranking the city in the 64th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 4.30%, ranking it in the 14th percentile, indicating a relatively high level of unemployment. This suggests that the market has some slack, making it easier for businesses to hire, but also potentially weakening local consumer demand. With an unemployment rate above the national average, businesses may find it relatively easier to staff, but may also face weaker demand for their products or services.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is +1.91%, ranking it in the 22nd percentile, indicating below-average wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is not increasing as quickly as in other cities. As a result, businesses may face moderate labor cost pressures, but workers may not have as much bargaining power.

Cost of Living

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a cost of living score ranking it in the 57th percentile, with a PSF of $277/sqft and average hourly earnings of $43.06/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.43. The PSF is also falling -3.5% YoY, making the city relatively more affordable. This suggests that the city has a moderate cost of living, which can be a talent attraction advantage, as businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to compensate for high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is contracting at a rate of -2.37% YoY, ranking it in the 4th percentile, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This suggests that the supply of workers is decreasing, creating a structural headwind for hiring and potentially limiting the city's ability to support business growth. As a result, businesses may face challenges in finding and retaining talent in this city.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is decreasing at a rate of -18.55% YoY, ranking it in the 16th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This suggests that the city's housing market may become less affordable in the future, potentially making it more difficult for workers to relocate and for businesses to attract talent. The decrease in building permits signals a potential supply squeeze, which could impact future workforce accommodation and affordability.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 60 days, with a YoY decrease of -4.8%, ranking it in the 30th percentile. This suggests that the housing market is relatively competitive, but still accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, the decreasing days on market indicates a relatively fast-paced market, which may make it challenging for workers to find housing.

Office Economy

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has an office economy score ranking it in the 74th percentile, with a share of professional and office workers indicating a deep talent pool. This suggests that the city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces. The city's strong office economy makes it an attractive location for businesses seeking specialized talent.

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area offers businesses a unique combination of genuine labor demand expansion and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses in this city is the contracting labor force, which may limit the city's ability to support long-term growth and create challenges in finding and retaining talent.
