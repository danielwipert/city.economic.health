Economic Analysis

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 44.8th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand, with a composite score at the 68th percentile, and its cost of living, which ranks at the 63rd percentile, indicating a relatively affordable environment. The labor demand and cost of living metrics are crucial in understanding the city's economic landscape, with employment growth at +0.10% and weekly hours deviation at +1.564%.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +0.10% and weekly hours deviation of +1.564% signal genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a real increase in labor demand, rather than just a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 5.94 further supports this conclusion, ranking at the 68th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 4.10%, which is near the median, ranking at the 48th percentile. This rate suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire compared to extremely tight markets. However, the relatively low unemployment rate still implies some competition for talent, and businesses may need to offer competitive wages to attract workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate is +2.15%, which is below average, ranking at the 28th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies that labor costs for employers are rising, but not extremely rapidly. For workers, this wage growth rate translates to modest increases in purchasing power, which may not be sufficient to keep pace with cost of living increases in other cities.

Cost of Living

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a cost of living percentile rank of 63, indicating that it is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The city's PSF of $280/sqft, which is decreasing at a rate of -3.5% YoY, combined with an average hourly earnings of $43.51/hr, results in a ratio of 6.44. This affordability advantage can be a significant talent attraction factor, as businesses may not need to offer substantial wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is contracting at a rate of -1.67% year-over-year, ranking at the 18th percentile. This decline in labor force supply implies that the city's hiring capacity may be constrained, making it more challenging for businesses to find and attract talent. The shrinking labor pool can be a structural headwind for companies looking to expand or establish operations in the area.

Building Permits

The city is experiencing a sharp decline in residential building permits, with a year-over-year change of -19.62%, ranking at the 16th percentile. This decrease in permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The decline in building permits is a concern for businesses that rely on a steady supply of housing for their employees.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 57 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -1.7%. This relatively fast market, ranking at the 22nd percentile, implies that homes are selling quickly, which can make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. The fast-paced market may require workers to act rapidly when finding a home, which can be stressful and competitive.

Office Economy

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy percentile rank of 64. This suggests that the city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. However, the city may be less suitable for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant, which require a different type of workforce.

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area offers businesses a relatively affordable environment and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain types of companies. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply, which may limit hiring capacity and create future affordability issues.
