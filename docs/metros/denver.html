Economic Analysis

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 43.5th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, with a composite score of 5.42 ranking it in the 62nd percentile, and its below-average wage growth of 1.94% year-over-year. These metrics suggest a city with a growing job market but relatively stagnant wages.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 0.31% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of 0.687% above its own trend, signaling genuine demand expansion. This indicates that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a strong labor market. The combination of these metrics points to a city with a growing economy and increasing labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 4.10%, ranking it near the median at the 48th percentile. This suggests a relatively balanced labor market with some slack, making it neither extremely tight nor overly loose. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate implies a manageable hiring process without excessive wage pressure.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 1.94%, which is below average, ranking it in the 26th percentile. This slow wage growth suggests a relatively flat cost environment for employers but weak bargaining power for workers. As a result, businesses may benefit from lower labor costs, but workers may have limited purchasing power.

Cost of Living

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a cost of living score ranking it in the 65th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $280/sqft to $42.59/hr, or 6.57. The fact that the PSF is falling by 3.5% year-over-year makes the city more affordable relative to peers. This affordability advantage can attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to hire.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is shrinking at a rate of -1.67% year-over-year, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may face challenges in finding qualified candidates due to the shrinking labor force.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Denver-Aurora-Centennial has decreased by 17.89% year-over-year, ranking it below average at the 20th percentile. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for housing in the future. This could pose a risk to workforce accommodation and attraction.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Denver-Aurora-Centennial is 57 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 1.7%. This relatively fast market, ranking in the 22nd percentile, may make it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing. The competitive housing market could be a drawback for businesses trying to attract talent from other areas.

Office Economy

Denver-Aurora-Centennial has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking it in the 60th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that require a knowledgeable and specialized workforce. However, it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented economies.

The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area offers a growing job market with above-average labor demand, but its slow wage growth and shrinking labor force supply pose significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for a business decision-maker is the tightening housing supply, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for housing, ultimately affecting workforce accommodation and attraction.
