Economic Analysis

The Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking at the 28.0th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 5.30% and its stagnant labor force growth rate of -0.53% YoY. The combination of these metrics suggests a tight labor market with limited supply of new workers.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is +0.17% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating +0.674% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. The labor demand composite score of 5.21 ranks near the median at the 46th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is 5.30%, ranking at the 8th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city will face significant competition for workers and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate also suggests that there is limited slack in the labor market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is +3.11%, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. At the same time, workers in this city are seeing their purchasing power increase, which can have positive effects on local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Chicago-Naperville-Elgin has a cost of living score ranking at the 26th percentile, with a PSF of $221/sqft and average hourly earnings of $37.34/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.92. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. However, the fact that the PSF is rising YoY at +2.8% suggests that the city's affordability is decreasing over time. This may make it more difficult to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is contracting at a rate of -0.53% YoY. This means that the supply of workers is shrinking, which can create a structural headwind for hiring in this city. Businesses may need to consider alternative locations or strategies to attract workers from other areas.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin has decreased by -42.35% YoY, ranking at the 0th percentile. This sharp decline suggests that housing supply is tightening, which can lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for housing in the future. This may make it more difficult for workers to relocate to this city and for businesses to attract talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is 35 days, with a YoY decrease of -2.8%. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, which can make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. The low days on market percentile rank of 12th suggests that the market is highly competitive, and workers may need to act quickly to secure housing.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is 2.22, ranking at the 38th percentile. This suggests that the city has a moderately deep talent pool in these sectors. The city is best suited for businesses that require a mix of professional and office workers, but may not be the best fit for businesses that require a highly specialized or technical workforce.

The Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area offers businesses a tight labor market with moderate wage growth, but also presents significant challenges in terms of labor force growth and housing affordability. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering this location is the limited supply of workers and the decreasing affordability of housing, which can make it difficult to attract and retain talent.
