Economic Analysis

The Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area has an overall grade of D, with a score of 22.5 out of 100, indicating significant economic challenges, particularly in its labor market, where employment growth is weak, with a mere -0.1% change over the past year. This sluggish growth, combined with a high unemployment rate of 4.4%, suggests that the city is struggling to create new job opportunities, with a score of 14 out of 100, indicating weaker hiring than most metros.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is losing jobs, with a -0.1% change in employment growth over the past year, and while workers' hours are rising by 0.7%, this is still a weakness, with a score of 14 out of 100, indicating weaker hiring than most metros. This means that the job market is not strong, and employers may not be confident in expanding their workforce, given the city's overall labor demand score.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 4.4% is a weakness, with a score of 20 out of 100, indicating higher unemployment than most metros, making it relatively easier for employers to hire, but also suggesting weaker local consumer demand. This higher unemployment rate means that there are more people looking for work, which can be beneficial for employers, but also indicates a less robust local economy.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 3.2% over the past year, which is a weakness, with a score of 34 out of 100, indicating slower pay growth than most metros, meaning that employers' labor costs are not rising too quickly, but workers' purchasing power is not increasing rapidly either. This slower wage growth may help keep labor costs in check, but it also means that workers may not have as much disposable income to spend in the local economy.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

Housing in the Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area is relatively expensive, with a price per square foot of $219 and hourly pay of $37.37, and it's getting worse, with a 3.8% increase in price per square foot over the past year, resulting in a score of 20 out of 100, indicating less affordability than most metros. This means that local workers may struggle to afford housing, which can make it harder for employers to attract and retain talent, given the city's cost of living score.

Labor Force Growth

The pool of workers is shrinking, with a -2.0% change over the past year, which is a weakness, with a score of 12 out of 100, indicating slower workforce growth than most metros, making it harder for employers to find new employees. This decline in the labor force can create a structural headwind for hiring, as there are fewer people available to fill job openings.

Building Permits

This relatively stable level of new home construction may not provide enough new housing units to meet demand, which can impact affordability and workforce attraction.

Days on Market

Homes are selling relatively quickly, with a median of 37 days on the market, but this is getting slower, with a -2.6% change over the past year, resulting in a score of 12 out of 100, indicating a weaker housing market than most metros. This slower pace of home sales can make it easier for buyers to find a home, but it also suggests that the housing market may be softening, which can impact the local economy.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is about average, with a score of 42 out of 100, indicating a moderate presence of tech, finance, and professional services jobs, making it suitable for businesses that rely on these sectors. This moderate office economy can provide a stable foundation for businesses that require a skilled workforce, but it may not be as strong as other metros.

The Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area's biggest strength is its relatively stable office economy, which can support businesses that rely on professional services, but its biggest risk is its weak labor market, which can make it harder for employers to find and retain workers, and ultimately impact the local economy's overall growth and competitiveness. Overall, the city's economic challenges, including weak employment growth and high unemployment, suggest that it may struggle to attract new businesses and talent, making it a less competitive location for corporate investment.
