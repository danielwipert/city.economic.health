Economic Analysis

The Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking in the 26.5th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 5.43. This city's economic character is most defined by its high unemployment rate of 5.20% and its negative labor force growth rate of -3.13% YoY, indicating significant challenges in the job market. The combination of these metrics suggests a struggling economy with limited job opportunities.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is +0.22% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating +0.823% from the city's own 12-month baseline, resulting in a labor demand composite score near the median. This combination signals a moderate expansion of genuine demand, as hours are running above trend during a period of slow job growth. However, the overall labor demand remains near the median, indicating a lack of strong momentum.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is 5.20%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This indicates a slack labor market with significant unemployment, making it easier for businesses to hire workers. However, the high unemployment rate also suggests weaker local consumer demand, which could impact businesses reliant on local spending.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is +3.28%, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. Workers in the area will see some improvement in their purchasing power, but the moderate growth rate may not be enough to drive significant economic expansion.

Cost of Living

Chicago-Naperville-Elgin has a cost of living score in the 20th percentile, with a PSF of $220/sqft and average hourly earnings of $37.65/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.84. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive, ranking below average in terms of affordability. The 3.3% YoY increase in PSF further exacerbates the affordability issue, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is contracting at a rate of -3.13% YoY, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction will create a structural headwind for hiring, making it more difficult for businesses to find and retain workers. The declining labor force will also limit the city's ability to support economic growth and expansion.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin has decreased by -15.18% YoY, indicating a tightening of the housing supply. This decline in building permits signals a potential supply squeeze in the future, which could lead to decreased affordability and make it more challenging for workers to relocate to the area.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is 36 days, with a -5.3% YoY change, indicating a relatively fast-paced market. However, the bottom-tier ranking at the 8th percentile suggests that homes are still selling quickly, making it challenging for relocating workers to find affordable housing. The fast-paced market may lead to increased competition for limited housing options.

Office Economy

Chicago-Naperville-Elgin has a professional and office worker share of 2.54, ranking near the median at the 50th percentile. This indicates a moderately deep talent pool, making the city suitable for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors. However, the city may not be as attractive to businesses requiring a highly specialized or knowledge-intensive workforce.

The Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area offers businesses a relatively low-cost labor market with moderate wage growth, but it is constrained by a high unemployment rate, declining labor force, and limited housing supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's struggling economy and limited job opportunities, which may impact businesses' ability to attract and retain talent.
