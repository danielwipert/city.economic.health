Economic Analysis

The Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it at the 28.5th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its high unemployment rate of 4.90% and its stagnant labor force growth of -1.66% YoY. The combination of these metrics suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to hire and expand.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is -0.01% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.300%. This combination signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, rather than genuine demand expansion. The labor demand composite score of 3.99 ranks near the median at the 40th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is 4.90%, ranking it at the 6th percentile, indicating a loose labor market with significant slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city will have an easier time finding candidates, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The low unemployment percentile rank suggests that the city's labor market is not tight.

Wage Growth

The YoY wage growth rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is +3.59%, ranking it near the median at the 46th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that employer labor costs are rising, but at a manageable pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing. However, the wage growth rate is not exceptionally high, which may limit the city's attractiveness to businesses looking for a highly competitive labor market.

Cost of Living

Chicago-Naperville-Elgin has a cost of living score ranking it at the 26th percentile, with a PSF of $221/sqft and an earnings ratio of $36.81/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.00. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which may require businesses to offer wage premiums to attract talent. The fact that PSF is rising by 1.8% YoY relative to wages further exacerbates the affordability challenge.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is contracting at a rate of -1.66% YoY, ranking it at the 12th percentile. This means that the workforce supply is shrinking, creating a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion. The declining labor force growth rate limits the city's ability to support long-term business growth.

Building Permits

The YoY change in residential building permits in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is -1.45%, ranking it near the median at the 44th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability challenges and limitations on workforce accommodation. The decline in building permits signals a potential supply squeeze ahead.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is 33 days, with a YoY direction of +0.0%, ranking it at the 10th percentile. This indicates a relatively slow market, which may make it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, the slow market also suggests that demand for housing may be weakening.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin ranks at the 36th percentile, with a composite score of 2.10. This suggests that the city has a moderately deep talent pool, making it suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors. However, the city may not be as attractive to businesses requiring a highly specialized or industrial workforce.

The Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area offers businesses a relatively loose labor market with moderate wage growth, but its high unemployment rate, stagnant labor force growth, and tightening housing supply pose significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's declining labor force growth rate, which limits its ability to support long-term business expansion and may require businesses to offer wage premiums to attract and retain talent.
