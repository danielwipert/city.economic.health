Economic Analysis

The Chicago-Naperville-Elgin metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it at the 27.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor force growth rate of -2.46% YoY and a significant decline in building permits of -42.35% YoY, indicating structural challenges in expanding its workforce and housing supply. The labor demand composite score of 5.21, ranking near the median at the 46th percentile, suggests a relatively stable but unremarkable job market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is +0.17% YoY, combined with a +0.674% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, signaling a genuine but modest demand expansion. This combination indicates that while jobs are being added, the increase in hours worked suggests that the existing workforce is being utilized more intensely, which could be a sign of a tightening labor market. However, the near-median percentile rank of 46th suggests that this demand is not exceptionally strong compared to other metros.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin stands at 4.90%, ranking below average at the 22nd percentile, which indicates a relatively tight labor market with some slack. For a business trying to hire in this market, the implication is that while it may be somewhat challenging to find talent, it is not as competitive as in markets with even lower unemployment rates. This could mean slightly higher wage pressure to attract and retain employees.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is +3.11%, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile, indicating a moderate pace of wage increases. This rate of wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising but not at an alarming rate, and worker purchasing power is increasing, which can support local consumer demand. However, for businesses, this means anticipating and planning for gradual increases in labor costs.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $221/sqft to $37.34/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.92, and a percentile rank of 22nd, Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is considered below average in terms of affordability. This means the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which could make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the higher cost of living. The 3.8% YoY increase in PSF further exacerbates this issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is contracting at a rate of -2.46% YoY, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing, which could exacerbate talent acquisition challenges for businesses locating or expanding in the area.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -42.35%, signaling a sharp decline in housing supply expansion. This significant decrease suggests that the future affordability of housing and the ability to accommodate a growing workforce are at risk, potentially leading to increased costs for both workers and employers and making the area less attractive for relocation.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Chicago-Naperville-Elgin is 36 days, with a -2.7% YoY change, indicating a relatively fast-paced market that is slightly slowing down. For a worker relocating to this city, the market is still competitive, meaning homes are selling relatively quickly, which could make finding suitable housing challenging and may require acting rapidly when a suitable property is found.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share composite score ranking below average at the 38th percentile, Chicago-Naperville-Elgin has a moderately deep talent pool suited for professional and office sectors. This city is more suited for businesses that can leverage a diverse economy with a notable presence of professional roles, though it may not be as ideal for those requiring a highly specialized tech or finance talent pool.

In conclusion, Chicago-Naperville-Elgin offers a business a relatively stable job market and moderate wage growth, but it comes with significant challenges in labor force expansion and housing supply, which are critical for long-term growth and talent attraction. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the shrinking labor pool and tightening housing market, which could lead to increased labor and housing costs, making it essential to carefully weigh these factors in any location decision.
