Economic Analysis

The Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 44.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand, with a composite score in the 76th percentile, and its labor force growth, which is in the bottom tier at 8th percentile with a -2.93% year-over-year change. The labor demand metrics, including employment growth and weekly hours, signal a genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler is +0.91% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating +0.771% from the city's own 12-month baseline, indicating a genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 6.34 is in the 76th percentile, further supporting this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler is 4.90%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city may face challenges in finding available workers, leading to potential wage pressure. With such a low unemployment rate, companies may need to offer competitive salaries to attract talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler is +3.28%, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively, and worker purchasing power is increasing. While this is not the fastest wage growth, it still indicates a relatively healthy labor market with some upward pressure on costs.

Cost of Living

Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler has a cost of living percentile rank of 55th, indicating a near-median affordability level. The city's PSF (price per square foot) is $264, with a -1.9% year-over-year change, and the ratio of PSF to hourly earnings ($38.11) is 6.93. This affordability level means that the city can attract talent without necessarily requiring significant wage premiums, as the cost of living is relatively manageable compared to other metros.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler is shrinking at a rate of -2.93% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may face challenges in finding the talent they need due to this shrinking labor pool.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler has increased by +10.99% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 56th percentile. This expansion in housing supply suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may improve, as more housing units become available to meet demand.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler is 62 days, with a -4.6% year-over-year change. This indicates a relatively fast-moving market that is becoming slightly more competitive. For workers relocating to this city, the housing market may be somewhat challenging, but it is still accessible compared to hotter markets.

Office Economy

Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler has a deep professional talent pool, ranking in the top tier at the 80th percentile in terms of the share of jobs in professional and office sectors. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that rely on a knowledgeable and skilled workforce. However, it may be less ideal for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented needs.

The Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler metro area offers businesses a labor market with genuine demand expansion and moderate wage growth, but it also presents challenges due to a tight labor market and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the labor force contraction, which could limit the city's ability to support long-term business growth and expansion. Despite this, the city's affordability and deep professional talent pool make it an attractive location for certain types of businesses.
