Economic Analysis

The Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 54.6th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong wage growth and building permits expansion. The city's economic character is defined by its top-tier wage growth of 5.79% year-over-year and a significant increase in building permits of 91.03% year-over-year. These metrics signal a city with a growing economy and increasing housing supply.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue is 0.81% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of -0.048% from its own trend, indicating a near median labor demand composite score of 5.39. This combination suggests a moderate expansion of jobs, but with hours worked slightly below trend, it may not indicate a strong survivor squeeze or genuine demand expansion. The labor market is growing, but not at an accelerated pace.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue is 5.00%, ranking in the 20th percentile, which means the market has more slack than most peers. This implies that businesses may find it easier to hire in this market compared to tighter labor markets, but it also suggests weaker local consumer demand due to the higher unemployment rate.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue is 5.79%, placing it in the top tier of the 84th percentile. This fast wage growth rate implies rising labor costs for employers but also indicates strong worker purchasing power, which can be beneficial for businesses relying on local consumer spending.

Cost of Living

Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue has a cost of living score that ranks in the 39th percentile, with a PSF of $438/sqft and average hourly earnings of $47.42, resulting in a ratio of 9.24. The PSF has decreased by 2.2% year-over-year, making the city less affordable than most of its peers. This suggests that businesses may need to offer wage premiums to attract talent, as the city's affordability is below average.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue has contracted by 0.96% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing, which could challenge businesses looking to expand their workforce in the area.

Building Permits

The city has seen a significant increase in residential building permits of 91.03% year-over-year, signaling an expansion of future housing supply. This increase in building permits is a positive indicator for future affordability and workforce accommodation, suggesting that the city is taking steps to address potential housing shortages and support its growing economy.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue is 44 days, with a year-over-year increase of 10.0%, ranking in the 92nd percentile. This slower market means that homes are sitting longer before being sold, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as they may have more time to find a suitable home and face less competition.

Office Economy

Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking in the 74th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that rely on a knowledgeable and specialized workforce. However, it may be less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

The Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth, expanding housing supply, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's below-average labor force growth, which could pose challenges for hiring and workforce expansion in the long term.
