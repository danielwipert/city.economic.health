Economic Analysis

The Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 57.6th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven significantly by its above-average labor demand and strong wage growth, at 66th and 76th percentiles, respectively. The city's economic character is most defined by its employment growth rate of 0.88% year-over-year and its weekly hours deviation of 0.406% above its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. With a wage growth rate of 5.44% year-over-year, this city presents a unique blend of labor market dynamics.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue is 0.88% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of 0.406% above its own trend, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing an increase in jobs and hours worked, suggesting a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.94 places the city in the 66th percentile, further reinforcing the notion of a thriving labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue stands at 4.80%, which ranks in the 28th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This tightness implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. The low unemployment rate suggests that the city's labor market has less slack, making it more competitive for employers to attract talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue is 5.44%, placing it in the 76th percentile, which signifies fast wage growth. This rapid increase in wages implies rising labor costs for employers but also indicates strong purchasing power for workers. As wages continue to grow at this pace, businesses should anticipate higher labor costs, while workers can expect increased disposable income.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $427/sqft to $48.08/hr, resulting in a ratio of 8.88, Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue ranks in the 45th percentile for affordability. This near-median ranking suggests that the city is neither exceptionally affordable nor overly expensive compared to its peers. The fact that PSF is falling by 2.5% year-over-year relative to wages is a key driver of its affordability score, making it more attractive for talent without necessitating significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue has contracted by 0.54% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses should be aware that the diminishing labor force could exacerbate the challenges of finding suitable talent in the area.

Building Permits

The city has seen a 23.71% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, signaling an expansion in housing supply. This increase suggests that developer confidence is high, and the future housing supply is expected to improve, which could lead to better affordability and easier accommodation for the workforce. The rising permits indicate a positive outlook for future workforce attraction and retention.

Days on Market

Homes in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue currently sit on the market for a median of 51 days, with an 8.5% year-over-year increase, placing it in the 69th percentile. This increase in days on market, combined with the absolute level, suggests a normalization of the housing market, making it slightly more accessible for relocating workers. However, the market remains relatively competitive, and workers may still face challenges in finding and securing housing.

Office Economy

Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue has a deep professional talent pool, ranking in the 74th percentile for its share of office and professional workers. This makes the city highly suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that rely on a knowledgeable and skilled workforce. Conversely, it may be less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles, which require different skill sets.

The Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for knowledge-economy sectors. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tight labor market and the associated upward pressure on wages, which could increase labor costs and require strategic hiring and retention practices to attract and keep top talent.
