Economic Analysis

The Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking at the 52.3th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven significantly by its top-tier wage growth and strong building permits expansion. The city's economic character is most defined by its +5.79% year-over-year wage growth and a 93.96% increase in building permits, signaling a dynamic labor market and expanding housing supply. These metrics suggest a city with a strong economy but also potential labor cost pressures.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue is +0.81% year-over-year, combined with a -0.048% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, indicating a near median labor demand composite score of 5.39. This combination signals a modest expansion in job numbers but with hours worked slightly below trend, suggesting some level of genuine demand but not at an accelerated pace. The labor market is growing, but the slight decrease in hours worked per week may indicate a degree of efficiency or caution among employers.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue stands at 5.00%, placing it in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, which means the labor market is relatively tight. This tightness implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages as companies compete for a limited pool of available workers. The practical implication for a business trying to hire in this market is the need to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of +5.79%, Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue experiences fast wage growth, ranking it in the top tier at the 84th percentile. This rapid wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, which can be a challenge for businesses but is beneficial for worker purchasing power, as employees see their earnings increase. The implication is a trade-off between higher labor costs for employers and increased consumer spending power in the local economy.

Cost of Living

Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue has a cost of living score that places it near the median at the 41st percentile, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $432/sqft to $47.42/hr, equating to a ratio of 9.11. Given that the PSF is decreasing by -1.8% year-over-year, the city is becoming more affordable relative to wages. This affordability, although not the highest, means that the city can attract talent without necessarily requiring significant wage premiums, making it somewhat competitive in the labor market.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue is shrinking at a rate of -0.77% year-over-year, indicating a contraction in the labor force supply. This negative growth rate suggests that the pool of available workers is decreasing, which can pose a structural headwind for hiring and may exacerbate the challenges of a tight labor market. Businesses looking to expand or relocate may find it challenging to find the necessary talent.

Building Permits

The city has seen a significant increase of 93.96% in residential building permits year-over-year, placing it in the top tier at the 96th percentile. This substantial growth in building permits signals that the housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate workforce growth in the future. The rapid expansion in permits is a positive sign for future affordability and the ability to attract and retain workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue is 45 days, with a year-over-year increase of 2.3%. This increase, coupled with the absolute level, suggests a market that is slightly slowing down, becoming more accessible for relocating workers. However, with a percentile rank of 62, the market remains relatively competitive, though the slight increase in days on market may indicate a healthy normalization rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue has a deep professional talent pool, with an office and professional worker share composite score ranking it above average at the 74th percentile. This suggests the city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that rely on a knowledgeable and specialized workforce. Conversely, it may be less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

The Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue metro area offers businesses a dynamic economy with strong wage growth and an expanding housing supply, making it an attractive location for talent. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tight labor market and potential for high labor costs due to rapid wage growth, which could impact operational expenses and require strategic hiring and retention practices.
