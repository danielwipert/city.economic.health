Economic Analysis

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 38.9th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its labor demand and cost of living metrics. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.87, combining a 0.76% employment growth rate and a 0.508% weekly hours deviation above its own trend, suggests genuine demand expansion. Additionally, the cost of living, with a PSF of $340/sqft and an hourly wage of $38.44, resulting in a ratio of 8.84, places the city in the bottom tier for affordability, ranking 19th percentile.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 0.76% and weekly hours deviation of 0.508% above trend indicate genuine demand expansion in the Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area. This combination signals that the city is experiencing an increase in jobs and hours worked, suggesting a strong labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.87, ranking in the 62nd percentile, further supports this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom stands at 5.20%, ranking in the 22nd percentile, indicating a market with some slack. This rate suggests that while it may not be extremely difficult to hire, there is still a notable pool of unemployed individuals seeking work. For businesses, this means there is a moderate level of competition for talent, but not to the extent seen in tighter labor markets.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in the metro area is 2.02%, ranking in the 24th percentile, which is considered below average. This moderate wage growth rate implies that labor costs for employers are increasing, but at a slower pace than in many other cities. Consequently, worker purchasing power is also growing, albeit slowly.

Cost of Living

Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom has a cost of living percentile rank of 19th, indicating it is one of the more expensive cities relative to its peers. With a PSF of $340/sqft and hourly wages of $38.44, the city's high cost of living may deter talent without offering wage premiums, making it challenging for businesses to attract and retain employees without significant compensation adjustments.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area is contracting at a rate of -1.54% year-over-year, ranking in the 38th percentile. This decline in labor force supply signals a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential employees is shrinking. Businesses may face increasing difficulty in finding qualified candidates due to this contraction.

Building Permits

The city has seen a 30.25% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, placing it in the top tier with an 88th percentile rank. This significant rise in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which could lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

Homes in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom are currently selling after a median of 51 days on the market, with a year-over-year decrease of 8.9%. This relatively fast pace, ranking in the 6th percentile, indicates a competitive market where homes sell quickly, potentially making it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office sector jobs in the metro area is 0.76, ranking in the 8th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, and may be more accommodating to industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area offers a mix of genuine labor demand expansion and challenges such as a high cost of living and a contracting labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering this location is the affordability of housing and the potential difficulty in attracting talent without offering significant wage premiums, which could offset the benefits of the city's growing labor demand and expanding housing supply.
