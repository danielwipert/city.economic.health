Economic Analysis

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it at the 27.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand composite score of 4.32, ranking it at the 28th percentile, and its stagnant wage growth of +1.05% year-over-year, ranking it at the 14th percentile. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is +0.34% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.457% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the small increase in employment is accompanied by a decrease in hours worked. This suggests that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 5.00%, ranking it at the 36th percentile. This indicates that the market has some slack, but it is not extremely loose. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate means that there may be a pool of available workers, but it may not be as large as in cities with higher unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is +1.05%, ranking it at the 14th percentile. This stagnant wage growth signals that labor costs for employers are not rising quickly, but it also means that worker purchasing power is not increasing rapidly. This may make it harder for businesses to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom has a cost of living score ranking it at the 16th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $339/sqft to $38.15/hr, or 8.89. This means that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers. The fact that the PSF is falling -0.9% year-over-year is a positive sign, but overall, the city's high cost of living may make it harder to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is contracting at a rate of -1.92% year-over-year, ranking it at the 10th percentile. This means that the supply of workers is shrinking, which may create a structural headwind for hiring in the city. Businesses looking to expand or relocate may face challenges in finding the talent they need.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is increasing by +21.94% year-over-year, ranking it at the 74th percentile. This signals that housing supply is expanding, which may improve affordability and make it easier for workers to relocate to the city. However, the current pace of growth may not be enough to fully address the city's affordability challenges.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 50 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.0%. This relatively fast pace of home sales may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing, as the market is still relatively competitive.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 0.72, ranking it at the 10th percentile. This indicates that the city has a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors, making it less suited for businesses in the tech, finance, or consulting industries. However, the city may be more attractive to businesses in industries that are less dependent on specialized office talent.

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area offers businesses a relatively low-cost labor environment, but it also presents significant challenges in terms of labor demand, wage growth, and cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's shrinking labor force, which may limit the availability of talent and create hiring challenges for businesses looking to expand or relocate.
