Economic Analysis

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it at the 29.5th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 4.32, and its stagnant wage growth, at +1.05% year-over-year. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is +0.34% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.457% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. Instead, it may be experiencing a period of slow growth or even a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 4.80%, ranking near the median at the 48th percentile. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire. However, the relatively low unemployment rate also means that businesses may face some wage pressure, particularly if they are looking to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is +1.05%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This stagnant wage growth signals a flat cost environment for employers, but it also means that workers have weak bargaining power. As a result, businesses may not face significant labor cost pressures, but they may also struggle to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom has a cost of living score that ranks in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, with a PSF of $344/sqft and average hourly earnings of $38.15/hr, resulting in a ratio of 9.02. This means that the city is relatively expensive, requiring wage premiums to attract talent. The fact that PSF is rising +0.3% year-over-year relative to wages further exacerbates the affordability challenge.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is contracting at a rate of -1.39% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile. This decline in labor force supply signals a structural headwind for hiring, making it challenging for businesses to find the talent they need.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is increasing by +16.17% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 74th percentile. This expansion in housing supply signals improving affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce. As a result, businesses may find it easier to attract and retain talent in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 46 days, with a year-over-year decline of -6.1%. This rapid pace of home sales signals a competitive market, making it challenging for relocating workers to find affordable housing. The low percentile rank of 6th percentile further emphasizes the competitive nature of the market.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 0.72, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile. This suggests that the city has a relatively shallow talent pool, making it less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms.

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area offers businesses a relatively low-cost labor environment, but it also presents significant challenges, including a contracting labor force and a competitive housing market. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand and stagnant wage growth, which may limit the availability of skilled talent and make it challenging to attract and retain top performers.
