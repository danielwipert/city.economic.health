Economic Analysis

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 38.6th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score defined largely by its labor demand and unemployment metrics. The city's labor demand composite score of 6.29, which combines a +0.82% employment growth rate and a +0.838% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, signals genuine demand expansion. This is further complicated by an unemployment rate of 5.00%, which is in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +0.82% and weekly hours deviation of +0.838% from its own trend indicate that Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is experiencing genuine demand expansion, with more jobs being added and hours running above trend. This combination suggests a strong labor market where businesses are likely to face competition for talent. The labor demand composite score of 6.29, ranking in the 74th percentile, reinforces this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 5.00% places Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile, indicating a relatively loose labor market with some slack. For businesses trying to hire, this means that while there may be available candidates, the market is not as competitive as in tighter labor markets, potentially easing hiring pressures but also signaling weaker local consumer demand.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of +2.60%, Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom experiences below-average wage growth, ranking in the 28th percentile. This moderate wage growth implies that labor costs for employers are increasing, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is not growing as quickly as in other metros. This could be a mixed blessing for businesses, offering some cost stability but also potentially weaker consumer spending.

Cost of Living

Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom has a cost of living score that ranks in the 22nd percentile, indicating that it is less affordable relative to its peers, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $338/sqft to $38.15/hr, or 8.86. Although the PSF is falling by -0.3% YoY, the city's affordability is still a concern, which could make it harder to attract talent without offering wage premiums to compensate for the higher cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is shrinking at a rate of -1.29% year-over-year, placing it in the 22nd percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may find it challenging to fill positions due to a dwindling pool of available workers.

Building Permits

The city saw a +15.85% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking in the 76th percentile. This significant expansion in housing supply signals improving affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future, which could help attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

Homes in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom are currently selling after 52 days on the market, with a -10.3% year-over-year change, placing the city in the bottom tier at the 6th percentile. This fast-paced market makes it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing, potentially complicating recruitment and retention efforts for businesses.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share ranking in the 10th percentile, Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom has a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This makes the city less suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations that rely heavily on professional and office workers, but it may be more accommodating to industries with different workforce needs.

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area offers businesses a mix of genuine labor demand expansion and a loose labor market, but it is constrained by a shrinking labor force and high cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the challenge of attracting and retaining talent due to the city's affordability issues and competitive housing market, which could necessitate wage premiums and strategic recruitment planning.
