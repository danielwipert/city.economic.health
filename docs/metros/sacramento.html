Economic Analysis

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 32.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low labor demand and stagnant wage growth, which are 30th percentile and 16th percentile, respectively. The labor demand composite score of 4.38 and wage growth of +1.15% year-over-year are key metrics defining this city's current economic character. These numbers indicate a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate is +0.69% year-over-year, while weekly hours are -0.889% below the city's own trend, signaling a contraction in labor demand rather than genuine demand expansion. This combination suggests that the job market is not as strong as the employment growth rate alone might indicate. The labor demand composite score of 4.38 is below average, ranking 30th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate is 4.20%, which is near the median, ranking 54th percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced labor market with neither significant slack nor extreme tightness. For a business trying to hire in this market, the moderate unemployment rate means that finding talent may not be overly difficult, but it also doesn't indicate a surplus of highly competitive candidates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth is +1.15%, which is in the bottom tier, ranking 16th percentile. This stagnant wage growth signals moderate labor costs for employers but weak bargaining power for workers. The slow wage growth environment may help keep labor costs flat but could also indicate weaker consumer demand due to limited purchasing power.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $343/sqft to $37.97/hr, resulting in a ratio of 9.03, and a percentile rank of 16th, Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is relatively expensive compared to its peers. This means that attracting talent without offering wage premiums could be challenging due to the high cost of living. The city's affordability is a concern for businesses looking to relocate or expand without significantly increasing compensation packages.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is shrinking at a rate of -1.52% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor pool. This negative growth rate poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses may face challenges in finding the talent they need due to this shrinking labor force.

Building Permits

Residential building permits are increasing by +16.17% year-over-year, which is above average, ranking 74th percentile. This rise in permits signals an expansion in future housing supply, potentially improving affordability and accommodating a growing workforce. The increase in building permits is a positive sign for future workforce attraction and retention.

Days on Market

Homes are currently sitting on the market for 43 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.3%. This relatively fast market, ranking below average at the 20th percentile, indicates a competitive environment for homebuyers, which could make it tough for relocating workers to find and secure housing quickly.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office sector jobs is 0.96, ranking in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile. This suggests a relatively shallow talent pool in professional and office sectors, making the city less suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, or HQ decisions that require deep knowledge-economy talent. The city's economy is more industrial or logistics-dominant, which may be better suited for businesses in those sectors.

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area presents a mixed bag for businesses considering relocation or expansion, with challenges in labor demand, wage growth, and cost of living being significant concerns. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor force, which poses a long-term challenge for hiring and talent acquisition. Despite some positive signs like an increase in building permits, the overall economic environment suggests caution and careful consideration of these factors in any business location decision.
