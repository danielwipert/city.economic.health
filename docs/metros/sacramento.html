Economic Analysis

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking in the 35.0th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 4.64 and a cost of living ratio of 8.96. The city's current economic character is most defined by its stagnant wage growth, at +1.25% year-over-year, and its bottom-tier labor force growth, at -1.52% year-over-year. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to hire and expand.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is +0.57% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.456% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a lack of genuine demand expansion, as hours are not running above trend despite modest job growth. The labor demand composite score of 4.64 is near the median, indicating a neutral labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 4.20%, which is near the median. This indicates a labor market with some slack, rather than being extremely tight. For a business trying to hire in this city, the relatively higher unemployment rate means it may be easier to find candidates, but it also suggests weaker local consumer demand.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is +1.25%, which is in the bottom tier. This stagnant wage growth means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also indicates weak bargaining power for workers and limited purchasing power. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but workers may not have the disposable income to drive local economic growth.

Cost of Living

Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom has a cost of living ratio of 8.96, with a price per square foot of $343 and average hourly earnings of $38.27. The city's cost of living percentile rank is in the bottom tier, at 14th percentile, indicating that it is relatively expensive. This means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums, as the high cost of living eats into workers' take-home pay.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is contracting at a rate of -1.52% year-over-year. This means that the supply of workers is shrinking, creating a structural headwind for hiring. Businesses looking to expand in this city may face challenges in finding the talent they need, as the labor pool is not growing.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is decreasing at a rate of -1.37% year-over-year. This indicates that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability challenges and make it harder for workers to relocate to the city. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering relocation assistance or other incentives to attract talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 43 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.3%. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the decreasing trend in days on market suggests that the market may be normalizing, making it slightly more accessible for workers to find homes.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is relatively low, with a percentile rank of 14th. This indicates that the city's talent pool is not particularly deep in these areas, making it less suited for businesses in the tech, finance, or consulting sectors. However, the city may be more attractive to businesses in industrial or logistics sectors.

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area offers businesses a relatively affordable labor market, but with significant challenges in terms of labor force growth and cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's stagnant wage growth and shrinking labor pool, which may limit the availability of skilled workers and drive up labor costs in the long run.
