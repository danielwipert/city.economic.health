Economic Analysis

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 30.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand and stagnant wage growth, with a labor demand composite score of 4.38 and a wage growth rate of +1.15% year-over-year. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is +0.69% year-over-year, while weekly hours are -0.889% below the city's own trend. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion. The negative hours deviation suggests that businesses may be absorbing the workload with existing staff rather than hiring new employees.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 4.80%, ranking near the median at the 48th percentile. This rate indicates a relatively balanced market with some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter labor markets. However, the moderate unemployment rate may not provide enough downward pressure on wages to offset other costs.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is +1.15%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile. This stagnant wage growth signals moderate labor costs for employers, but weak bargaining power for workers. As a result, businesses may not face significant upward pressure on wages, but workers may not have strong purchasing power.

Cost of Living

Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom has a cost of living score ranking it in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile, with a PSF-to-earnings ratio of $344/sqft to $37.97/hr, resulting in a ratio of 9.06. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to peers, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The slight increase in PSF of +0.3% year-over-year further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is contracting at a rate of -1.39% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile. This decline in labor force supply signals a structural headwind for hiring, making it more challenging for businesses to find qualified workers. The shrinking labor pool may limit the city's ability to support business expansion.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom has increased by +16.17% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 74th percentile. This expansion in housing supply signals improving affordability and accommodation for the workforce. The increase in building permits may help mitigate the city's affordability issues and support future business growth.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom is 46 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -6.1%. This rapid sales pace indicates a competitive market, making it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. The low days on market may deter some businesses from locating in the area, as their employees may face difficulties in finding affordable and available housing.

Office Economy

Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom has a professional and office worker share ranking it in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile, with a composite score of 0.96. This indicates a relatively shallow talent pool, making the city less suited for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy workers, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. The city may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The Sacramento-Roseville-Folsom metro area offers businesses a relatively balanced labor market and expanding housing supply, but its low labor demand, stagnant wage growth, and high cost of living pose significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's shrinking labor force, which may limit its ability to support business expansion and require businesses to offer wage premiums to attract talent.
