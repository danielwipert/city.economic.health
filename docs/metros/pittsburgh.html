Economic Analysis

Pittsburgh earns an overall grade of B, with a score of 56.9 out of 100, indicating it is an average city for business and economic growth. One of the defining features of Pittsburgh's economy right now is its low unemployment rate of 3.0%, which is a strength, scoring 96 out of 100, meaning it has lower unemployment than most of the 50 metros.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

Pittsburgh is losing jobs at a rate of -0.5% over the past year, and workers' hours are only slightly rising by 0.4%, indicating a weak job market with a score of 12 out of 100. This means that the city is not adding jobs at a rate comparable to most other metros, making it a challenging environment for job seekers.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.0%, which is a strength, scoring 96 out of 100, indicating that it has lower unemployment than most of the 50 metros. This low unemployment rate makes it harder for employers to hire, as there are fewer available workers, and may lead to upward pressure on wages.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages in Pittsburgh rose by 3.5% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 44 out of 100. This moderate wage growth means that employers are facing rising labor costs, but workers are also seeing an increase in their paychecks, which can contribute to a stronger local economy.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Pittsburgh is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $168 and hourly pay of $33.92, and it is getting even more affordable as housing prices are falling by 1.8% over the past year, scoring 88 out of 100. This makes Pittsburgh an attractive location for workers and businesses, as the cost of living is lower than in most other metros.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force in Pittsburgh is growing at a rate of 0.2% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 58 out of 100. This slow growth in the labor force means that hiring may become more challenging over time, as the pool of available workers is not expanding rapidly.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in Pittsburgh increased by 26.4% over the past year, which is a strength, scoring 86 out of 100, indicating stronger home-building activity than most metros. This surge in building permits suggests that the city's housing supply will expand in the future, which can help keep housing costs affordable and attract more workers.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Pittsburgh are taking 57 days to sell, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 52 out of 100, and this time is increasing by 5.6% over the past year. This moderate pace of home sales indicates a relatively healthy housing market, where buyers have a reasonable amount of time to find a home, but the market is not too soft.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

Pittsburgh's office-based workforce is smaller than in most other metros, scoring 20 out of 100, indicating that it may not be the best fit for businesses in the tech, finance, or professional services sectors. This smaller office workforce means that Pittsburgh may be more suited to industries that are not heavily reliant on a large pool of office workers, such as manufacturing or logistics.

The bottom line is that Pittsburgh's biggest strength is its low unemployment rate and affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for workers and businesses. However, its biggest risk is its weak labor demand, with job losses and slow wage growth, which may make it challenging for businesses to find the workers they need to grow and thrive.
