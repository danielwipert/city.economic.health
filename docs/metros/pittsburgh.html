Economic Analysis

Pittsburgh has an overall grade of B, ranking 58.4th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low unemployment rate of 3.90% and a cost of living score that indicates relative affordability. The city's labor demand composite score is 4.06, which is below average, and its wage growth is near median at +3.33% year-over-year. These metrics suggest a city with a tight labor market but moderate labor cost pressures.

Labor Demand

Pittsburgh's employment growth rate is -0.05% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating +0.173% from the city's own 12-month baseline, indicating a scenario where hours are above trend during job losses, which suggests a survivor squeeze. This combination signals that while there may not be significant job additions, existing workers are taking on more hours, potentially indicating a need for more labor supply. The labor demand composite score of 4.06 is below average, ranking in the 24th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.90%, which ranks in the 90th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This tight market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as there are fewer unemployed individuals seeking work, leading to potential wage pressure. For a business trying to hire in Pittsburgh, this means being prepared to offer competitive wages to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Pittsburgh is +3.33% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising but not at an alarming rate. For workers, this means their purchasing power is increasing, which can be beneficial for local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Pittsburgh has a cost of living score that indicates it is more affordable than many of its peers, with a ratio of $170/sqft to $33.44/hr, and the price per square foot is falling by -1.2% year-over-year. Ranking in the 73rd percentile for affordability, this means Pittsburgh has a talent attraction advantage, as workers can afford a higher quality of life without necessitating wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Pittsburgh is growing at a rate of +1.07% year-over-year, which is in the top tier, ranking in the 84th percentile. This expanding labor force supply is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it indicates a growing pool of potential workers. This growth can help mitigate some of the challenges posed by the tight labor market.

Building Permits

Building permits in Pittsburgh are increasing by +24.71% year-over-year, ranking above average in the 74th percentile. This significant increase in residential building permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which can improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. This is a positive signal for future workforce attraction and retention.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Pittsburgh is 54 days, with a year-over-year increase of +3.9%. Ranking in the 56th percentile, this indicates a near-median pace of home sales. For workers relocating to Pittsburgh, this suggests a relatively accessible housing market, neither extremely competitive nor overly slow.

Office Economy

Pittsburgh's professional and office worker share ranks in the bottom tier, at the 18th percentile. This indicates a less deep talent pool in professional and office sectors compared to other cities, making it less suited for businesses that require a large number of tech, finance, consulting, or HQ roles. However, it may be more suited for industries with different workforce needs.

In conclusion, Pittsburgh offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market with moderate wage growth and an affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for talent. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the city's below-average labor demand and the potential for a survivor squeeze, which could impact hiring and labor costs in the long term.
