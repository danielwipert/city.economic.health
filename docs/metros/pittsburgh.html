Economic Analysis

Pittsburgh earns an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 56.0th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor force growth, at +1.99% year-over-year, and its above-average wage growth, at +5.61% year-over-year. These metrics suggest a city with a expanding workforce and rising labor costs.

Labor Demand

Pittsburgh's employment growth rate is +0.06% year-over-year, and its weekly hours deviation is -0.272% below its own trend. This combination signals a labor demand environment that is not expanding genuinely, as hours are below trend despite modest job growth. This may indicate some level of survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.80%, ranking it at the 64th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This tightness implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure to attract and retain talent. In practical terms, companies looking to staff up in Pittsburgh may need to offer competitive salaries to stand out.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of +5.61%, Pittsburgh is experiencing relatively fast wage growth, ranking it at the 74th percentile. This rapid wage growth implies rising labor costs for employers but also means workers have increasing purchasing power. Businesses should factor in these higher labor costs when considering operations in Pittsburgh.

Cost of Living

Pittsburgh's cost of living, with a PSF of $174/sqft and average hourly earnings of $32.99/hr, results in a ratio of 5.27, ranking it at the 63rd percentile in terms of affordability. This means the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which can be a talent attraction advantage without needing significant wage premiums. The modest increase in PSF (+0.6% YoY) suggests that affordability is not significantly eroding.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Pittsburgh is growing at a rate of +1.99% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that hiring capacity should not be constrained by a lack of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to find the talent they need.

Building Permits

Residential building permits in Pittsburgh are increasing by +13.14% year-over-year, signaling an expansion in future housing supply. This growth in permits is a positive indicator for future affordability and the ability to accommodate a growing workforce, suggesting that the city is likely to remain attractive to workers and businesses alike.

Days on Market

Homes in Pittsburgh are currently sitting on the market for a median of 47 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.1%. This relatively fast market suggests that relocating workers may face a competitive environment when looking for housing, potentially affecting their decision to move to the area.

Office Economy

Pittsburgh's professional and office worker share ranks at the 36th percentile, indicating a less deep talent pool in these sectors compared to other cities. This suggests that Pittsburgh is less suited for businesses requiring a large pool of tech, finance, or consulting talent but may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs.

In conclusion, Pittsburgh offers businesses a unique combination of a growing labor force and relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for certain types of businesses. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the tight labor market, which may lead to higher wage pressures and challenges in attracting the right talent, particularly in professional and office sectors.
