Economic Analysis

Pittsburgh earns an overall grade of B, ranking in the 56.9th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score defined largely by its low unemployment rate of 3.00% and a cost of living score that indicates affordability, with a PSF to wages ratio of 4.95. The city's labor demand and office economy metrics, however, suggest challenges, with a labor demand composite score of 3.17 and an office/professional worker share ranking in the 20th percentile. These numbers suggest a complex economic environment that offers both advantages and disadvantages for businesses considering relocation.

Labor Demand

Pittsburgh's employment growth rate is -0.54% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of +0.372% from its own trend, signaling a scenario where hours are increasing during a period of job losses, which may indicate a survivor squeeze. This suggests that while there is some level of demand, it is not translating into significant job growth. The labor demand composite score of 3.17 ranks in the bottom tier, at the 12th percentile, indicating that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.00%, ranking in the top tier at the 96th percentile, which means the market is tight. This tight labor market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure as a result of the low unemployment rate. Companies looking to expand or relocate here may need to offer competitive salaries to attract talent.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Pittsburgh is +3.55% year-over-year, placing it near the median at the 44th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not at an alarming rate, while workers are experiencing an increase in purchasing power. This balance could be attractive for businesses looking for a stable labor cost environment without sacrificing worker morale.

Cost of Living

Pittsburgh's cost of living, with a PSF of $168/sqft and wages of $33.92/hr, results in a ratio of 4.95, ranking in the top tier at the 88th percentile, indicating that the city is more affordable compared to its peers. The PSF has decreased by -1.8% year-over-year, contributing to its high affordability score. This affordability is a significant talent attraction advantage, as businesses can offer competitive wages without needing large premiums to offset the cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in Pittsburgh is growing at a rate of +0.22% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 58th percentile. This positive growth indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, albeit slowly, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire. However, the slow pace of growth may not keep up with rapid expansion plans.

Building Permits

The city has seen a +26.35% year-over-year increase in building permits, ranking in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This significant increase in residential building permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. This is a positive signal for future workforce attraction and retention.

Days on Market

Homes in Pittsburgh are currently sitting on the market for a median of 57 days, with a year-over-year increase of +5.6%. This places the city near the median in terms of market speed, at the 52nd percentile. For workers relocating to the city, this indicates a relatively accessible market, neither overly competitive nor excessively slow, making it easier for new hires to find housing.

Office Economy

Pittsburgh's office economy, with an office/professional worker share ranking in the 20th percentile, indicates a less deep talent pool in professional and office sectors compared to other cities. This suggests that the city may be less suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations that require a dense knowledge-economy talent pool, but could be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs.

In conclusion, Pittsburgh offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market, moderate wage growth, and an affordable cost of living, which can be attractive for talent retention and recruitment without excessive wage premiums. However, the city's slow labor force growth and limited office economy talent pool present challenges, particularly for businesses reliant on professional and office sectors, making these factors crucial to consider in relocation decisions.
