Economic Analysis

Pittsburgh earns an overall grade of B, ranking 58.6th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low unemployment rate of 3.90% and a cost of living score that indicates relative affordability. The city's labor demand and office economy metrics, however, suggest a more mixed economic character, with employment growth and professional sector job share being below average. Specifically, the labor demand composite score of 4.06 and an office/professional worker share percentile rank of 20th pct are key defining metrics.

Labor Demand

Pittsburgh's employment growth rate is -0.05% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of +0.173% from its own trend, signaling a scenario where hours are slightly above trend during a period of job losses, which may indicate a survivor squeeze. This suggests that while there might be some demand for labor, it's not translating into significant job additions. The labor demand composite score of 4.06, ranking in the 24th percentile, further reinforces this interpretation of below-average labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Pittsburgh is 3.90%, placing it in the top tier with a 90th percentile rank, indicating a very tight labor market. This tightness implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring due to the limited availability of workers, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. In practical terms, a company looking to expand or establish operations in Pittsburgh may need to offer competitive salaries to attract talent.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Pittsburgh is +3.33% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but not at an alarming rate, while workers are experiencing some improvement in their purchasing power. However, the pace of wage growth is not exceptionally high, which might influence business decisions regarding labor costs and investment in the area.

Cost of Living

Pittsburgh's cost of living, with a PSF of $170/sqft and average hourly earnings of $33.44, results in a ratio of 5.08, and the PSF is decreasing by -1.2% year-over-year, contributing to an above-average affordability score of 74th percentile. This relative affordability is a significant advantage for talent attraction, as it means that workers can maintain a good standard of living without necessitating wage premiums from employers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Pittsburgh is growing at a rate of +1.07% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 84th percentile. This expansion in the labor force supply is a positive indicator for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a growing pool of potential workers. However, the rate of growth, while positive, is not exceptionally high, which may still pose some challenges in finding specific skill sets.

Building Permits

Residential building permits in Pittsburgh are increasing by +24.71% year-over-year, ranking in the 74th percentile. This significant increase in building permits signals that housing supply is expected to expand, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. This trend is favorable for businesses considering relocation or expansion, as it indicates a future supply of housing that can support their workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Pittsburgh is 54 days, with a year-over-year increase of +3.9%. This places the city near the median in terms of housing market speed, suggesting a relatively balanced market that is neither too competitive nor too slow. For workers relocating to Pittsburgh, this indicates a moderately accessible housing market, though the slight increase in days on market may signal a minor normalization in the market's pace.

Office Economy

Pittsburgh's professional and office sector job share ranks in the 20th percentile, indicating a less deep talent pool in these areas compared to other cities. This suggests that Pittsburgh is less suited for businesses heavily reliant on tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations that require a dense network of professional services. However, it may be more accommodating to industries with different workforce requirements.

In conclusion, Pittsburgh offers a unique blend of a tight labor market, moderate wage growth, and relative affordability, making it an attractive location for certain types of businesses, especially those that can leverage its specific economic strengths. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker to factor in is the below-average labor demand and the relatively shallow professional talent pool, which could pose challenges for businesses requiring rapid expansion or highly specialized labor forces.
