Economic Analysis

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 18.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 2.57. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with employment growth at -0.47% year-over-year, and its high unemployment rate of 5.20%, which is in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. These metrics signal significant challenges for businesses looking to hire and expand in the area.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is -0.47% year-over-year, and weekly hours are 1.071% above the city's own 12-month trend. This combination signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, rather than genuine demand expansion. The labor demand composite score of 2.57 is in the bottom tier, at the 6th percentile, indicating a weak job market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is 5.20%, which is in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. This indicates a slack labor market, where businesses may find it easier to hire, but local consumer demand may be weaker. The practical implication for a business trying to hire in this area is that they may have a larger pool of potential candidates to choose from, but may also face lower demand for their products or services.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is +1.17%, which is in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile. This indicates moderate wage growth, which may lead to rising labor costs for employers, but also provides workers with some purchasing power. The implication for employer labor costs is that they may need to budget for modest wage increases, while workers may see some improvement in their standard of living.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Louisville-Jefferson County is relatively affordable, with a percentile rank of 33. The city has a PSF of $178/sqft, which is 5.80 times the average hourly earnings of $30.70/hr. This means that the city may have a talent attraction advantage, as workers may not require wage premiums to afford the cost of living. The affordability of the city could be a draw for businesses looking to relocate or expand.

Labor Force Growth

The year-over-year growth rate of the civilian labor force in Louisville-Jefferson County is +0.08%, which is near the median at the 46th percentile. This indicates that the labor force supply is slowly expanding, which may provide businesses with a slightly larger pool of potential candidates to hire from. However, the slow growth rate may also indicate that the labor market is not particularly dynamic or attractive to new workers.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits in Louisville-Jefferson County is -27.55%, which is in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile. This indicates that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability and workforce accommodation challenges. The decline in building permits signals that developer confidence is low, and the city may struggle to provide enough housing for a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Louisville-Jefferson County is 43 days, with a year-over-year increase of 10.3%. This is in the top tier at the 94th percentile, indicating a slower market where homes are sitting longer before being sold. For a worker relocating to this city, the slower market may make it easier to find a home, but it also signals a lack of urgency and competition in the housing market.

Office Economy

The professional talent pool in Louisville-Jefferson County is relatively shallow, with an office economy percentile rank of 4. This indicates that the city is not well-suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. The city may be more suitable for businesses with industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area offers businesses a relatively affordable cost of living and a slow-growing labor force, but it also presents significant challenges, including low labor demand and a high unemployment rate. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker to factor in is the weak job market, which may limit the city's ability to attract and retain top talent, and ultimately hinder business growth and expansion.
