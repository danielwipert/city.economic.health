Economic Analysis

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 28.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 2.98, which falls in the bottom tier. The city's economic character is most defined by its weak labor demand, with employment growth of -0.13% YoY, and its slow wage growth, at +1.74% YoY. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is -0.13% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +1.140%, indicating a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion. The labor demand composite score of 2.98 falls in the bottom tier, at the 10th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is 4.10%, which is near the median, ranking at the 44th percentile. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire, but still with some wage pressure. However, the relatively high unemployment rate compared to other metros may also indicate weaker local consumer demand.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Louisville-Jefferson County is +1.74%, which is below average, ranking at the 20th percentile. This slow wage growth signals a relatively flat cost environment for employers, but also weak bargaining power for workers. As a result, businesses may not face significant labor cost pressures, but workers may not have strong purchasing power.

Cost of Living

Louisville-Jefferson County has a cost of living score that ranks at the 31st percentile, with a PSF of $178/sqft and average hourly earnings of $31.41, resulting in a ratio of 5.67. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, making it less attractive for talent without wage premiums. The fact that PSF is not falling YoY also suggests that affordability is not improving.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Louisville-Jefferson County is contracting at a rate of -0.83% YoY, which is near the median, ranking at the 54th percentile. This signals a shrinking labor pool, creating a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion. As a result, businesses may face challenges in finding and retaining talent.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Louisville-Jefferson County has decreased by -31.05% YoY, which is in the bottom tier, ranking at the 8th percentile. This sharp decline signals a tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Louisville-Jefferson County is currently 45 days, with a YoY increase of +12.5%, ranking at the 90th percentile. This suggests a slower market, where homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, this may also indicate a weakening demand for housing.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in Louisville-Jefferson County is 0.32, ranking at the 2nd percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics-dominant companies, and less suited for tech, finance, or consulting firms.

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area offers businesses a relatively flat cost environment and a slower housing market, but it also presents significant challenges, including a shrinking labor pool and a tightening housing supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's weak labor demand and slow wage growth, which may limit business expansion and talent attraction.
