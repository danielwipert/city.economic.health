Economic Analysis

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 27.8th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score of 2.99 ranking it in the bottom tier at 6th percentile, and its slow wage growth, at +1.61% year-over-year, ranking it below average at 20th percentile. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is -0.07% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +1.215%. This combination signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, rather than genuine demand expansion. This is a concern for businesses looking to hire, as it may indicate a lack of job creation and a tight labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is 4.80%, ranking it below average at 34th percentile. This indicates a relatively tight labor market, which can make it harder for businesses to hire and may lead to upward pressure on wages. As a result, businesses may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Louisville-Jefferson County is +1.61%, ranking it below average at 20th percentile. This slow wage growth indicates a relatively flat cost environment for employers, but it also means that workers may have weak bargaining power. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but workers may not have the purchasing power to drive local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Louisville-Jefferson County has a cost of living score ranking it below average at 39th percentile, with a PSF of $178/sqft and average hourly earnings of $31.29/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.69. This means that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which can make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. However, the fact that PSF is rising by +0.6% year-over-year may indicate that the city is becoming even less affordable.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Louisville-Jefferson County is growing at a rate of +0.25% year-over-year, ranking it above average at 62nd percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which can make it easier for businesses to hire and find the talent they need. However, the slow labor demand and low wage growth may offset this advantage.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Louisville-Jefferson County is decreasing by -31.66% year-over-year, ranking it in the bottom tier at 8th percentile. This signals that housing supply is tightening, which can lead to future affordability concerns and make it challenging for workers to relocate to the city. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering relocation assistance or other benefits to attract talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Louisville-Jefferson County is 44 days, with a year-over-year increase of +12.8%, ranking it in the top tier at 94th percentile. This indicates a relatively slow market, which can make it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, this may also signal a weakening demand for housing, which can have implications for the local economy.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Louisville-Jefferson County is 0.38, ranking it in the bottom tier at 4th percentile. This indicates a relatively shallow talent pool, making the city less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, the city may be more suitable for businesses with industrial or logistics-dominated economies.

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area offers businesses a relatively slow-growing economy with a tight labor market and slow wage growth. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand and tightening housing supply, which can make it challenging to attract and retain talent. As a result, businesses may need to carefully consider these factors when deciding whether to expand or relocate to the city.
