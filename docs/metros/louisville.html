Economic Analysis

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 20.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 2.99. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand and high unemployment rate, with employment growth at -0.07% year-over-year and an unemployment rate of 5.20%. The combination of these metrics suggests a challenging economic environment.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is -0.07% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +1.215%. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a decline in job creation and hours worked. The labor demand composite score of 2.99, ranking in the bottom tier at 6th percentile, further reinforces this signal.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is 5.20%, ranking in the bottom tier at 2nd percentile. This indicates a relatively loose labor market with significant slack, making it easier for businesses to hire workers. However, this also suggests weaker local consumer demand, which could impact businesses reliant on local spending.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Louisville-Jefferson County is +1.61%, ranking below average at 20th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace than in many other cities. Workers in the area will experience some increase in purchasing power, but it may not be sufficient to drive significant economic growth.

Cost of Living

Louisville-Jefferson County has a cost of living ratio of $178/sqft to $31.29/hr, with a percentile rank of 47th. This indicates that the city is near the median in terms of affordability, with a relatively stable cost of living. The city's affordability may attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, but it is not a major differentiator.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Louisville-Jefferson County is growing at a rate of +0.08% year-over-year, ranking near the median at 46th percentile. This slow growth rate suggests that the labor force supply is expanding, but at a limited pace. Businesses may face some challenges in finding new talent, but the growth rate is not a major constraint.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Louisville-Jefferson County has decreased by -27.55% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at 8th percentile. This sharp decline signals a tightening of the housing supply, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for housing in the future. This could negatively impact the city's ability to attract and accommodate a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Louisville-Jefferson County is 43 days, with a year-over-year increase of +10.3%, ranking in the top tier at 94th percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are taking longer to sell. For workers relocating to the city, this may make it easier to find a home, but it also suggests a less competitive housing market.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Louisville-Jefferson County is 0.38, ranking in the bottom tier at 4th percentile. This suggests a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors, making the city less suited for businesses in the tech, finance, or consulting industries. However, the city may be more attractive to businesses in industrial or logistics sectors.

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area offers businesses a relatively loose labor market and moderate wage growth, but its low labor demand and declining housing supply are significant concerns. The single biggest risk for decision-makers is the city's limited ability to attract and accommodate a growing workforce, which may constrain business growth and expansion plans.
