Economic Analysis

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking 28.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 2.98. The city's economic character is most defined by its weak labor demand, with employment growth of -0.13% year-over-year, and its slow wage growth, at +1.74% year-over-year. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of -0.13% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of +1.140% from the city's own trend, suggests a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This indicates a lack of genuine demand expansion, as hours are above trend during a period of job losses. The labor demand composite score of 2.98, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, further reinforces this notion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 4.10% is near the median, ranking at the 44th percentile, indicating a moderate level of slack in the labor market. This means that businesses may not face extreme difficulty in hiring, but may still encounter some competition for talent. The relatively average unemployment rate suggests that wage pressure is not excessively high, but still present.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +1.74% is below average, ranking at the 20th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth implies a relatively flat cost environment for employers, but also weak bargaining power for workers. As a result, businesses may not face significant labor cost increases, but workers may not have strong purchasing power.

Cost of Living

The city's cost of living, with a PSF of $178/sqft and an earnings ratio of 5.67, ranks at the 31st percentile, indicating a below-average level of affordability. However, since the PSF is not falling year-over-year, the affordability score is not driven by declining housing costs. This means that the city may not offer a significant talent attraction advantage without wage premiums, as the cost of living is relatively high compared to peers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -0.83% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This represents a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses may face challenges in finding and recruiting talent in this environment.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -31.05%, indicating a sharp decline in housing supply expansion. This signals a potential supply squeeze building ahead, which could lead to decreased affordability and increased challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 45 days, with a year-over-year increase of +12.5%, ranking at the 90th percentile. This indicates a slower market, where homes are sitting longer before being sold. For a worker relocating to this city, this means a more accessible and less competitive housing market.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers is 0.32, ranking at the 2nd percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics-dominant companies. However, it may not be ideal for tech, finance, or consulting businesses that rely on specialized office roles.

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area offers a relatively flat cost environment and a moderate labor market, but is constrained by weak labor demand, slow wage growth, and a shrinking labor pool. The single biggest risk or constraint for businesses considering this location is the potential supply squeeze in housing, which could lead to decreased affordability and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.
