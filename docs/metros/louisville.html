Economic Analysis

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area has an overall grade of D, ranking in the 20.2th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 2.11. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with an employment growth rate of -0.85% and a weekly hours deviation of +0.999%, as well as its high unemployment rate of 4.60%. The combination of these metrics signals a challenging economic environment.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is -0.85%, indicating a contraction in job opportunities, while the weekly hours deviation is +0.999%, suggesting that remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, characteristic of a survivor squeeze. This combination signals a lack of genuine demand expansion. The labor demand composite score of 2.11 is in the bottom tier, ranking 6th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is 4.60%, which is relatively high, indicating a market with some slack. This means that businesses may find it easier to hire workers, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The unemployment rate ranks in the 18th percentile, indicating a less competitive labor market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is +1.79%, which is below average, ranking in the 24th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, but not rapidly. This may lead to a relatively stable labor cost environment.

Cost of Living

Louisville-Jefferson County has a cost of living ratio of $179/sqft to $31.09/hr, with a PSF ratio of 5.76, ranking in the 31st percentile. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive, but not extremely so, making it less attractive for talent attraction without wage premiums. The cost of living is not decreasing, with a 0.0% YoY change in PSF.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Louisville-Jefferson County is contracting at a rate of -0.96% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This implies a structural headwind for hiring, making it more challenging for businesses to find workers. The labor force growth rate ranks below average, in the 36th percentile.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Louisville-Jefferson County has decreased by -31.15% year-over-year, indicating a tightening housing supply. This signals a potential supply squeeze, which may lead to decreased affordability and make it more difficult for workers to relocate to the area. The building permits growth rate is in the bottom tier, ranking 4th percentile.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Louisville-Jefferson County is 40 days, with a +5.3% year-over-year increase, ranking in the 72nd percentile. This indicates a slower market, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the area. However, this may also signal a weakening demand for housing.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in Louisville-Jefferson County is 0.50, ranking in the 6th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics-dominant companies, and less suited for tech, finance, or consulting firms.

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area offers a relatively low-cost labor environment, but its low labor demand, high unemployment rate, and shrinking labor force make it a challenging location for businesses. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential supply squeeze in the housing market, which may lead to decreased affordability and make it more difficult for workers to relocate to the area.
