Economic Analysis

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it at the 27.0th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier, and its slow wage growth, at +1.74% year-over-year. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or relocate.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is -0.13% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +1.140%. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion, as hours are above trend during a period of job losses, indicating a survivor squeeze. This suggests that remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Louisville-Jefferson County is 4.80%, ranking in the 34th percentile, indicating a below-average labor market with some slack. This means that businesses may find it relatively easier to hire workers, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The labor market is not tight, giving employers some flexibility in hiring and wage setting.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Louisville-Jefferson County is +1.74%, ranking in the 20th percentile, indicating slow wage growth. This means that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a moderate pace, and worker purchasing power is not increasing rapidly. The slow wage growth may limit the attractiveness of the city to businesses looking for a highly skilled and highly paid workforce.

Cost of Living

Louisville-Jefferson County has a cost of living score ranking it in the 24th percentile, with a PSF of $178/sqft and average hourly earnings of $31.41, resulting in a ratio of 5.67. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, making it less attractive for talent attraction without wage premiums. The cost of living is a concern for businesses looking to relocate or expand in the area.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Louisville-Jefferson County is growing at a rate of +0.25% year-over-year, ranking in the 62nd percentile. This indicates that the labor force supply is expanding, providing a positive implication for hiring capacity. Businesses may find it easier to attract and retain workers in the area due to the growing labor force.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Louisville-Jefferson County has decreased by -31.66% year-over-year, ranking in the 8th percentile. This signals a tightening of the housing supply, which may lead to future affordability concerns and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The decline in building permits is a concern for businesses looking to attract and retain workers in the area.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Louisville-Jefferson County is 44 days, with a year-over-year increase of +12.8%, ranking in the 94th percentile. This indicates a slower market, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, the rising days on market may also signal a normalization of the market, rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Louisville-Jefferson County is 0.32, ranking in the 2nd percentile. This indicates a relatively shallow talent pool, making the city less suited for businesses in the tech, finance, or consulting sectors. The city's economy is more dominated by industrial or logistics sectors, making it more attractive to businesses in those fields.

The Louisville-Jefferson County metro area offers businesses a relatively low-cost labor market with some slack, but also slow wage growth and a tightening housing supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's low labor demand and limited professional talent pool, which may limit the attractiveness of the city to businesses looking for a highly skilled and highly paid workforce.
