Economic Analysis

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking it at the 61.2th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its top-tier labor demand, with an 84th percentile ranking, and its highly affordable cost of living, ranked at the 96th percentile. The combination of a 1.70% employment growth rate and a cost of living ratio of $234/sqft to $37.54/hr, with PSF falling 8.6% YoY, sets Austin apart.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Austin is 1.70% YoY, paired with a weekly hours deviation of -0.262% from its own 12-month baseline, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 6.42. This signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are not excessively above trend during job growth, indicating a healthy labor market. The top-tier ranking of 84th percentile suggests a strong jobs market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Austin is 4.10%, ranking below average at the 36th percentile. This indicates a moderate level of slack in the labor market, making it somewhat easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, the practical implication is that wage pressure might not be as high as in markets with lower unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

Austin experiences a year-over-year wage growth rate of 4.80%, ranking above average at the 64th percentile. This signifies moderately fast wage growth, implying rising labor costs for employers but increased purchasing power for workers. As wages grow, businesses may face higher labor costs, but the local workforce will have more disposable income.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living percentile rank of 96th, Austin is highly affordable relative to its peers, given its PSF of $234/sqft falling 8.6% YoY relative to hourly earnings of $37.54. This affordability advantage means that businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs. The falling PSF is a key driver of this high affordability score.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Austin is growing at a rate of 1.27% YoY, ranking above average at the 78th percentile. This indicates an expanding workforce supply, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a structural advantage in terms of hiring capacity. A growing labor force can support business expansion.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -37.80%, ranking at the bottom tier of the 2nd percentile. This sharp decline signals a tightening housing supply, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The decrease in building permits is a concern for long-term workforce attraction and retention.

Days on Market

Homes in Austin currently sit on the market for a median of 67 days, with a year-over-year increase of 1.5%. This below-average ranking of 22nd percentile indicates a relatively fast market, which could make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the rising DOM could also signal a healthy normalization of the market.

Office Economy

Austin has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share composite ranking at the 94th percentile. This makes the city highly suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions that require a knowledgeable workforce. Conversely, it might be less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, affordable cost of living, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the sharp decline in building permits, which could lead to future housing supply constraints and affordability issues, potentially impacting workforce attraction and retention.
