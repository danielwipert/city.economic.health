Economic Analysis

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area has an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking at the 62.6th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 84th percentile, and its highly affordable cost of living, ranked at the 90th percentile. The combination of these metrics suggests a city with a robust job market and attractive living costs.

Labor Demand

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area has an employment growth rate of +1.70% year-over-year and a weekly hours deviation of -0.262% from its own trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 6.42. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs and hours are running slightly below trend, indicating a healthy and sustainable labor market. The top-tier percentile rank of 84th confirms the city's strong labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is 4.00%, ranking at the 44th percentile, indicating a near-median level of unemployment. This suggests that the market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to cities with tighter labor markets. However, the unemployment rate is not so low that it would drive up wage pressure significantly.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is +4.80%, ranking above average at the 64th percentile. This moderate to fast wage growth rate implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing purchasing power for workers. As a result, businesses may need to adjust their compensation packages to remain competitive in the market.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $231/sqft to $37.54/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.15, and a year-over-year decrease of -8.0% in PSF, Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is highly affordable, ranking at the 90th percentile. This makes the city an attractive location for talent, as workers can enjoy a relatively low cost of living without requiring significant wage premiums. The falling PSF is a key driver of the high affordability score.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is growing at a rate of +0.80% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 78th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, providing a positive implication for hiring capacity and the ability of businesses to find skilled workers.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -29.04%, ranking at the 10th percentile, indicating a significant tightening of housing supply. This signals a potential future affordability issue and a challenge for workforce accommodation, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 73 days, with a year-over-year increase of +1.4%. This ranks at the 16th percentile, indicating a slower market. For workers relocating to this city, the relatively slower pace of home sales may make it more accessible to find a home, but it also suggests a less competitive market.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share of 4.22, ranking at the 94th percentile, Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos has a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses in these sectors, but less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented economies.

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, affordable cost of living, and a deep professional talent pool. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.
