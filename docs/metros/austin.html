Economic Analysis

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area has an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking at the 61.4th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score at the 64th percentile, and its high office economy ranking, at the 92nd percentile, indicating a deep talent pool in professional sectors. The labor demand and office economy metrics are particularly notable, with employment growth at 1.06% year-over-year and a high share of professional workers at 4.04 out of 5.

Labor Demand

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area has an employment growth rate of 1.06% year-over-year and weekly hours deviation of -0.642% from its own trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 5.14, which ranks at the 64th percentile. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are slightly below trend during a period of job growth, indicating that the labor market is absorbing new workers without significant strain. This suggests a healthy and growing labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is 3.50%, ranking at the 67th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. With an unemployment rate below the national average, businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the market has limited slack. This tight market may lead to upward pressure on wages as companies compete for scarce labor.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is 5.67%, ranking above average at the 72nd percentile. This rapid wage growth signals rising labor costs for employers, but it also indicates strong worker purchasing power, which can drive local consumer demand. As wages continue to rise, businesses may need to adjust their compensation packages to remain competitive.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is near the median, ranking at the 50th percentile, indicating a relatively average affordability level compared to peer cities. This means that the city neither offers a significant talent attraction advantage due to low costs nor requires substantial wage premiums to offset high costs. The cost of living is not a major differentiator for this city.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is growing at a rate of 0.44% year-over-year, ranking at the 74th percentile, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This growth in the labor force suggests that the city has a structural advantage in terms of hiring capacity, as the pool of potential workers is increasing.

Building Permits

Data on the year-over-year change in building permits is not available for Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos. However, this metric is crucial in understanding the future trajectory of housing supply and affordability. An increase in building permits would signal an expansion of housing supply, potentially improving affordability, while a decrease would indicate a tightening supply, potentially leading to higher housing costs.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is not available, but the city ranks at the bottom tier, or 0th percentile, indicating a very competitive market where homes sell quickly. This fast-paced market may pose challenges for relocating workers, as they may face intense competition for available housing.

Office Economy

The office economy in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is deep, with a professional and office worker share ranking at the 92nd percentile. This suggests that the city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. Conversely, the city may be less suitable for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant.

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with a deep talent pool in professional sectors, making it an attractive location for companies in the tech and finance industries. However, the city's tight labor market and rapid wage growth may pose challenges for businesses in terms of hiring and labor costs, and the competitive housing market may make it difficult for relocating workers to find affordable housing.
