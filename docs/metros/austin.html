Economic Analysis

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 68.0th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven significantly by its top-tier labor demand and cost of living metrics, scoring 80th and 96th percentiles respectively. The city's labor demand composite score of 6.59, combining a +1.16% employment growth rate and a +0.667% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, signals a genuine demand expansion. This, coupled with its highly affordable cost of living, makes Austin an attractive location for businesses.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Austin stands at +1.16% year-over-year, accompanied by a +0.667% deviation in weekly hours above its own 12-month trend, indicating a strong and genuine demand for labor. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a period of job growth where hours are also increasing, pointing to a healthy expansion of economic activity rather than a survivor squeeze. The labor demand composite score of 6.59 further reinforces this, ranking in the top tier at the 80th percentile.

Unemployment

Austin's unemployment rate is 3.90%, placing it near the median at the 46th percentile. This rate suggests that while the market is not extremely tight, it does have some slack, making it somewhat easier for businesses to hire compared to cities with lower unemployment rates. However, the relatively low unemployment rate still implies some level of competition for talent, which could lead to moderate wage pressure.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Austin is +5.08%, which is above average, ranking in the 70th percentile. This level of wage growth indicates that labor costs for employers are rising, but it also means that workers have increasing purchasing power, which can be beneficial for local consumer demand. The wage growth suggests a dynamic labor market where employers may need to adjust their compensation packages to attract and retain talent.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of 6.01, derived from $228/sqft (which has decreased by 8.4% year-over-year) compared to average hourly earnings of $37.93, Austin ranks in the top tier for affordability at the 96th percentile. This high affordability score means that the city offers a significant talent attraction advantage without the need for substantial wage premiums, as the cost of living is relatively low compared to earnings.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Austin is growing at a rate of +0.64% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the city's hiring capacity is not constrained by a shrinking labor pool, providing businesses with a growing talent base from which to recruit.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Austin has increased by +2.50% year-over-year, signaling a modest expansion in housing supply. Although this growth is only at the 50th percentile, it suggests that developer confidence is present, and future housing supply is likely to improve, which can help maintain affordability and accommodate a growing workforce.

Days on Market

Homes in Austin are currently sitting on the market for a median of 80 days, with a year-over-year increase of +1.3%. This situation, ranking in the 20th percentile, indicates a relatively fast-paced market that may be challenging for relocating workers, as homes are selling quickly. However, this also suggests a strong demand for housing, which can be a sign of a vibrant local economy.

Office Economy

Austin's professional and office worker share is ranked in the top tier at the 94th percentile, indicating a deep and specialized talent pool. This makes the city particularly well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less ideal for those primarily involved in industrial or logistics activities.

In conclusion, Austin offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, highly affordable cost of living, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for a variety of industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the potential for housing market pressures, given the relatively fast-paced sales environment, which could impact the affordability and attractiveness of the city for relocating workers over time.
