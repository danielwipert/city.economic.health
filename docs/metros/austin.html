Economic Analysis

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 63.5th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong labor demand and low cost of living, scoring 64th and 96th percentiles, respectively. The city's economic character is defined by its above-average employment growth rate of 1.06% and its top-tier cost of living ratio of 6.22, with PSF at $237/sqft and hourly earnings at $38.10/hr. This combination signals a highly competitive market with attractive conditions for both businesses and workers.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is 1.06% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of -0.642% from its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion. This signals that the city is adding jobs and, despite a slight decrease in hours worked, is experiencing a period of growth. The labor demand composite score of 5.14 further supports this, ranking in the 64th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is 3.50%, ranking in the 68th percentile, which indicates a relatively tight market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring due to the low availability of workers, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. However, it also suggests a strong and stable workforce.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is 5.67%, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating fast wage growth. This rapid increase in wages suggests that labor costs for employers may rise, but it also means that workers will have greater purchasing power, contributing to a robust local economy.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of 6.22, driven by a PSF of $237/sqft that is decreasing by 8.1% year-over-year relative to hourly earnings of $38.10/hr, Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos scores in the 96th percentile for affordability. This high ranking means the city is very attractive for talent without needing significant wage premiums, offering a competitive advantage in recruitment.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos is growing at a rate of 0.44% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the hiring capacity for businesses is likely to improve over time, as more workers become available.

Building Permits

The city has experienced a significant decline in residential building permits, with a year-over-year change of -40.35%, ranking in the 2nd percentile. This sharp decrease signals a potential tightening of the housing supply, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

Homes in Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos are currently sitting on the market for 63 days, with an 8.6% year-over-year increase, ranking in the 24th percentile. This indicates a relatively competitive market for relocating workers, as homes are selling fairly quickly, suggesting that workers may face challenges in finding and securing housing.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share composite score of 4.04, ranking in the 92nd percentile, Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos has a deep and talented pool of professional workers. This makes the city highly suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less ideal for industries requiring large industrial or logistics workforces.

The Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, low cost of living, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for a variety of industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the sharp decline in building permits, which could lead to future housing affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce, potentially offsetting some of the city's current advantages.
