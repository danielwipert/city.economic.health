Economic Analysis

The Baltimore-Columbia-Towson metro area has earned an overall grade of A-, with a score of 64.7 out of 100, indicating it is a good location for businesses, with a strong labor market and affordable cost of living. One of the key defining features of this city's economy is its low unemployment rate of 3.5%, which is lower than most of the 50 metros, and its fast wage growth of 6.4% over the past year.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is losing jobs at a rate of 0.2% over the past year, and workers' hours are only slightly rising by 0.1%, indicating a weak job market with slower hiring than most metros. This means that employers may not be expanding their workforce as quickly as they are in other cities, which could be a challenge for businesses looking to grow.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 3.5% is a strength, scoring 84 out of 100, meaning it is lower than most of the 50 metros, making it harder for employers to hire and potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. With a low unemployment rate, businesses may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent in this tight labor market.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages have risen by 6.4% over the past year, which is a strength, scoring 90 out of 100, indicating faster pay growth than most metros, resulting in higher labor costs for employers but more purchasing power for workers. This rapid wage growth suggests that businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, but it also means that workers have more disposable income to spend in the local economy.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is relatively affordable, with a score of 90 out of 100, and a price per square foot of $213, which is 2.7% lower than the previous year, compared to an hourly pay of $37.72, making it more affordable than most metros. This affordability is a strength, as it can attract talent and make it easier for businesses to recruit and retain employees who can afford to live in the area.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force is growing at a rate of 0.4% over the past year, which is a strength, scoring 64 out of 100, indicating a faster workforce growth than most metros, providing a larger pool of potential employees for businesses to hire from. This growth in the labor force is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand their operations in the area.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals have decreased by 2.8% over the past year, scoring 52 out of 100, which is about average for the 50 metros, signaling that the future housing supply may not be expanding as quickly as in other cities. This slowdown in building permits could lead to a more competitive housing market, making it harder for workers to find affordable housing options.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes are taking 46 days to sell, which is 12.2% longer than the previous year, scoring 92 out of 100, indicating a healthier housing market than most metros, with a good balance between supply and demand. This relatively fast pace of home sales suggests that the housing market is still active, but not overly competitive, making it easier for workers to find and purchase homes.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is about average, scoring 48 out of 100, compared to other metros, which means it has a moderate-sized talent pool suited for tech, finance, and professional services businesses. This average score suggests that while the city has some strengths in these sectors, it may not be as dominant as other cities, and businesses may need to look elsewhere for specialized talent.

The bottom line is that Baltimore-Columbia-Towson's biggest strength is its affordable cost of living and low unemployment rate, making it an attractive location for businesses and workers alike. However, the city's biggest risk is its slow job growth, which could make it challenging for businesses to expand their operations and attract top talent in the area.
