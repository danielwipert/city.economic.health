Economic Analysis

The Baltimore-Columbia-Towson metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 50.9th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its cost of living and labor force growth. The city's cost of living, with a PSF of $216/sqft and a ratio of 5.84 to hourly earnings of $36.97, ranks in the 88th percentile, indicating a highly affordable environment. Additionally, the labor force growth rate of 1.23% YoY suggests an expanding workforce supply.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is -0.37% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating -0.648% from the city's own 12-month baseline, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 3.78, which ranks in the bottom tier (14th percentile). This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the city is experiencing a decline in job growth and hours worked. This suggests a lack of genuine demand expansion in the labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is 4.00%, ranking in the 60th percentile, which indicates a relatively tight labor market with some slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, this means that while it may be slightly easier to find talent than in tighter markets, there is still some competition for workers, and wage pressure may be moderate.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is +3.93%, ranking near the median (54th percentile), indicating moderate wage growth. This implies that employer labor costs are rising at a moderate pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, but not at an exceptionally fast rate. As a result, businesses may need to balance wage increases with productivity gains to maintain profitability.

Cost of Living

Baltimore-Columbia-Towson has a cost of living ratio of 5.84, with a PSF of $216/sqft (decreasing by 2.3% YoY) relative to hourly earnings of $36.97, ranking in the 88th percentile, indicating a highly affordable environment. This means that the city has a significant talent attraction advantage, as workers can maintain a high standard of living without requiring large wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is growing at a rate of 1.23% YoY, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This implies that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to hire and expand their operations. As a result, the city's labor force growth is a positive factor for businesses considering relocation or expansion.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is decreasing by 2.81% YoY, indicating a tightening of the housing supply. This signals that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact of rising housing costs on their employees' quality of life and affordability.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is 39 days, with a YoY increase of 5.4%, ranking in the 86th percentile, indicating a slower market. For a worker relocating to this city, this means that the housing market is relatively accessible, with homes taking longer to sell, giving buyers more time to find the right property.

Office Economy

Baltimore-Columbia-Towson has an office economy percentile rank of 22, indicating a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This means that the city is less suited for businesses that require a deep pool of specialized office workers, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The Baltimore-Columbia-Towson metro area offers businesses a highly affordable cost of living and an expanding labor force supply, making it an attractive location for companies looking to relocate or expand. However, the city's labor demand contraction and tightening housing supply are significant risks that decision-makers should factor into their location decisions, as they may impact the availability and affordability of talent and housing in the future.
