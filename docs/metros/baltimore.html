Economic Analysis

The Baltimore-Columbia-Towson metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking 51.3th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average wage growth and highly affordable cost of living, scoring 84th percentile. The city's economic character is defined by these two metrics, with wage growth at +4.94% YoY and a cost of living ratio of $214/sqft to $37.54/hr, indicating a relatively affordable environment. This affordability is further underscored by the PSF falling -3.2% YoY.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is -0.10% YoY, combined with weekly hours deviating -0.673% from the city's own 12-month baseline, signaling a contraction in labor demand. This combination indicates a lack of genuine demand expansion, suggesting that the labor market is not experiencing significant growth. The labor demand composite score of 4.16 further supports this, ranking below average at the 22nd percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is 4.20%, placing it near the median at the 56th percentile. This rate suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it somewhat easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, it does not indicate a highly competitive market for workers, which could influence wage negotiations and hiring strategies.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in the area is +4.94% YoY, which is above average, ranking at the 68th percentile. This level of wage growth signifies a moderate to fast increase in labor costs for employers but also indicates strong worker purchasing power. Businesses should factor in rising labor costs, but the positive impact on local consumer demand could offset some of these expenses.

Cost of Living

Baltimore-Columbia-Towson has a cost of living score that ranks at the 84th percentile, indicating it is more affordable compared to its peers. The PSF to earnings ratio of $214/sqft to $37.54/hr, coupled with a -3.2% YoY decrease in PSF, makes the city attractive for talent without necessitating significant wage premiums. This affordability is a talent attraction advantage, potentially easing hiring and relocation processes for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson has grown -0.04% YoY, indicating a nearly stagnant labor force supply. This slight contraction suggests that the workforce supply is not expanding significantly, which could pose a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion plans.

Building Permits

The city has experienced a -10.35% YoY change in residential building permits, signaling a tightening in future housing supply. This decrease in permits suggests that affordability and workforce accommodation might become more challenging in the future, potentially affecting the city's attractiveness for both businesses and workers.

Days on Market

Homes in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson currently sit on the market for 44 days, with a +12.8% YoY increase, ranking at the 94th percentile. This slower market indicates that homes are taking longer to sell, making the city more accessible for relocating workers who have more time to find suitable housing. However, this trend also suggests a less competitive housing market, which could have implications for local demand and economic activity.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson ranks at the 28th percentile, indicating a less deep talent pool in these areas compared to other cities. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring a large professional or office-based workforce, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but might be more appropriate for industries with different workforce needs.

In conclusion, Baltimore-Columbia-Towson offers businesses an environment with above-average wage growth and a highly affordable cost of living, making it attractive for talent acquisition without significant wage premiums. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's below-average labor demand and tightening housing supply, which could impact future hiring capacity and workforce accommodation.
