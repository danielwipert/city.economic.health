Economic Analysis

The Baltimore-Columbia-Towson metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 54.9th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average unemployment rate of 4.00% and strong wage growth of +4.94% year-over-year. The city's economic character is also defined by its cost of living, which is in the 86th percentile for affordability, with a price per square foot of $216 and average hourly earnings of $37.54, resulting in a ratio of 5.75. This affordability, combined with the low unemployment rate, suggests a competitive labor market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is -0.10% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating -0.673% from the city's own 12-month baseline, indicating a contraction in labor demand. This combination signals a lack of genuine demand expansion, as both job growth and hours worked are below trend. The labor demand composite score of 4.16 is in the 22nd percentile, further emphasizing the below-average labor market conditions.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is 4.00%, which is in the 60th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market with some slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, the low unemployment rate means that it may be more challenging to find available workers, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. However, the market is not extremely tight, suggesting that hiring is still feasible with competitive offers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is +4.94%, which is in the 68th percentile, indicating relatively fast wage growth. This rapid wage growth implies that employer labor costs are rising, but it also means that workers have increasing purchasing power, which can benefit local businesses. The strong wage growth suggests a competitive labor market where businesses must offer attractive compensation packages to attract and retain talent.

Cost of Living

Baltimore-Columbia-Towson has a cost of living score in the 86th percentile, making it one of the more affordable cities relative to its peers, with a price per square foot of $216 and average hourly earnings of $37.54. This affordability advantage means that the city can attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to minimize labor costs while still accessing a skilled workforce.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is growing at a rate of +1.23% year-over-year, which is in the 74th percentile, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This growth in the labor force suggests that the city has a increasing pool of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to hire and expand their operations. The positive labor force growth is a significant advantage for companies looking to grow their workforce.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is -2.81%, which is near the median, indicating a slight tightening in housing supply. This decrease in building permits suggests that the city's housing supply may not be expanding fast enough to keep up with demand, potentially leading to future affordability issues and constraints on workforce accommodation. However, the decline is not sharp, suggesting that the impact may be manageable.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Baltimore-Columbia-Towson is 39 days, with a year-over-year increase of +5.4%, which is in the 86th percentile, indicating a slower market. For a worker relocating to this city, the slower market means that there is more time to find a home, making the relocation process less competitive and more accessible. The rising days on market suggests a healthy normalization of the housing market.

Office Economy

Baltimore-Columbia-Towson has an office economy score in the 28th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but may be more appropriate for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing. The city's economy is more industrial or logistics-dominant, which can still support a range of business activities.

The Baltimore-Columbia-Towson metro area offers businesses a unique combination of affordability, relatively low unemployment, and strong wage growth, making it an attractive location for companies looking to balance labor costs with access to a skilled workforce. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's below-average labor demand and tightening housing supply, which may limit the city's ability to support rapid business expansion and require careful planning to ensure adequate workforce accommodation.
