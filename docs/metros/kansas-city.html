Economic Analysis

Kansas City earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 69.3th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 7.38%, and its low unemployment rate of 3.80%, which signals a tight labor market. These metrics suggest a city with a high demand for labor and rising labor costs.

Labor Demand

Kansas City's employment growth rate is 0.69% year-over-year, combined with a 0.321% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a real increase in labor demand, with both more jobs being added and existing workers putting in more hours. This is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand or establish operations in the city.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Kansas City is 3.80%, which is relatively low and indicates a tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. However, this also suggests that the local consumer demand is strong, which can be beneficial for businesses.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Kansas City is 7.38%, which is a fast pace and indicates rising labor costs for employers. This strong wage growth is good for worker purchasing power, as employees have more disposable income to spend on goods and services. However, it also means that businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs in the future.

Cost of Living

Kansas City has a cost of living score that ranks it in the 80th percentile, meaning it is more affordable than most of its peer cities. The city's price-to-salary ratio is $196/sqft vs $36.13/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.42. This affordability makes it an attractive location for talent, as workers can maintain a high standard of living without requiring large wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Kansas City is growing at a rate of 0.34% year-over-year, indicating that the supply of workers is expanding. This growth in the labor force is a positive sign for businesses, as it suggests that there will be a larger pool of potential employees to hire from in the future.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Kansas City has decreased by 5.58% year-over-year, which may indicate a tightening of the housing supply. This decrease in permits could signal future affordability challenges and make it more difficult for workers to find housing, potentially impacting the city's ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Kansas City is 50 days, with no year-over-year change. This relatively fast pace of home sales suggests that the housing market is competitive, and relocating workers may face challenges in finding available homes. However, the stable days on market also indicates a balanced market, which can be beneficial for businesses looking to establish operations in the city.

Office Economy

Kansas City's professional and office worker share ranks in the 18th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city may not be the best fit for businesses that require a deep pool of specialized office or professional talent, such as tech or finance companies. However, it may be more suitable for industries with different labor requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Kansas City offers businesses a strong labor market with rising wages and low unemployment, making it an attractive location for companies looking to expand or establish operations. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the potential tightening of the housing supply, which could impact the city's ability to attract and retain talent in the future.
