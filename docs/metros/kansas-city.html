Economic Analysis

Kansas City earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 68.4th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. The city's economic character is largely defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 7.38%, and its low unemployment rate of 3.70%, which ranks in the 80th percentile. These metrics suggest a highly competitive labor market with rising labor costs.

Labor Demand

Kansas City's employment growth rate of 0.69% and weekly hours deviation of 0.321% above its own trend indicate genuine demand expansion, signaling that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours. This combination suggests a strong labor market with increasing demand for workers. The labor demand composite score of 5.59, ranking in the 66th percentile, further supports this assessment.

Unemployment

The city's unemployment rate of 3.70% is relatively low, ranking in the 80th percentile, which means the labor market is tight. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as workers have more bargaining power and may command higher wages. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 7.38%, Kansas City is experiencing rapid wage increases, ranking in the 98th percentile. This fast wage growth will lead to rising labor costs for employers, but it also means that workers will have increased purchasing power, benefiting local businesses that rely on consumer spending.

Cost of Living

Kansas City's cost of living, with a PSF of $197/sqft and an earnings ratio of $36.13/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.45, ranks in the 80th percentile, indicating that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. This affordability advantage will help attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The city's labor force is growing at a rate of 0.50% year-over-year, ranking in the 60th percentile, which means that the workforce supply is expanding. This growth in the labor force will provide businesses with a larger pool of potential employees, making it easier to hire and staff operations.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -5.58%, ranking in the 44th percentile, indicating that housing supply is tightening. This decrease in building permits may lead to future affordability challenges and constraints on workforce accommodation, potentially limiting the city's ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 47 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -2.1%, ranking in the 14th percentile. This fast-paced market means that homes are selling quickly, making it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. Businesses may need to provide relocation assistance or adjust their hiring timelines to accommodate the competitive housing market.

Office Economy

Kansas City's professional and office worker share ranks in the 18th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This makes the city less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but more suitable for industries with different workforce requirements.

In conclusion, Kansas City offers businesses a highly competitive labor market with strong wage growth and a relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for talent attraction and retention. However, the city's tightening housing supply and competitive housing market pose significant risks and constraints that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating Kansas City as a potential business location.
