Economic Analysis

Kansas City boasts an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 69.3th percentile, ranking it among the top metros in the US. The city's economic character is largely defined by its exceptional wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 7.38%, and its low unemployment rate of 3.80%, which signals a tight labor market. These metrics suggest a city with strong labor demand and a competitive job market.

Labor Demand

Kansas City's employment growth rate of 0.69% and weekly hours deviation of 0.321% above its own trend indicate genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours are increasing. This combination signals a healthy labor market with growing opportunities. The labor demand composite score of 5.59, ranking in the 66th percentile, further supports this assessment.

Unemployment

The city's unemployment rate of 3.80% ranks in the 78th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is relatively small, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 7.38%, Kansas City experiences fast wage growth, ranking in the 98th percentile. This rapid growth implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing purchasing power for workers, which can benefit local businesses.

Cost of Living

Kansas City's cost of living, with a PSF of $196/sqft and an earnings ratio of 5.42, ranks in the 80th percentile, indicating a relatively affordable city. This affordability advantage can attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The city's labor force growth rate of 0.34% indicates a slowly expanding workforce supply. While this growth is modest, it suggests that the labor pool is not contracting, providing a relatively stable hiring environment for businesses.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits of -5.58% signals a tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability concerns and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This decline in permits, ranking in the 44th percentile, is a notable risk factor for businesses considering relocation.

Days on Market

With a median days on market of 50 days and a year-over-year change of 0.0%, Kansas City's housing market is relatively competitive, but not extremely fast-paced. This balance suggests that relocating workers may face some competition, but the market is not overly inaccessible.

Office Economy

Kansas City's professional and office worker share ranks in the 18th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city is better suited for businesses outside of the tech, finance, or consulting industries, and may not be the ideal location for companies requiring a deep knowledge-economy talent pool.

In conclusion, Kansas City offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, low unemployment, and rapid wage growth, making it an attractive location for companies willing to invest in their workforce. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability concerns and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.
