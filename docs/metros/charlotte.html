Economic Analysis

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking at the 67.2th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier ranking of 96th percentile, and its relatively low unemployment rate of 3.70%, ranking at the 84th percentile. The combination of these metrics signals a city with genuine demand expansion and a tight labor market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is +1.54% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating +1.361% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours worked are increasing, indicating a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 7.82 further reinforces this, ranking at the 96th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is 3.70%, which is relatively low and ranks at the 84th percentile. This indicates a tight labor market with limited slack, making it more challenging for businesses to hire new employees. As a result, businesses may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is +4.22%, which is above average and ranks at the 62nd percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a manageable pace. Meanwhile, workers will benefit from increased purchasing power, supporting local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia has a cost of living score ranking at the 78th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $217/sqft to $38.91/hr, or 5.58. The fact that PSF is falling -1.4% year-over-year relative to wages makes the city more affordable. This affordability advantage will help attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is shrinking at a rate of -1.49% year-over-year, ranking at the 40th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to compensate for this shrinking labor pool.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia has decreased by -22.75% year-over-year, ranking at the 12th percentile. This sharp decline in permits signals a tightening housing supply, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for housing in the future. This could negatively impact the city's ability to attract and accommodate a growing workforce.

Days on Market

Unfortunately, the current median days on market is not available for Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia. However, given the city's strong labor market and low unemployment, a rising days on market would likely indicate a healthy normalization of the housing market. Conversely, a falling days on market would suggest a highly competitive market, making it challenging for relocating workers to find housing.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is significant, ranking at the 86th percentile. This deep talent pool makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. However, it may be less ideal for industries with predominantly industrial or logistics-based workforces.

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion, a relatively low unemployment rate, and a moderate wage growth rate. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the shrinking labor force supply and the tightening housing market, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for talent in the future.
