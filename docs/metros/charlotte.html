Economic Analysis

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 73.2th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is largely defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score of 7.47, and its low unemployment rate of 3.60%, which signals a tight labor market. The combination of a 1.57% employment growth rate and a 0.970% weekly hours deviation from its own trend suggests genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has seen a 1.57% employment growth rate and a 0.970% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, indicating a strong labor demand. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This suggests that businesses are not only adding jobs but also increasing the workload for existing employees, indicating a healthy and growing economy.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is 3.60%, which is in the top tier of US metros, ranking 86th percentile. This low unemployment rate indicates a tight labor market, making it harder for businesses to hire and potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. For a business trying to hire in this city, it may be challenging to find available talent, and they may need to offer competitive salaries to attract workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is 4.70%, which is above average, ranking 60th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a manageable pace. At the same time, workers are seeing an increase in their purchasing power, which can have a positive impact on local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia has a cost of living score that ranks 76th percentile, indicating that it is more affordable than many of its peer cities. With a PSF of $220/sqft and average hourly earnings of $38.03/hr, the ratio of 5.78 is relatively low, especially considering that PSF is falling by 0.5% year-over-year. This makes the city an attractive location for talent, as workers can afford a relatively high standard of living without requiring large wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is growing at a rate of 0.33% year-over-year, which is near the median of US metros, ranking 56th percentile. While this growth rate is not exceptionally high, it still indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, providing a modest increase in hiring capacity for businesses.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia has decreased by 5.10% year-over-year, ranking 40th percentile. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is not expanding as quickly as demand, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is currently 57 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.5%. This indicates a slightly slower market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as they have more time to find a suitable home. However, this trend also suggests that the market may be normalizing after a period of rapid growth.

Office Economy

Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking 78th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. On the other hand, the city may be less suitable for industries that rely heavily on industrial or logistics workers.

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area offers businesses a strong labor market, a talented workforce, and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential for future affordability issues and housing supply constraints, which may impact the city's ability to attract and retain top talent.
