Economic Analysis

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area earns an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 73.3th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score of 7.16, and its high office economy share, ranking in the 82nd percentile. The labor demand is driven by a 1.37% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 0.945% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend.

Labor Demand

The combination of a 1.37% employment growth rate and a 0.945% increase in weekly hours signals genuine demand expansion in the Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area. This indicates that the city is experiencing a surge in job creation, with hours worked above the trend, suggesting a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 7.16 further reinforces this notion, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia stands at 3.60%, which corresponds to an above-average percentile rank of 69th. This rate indicates a relatively tight labor market, although not extremely so, suggesting that businesses may face some competition for talent but not to an extreme degree. For a business trying to hire in this market, it may be moderately challenging to find the right candidates, potentially leading to some wage pressure.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is 3.91%, placing it near the median at the 48th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are increasing, but not at an alarming rate, while workers are seeing a modest improvement in their purchasing power. This balance could support a stable business environment, where costs are manageable, and worker satisfaction is maintained.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $221/sqft to $38.36/hr, and a year-over-year decrease of 1.3% in PSF, Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia ranks in the top tier for affordability at the 81st percentile. This means the city is relatively more affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without the need for significant wage premiums. The decreasing PSF relative to wages is a key driver of this affordability, enhancing the city's appeal for both businesses and workers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is growing at a rate of 0.32% year-over-year, indicating a modest expansion in the workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the hiring capacity for businesses is supported by an increasing pool of potential workers, albeit at a slow pace. This slow growth may not fully keep up with demand in a rapidly expanding sector but is generally a positive sign for businesses looking to hire.

Building Permits

The city is seeing an 8.18% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, signaling an expansion in future housing supply. This growth in permits is a positive indicator for future affordability and the ability to accommodate a growing workforce, suggesting that the city is likely to remain attractive to workers and businesses alike in terms of housing availability and costs.

Days on Market

Homes in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia are currently sitting on the market for a median of 51 days, with a year-over-year increase of 6.2%. This increase in days on market, while still relatively fast, suggests a normalization of the housing market, making it slightly more accessible for relocating workers. However, the market remains competitive, and workers may still face challenges in finding and securing housing quickly.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share ranked in the 82nd percentile, Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia boasts a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This makes the city an excellent location for businesses in these sectors, offering access to specialized skills and knowledge. Conversely, it may be less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles, where the talent pool might be less concentrated.

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area offers businesses a strong economy with genuine labor demand expansion, a moderate unemployment rate, and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the potential for moderate wage pressure due to a relatively tight labor market, which could impact labor costs and the competitiveness of businesses in the area.
