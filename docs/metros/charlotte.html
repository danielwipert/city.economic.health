Economic Analysis

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 68.6th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. The city's economic character is largely defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score of 7.35, and its low unemployment rate of 3.60%, which signals a tight labor market. These metrics, combined with a top-tier employment growth rate of +1.36% and weekly hours deviation of +1.141%, indicate a city with genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +1.36% and weekly hours deviation of +1.141% signal a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 7.35, ranked in the top tier at the 94th percentile, further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.60% is relatively low, ranking in the top tier at the 86th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This means that businesses may face challenges in finding and hiring qualified workers, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate also suggests that workers have more bargaining power, which could drive up labor costs.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +4.14% is moderate, ranking near the median at the 56th percentile. This rate suggests that labor costs are rising, but not excessively so, and that workers are seeing some improvement in their purchasing power. However, the moderate wage growth may not be sufficient to keep pace with the low unemployment rate and tight labor market.

Cost of Living

The city's cost of living, with a PSF of $220/sqft and an earnings ratio of $37.80/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.82, is relatively affordable, ranking above average at the 61st percentile. This means that the city has a talent attraction advantage, as workers can afford to live and work in the area without requiring significant wage premiums. The fact that PSF is falling by -0.5% YoY further enhances the city's affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of +0.33% year-over-year, which is near the median at the 56th percentile. This indicates that the labor force supply is expanding, albeit slowly, which should help to alleviate some of the hiring challenges posed by the tight labor market. However, the slow growth rate may not be sufficient to keep pace with the city's job growth.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -23.67%, which is a significant decline, ranking in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability challenges and make it more difficult for workers to relocate to the area. The decline in building permits is a concern for businesses that rely on a steady supply of new talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 57 days, with a year-over-year increase of +7.5%, ranking above average at the 60th percentile. This indicates that the housing market is slowing down, making it more accessible for workers to relocate to the area. However, the relatively fast pace of sales still suggests a competitive market, which may require businesses to offer relocation assistance or other incentives to attract talent.

Office Economy

The city's professional and office worker share is 3.36, ranking in the top tier at the 80th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses that require specialized knowledge workers. However, the city may be less suited for industries that require a large workforce with more general skills.

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion, a tight labor market with limited slack, and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses is the tightening housing supply, which could lead to future affordability challenges and make it more difficult to attract and retain talent.
