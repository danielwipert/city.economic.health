Economic Analysis

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 63.8th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and low unemployment rate being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, at 94th and 80th percentiles, respectively. The labor demand composite score of 7.35, driven by a 1.36% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.141% deviation in weekly hours above trend, signals a genuine demand expansion. This combination of metrics suggests a city with a robust job market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 1.36% and weekly hours deviation of 1.141% above trend indicate a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs at a faster rate than the national average, and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a high level of economic activity. The labor demand composite score of 7.35, at the 94th percentile, reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.70% is relatively low, ranking in the 80th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate also suggests that workers have more bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of 4.14% is moderate, ranking in the 56th percentile, indicating that labor costs are rising, but not excessively so. This rate suggests that workers are seeing some increase in their purchasing power, but employers are not facing extreme pressure on labor costs. The moderate wage growth rate may help attract and retain workers.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia, with a PSF of $218/sqft and an earnings ratio of $37.80/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.77, ranks in the 61st percentile, indicating a relatively affordable city. The fact that PSF is falling by 0.9% year-over-year suggests that the city is becoming even more affordable, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The year-over-year growth rate of the civilian labor force is -0.70%, ranking in the 40th percentile, indicating a contracting labor pool. This suggests that the supply of workers is shrinking, which may create structural headwinds for hiring and business expansion. The negative growth rate may limit the city's ability to accommodate growing businesses.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -23.67%, ranking in the 12th percentile, indicating a sharp decline in housing supply. This suggests that the city's housing market may be tightening, which could lead to affordability issues and challenges in attracting and retaining workers. The low ranking in building permits signals a potential risk for future workforce accommodation.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 61 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.0%, ranking in the 20th percentile, indicating a relatively slow market. This means that homes are taking longer to sell, making it a more accessible market for workers relocating to the city. However, the slow market may also suggest some softening in demand.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers is 3.36, ranking in the 80th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but may be less suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses. The strong office economy is a significant advantage for businesses seeking specialized talent.

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area offers businesses a strong labor market, low unemployment, and a relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for talent and business expansion. However, the single biggest risk or constraint is the sharp decline in building permits, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This risk should be carefully considered by decision-makers evaluating the city as a potential business location.
