Economic Analysis

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 72.9th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. The city's economic character is largely defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score of 7.47, and its relatively low cost of living, with a ratio of $221/sqft to $38.03/hr, resulting in a top-tier percentile rank of 84th. These metrics signal a city with a thriving job market and attractive affordability.

Labor Demand

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has seen employment growth of +1.57% year-over-year, combined with a +0.970% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a strong increase in jobs, with hours worked also rising above trend, pointing to a healthy and growing labor market. The labor demand composite score of 7.47 further reinforces this assessment, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia stands at 3.60%, which corresponds to an above-average percentile rank of 70th. This rate indicates a relatively tight labor market, although not excessively so, suggesting that businesses may face some competition for talent but still have a manageable hiring environment. The practical implication for a business trying to hire in this market is that it may need to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract the best candidates.

Wage Growth

Year-over-year wage growth in the Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area is +4.70%, placing it in the above-average category with a 60th percentile rank. This rate of wage growth is moderate, indicating that employer labor costs are rising but not at an alarming rate. For workers, this means increasing purchasing power, which can contribute to a stronger local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $221/sqft to $38.03/hr and a year-over-year decrease of -1.3% in PSF, Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia ranks in the top tier for affordability at the 84th percentile. This means the city is more affordable relative to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without the need for significant wage premiums. The decreasing PSF relative to wages is a key driver of this high affordability score.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia has grown by +0.32% year-over-year, indicating a slowly expanding workforce supply. Although the growth rate is not robust, it suggests that the labor pool is not contracting, providing a stable foundation for businesses looking to hire. This moderate growth implies that hiring capacity, while not unlimited, is supported by a gradually increasing labor force.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -5.10%, signaling a tightening in housing supply. This decrease, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile, may indicate future challenges in affordability and workforce accommodation, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand. This could potentially impact the city's ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is 51 days, with a year-over-year increase of +6.2%. This indicates a slightly slower market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as it may provide them with more time to find suitable housing. However, the market remains relatively competitive, and relocating workers should still be prepared to act fairly quickly.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia, combined with its growth trend, places it at an above-average percentile rank of 78th. This suggests a deep talent pool suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ operations. The city is less suited for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant, given its stronger orientation towards knowledge-economy roles.

In conclusion, Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia offers businesses a thriving labor market with strong demand, a relatively low cost of living, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for a variety of industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the tightening housing supply, signaled by the decrease in building permits, which could impact future affordability and workforce accommodation.
