Economic Analysis

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score ranking in the 71.4th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 94th percentile, driven by employment growth of 1.36% year-over-year and weekly hours 1.141% above trend. The combination of these metrics signals genuine demand expansion, indicating a healthy and growing job market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 1.36% year-over-year and weekly hours deviation of 1.141% above trend suggest a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, signaling a robust economy. The labor demand composite score of 7.35, ranking in the 94th percentile, further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.60% is relatively low, ranking in the 86th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight labor market also suggests that workers may have more bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of 4.14% is moderate, ranking in the 56th percentile, indicating that labor costs are rising, but not excessively so. This moderate wage growth suggests that employers may face increasing labor costs, while workers will see their purchasing power improve. However, the wage growth is not so high that it would significantly erode profit margins.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is relatively affordable, with a percentile rank of 61, driven by a PSF of $220/sqft and average hourly earnings of $37.80/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.82. This affordability makes the city attractive for talent, as workers can maintain a decent standard of living without requiring excessive wage premiums. The fact that PSF is falling by 0.5% year-over-year further enhances the city's affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of 0.33% year-over-year, ranking in the 56th percentile, indicating a slowly expanding workforce supply. This moderate growth suggests that hiring capacity is improving, but not rapidly, and businesses may still face some challenges in finding talent. However, the growing labor force does provide a positive outlook for future hiring needs.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -5.10%, ranking in the 40th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This decline in permits suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand. This could lead to increased housing costs and reduced attractiveness for relocating workers.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 57 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.5%, ranking in the 60th percentile. This suggests a relatively accessible market for relocating workers, as homes are not selling extremely quickly, providing a more competitive environment for buyers. However, the rising days on market does indicate a slight normalization of the market.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers is 3.36, ranking in the 80th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy talent, but may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion, a relatively affordable cost of living, and a deep talent pool of professional and office workers. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, which may lead to reduced affordability and attractiveness for relocating workers, potentially impacting future hiring capacity and labor costs.
