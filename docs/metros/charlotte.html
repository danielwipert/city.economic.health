Economic Analysis

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 72.4th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score of 7.89, and its low unemployment rate of 3.30%, which signals a tight labor market. The combination of a 1.84% employment growth rate and a 1.012% weekly hours deviation from trend also highlights the city's genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area has seen a 1.84% employment growth rate and a 1.012% weekly hours deviation from trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 7.89, ranked in the 96th percentile, further reinforces this notion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is 3.30%, ranking in the 84th percentile, which indicates a tight labor market with limited slack. This means that businesses may face challenges in finding available workers, and wage pressure may be higher. As a result, companies looking to hire in this city may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The city has experienced a 4.37% year-over-year wage growth rate, ranking in the 64th percentile, which is above average but not exceptionally high. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not at an alarming rate, while workers are seeing an increase in their purchasing power. However, this growth may not be sufficient to keep pace with the city's overall economic expansion.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living percentile rank of 78th, Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is relatively affordable, particularly given its PSF of $216/sqft, which has decreased by 1.8% year-over-year, and an average hourly earnings of $38.60/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.60. This affordability advantage can be a significant talent attraction factor, as workers may not require wage premiums to maintain their standard of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia has grown by 0.69% year-over-year, ranking in the 70th percentile, indicating a moderate expansion of the workforce supply. This growth suggests that the city's hiring capacity is increasing, but at a slower rate than some of its peers. As a result, businesses may still face some challenges in finding available workers, but the situation is not as dire as in cities with contracting labor forces.

Building Permits

The city has seen a 12.73% year-over-year decline in building permits, ranking in the 26th percentile, which signals a tightening of the housing supply. This decrease may lead to future affordability concerns and could impact the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential long-term implications of this trend on their ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia is currently 65 days, with a 10.2% year-over-year increase, ranking in the 10th percentile. This suggests a relatively slow market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as they may have more time to find a suitable home. However, this trend may also indicate a normalization of the market after a period of rapid growth.

Office Economy

With an office economy percentile rank of 86th, Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia has a deep talent pool in professional and office sectors, making it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. However, the city may be less suited for industries that require a large workforce in industrial or logistics roles.

The Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia metro area offers businesses a strong labor market, a talented workforce, and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability concerns and impact the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce.
