Economic Analysis

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 37.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 4.00, which is below average. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth of +6.17% year-over-year and its weak labor force growth of -2.31% year-over-year. These two metrics signal a complex labor market with rising costs and shrinking supply.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate is +0.65% year-over-year, while weekly hours are -1.215% below the city's own trend, indicating a labor demand composite score of 4.00, which is below average. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than genuine demand expansion or survivor squeeze. The below-average labor demand suggests that the city's job market is not as robust as others.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate is 4.60%, which is near the median, ranking 40th percentile. This rate indicates a labor market with some slack, rather than being extremely tight. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate means that there may be a decent pool of available workers, but wage pressure is not extremely high.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate is +6.17%, which is in the top tier, ranking 86th percentile. This fast wage growth rate implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing worker purchasing power. As a result, businesses may face higher labor costs, but workers will have more disposable income to spend in the local economy.

Cost of Living

The city's cost of living is near median, with a percentile rank of 41st, and a PSF to earnings ratio of $335/sqft to $35.56/hr, which is 9.42. The fact that PSF is falling -2.3% year-over-year is a key driver of the city's affordability. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which can be a talent attraction advantage without requiring wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The year-over-year growth rate of the civilian labor force is -2.31%, which is in the bottom tier, ranking 6th percentile. This contracting labor force supply implies that the city's hiring capacity may be constrained, making it challenging for businesses to find and retain workers.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -9.60%, which is below average, ranking 32nd percentile. This decline in building permits signals that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 59 days, with a year-over-year increase of +1.7%. This indicates a relatively slow market, which can be accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, the rising days on market may also signal a normalization of the housing market, rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

The city's professional and office worker share is 0.22, which is in the bottom tier, ranking 0th percentile. This suggests that the city's talent pool is not particularly deep in professional and office sectors, making it less suited for tech, finance, or consulting businesses. Instead, the city may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area offers a complex labor market with strong wage growth, but also shrinking labor force supply and tightening housing market. The single biggest risk or constraint for a business decision-maker is the city's limited labor force growth, which may lead to hiring challenges and rising labor costs. Despite these challenges, the city's relatively affordable cost of living and accessible housing market may still attract workers and businesses looking for a more affordable alternative.
