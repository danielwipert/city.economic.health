Economic Analysis

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area has an overall grade of C- with a composite score ranking it 35.4th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its high wage growth rate of 5.51% year-over-year and its low unemployment rate of 5.90%, which signals a tight labor market. The combination of these metrics suggests that the city is experiencing a period of strong labor demand, but with significant constraints on hiring and talent attraction.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is 0.75% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.149% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a moderate expansion of labor demand, with some genuine demand for new jobs, but not at a rate that is significantly above trend. The labor demand composite score of 5.20 ranks near the median at 44th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is 5.90%, ranking in the bottom tier at 6th percentile. This indicates a very tight labor market with little slack, making it challenging for businesses to hire new workers. As a result, businesses may face upward pressure on wages to attract and retain talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is 5.51%, ranking above average at 78th percentile. This rapid wage growth signals rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing purchasing power for workers. Businesses should factor in the potential for higher labor costs when considering locating in this city.

Cost of Living

Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario has a cost of living score ranking below average at 35th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $330/sqft to $35.45/hr, or 9.31. Although the city's PSF is falling -1.5% year-over-year, the overall affordability is still relatively low. This means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is contracting at a rate of -1.74% year-over-year, ranking below average at 32nd percentile. This decline in labor force supply signals a structural headwind for hiring, making it even more challenging for businesses to find the talent they need.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is falling sharply at -19.99% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at 14th percentile. This decline in building permits signals a tightening of housing supply, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for housing in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is 64 days, with a year-over-year decline of -4.5%, ranking near the median at 54th percentile. This relatively fast pace of home sales may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing, potentially affecting a business's ability to attract talent.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is relatively low, ranking in the bottom tier at 0th percentile. This suggests that the city's talent pool is not particularly deep in specialized office roles, making it less suited for businesses in the tech, finance, or consulting sectors.

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area offers businesses a unique combination of high wage growth and low unemployment, but also presents significant challenges in terms of hiring and talent attraction due to its tight labor market and high cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's declining labor force supply and tightening housing market, which may limit the availability of skilled workers and drive up labor costs.
