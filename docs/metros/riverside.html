Economic Analysis

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 36.2th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low unemployment rate and high wage growth. The city's economic character is most defined by its 5.70% unemployment rate and 6.02% year-over-year wage growth, indicating a tight labor market with rising labor costs. These metrics suggest that the city's economy is experiencing a mix of positive and negative trends.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is 0.79% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.845% from the city's own 12-month baseline, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 4.56. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the modest job growth is accompanied by a decrease in hours worked. This suggests that the city's labor market is not experiencing genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is 5.70%, which is relatively high, indicating a labor market with some slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city may have an easier time finding candidates, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The practical implication is that companies may need to consider the potential impact of a relatively high unemployment rate on their hiring and sales strategies.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is 6.02%, which is relatively fast. This suggests that employer labor costs are rising, while worker purchasing power is increasing. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs when considering this location, while also benefiting from a workforce with increasing disposable income.

Cost of Living

Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario has a cost of living score that ranks 35th percentile, with a PSF of $332/sqft and average hourly earnings of $35.60/hr, resulting in a ratio of 9.33. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive, which may make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is falling -1.2% year-over-year is a positive sign, but the overall affordability score remains below average.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is contracting at a rate of -1.75% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This implies that the city's hiring capacity may be constrained, making it more challenging for businesses to find the talent they need. As a result, companies may need to consider alternative locations or strategies to attract workers.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is decreasing by -13.30% year-over-year, indicating a tightening of the housing supply. This signals that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand. As a result, businesses may need to factor in the potential impact of a tightening housing market on their ability to attract and retain workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario is 63 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -4.5%. This suggests that the housing market is relatively competitive, but still accessible for workers relocating to the city. The fact that days on market are decreasing indicates a relatively hot market, which may make it challenging for workers to find housing.

Office Economy

Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario has a professional and office worker share that ranks 0th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This suggests that the city is best suited for businesses that do not require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics companies. On the other hand, the city may not be the best fit for tech, finance, or consulting firms that require a highly specialized workforce.

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market with rising labor costs, and a relatively expensive cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's shrinking labor pool, which may limit the availability of talent and make it challenging for businesses to grow and expand. As a result, companies should carefully consider the potential implications of this trend when evaluating the city as a potential location.
