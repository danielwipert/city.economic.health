Economic Analysis

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 39.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 6.02%, and its struggling labor demand, with employment growth of only 0.79% and weekly hours 0.845% below trend. These metrics signal a mixed economic environment, with some positive trends but also significant challenges.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 0.79% is modest, and when combined with weekly hours 0.845% below trend, it signals a labor demand environment that is not expanding genuinely. This suggests that while some jobs are being added, the overall labor market is not experiencing significant growth, and hours are not increasing as they would in a strong demand environment. This combination may indicate a degree of survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 5.30% indicates a labor market with some slack, ranking below average at the 32nd percentile. This means that businesses may find it somewhat easier to hire in this market, as there are more potential candidates available. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand, which could impact businesses reliant on local spending.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 6.02%, this city is experiencing fast wage growth, ranking in the top tier at the 88th percentile. This rapid wage growth will lead to rising labor costs for employers but will also increase worker purchasing power, potentially benefiting local businesses that rely on consumer spending.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in this city, with a PSF of $333/sqft and average hourly earnings of $35.60, resulting in a ratio of 9.35, is below average in terms of affordability, ranking at the 35th percentile. This means the city is not particularly affordable relative to its peers, which could make it harder to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -1.87% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction signals a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing, making it more challenging for businesses to find the talent they need.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -9.60%, indicating that housing supply is tightening. This sharp decline in building permits signals a potential supply squeeze ahead, which could lead to decreased affordability and make it more difficult for the workforce to find accommodation, ultimately affecting the city's ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

Homes are currently sitting on the market for 61 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -1.6%. This relatively fast market, with homes selling quickly, could make it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing, potentially impacting a business's ability to attract talent from other areas.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share ranking at the 0th percentile, this city has a very shallow professional talent pool. This makes it less suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, or HQ sectors that rely on deep knowledge-economy talent pools, but it may be more appropriate for industries with different workforce requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

The Riverside-San Bernardino-Ontario metro area offers businesses a mixed bag, with strong wage growth being a significant positive, but it also presents challenges such as a shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker considering this location is the difficulty in attracting and retaining talent due to the combination of a less affordable cost of living and a shrinking labor pool, which could hinder a business's growth and operational capacity.
