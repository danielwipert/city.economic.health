Economic Analysis

Birmingham has an overall grade of C-, with a score of 34.3 out of 100, indicating it is struggling compared to other metros. The city's economy is defined by slow job growth, with employment increasing by 1.2% over the past year, and a relatively high unemployment rate of 3.5%, which is higher than most metros.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

Birmingham is adding jobs, with a 1.2% increase in employment over the past year, and workers' hours are steady, with no change from the 12-month average. This suggests a relatively stable job market, with a score of 68, indicating stronger hiring than most metros.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Birmingham is 3.5%, which is higher than most metros, making it easier for employers to hire, but also indicating a weaker local economy, with a score of 36. This higher unemployment rate means that employers may have an easier time finding workers, but it also suggests that the local consumer demand may be weaker.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay in Birmingham rose by -0.4% over the past year, which is slower than most metros, with a score of 2, indicating weaker wage growth. This slow wage growth means that employers may face lower labor costs, but workers may see little to no increase in their paychecks.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

Housing in Birmingham is relatively expensive, with a price of $157 per square foot, compared to an hourly pay of $32.33, and the price per square foot is falling by 0.6% over the past year, but the city still scores 29, indicating it is less affordable than most metros. This means that workers may struggle to afford housing, and employers may need to offer higher wages to attract and retain talent.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in Birmingham is shrinking, with a -0.7% change over the past year, and a score of 34, indicating slower workforce growth than most metros. This decline in the labor force means that employers may face challenges in finding workers, and the local economy may struggle to grow.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in Birmingham have decreased by 23.8% over the past year, with a score of 10, indicating weaker home-building than most metros. This decline in building permits suggests that the future housing supply may be limited, which could lead to higher housing costs and make it harder for workers to afford homes.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Birmingham are taking 66 days to sell, which is 10% longer than the previous year, and the city scores 8, indicating a weaker housing market. This slower pace of home sales suggests a softening housing market, which could make it harder for workers to relocate to the area.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

Birmingham has an office-based workforce that is about average compared to other metros, with a score of 52, indicating a moderate presence of tech, finance, and professional services. This makes the city suitable for businesses that require a mix of office-based and other types of workers, but may not be as attractive to companies that require a large pool of specialized office workers.

The bottom line is that Birmingham's biggest strength is its relatively stable job market, with stronger hiring than most metros, but its biggest risk is its slow wage growth and limited housing supply, which could make it harder to attract and retain workers. Overall, the city's economy is facing challenges, and employers and workers alike may need to adapt to a slower-growing and more competitive job market.
