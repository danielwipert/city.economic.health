Economic Analysis

Birmingham, with an overall grade of B- and a composite score ranking it 45.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, is characterized by its near-median labor demand and low wage growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.35, combining a +0.65% employment growth rate and a +0.140% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, suggests a genuine but modest demand expansion. The low wage growth rate of +0.57% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, is a key factor defining the city's economic character.

Labor Demand

Birmingham's employment growth rate of +0.65% and weekly hours deviation of +0.140% from its own trend indicate a moderate expansion of labor demand, signaling genuine demand for workers rather than a survivor squeeze. This combination suggests that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours than the recent average, a positive sign for businesses looking to hire. However, the near-median ranking of this metric suggests that labor demand is not exceptionally strong.

Unemployment

The city's unemployment rate of 3.70% ranks in the near-median 42nd percentile, indicating a relatively balanced labor market with some slack. This rate suggests that while it may not be extremely difficult to hire workers, there is still some competition for talent. For businesses trying to hire in Birmingham, this means they may need to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract top candidates.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of +0.57%, Birmingham ranks in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. For businesses, this could mean a relatively flat cost environment, but it may also limit the attractiveness of the city to workers seeking better compensation.

Cost of Living

Birmingham's cost of living, with a PSF of $160/sqft and an earnings ratio of 4.88, ranks in the near-median 57th percentile, suggesting that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The fact that PSF is falling by -1.2% year-over-year further supports this affordability. This means that businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The city's labor force is contracting at a rate of -0.28% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 38th percentile. This decline in the labor force supply implies that hiring capacity may be constrained in the future, making it potentially more challenging for businesses to find the workers they need.

Building Permits

Birmingham is experiencing a significant increase in residential building permits, with a year-over-year change of +31.24%, ranking in the top tier at the 84th percentile. This expansion in housing supply suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation are likely to improve, making the city more attractive to relocating workers and businesses.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Birmingham is 58 days, with a year-over-year increase of +1.8%. This near-median ranking suggests a relatively balanced housing market that is not extremely competitive or accessible. For workers relocating to the city, this means they may have a moderate amount of time to find a home without feeling undue pressure.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share ranking in the near-median 46th percentile, Birmingham has a moderately deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. However, the city may be less suited for industries requiring a large pool of specialized or industrial workers.

In conclusion, Birmingham offers businesses a relatively affordable cost of living and a moderate labor market, but its slow wage growth and contracting labor force supply are significant concerns. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential difficulty in attracting and retaining top talent due to the stagnant wage growth, which could limit the city's appeal to workers and businesses alike.
