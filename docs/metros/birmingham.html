Economic Analysis

Birmingham has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 48.4th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, with a composite score of 5.63 ranking it in the 76th percentile, and its stagnant wage growth, with a year-over-year change of +0.37% ranking it in the 8th percentile. These metrics suggest a city with a strong job market but limited upward pressure on wages.

Labor Demand

Birmingham's employment growth rate is +0.63% year-over-year, and its weekly hours deviation from trend is +0.445%, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours than usual, suggesting a strong and growing labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.63 reinforces this interpretation, ranking the city in the 76th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Birmingham is 3.20%, ranking it in the 50th percentile, indicating a near-median level of slack in the labor market. This rate suggests that the market is neither extremely tight nor extremely loose, with some availability of workers for hire. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate implies a manageable but not overly competitive hiring environment.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Birmingham is +0.37%, ranking it in the 8th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies limited bargaining power for workers and potentially weak purchasing power. As a result, businesses may face a relatively flat cost environment, but workers may not have strong incentives to relocate to the city.

Cost of Living

Birmingham has a cost of living percentile rank of 61, with a PSF of $160/sqft and average hourly earnings of $32.80/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.88. The city's PSF is falling by -1.8% year-over-year, making it more affordable relative to peers. This affordability advantage could attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to hire.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Birmingham is shrinking at a rate of -0.77% year-over-year, ranking it in the 42nd percentile. This contraction in labor force supply implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may face challenges in finding qualified candidates, potentially limiting their growth prospects in the city.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Birmingham is -8.10%, ranking it in the 36th percentile. This decline in permits suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for workforce accommodation in the future. This trend may pose a risk to businesses relying on a steady supply of skilled workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Birmingham is 54 days, with a year-over-year change of +0.0%, ranking it in the 30th percentile. This relatively fast market implies that homes are selling quickly, making it challenging for relocating workers to find available housing. However, the stable year-over-year direction suggests that the market is not becoming significantly more competitive.

Office Economy

Birmingham's office economy has a percentile rank of 52, indicating a near-median depth of professional talent pool. The city's share of jobs in professional and office sectors is 2.76, suggesting a moderate presence of specialized roles. This city is best suited for businesses that do not require a highly specialized or deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as industrial or logistics-dominant companies.

In conclusion, Birmingham offers a strong labor market with above-average demand, but its stagnant wage growth and tightening housing supply pose significant risks for businesses. The city's affordability advantage and moderate unemployment rate make it an attractive location for certain types of businesses, but decision-makers should carefully consider the potential challenges in finding and accommodating skilled workers. Overall, Birmingham's economic character is complex, and businesses must weigh the benefits and drawbacks carefully when considering a location decision.
