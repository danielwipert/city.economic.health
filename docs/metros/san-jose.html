Economic Analysis

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 42.4th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong labor demand and deep office economy. The city's labor demand composite score of 7.81, which combines a 1.59% employment growth rate and a 1.289% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, signals genuine demand expansion. The unemployment rate and cost of living metrics, however, paint a more nuanced picture, with a 4.30% unemployment rate and a cost of living ratio of 15.31, driven by a $827/sqft housing price and $54.03/hr average wage.

Labor Demand

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a strong labor demand, with a 1.59% employment growth rate and a 1.289% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, a positive sign for businesses looking to expand. The labor demand composite score of 7.81 is in the top tier, ranking 94th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is 4.30%, ranking 56th percentile, indicating a near-median level of slack in the labor market. This means that while the market is not extremely tight, it is still relatively competitive, and businesses may face some challenges in hiring. However, the unemployment rate is not so high that it would indicate a large pool of available workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is -0.78%, ranking 0th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power is also not increasing. The stagnant wage growth may make it easier for businesses to control labor costs, but it may also limit the attractiveness of the city to workers.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is relatively expensive, with a ratio of 15.31, driven by a $827/sqft housing price and $54.03/hr average wage, ranking 0th percentile. This means that the city is less affordable than many of its peers, which may make it harder to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The high cost of living may be a significant constraint for businesses looking to locate in the area.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is contracting, with a -1.08% year-over-year growth rate, ranking 52nd percentile. This means that the supply of workers is shrinking, which may make it harder for businesses to find the talent they need. The contracting labor force may be a structural headwind for hiring in the area.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is falling sharply, with a -43.67% year-over-year change, ranking 0th percentile. This indicates that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to future affordability and workforce accommodation challenges. The decline in building permits may signal a supply squeeze ahead, making it harder for workers to find housing.

Days on Market

The median days on market in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is 35 days, with a -2.8% year-over-year change, ranking 12th percentile. This means that homes are selling relatively quickly, making it a competitive market for workers relocating to the area. However, the fast-paced market may also indicate a strong demand for housing, which could drive up prices.

Office Economy

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy percentile rank of 64th. This makes it well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors, but less suited for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses. The city's strong office economy is a significant advantage for businesses looking to tap into a knowledgeable and skilled workforce.

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area offers a strong labor demand and a deep office economy, making it an attractive location for businesses in the tech and finance sectors. However, the high cost of living, stagnant wage growth, and tightening housing supply are significant constraints that businesses should factor into their decision-making. The single biggest risk for businesses locating in the area is the high cost of living, which may require wage premiums to attract and retain talent.
