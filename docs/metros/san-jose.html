Economic Analysis

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has an overall grade of C, with a score of 40.3 out of 100, indicating a very poor performance compared to other metros. The city's economy is currently defined by its strong job market, with employment growth of 1.6% over the past year, but also by its high cost of living, with a price per square foot of $821 and hourly pay of $53.28.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.6% over the past year, and workers' hours are rising by 1.1%, indicating a strong demand for labor. This means that the job market is healthy, with employers hiring more workers and existing employees working more hours, a strength that sets it apart from most metros, with a score of 92 out of 100.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate is 4.2%, which is about average for the 50 metros, making it neither particularly easy nor hard for employers to hire. With a score of 48 out of 100, the city's unemployment rate is not a significant advantage or disadvantage compared to other metros.

Wage Growth

Pay rose by -0.5% over the past year, which is slower than most metros, with a score of 0 out of 100, indicating a weakness. This slow wage growth means that employers' labor costs are not increasing as quickly, but it also means that workers' paychecks are not growing as fast as they are in other cities.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is high, with a price per square foot of $821 and hourly pay of $53.28, and it is getting worse, with a score of 0 out of 100, indicating a weakness. The price per square foot has fallen by 2.0% over the past year, but it remains relatively high compared to other metros, making it less affordable for local workers.

Labor Force Growth

The pool of workers is shrinking by 0.3% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 48 out of 100. This slow growth means that hiring may become more challenging in the future, as the labor force is not expanding quickly.

Building Permits

New home approvals have fallen by 50.0% over the past year, which is a significant decline, with a score of 2 out of 100, indicating a weakness. This sharp drop in building permits signals that the future housing supply may be limited, which could exacerbate affordability issues and make it harder for workers to find homes.

Days on Market

A typical home takes 37 days to sell, which is getting slightly faster, with a decline of 2.6% over the past year, but the score of 12 out of 100 indicates a weakness. This fast pace of home sales may make it challenging for relocating workers to find a home, as the housing market is relatively tight.

Office Economy

The city has a large office-based workforce, with a score of 70 out of 100, indicating a strength, and this makes it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. The city's strong office economy is well-suited for companies that require a deep pool of skilled workers in these fields.

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area's biggest strength is its strong labor demand, with a score of 92 out of 100, indicating a healthy job market with plenty of opportunities for workers. However, the city's biggest risk is its high cost of living, with a score of 0 out of 100, which may make it difficult for workers to afford homes and for employers to attract and retain talent.
