Economic Analysis

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking 54.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong labor demand and building permits growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 7.01, combining a 1.34% employment growth rate and a 0.839% weekly hours deviation above trend, signals genuine demand expansion. The unemployment rate and cost of living metrics also play a significant role in defining the city's economic character, with an unemployment rate of 4.10% and a cost of living ratio of 15.08.

Labor Demand

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a labor demand composite score of 7.01, driven by a 1.34% employment growth rate and a 0.839% weekly hours deviation above trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, indicating that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours than usual. This suggests a strong and growing economy, with businesses likely to face competition for talent.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area is 4.10%, ranking in the 70th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as there are fewer unemployed workers available to fill open positions. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area is 0.51%, ranking in the 8th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth may help keep labor costs under control for employers, but it also means that workers may have limited bargaining power and purchasing power. As a result, businesses may not need to worry about rapidly rising labor costs, but they may also struggle to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a cost of living ratio of 15.08, with a price per square foot of $811 and average hourly earnings of $53.77, ranking in the 2nd percentile, indicating that the city is relatively expensive. However, the price per square foot is falling by 1.3% year-over-year, which may help improve affordability in the future. This high cost of living may make it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums, as the cost of living is higher than in many other cities.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area is growing at a rate of -0.48% year-over-year, ranking near the median, indicating a slightly contracting labor pool. This means that the supply of workers is not expanding as quickly as demand, which may lead to hiring challenges and upward pressure on wages. As a result, businesses may need to be strategic in their hiring efforts and consider offering competitive compensation packages to attract top talent.

Building Permits

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has seen a 120.21% year-over-year increase in building permits, ranking in the 98th percentile, indicating a rapid expansion of housing supply. This growth in building permits suggests that the city is investing in its future and preparing for an influx of new residents and workers. As a result, businesses may find it easier to attract and retain talent in the future, as the housing market becomes more affordable and accessible.

Days on Market

The median days on market in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area is 36 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 10.0%, ranking in the 2nd percentile, indicating a very competitive market. This means that homes are selling quickly, and workers relocating to the area may face challenges in finding affordable housing. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering relocation assistance or other benefits to help attract and retain top talent.

Office Economy

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy percentile rank of 70, indicating a strong presence of tech, finance, and consulting industries. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses in these sectors, as they can tap into a highly skilled and experienced workforce. However, the city may be less suited for industries that require a large workforce with limited specialized skills.

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area offers businesses a strong and growing economy, with a highly skilled workforce and a rapidly expanding housing supply. However, the city's high cost of living and competitive housing market may pose challenges for businesses looking to attract and retain top talent, making it essential for decision-makers to factor in these costs and develop strategies to mitigate them. Overall, the city's strengths make it an attractive location for businesses in the tech and finance sectors, but its weaknesses require careful consideration and planning.
