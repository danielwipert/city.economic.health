Economic Analysis

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has an overall grade of C, with a score of 40.3 out of 100, indicating it is a very poor performer compared to other metros. What most defines this city's economy right now is its strong job growth, with employment increasing by 1.65% over the past year, and workers putting in 1.1% more hours, but this is paired with a high cost of living and stagnant wages.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.65% over the past year, with workers also putting in 1.1% more hours, indicating a strong demand for labor. This means that the job market is active, with employers looking to hire and workers seeing more opportunities, earning the city a top-tier score of 92, better than most of the 50 metros.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate is 4.20%, which is near the median, earning a score of 48, indicating that it is neither particularly easy nor hard for employers to hire. This rate suggests that while there are still workers available, the labor market is not overly loose, making it a relatively neutral environment for hiring.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Wages have actually fallen by 0.50% over the past year, earning a bottom-tier score of 0, which means that employers' labor costs are not increasing, but workers' paychecks are not growing either. This stagnant wage growth is a weakness for the city, as it may make it harder to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is high, with a price per square foot of $821 and hourly pay of $53.28, earning a bottom-tier score of 0, indicating that housing is not affordable for local workers compared to other metros. The fact that the price per square foot is falling by 2.0% over the past year is a slight positive, but overall, the high cost of living remains a significant challenge.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force is shrinking by 0.34% over the past year, earning a near-median score of 48, which means that the pool of available workers is decreasing, making it slightly harder for employers to find new hires. This decline in the labor force is a concern, as it may limit the city's ability to support growing businesses.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals have fallen by 50.02% over the past year, earning a bottom-tier score of 2, which signals that the future housing supply is likely to be constrained, making it even harder for workers to find affordable housing. This sharp decline in building permits is a significant risk, as it may exacerbate the city's existing housing affordability challenges.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes are selling in just37days, with the days on market falling by 2.6% over the past year, earning a bottom-tier score of 12, indicating a very tight housing market that is not healthy for relocating workers. This fast pace of home sales makes it difficult for buyers to find and purchase homes, which can be a challenge for employers looking to attract new talent to the area.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a large office-based workforce, with a score of 70, better than most of the 50 metros, which makes it well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This strong office economy is a significant strength, as it provides a deep pool of skilled workers and a vibrant business community.

The bottom line is that San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara's biggest strength is its strong job growth and large office-based workforce, but its biggest risk is its high cost of living and stagnant wages, which may make it harder to attract and retain top talent. Overall, the city's poor overall grade and low scores in key areas like wage growth and affordability make it a challenging location for businesses and workers alike.
