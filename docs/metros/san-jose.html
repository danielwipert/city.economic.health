Economic Analysis

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 65.0th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and a thriving office economy being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, driven by a 96th percentile labor demand composite score and an 84th percentile office/professional worker share. The city's labor demand is characterized by a 1.52% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.801% deviation in weekly hours above its own 12-month trend. These numbers signal a genuine demand expansion, with jobs being added and hours running above trend.

Labor Demand

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a labor demand composite score of 7.93, driven by a 1.52% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 1.801% deviation in weekly hours above its own 12-month trend, indicating a strong and growing labor market. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, with jobs being added and hours running above trend, suggesting a healthy and vibrant economy. The top-tier percentile rank of 96th percentile further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area is 3.50%, ranking in the 86th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure and competition for talent. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract and retain workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area is 0.98%, ranking in the 12th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth implies a flat cost environment for employers, but weak bargaining power for workers, which may impact worker purchasing power and overall consumer demand. The low percentile rank suggests that wage growth is a concern in this market.

Cost of Living

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a cost of living score that ranks in the 10th percentile, with a PSF of $813/sqft and an average hourly earnings of $55.01/hr, resulting in a ratio of 14.78, indicating an expensive market. However, the PSF is falling by 4.9% year-over-year, which is a key driver of affordability. Despite this, the city's high cost of living may require businesses to offer wage premiums to attract talent, making it challenging to maintain a competitive edge.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area is growing at a rate of -0.76% year-over-year, ranking in the 44th percentile, indicating a contracting labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of workers is shrinking, making it challenging for businesses to find and retain talent. The near-median percentile rank suggests that labor force growth is a moderate concern in this market.

Building Permits

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has seen a 112.94% year-over-year increase in building permits, ranking in the 96th percentile, indicating a rapid expansion of housing supply. This surge in building permits signals a future improvement in affordability and workforce accommodation, as more housing units become available, which may help to alleviate some of the pressure on the labor market. The top-tier percentile rank reinforces this positive outlook.

Days on Market

The median days on market in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area is 37 days, with an 8.8% year-over-year increase, ranking in the 86th percentile, indicating a relatively slow market. This slower market implies a more accessible and competitive environment for workers relocating to the city, as they have more time to find and secure housing. The top-tier percentile rank suggests that the market is favorable for relocating workers.

Office Economy

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share ranking in the 84th percentile, indicating a strong presence of tech, finance, and consulting industries. This city is well-suited for businesses that require a highly skilled and specialized workforce, but may be less suitable for industries that require a large, unskilled labor force. The high percentile rank reinforces the city's strengths in the office economy.

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area offers businesses a strong labor market, a thriving office economy, and a rapidly expanding housing supply, making it an attractive location for companies that require a highly skilled workforce. However, the city's high cost of living and stagnant wage growth pose significant risks and constraints, particularly for businesses that require a large, unskilled labor force or struggle to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract and retain talent.
