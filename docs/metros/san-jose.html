Economic Analysis

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 58.1th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier composite score of 7.01, and its extremely high cost of living, ranked at the 4th percentile. The labor demand is driven by a 1.34% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 0.839% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend.

Labor Demand

The combination of a 1.34% employment growth rate and a 0.839% increase in weekly hours above trend signals genuine demand expansion in the San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area. This indicates that the city is experiencing a surge in job creation and workers are putting in more hours, suggesting a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 7.01, ranked at the 90th percentile, further reinforces this notion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara stands at 4.00%, ranking at the 72nd percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in finding available workers, and wage pressure is likely to be higher. As a result, companies looking to hire in this area may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is 0.51%, ranked at the 8th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also means that workers may have weaker bargaining power. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but workers may not have the same level of purchasing power as in other areas.

Cost of Living

San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara has a cost of living ranked at the 4th percentile, with a PSF of $797/sqft and an average hourly earnings of $53.77/hr, resulting in a ratio of 14.82. This indicates that the city is extremely expensive relative to peers. However, the fact that PSF is falling by 3.6% year-over-year is a key driver of its relatively low affordability score. Despite this, the high cost of living may make it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is contracting at a rate of -0.60% year-over-year, ranked at the 32nd percentile. This decline in labor force supply may pose a structural headwind for hiring, making it more challenging for businesses to find the workers they need. As a result, companies may need to consider strategies to attract workers from other areas or invest in training and development programs.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara has increased by 120.21% year-over-year, ranked at the 98th percentile. This surge in building permits suggests that housing supply is expanding rapidly, which may lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market in San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara is 40 days, with a year-over-year increase of 8.1%, ranked at the 88th percentile. This indicates a relatively slow market, which may be beneficial for workers relocating to the area as they have more time to find a home. However, this also suggests that the housing market may be normalizing, which could lead to more stable and predictable conditions for businesses and workers alike.

Office Economy

San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara has a professional and office worker share ranked at the 70th percentile, with a composite score of 3.10. This indicates a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. The city is well-suited for businesses that require specialized knowledge-economy workers, but may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area offers businesses a strong labor demand and a deep talent pool, making it an attractive location for companies in the tech and finance sectors. However, the high cost of living and stagnant wage growth may pose challenges for businesses looking to attract and retain talent, and the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential difficulty in finding available workers due to the contracting labor force supply.
