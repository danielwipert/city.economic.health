Economic Analysis

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking at the 59.4th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a top-tier percentile rank of 94th, and its extremely high cost of living, ranked at the 4th percentile. The labor demand is driven by a combination of employment growth and hours worked above trend, signaling genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has an employment growth rate of 1.44% year-over-year and a weekly hours deviation of 1.369% above its own trend. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, with more jobs being added and hours worked above the historical baseline. The labor demand composite score of 7.69 further reinforces this trend, indicating a strong and growing labor market.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 4.00%, ranking at the 72nd percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight labor market also suggests that workers may have more bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in this metro area is 0.27%, ranking at the 8th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies that workers may not have strong bargaining power or increasing purchasing power. The slow wage growth may be a concern for businesses that rely on a highly skilled and motivated workforce.

Cost of Living

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a cost of living ratio of $797/sqft to $53.33/hr, with a year-over-year decrease of 3.6% in PSF, ranking at the 4th percentile. This indicates that the city is extremely expensive relative to peers, making it challenging to attract talent without offering significant wage premiums. The high cost of living may be a major constraint for businesses considering relocation to this area.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in this metro area is shrinking at a rate of -0.60% year-over-year, ranking at the 32nd percentile. This contraction in labor force supply may create structural headwinds for hiring, making it more challenging for businesses to find the talent they need. The shrinking labor force may also lead to increased competition for workers, driving up wages and labor costs.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in this metro area has increased by 132.71% year-over-year, ranking at the 98th percentile. This rapid expansion in housing supply is a positive sign for future affordability and workforce accommodation, indicating that the city is investing in its infrastructure to support growing demand. The increase in building permits may help to alleviate some of the pressure on the housing market and make it more accessible to relocating workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in this metro area is 40 days, with a year-over-year increase of 8.1%, ranking at the 88th percentile. This indicates a relatively slow market, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the area. The slower market may give buyers more negotiating power and time to find the right home, reducing some of the stress associated with relocating to a new city.

Office Economy

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share of 3.40, ranking at the 82nd percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which rely on highly skilled and specialized workers. The city's strong office economy is a major advantage for businesses that need to tap into a talented and knowledgeable workforce.

The San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, a deep professional talent pool, and a growing housing supply. However, the extremely high cost of living and stagnant wage growth are major concerns that businesses should factor into their decision-making process, as they may need to offer significant wage premiums to attract and retain talent in this competitive market.
