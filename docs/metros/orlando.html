Economic Analysis

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has earned an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 61.0th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the 72nd percentile, and its top-tier wage growth, which stands at 6.00% year-over-year. These metrics signal a city with a robust job market and increasing labor costs.

Labor Demand

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has seen employment growth of 0.96% year-over-year, combined with a 0.635% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours worked are increasing, indicating a strong and growing labor market. This suggests that businesses can expect to find a vibrant and dynamic workforce in the area.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford stands at 4.20%, which ranks in the 32nd percentile, indicating a below-average level of unemployment. This tight labor market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as well as upward pressure on wages. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract top talent in this market.

Wage Growth

With a year-over-year wage growth rate of 6.00%, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is experiencing rapid labor cost increases, ranking in the 86th percentile. This fast wage growth is a double-edged sword, as it benefits workers' purchasing power but also increases labor costs for employers. Businesses should factor in these rising costs when considering relocation or expansion in the area.

Cost of Living

Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a cost of living score that ranks in the 65th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $222/sqft to $33.42/hr, or 6.64. The fact that PSF is falling by 2.6% year-over-year relative to wages makes the city more affordable. This affordability advantage can be a significant talent attraction factor, as workers can maintain a high standard of living without requiring excessive wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is growing at a rate of 0.69% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that businesses can expect a steady stream of new workers to recruit from, making it easier to fill open positions and support expansion plans.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has decreased by 12.18% year-over-year, indicating a tightening housing supply. This decline in permits may signal future affordability challenges and potential constraints on workforce accommodation, as a limited housing stock can drive up prices and make it harder for workers to relocate to the area.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is 78 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 3.7%. This relatively fast pace of home sales, combined with the declining days on market, indicates a competitive housing market that may be challenging for relocating workers to navigate. However, this also suggests a strong and active real estate market.

Office Economy

Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking in the 90th percentile. This makes the city an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. Conversely, the city may be less suited for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, rapid wage growth, and a relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability challenges and constraints on workforce accommodation, potentially offsetting some of the city's attractive economic features.
