Economic Analysis

Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has earned an overall grade of B+, scoring 61.0 out of 100, with its strong labor demand and wage growth being key defining features of its economy right now, including a 1.0% increase in employment over the past year and 6.0% rise in hourly earnings. This suggests a city with a growing job market and rising paychecks, making it an attractive location for businesses and workers alike.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.0% over the past year, with workers' hours also rising by 0.6%, indicating a strong demand for labor and a healthy job market. This means that businesses are hiring and workers are putting in more hours, signaling a genuine demand for labor and a positive outlook for the local economy.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate stands at 4.2%, which is higher than most metros, making it relatively easier for employers to hire but also indicating weaker local consumer demand. This higher unemployment rate gives employers a larger pool of potential workers to choose from, but it also means that some workers may be struggling to find employment.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Hourly earnings have risen by 6.0% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, meaning that employers may face rising labor costs, but workers will see an increase in their paychecks. This strong wage growth is a positive sign for workers, as it indicates that their pay is keeping pace with the cost of living and potentially even rising faster.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $222 and hourly pay of $33.42, and it's getting even more affordable as housing prices have fallen by 2.6% over the past year. This makes the city an attractive location for workers and businesses looking for a relatively low cost of living, with housing prices that are not overly burdensome compared to local wages.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The labor force is growing at a rate of 0.7% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, indicating a expanding pool of workers and a positive sign for hiring. This growth in the labor force means that businesses will have access to a larger and more diverse pool of workers, making it easier to find the right talent.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals have fallen by 12.2% over the past year, which is weaker than most metros, signaling a potential supply squeeze in the future and potentially making it harder for workers to find affordable housing. This decline in building permits may lead to a shortage of housing supply, which could drive up prices and make it more difficult for workers to afford housing.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

The typical home takes 78 days to sell, which is getting slightly faster, with a 3.7% decrease over the past year, indicating a relatively healthy housing market. This suggests that the housing market is balanced, with neither too much nor too little supply, making it easier for buyers and sellers to negotiate and for workers to find a place to live.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a large office-based workforce, with a score of 90 out of 100, indicating a deep talent pool in tech, finance, and professional services, making it an attractive location for businesses in these sectors. This large office workforce makes Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford a prime location for companies looking to establish or expand their operations in these industries.

Overall, Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford's biggest strength is its strong labor demand and wage growth, making it an attractive location for businesses and workers alike, while its biggest risk is the decline in building permits, which could lead to a housing supply squeeze and make it harder for workers to afford housing. With its relatively affordable cost of living and growing labor force, the city is well-positioned for continued economic growth, but it will need to address the potential housing supply issue to maintain its attractiveness to businesses and workers.
