Economic Analysis

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 53.6th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong labor demand and deep professional talent pool. The city's labor demand composite score of 6.45, which combines a +1.22% employment growth rate and a +0.442% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, signals genuine demand expansion. This, coupled with its high office economy percentile rank, defines the city's current economic character.

Labor Demand

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area exhibits a +1.22% employment growth rate and a +0.442% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing an increase in jobs and hours worked, suggesting a strong and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 6.45 further reinforces this notion, placing the city in the top tier of labor demand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford stands at 4.40%, which corresponds to a bottom tier percentile rank of 12th, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as well as potential upward pressure on wages. As a result, companies looking to staff up in this city may need to offer competitive compensation packages to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The city's year-over-year wage growth rate is +4.17%, placing it near the median at the 52nd percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, while workers are experiencing an increase in purchasing power. However, the relatively modest wage growth may not be sufficient to offset the potential hiring challenges posed by the tight labor market.

Cost of Living

Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a cost of living percentile rank of 76th, indicating that it is more affordable than many of its peer cities, with a PSF of $224/sqft and average hourly earnings of $33.76/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.64. The fact that PSF is falling by 3.0% year-over-year further enhances the city's affordability. This affordability advantage can be a significant talent attraction factor, as businesses may not need to offer wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is growing at a rate of +1.11% year-over-year, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the city's hiring capacity is increasing, which can be beneficial for businesses looking to staff up. A growing labor force can provide a steady pipeline of potential employees, reducing the likelihood of labor shortages.

Building Permits

The city's residential building permits have decreased by 29.77% year-over-year, signaling a tightening of the housing supply. This sharp decline in permits may lead to future affordability challenges and potentially hinder the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential long-term implications of a constrained housing market on their ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is 70 days, with no year-over-year change. This relatively fast pace of home sales suggests a competitive market, which may pose challenges for relocating workers. However, the stable days on market metric indicates that the market is not becoming increasingly competitive, providing some relief for businesses looking to attract talent to the area.

Office Economy

Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy percentile rank of 90th, indicating a strong presence of professional and office sectors. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which rely on a knowledgeable and skilled workforce. Conversely, the city may be less suitable for industries that require a large industrial or logistics workforce.

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand, a deep professional talent pool, and relatively affordable cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the city's tightening housing supply, which may lead to future affordability challenges and hinder the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce, ultimately affecting businesses' ability to attract and retain top talent.
