Economic Analysis

Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has earned an overall grade of B+, scoring 61.0 out of 100 across 50 US metros, with a strong job market defined by 1.0% employment growth over the past year and a 6.0% rise in hourly earnings. This growth is driven by a combination of factors, including a low cost of living and a large office-based workforce, with the city scoring 90 out of 100 for its share of professional and office jobs.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs at a rate of 1.0% over the past year, with workers' hours also rising by 0.6%, indicating a strong demand for labor and a healthy job market. This means that businesses are hiring and workers are putting in more hours, signaling a genuine demand for labor and a positive outlook for the local economy.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is 4.2%, which is higher than most metros, making it relatively easier for employers to hire, but also indicating weaker local consumer demand. With a score of 32 out of 100, the city's unemployment rate is a weakness, suggesting that there are more people looking for work than in other metros.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Hourly earnings in the city have risen by 6.0% over the past year, with a score of 86 out of 100, indicating faster pay growth than most metros, which means higher labor costs for employers but more purchasing power for workers. This strong wage growth is a strength for the local economy, as it attracts and retains top talent.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $222 and hourly pay of $33.42, and a score of 65 out of 100, indicating that housing is more affordable than in most metros, with prices falling by 2.6% over the past year. This makes the city an attractive location for workers and businesses, as the cost of living is lower than in many other metros.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in the city is growing at a rate of 0.7% over the past year, with a score of 70 out of 100, indicating faster workforce growth than most metros, which is a strength for hiring and business expansion. This growing labor force provides a steady supply of workers for businesses to hire from.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in the city have fallen by 12.2% over the past year, with a score of 28 out of 100, indicating weaker home-building activity than most metros, which could lead to a supply squeeze and higher housing costs in the future. This decline in building permits is a weakness, as it may limit the availability of housing for a growing workforce.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

The typical home in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford takes 78 days to sell, with a score of 68 out of 100, and the time to sell is getting shorter by 3.7% over the past year, indicating a relatively healthy housing market. This suggests that the housing market is balanced, with a steady supply of homes available for buyers.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a large and growing office-based workforce, with a score of 90 out of 100, indicating a deeper talent pool than most metros, making it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This strong office economy is a strength, as it provides a skilled and knowledgeable workforce for businesses to draw from.

The bottom line is that Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford's biggest strength is its strong job market and growing office-based workforce, which attracts and retains top talent, while its biggest risk is the decline in building permits, which could lead to a housing supply squeeze and higher costs in the future. Overall, the city's B+ grade and strong labor demand make it an attractive location for businesses and workers alike.
