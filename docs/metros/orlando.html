Economic Analysis

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking in the 60.7th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the top tier at the 82nd percentile, and its high share of office and professional workers, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. Specifically, the labor demand is driven by a 1.39% year-over-year employment growth rate and a 0.025% deviation in weekly hours above the city's own trend.

Labor Demand

The combination of a 1.39% employment growth rate and a 0.025% increase in weekly hours above trend signals genuine demand expansion in the Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area. This indicates that jobs are being added and hours are running above the normal trend, suggesting a healthy and growing labor market. The labor demand composite score of 6.27 further reinforces this assessment, ranking in the top tier at the 82nd percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford stands at 4.60%, which is below average, ranking in the 22nd percentile. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it relatively easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, the practical implication for a business trying to hire here is that while it may be easier to find candidates, the market is not so loose that it would lead to significant downward pressure on wages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is 5.04%, which is above average, ranking in the 66th percentile. This rate of wage growth indicates that labor costs for employers are rising, but it also means that workers have increasing purchasing power. The implication for businesses is that they need to factor in higher labor costs, but the growing consumer demand can also present opportunities.

Cost of Living

Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is relatively affordable, with a cost of living percentile rank in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. The city's PSF (price per square foot) is $223, and it has decreased by 3.0% year-over-year, compared to an hourly wage of $33.41, resulting in a ratio of 6.67. This affordability is a significant advantage for talent attraction, as businesses can offer competitive wages without needing large premiums to offset the cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is growing at a rate of 1.74% year-over-year, which is in the top tier at the 84th percentile. This expansion in the labor force supply is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it indicates a growing pool of potential workers. The implication is that the city has a structural advantage in terms of hiring capacity.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is -17.79%, which is below average, ranking in the 20th percentile. This decline suggests that the housing supply is not expanding as quickly as demand, potentially leading to future affordability issues and constraints on workforce accommodation. This could signal a tightening in the housing market, which might impact the city's attractiveness for relocating workers.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford is 74 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 1.3%. This indicates a relatively fast-moving market, although not extremely competitive, ranking near the median at the 58th percentile. For a worker relocating to this city, the housing market is accessible, but the trend suggests it might be becoming slightly more competitive.

Office Economy

Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford has a deep professional talent pool, with an office and professional worker share composite ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that rely on specialized knowledge workers. Conversely, it may be less suited for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant.

The Orlando-Kissimmee-Sanford metro area offers businesses a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion, a relatively affordable cost of living, and a deep talent pool of professional workers. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the decline in residential building permits, which could lead to future housing affordability issues and impact the city's ability to attract and accommodate a growing workforce.
