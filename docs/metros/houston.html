Economic Analysis

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 39.4th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its labor demand, with a composite score in the 60th percentile, and its high unemployment rate, which ranks in the 6th percentile. The combination of these metrics suggests a complex economic environment, with both positive and negative signals.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 1.14% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by -0.342%. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs, albeit with slightly fewer hours worked than expected. This suggests that the labor market is growing, but not at an explosive pace.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 5.20%, ranking it in the 6th percentile, indicating a relatively loose labor market. This means that there is some slack in the market, making it easier for businesses to hire, but also potentially weakening local consumer demand. As a result, businesses may face less wage pressure when hiring, but may also need to consider the potential impact on local sales.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 1.52%, ranking it in the 22nd percentile, indicating relatively slow wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power may also be limited. As a result, businesses may enjoy a relatively flat cost environment, but workers may have limited bargaining power.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $171/sqft to $36.01/hr, and a year-over-year decrease of 2.3% in PSF, this city ranks in the 57th percentile for affordability. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which could be a talent attraction advantage without requiring significant wage premiums. The decreasing PSF is a key driver of this affordability, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to relocate or expand.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is growing at a rate of 0.88% year-over-year, ranking it in the 70th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive signal for businesses looking to hire. As a result, the city's hiring capacity is likely to increase, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to grow.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -14.66%, ranking it in the 24th percentile, indicating a tightening of the housing supply. This suggests that the city's housing market may become less affordable in the future, potentially making it more difficult for workers to relocate. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact on workforce accommodation and future affordability.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 50 days, with no year-over-year change. This indicates a relatively stable housing market, which may be neither highly competitive nor highly accessible for relocating workers. As a result, businesses may not need to worry about extreme competition for housing, but may still need to consider the potential impact on worker relocation.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share ranking in the 78th percentile, Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands has a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. However, this also means that the city may be less suited for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses, which may require a different type of workforce.

In conclusion, Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands offers businesses a relatively affordable location with a growing labor force and a deep professional talent pool. However, the city's high unemployment rate and tightening housing supply are significant risks that decision-makers should factor in, as they may impact local consumer demand and future affordability.
