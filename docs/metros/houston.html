Economic Analysis

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 44.4th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, with a composite score at the 60th percentile, and its below-average unemployment rate, at 22nd percentile, indicating a tight labor market. The labor demand is driven by a 1.14% year-over-year employment growth rate and a -0.342% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 1.14% and the -0.342% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend signal a genuine demand expansion, as hours are running slightly below trend during a period of job growth. This combination indicates that the labor market is experiencing real growth, but the slight decrease in hours worked suggests that the growth might not be as robust as it seems. The labor demand composite score of 5.55 further supports this interpretation, ranking at the 60th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 4.60% is relatively high, ranking at the 22nd percentile, which means the labor market has some slack. However, this still presents a challenge for businesses trying to hire, as the market is not as loose as the unemployment rate might suggest, and wage pressure could be a concern. The practical implication for a business is that hiring might be somewhat easier than in tighter markets, but it's not an overly favorable environment.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of 1.52% is below average, ranking at the 22nd percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth implies a relatively flat cost environment for employers, but it also means workers have weak bargaining power. For businesses, this could mean labor costs are not increasing rapidly, but it also suggests that worker purchasing power is not growing significantly.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of 4.78, based on $172/sqft (which is decreasing by 2.3% year-over-year) versus $36.01/hr, Houston ranks at the 53rd percentile in terms of affordability. This near-median ranking suggests that the city is neither particularly affordable nor expensive relative to its peers. For talent attraction, this means that Houston does not have a significant advantage in terms of cost of living, as it does not offer exceptionally low costs without wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of 0.03% year-over-year, which is above average, ranking at the 68th percentile. This positive growth rate indicates that the workforce supply is slowly expanding. The implication for hiring capacity is that businesses have a slightly increasing pool of potential workers to draw from, which could ease hiring challenges over time.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -14.66%, ranking below average at the 24th percentile. This significant decrease in permits suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This trend signals a potential risk for businesses relying on an expanding and affordable housing supply for their workers.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 50 days, with an 8.7% year-over-year increase. This increase, ranking at the 76th percentile, indicates a slower market. For a worker relocating to this city, the slower market could make it easier to find a home, as properties are not being snapped up as quickly as in hotter markets.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share composite score of 3.28, ranking at the 78th percentile, Houston has a deep talent pool suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for knowledge-economy businesses but might be less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

In conclusion, Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands offers a mix of above-average labor demand and a relatively slow wage growth environment, which could be attractive for businesses looking to manage labor costs. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the tightening housing supply, signaled by the sharp decrease in building permits, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in attracting and retaining workers.
