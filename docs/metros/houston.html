Economic Analysis

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area has an overall grade of C+, scoring 45.1 out of 100 across 50 US metros, with its economy defined by a mix of average job growth and slower pay increases, including a 1.0% rise in employment over the past year. The city's unemployment rate of 4.6% is higher than most metros, which could impact hiring and local consumer demand.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs, with a 1.0% increase in employment over the past year, but workers' hours are falling by 0.5%, indicating a relatively stable job market. This means that while the city is creating new positions, the existing workforce is not seeing a significant increase in working hours, resulting in a labor demand score of 48, about average for the 50 metros.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 4.6% makes it easier for employers to hire, as there is a larger pool of available workers, but it also indicates weaker local consumer demand, scoring 30, a weakness compared to other metros. This higher unemployment rate means that employers may have an advantage in terms of hiring, but it also suggests that the local economy may not be as strong as others.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 2.2% over the past year, which is slower than most metros, scoring 26, a weakness that may help keep labor costs in check for employers but also means workers' purchasing power is not increasing as quickly. This slower wage growth may be beneficial for businesses looking to control costs, but it also means that workers may not see significant increases in their take-home pay.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

Housing is relatively affordable in the city, with a price per square foot of $168 and hourly pay of $37.07, and it's getting even more affordable as prices are falling by 2.3% over the past year, scoring 76, a strength. This affordability, with a score better than most metros, could be a major draw for talent and businesses looking to relocate.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers is growing at a rate of 0.7% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, scoring 70, a strength that should help employers find the talent they need. This growing labor force is a positive sign for the city's economy, as it indicates a increasing supply of workers.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals fell by 9.2% over the past year, scoring 32, a weakness that could signal a future housing supply shortage and potential affordability issues. This decline in building permits may indicate that the city's housing market is not keeping pace with demand, which could lead to higher prices and reduced affordability in the future.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes are taking 57 days to sell, which is relatively stable, with no change over the past year, scoring 30, a weakness that suggests a softer housing market. This stable but slower pace of home sales may indicate that the housing market is not as healthy as it could be, making it more challenging for buyers and sellers.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a large office-based workforce, with a score of 82, a strength that makes it an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This strong office economy is well-suited for companies looking to establish or expand their presence in these industries.

The city's biggest strength is its affordable cost of living, which could attract talent and businesses, while its biggest risk is its slower wage growth, which may impact workers' purchasing power and the overall health of the local economy. Overall, the Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area presents a mixed picture, with both opportunities and challenges that businesses and individuals should carefully consider when making location decisions.
