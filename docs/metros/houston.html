Economic Analysis

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 49.2th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its high labor demand composite score, ranking at the 68th percentile, and its extremely affordable cost of living, with a ratio of $169/sqft to $36.90/hr, ranking at the 96th percentile. The labor demand and cost of living metrics suggest a city with genuine job growth and an attractive environment for talent.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is +1.46% year-over-year, combined with a -0.639% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, indicating a genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and hours are running slightly below trend, suggesting a healthy labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.71 further supports this interpretation, ranking at the 68th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 5.10%, ranking at the 8th percentile, indicating a relatively high level of unemployment. This suggests that the labor market has some slack, making it easier for businesses to hire workers. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand, which could impact businesses reliant on local spending.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is +1.71%, ranking at the 22nd percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth suggests a flat cost environment for employers, but it also means workers have weak bargaining power. As a result, businesses may not face significant labor cost pressures, but workers may not have strong purchasing power.

Cost of Living

Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands has a cost of living ratio of $169/sqft to $36.90/hr, with the price per square foot falling by -2.3% year-over-year, ranking at the 96th percentile. This makes the city extremely affordable relative to its peers, which is a significant talent attraction advantage. Businesses can attract workers without needing to offer substantial wage premiums to offset high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is growing at a rate of +0.28% year-over-year, ranking at the 64th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is slowly expanding, which is a positive sign for hiring capacity. However, the growth rate is relatively modest, suggesting that businesses may still face some challenges in finding workers.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is decreasing by -6.61% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 42nd percentile. This decline in permits suggests that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 52 days, with a year-over-year increase of +2.0%, ranking near the median at the 56th percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced market, neither extremely competitive nor extremely accessible, for workers relocating to the city.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 3.34, ranking at the 78th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less suited for industries with primarily industrial or logistics-based workforces.

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area offers businesses a unique combination of genuine labor demand and extremely affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for talent. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the relatively high unemployment rate, which could impact local consumer demand and, in turn, businesses reliant on it.
