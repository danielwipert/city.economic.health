Economic Analysis

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 49.0th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, with a composite score at the 60th percentile, and its highly affordable cost of living, ranked at the 90th percentile. Specifically, the cost of living is characterized by a PSF of $172/sqft, which has decreased by 2.3% YoY, combined with an average hourly wage of $36.50, resulting in a ratio of 4.71.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 0.61% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by -0.091%. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, as hours are slightly below trend during a period of job growth, indicating that the labor market is absorbing new jobs without overburdening existing workers. The labor demand composite score of 5.06 further supports this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 4.60%, which is below average, ranking at the 22nd percentile. This indicates a relatively tight labor market with some slack, making it moderately challenging for businesses to hire. As a result, businesses may face some wage pressure when trying to attract talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 2.01%, which is below average, ranking at the 28th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, albeit not rapidly. This could be beneficial for businesses looking to manage labor costs.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living percentile rank of 90, Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is one of the more affordable cities relative to its peers. The decreasing PSF of $172/sqft (-2.3% YoY) compared to the average hourly wage of $36.50/hr makes it an attractive location for talent, as businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to compensate for high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in this metro area is growing at a rate of 0.03% YoY, indicating a very slow expansion of the workforce supply. While this growth is positive, its slow pace may pose a structural headwind for hiring, particularly if businesses are looking to rapidly expand their operations.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -18.95%, signaling a tightening of the housing supply. This sharp decline in permits may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce, potentially affecting the city's attractiveness for both businesses and workers.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 50 days, with an 8.7% YoY increase. This indicates a slowing market, which can be beneficial for workers relocating to the city, as they may have more time to find suitable housing. However, this trend also suggests a normalization of the market rather than a rapid expansion.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share ranked at the 80th percentile, Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands has a deep talent pool suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors. This makes the city an attractive location for knowledge-economy businesses but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors.

In conclusion, Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands offers businesses an attractive combination of affordable living costs and a deep professional talent pool, making it suitable for knowledge-economy sectors. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the sharp decline in building permits, which may lead to future housing affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.
