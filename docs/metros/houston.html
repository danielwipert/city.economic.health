Economic Analysis

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking at the 47.8th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score in the 74th percentile, and its high cost of living affordability, ranked in the 82nd percentile. The labor demand is driven by a combination of employment growth and hours worked above trend, while the cost of living is influenced by a PSF to earnings ratio of $169/sqft to $36.92/hr, which is decreasing by 2.3% YoY.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 1.30% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating 0.091% above the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This indicates a strong labor market with increasing job opportunities.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 5.10%, which corresponds to a bottom-tier percentile rank of 8th. This suggests a relatively loose labor market with some slack, making it easier for businesses to hire workers without excessive wage pressure. However, this also implies weaker local consumer demand compared to tighter labor markets.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 1.21%, which is ranked in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile. This slow wage growth indicates a relatively flat cost environment for employers but weak bargaining power for workers. As a result, worker purchasing power is not increasing rapidly.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living percentile rank of 82nd, Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The PSF to earnings ratio of $169/sqft to $36.92/hr, combined with a 2.3% YoY decrease in PSF, makes this city attractive for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. This affordability advantage can be a key factor in talent attraction and retention.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in this metro area is growing at a rate of 0.28% YoY, which is ranked in the 64th percentile. This positive growth indicates an expanding workforce supply, providing a favorable environment for hiring and business expansion. As the labor force grows, businesses can tap into a increasing pool of potential workers.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -2.18%, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This decline suggests that housing supply is not expanding as rapidly as in other metros, which could lead to future affordability challenges and constraints on workforce accommodation. However, the current decrease is not sharply negative, indicating a relatively stable housing market.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 52 days, with a 2.0% YoY increase. This indicates a relatively accessible market for workers relocating to the city, as homes are not selling extremely quickly. The rising days on market, despite a strong job market, may signal a healthy normalization of the housing market, rather than demand erosion.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share ranked in the 84th percentile, Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands has a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses requiring specialized knowledge-economy roles, but may be less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

In conclusion, Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands offers businesses a unique combination of strong labor demand and affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for talent attraction and retention. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the relatively slow wage growth, which may impact worker purchasing power and bargaining ability, potentially affecting the local consumer demand and overall economic growth.
