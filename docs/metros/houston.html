Economic Analysis

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 48.5th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, with a composite score of 6.21 ranking it in the 74th percentile, and its relatively low unemployment rate of 5.00%, which ranks in the 18th percentile. The labor demand and unemployment metrics suggest a city with genuine job growth but also some challenges in hiring due to a relatively tight labor market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is +1.30% year-over-year, combined with a +0.091% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing real job growth, with hours worked above trend, suggesting that businesses are not just maintaining existing staff levels but are actually expanding their workforces. This is a positive indicator for businesses looking to locate in an area with growing economic activity.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 5.00%, ranking in the 18th percentile, which indicates a relatively tight labor market. This tight market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as there are fewer unemployed individuals available to fill open positions, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. For a business trying to hire in this market, it may be necessary to offer competitive salaries and benefits to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is +1.21%, ranking in the 12th percentile, which is relatively stagnant. This slow wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also indicates that workers may have weaker bargaining power. For businesses, this could mean a more stable labor cost environment, but it may also limit the attractiveness of the area for workers seeking higher pay.

Cost of Living

Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands has a cost of living score ranking it in the 69th percentile, with a price per square foot of $169 and average hourly earnings of $36.92, resulting in a ratio of 4.58. This suggests that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, which could be a significant advantage for talent attraction without needing to offer wage premiums. The fact that PSF is falling by -1.7% year-over-year further enhances the city's affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is growing at a rate of +0.64% year-over-year, ranking in the 80th percentile, indicating that the workforce supply is expanding. This expansion in the labor force is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests that there will be a growing pool of potential employees available. This can help mitigate some of the challenges posed by the tight labor market.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is -2.18%, ranking near the median at the 45th percentile. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is not expanding as rapidly as it could be, which may lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. This could be a concern for businesses relying on a mobile workforce or looking to attract talent from other areas.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 55 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -1.8%, ranking in the 28th percentile. This relatively fast pace of home sales indicates a competitive market, which could make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the decrease in days on market suggests some normalization in the housing market.

Office Economy

Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share composite ranking it in the 84th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that rely on specialized knowledge-economy talent. Conversely, it may be less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area offers businesses a unique combination of growing labor demand and an expanding workforce, making it an attractive location for companies looking to tap into a diverse and growing talent pool. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the potential for future affordability issues due to the decline in residential building permits, which could impact the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce and attract new talent without significant wage premiums.
