Economic Analysis

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking at the 48.5th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its above-average labor demand, with a composite score at the 74th percentile, and its relatively low unemployment rate of 5.00%, which ranks at the 18th percentile. The labor demand and unemployment metrics suggest a city with genuine job growth but also some challenges in finding the right talent.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is +1.30% year-over-year, combined with a +0.091% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing real job growth, with hours worked above trend, suggesting that businesses are hiring and workers are in demand. The labor demand composite score of 6.21 further supports this interpretation, ranking at the 74th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is 5.00%, ranking at the 18th percentile, which indicates a relatively high level of unemployment compared to other cities. This suggests that the job market has some slack, making it easier for businesses to hire, but it also implies weaker local consumer demand. For a business trying to hire in this city, the relatively high unemployment rate could be an advantage in terms of finding available talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is +1.21%, ranking at the 12th percentile, which indicates stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth implies a flat cost environment for employers, but it also means that workers have weak bargaining power and limited purchasing power. As a result, businesses may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but they may also struggle to attract and retain top talent.

Cost of Living

Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands has a cost of living score ranking at the 69th percentile, with a PSF of $169/sqft and average hourly earnings of $36.92/hr, resulting in a ratio of 4.58. The fact that PSF is falling by -1.7% year-over-year suggests that the city is becoming more affordable. This makes it an attractive location for talent, as businesses can offer competitive wages without needing significant premiums to account for the cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is growing at a rate of +0.64% year-over-year, ranking at the 80th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire. The growing labor force suggests that the city has a strong pipeline of potential employees, making it easier for companies to find the talent they need.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands has decreased by -2.18% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact of a tightening housing market on their ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands is currently 55 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -1.8%. This relatively fast pace of home sales, ranking at the 28th percentile, suggests a competitive market that may be challenging for relocating workers. However, the decreasing days on market could also indicate a healthy and dynamic housing market, which could be attractive to some businesses and employees.

Office Economy

Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands has a deep professional talent pool, with an office economy score ranking at the 84th percentile. This suggests that the city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized knowledge workers. However, the city may be less suited for industries that are more industrial or logistics-dominant, which may require a different type of workforce.

The Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro area offers businesses a unique combination of above-average labor demand and a relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for talent. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential impact of a tightening housing market on future affordability and workforce accommodation, which could limit the city's ability to support long-term business growth and expansion.
