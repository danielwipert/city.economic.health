Economic Analysis

Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin has an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking it at the 60.3th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 3.10% and its above-average labor demand composite score of 6.06, which combines a +1.56% employment growth rate with a -0.424% weekly hours deviation from its own trend. These metrics suggest a city with a strong job market and genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +1.56% and weekly hours deviation of -0.424% indicate a city with genuine demand expansion, as hours are running slightly below trend during a period of job growth. This combination signals that the labor market is experiencing real growth, rather than just a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 6.06 ranks in the 72nd percentile, confirming the city's above-average labor market performance.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.10% ranks in the 92nd percentile, indicating a very tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin may face challenges in finding available workers, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate also suggests that workers have significant bargaining power in the job market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +4.66% ranks in the 62nd percentile, indicating moderate to fast wage growth. This implies that employer labor costs are rising, but workers are also experiencing increased purchasing power. As a result, businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, but can also expect to benefit from a more affluent local consumer base.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin, with a PSF of $259/sqft and average hourly earnings of $35.38/hr, results in a ratio of 7.32, ranking in the 51st percentile. This means that the city is near the median in terms of affordability, neither particularly expensive nor unusually affordable. As a result, businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to attract talent, but may still face some competition for workers.

Labor Force Growth

The year-over-year growth rate of the civilian labor force is -0.95%, ranking in the 30th percentile, indicating a contracting labor pool. This suggests that the supply of workers is shrinking, which may create structural headwinds for hiring and business expansion in the city. Businesses may need to invest in training and development programs to attract and retain talent in this environment.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -21.89%, ranking in the 14th percentile, indicating a sharp decline in housing supply. This signals that the city may be facing a future affordability crisis and workforce accommodation challenges, as the supply of housing is not keeping pace with demand. Businesses may need to consider the potential impact of rising housing costs on their employees' quality of life and affordability.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 60 days, with a year-over-year increase of +1.7%. This suggests that the housing market is slowing down, with homes taking slightly longer to sell than in the past. For workers relocating to the city, this may make it easier to find a home, as the market is becoming less competitive.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors is 3.86, ranking in the 88th percentile, indicating a deep and talented pool of knowledge workers. This makes Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin an attractive location for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less suited for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

In conclusion, Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin offers businesses a strong job market with genuine demand expansion, a talented pool of knowledge workers, and a moderate cost of living. However, the city's low unemployment rate and shrinking labor pool also pose significant challenges for hiring and business expansion, making it essential for decision-makers to carefully consider these factors when evaluating the city as a potential location.
