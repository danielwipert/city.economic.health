Economic Analysis

Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin has an overall grade of B, ranking 52.4th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score reflecting a near median performance across key economic metrics. The city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 3.10% and its near median labor demand composite score of 5.26, indicating a balance between job growth and labor utilization. Specifically, the combination of a 92nd percentile unemployment rate and 46th percentile labor demand score suggests a tight labor market with genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The city's employment growth rate is +0.65% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating +0.047% from the 12-month baseline, signaling genuine demand expansion. This combination indicates that jobs are being added and hours are running above trend, suggesting a healthy labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.26, ranking near the median at 46th percentile, further supports this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin is 3.10%, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This means that there is little slack in the market, making it harder for businesses to hire and potentially leading to upward wage pressure. For a business trying to hire in this city, the low unemployment rate implies a competitive hiring environment.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin is +3.92%, ranking near the median at the 52nd percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively, and that worker purchasing power is increasing. The moderate wage growth rate implies a relatively stable labor cost environment for businesses.

Cost of Living

Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin has a cost of living score ranking near the median at the 51st percentile, with a PSF of $259/sqft and average hourly earnings of $35.74/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.25. The city's cost of living is relatively affordable, with the PSF decreasing by -1.1% year-over-year, making it more attractive for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. This affordability advantage can aid in talent attraction for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin is contracting at a rate of -0.95% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 30th percentile. This decline in labor force supply implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is shrinking. Businesses may face challenges in finding qualified candidates due to the contracting labor force.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -17.83%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 18th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This sharp decline in permits suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, potentially limiting the city's ability to attract and retain workers. The decrease in building permits is a concern for businesses relying on a growing and accessible workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin is 60 days, with a year-over-year increase of +1.7%, ranking below average at the 28th percentile. This indicates a relatively slow market, which can be challenging for relocating workers. However, the rising days on market in a strong job market may also signal a healthy normalization, providing more options for workers.

Office Economy

Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share composite score ranking above average at the 78th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that rely on specialized knowledge-economy talent. Conversely, it may be less ideal for industries with more industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The city of Nashville-Davidson--Murfreesboro--Franklin offers businesses a tight labor market with moderate wage growth and a relatively affordable cost of living, making it attractive for talent acquisition. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the contracting labor force and tightening housing supply, which may limit the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce and attract new talent, potentially impacting businesses' hiring capacity and future growth.
