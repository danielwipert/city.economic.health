Economic Analysis

Columbus, with an overall grade of B and a 50.0th percentile composite score, is characterized by its low unemployment rate of 2.70% and a labor demand composite score of 4.60, which is near the median at the 48th percentile. These metrics suggest a city with a tight labor market but moderate job growth. The combination of a low unemployment rate and near-median labor demand score defines Columbus's current economic character, with specific numbers indicating a challenging hiring environment.

Labor Demand

Columbus has an employment growth rate of +0.23% and a weekly hours deviation of -0.025% from its own trend, signaling a moderate expansion of jobs but with hours worked slightly below trend. This combination suggests genuine demand expansion, albeit at a slow pace. The labor demand composite score of 4.60, at the 48th percentile, reinforces this interpretation, indicating a city with job growth but not excessively strong.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Columbus is 2.70%, ranking at the 96th percentile, which means the market is extremely tight. This low unemployment rate implies that businesses may face significant challenges in hiring, with upward pressure on wages. For a business trying to hire in Columbus, the tight labor market means that attracting and retaining talent will be difficult and potentially costly.

Wage Growth

Columbus experiences a year-over-year wage growth rate of +3.58%, placing it near the median at the 44th percentile. This moderate wage growth indicates that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so, while workers enjoy some increase in purchasing power. The implication is that businesses should expect labor costs to increase, but the pace of growth is manageable.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $208/sqft to $34.02/hr, Columbus ranks at the 37th percentile, indicating that the city is less affordable than many of its peers. This relatively low affordability score means that attracting talent without offering wage premiums may be challenging, as the cost of living is higher than in many other cities.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Columbus is shrinking at a rate of -1.50% year-over-year, placing it in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile. This contraction in the labor force supply signals a structural headwind for hiring, making it even more challenging for businesses to find the talent they need. The implication is that businesses may need to consider strategies to attract workers from other areas or invest in training and development.

Building Permits

Columbus has seen a -20.36% year-over-year change in residential building permits, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This sharp decline in permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The signal is clear: future workforce attraction and retention may be at risk due to limited housing options.

Days on Market

Homes in Columbus currently sit on the market for 39 days, with an 8.3% year-over-year increase, placing it in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This slower market means that workers relocating to Columbus may find it easier to find a home, as the market is less competitive. However, this also indicates a normalization of the housing market, which could be a positive sign for long-term stability.

Office Economy

Columbus has a professional and office worker share that ranks below average at the 24th percentile. This indicates a less deep talent pool in professional and office sectors, making the city less suited for businesses requiring a high concentration of tech, finance, or consulting talent. However, it may be more suitable for industries with different workforce requirements, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Columbus offers a business a tight labor market with moderate job growth and a relatively high cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply, which could significantly impact the ability to attract and retain talent. Businesses considering Columbus must carefully weigh these factors and potentially develop strategies to mitigate the challenges posed by the city's economic character.
