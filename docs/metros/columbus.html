Economic Analysis

Columbus, ranked 47.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, earns an overall grade of C+, characterized primarily by its low labor demand and tight unemployment market, with an unemployment rate of 3.30% and a labor demand composite score of 3.65. The city's economic character is also defined by its moderate wage growth and relatively affordable cost of living, with a cost of living ratio of 5.94. Specifically, the labor demand and unemployment metrics, with employment growth at -0.13% and weekly hours deviation at +0.468%, set the tone for the city's economic landscape.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Columbus is -0.13%, and the weekly hours deviation is +0.468%, indicating a scenario where hours are above trend during job losses, suggesting a survivor squeeze. This combination signals that while there is some level of labor utilization, the overall labor market is contracting. The labor demand composite score of 3.65 further reinforces this notion, placing Columbus in the bottom tier.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Columbus is 3.30%, which is in the top tier, indicating a tight labor market with low slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city will face significant competition for talent, potentially driving up wages. With such a low unemployment rate, Columbus presents a challenging environment for staffing.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Columbus is +4.53%, which is above average, ranking in the 68th percentile. This rate of wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but it also indicates good worker purchasing power. As wages continue to grow at this pace, businesses should anticipate increasing labor costs.

Cost of Living

Columbus has a cost of living ratio of 5.94, with PSF at $205/sqft and hourly earnings at $34.52/hr, placing it near the median in terms of affordability. Given its 51st percentile rank, the city is neither exceptionally affordable nor expensive compared to its peers. This means that Columbus can attract talent without necessarily requiring significant wage premiums to offset the cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Columbus is shrinking at a rate of -2.01% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor pool. This negative growth rate presents a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses should consider this trend when planning for future staffing needs.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Columbus is -3.05%, indicating a slight tightening in housing supply. Although this change is near the median, it suggests that the future affordability and workforce accommodation in the city might be at risk if the trend continues. Developer confidence appears to be waning slightly.

Days on Market

Homes in Columbus are currently sitting on the market for 50 days, with a year-over-year increase of +6.4%. This increase, placing the city in the 72nd percentile, indicates a slower market that is more accessible for relocating workers. The rising days on market suggest a healthy normalization of the housing market.

Office Economy

Columbus has a relatively shallow professional talent pool, ranking in the 10th percentile. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring deep knowledge-economy talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. Instead, Columbus might be more appropriate for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

In conclusion, Columbus offers a business environment with moderate wage growth and a relatively affordable cost of living, but it is constrained by a tight labor market and a shrinking labor force. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker considering Columbus is the challenging hiring environment due to low unemployment and the potential for increasing labor costs. This must be carefully weighed against the city's other attributes when making a location decision.
