Economic Analysis

Columbus earns an overall grade of B+ with a composite score ranking it at the 55.8th percentile among 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 3.30% and its sluggish labor force growth rate of -0.43% YoY. These metrics suggest a tight labor market with limited workforce expansion.

Labor Demand

Columbus has an employment growth rate of +0.44% YoY and a weekly hours deviation of +0.246% from its own trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 5.16. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit at a moderate pace. The city's labor market is adding jobs and hours are running above trend, indicating a healthy demand for labor.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Columbus is 3.30%, ranking it in the top tier at the 96th percentile. This tight labor market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure and competition for talent. As a result, companies may need to offer higher salaries to attract and retain workers.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Columbus is +3.49% YoY, ranking it near the median at the 48th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. Workers in Columbus are seeing their purchasing power increase, which can have a positive impact on local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Columbus has a cost of living ratio of $207/sqft to $34.31/hr, resulting in a ratio of 6.03, which ranks it near the median at the 59th percentile. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. The stable PSF YoY change suggests that Columbus can attract talent without requiring significant wage premiums, making it an attractive location for businesses.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in Columbus is shrinking at a rate of -0.43% YoY, ranking it below average at the 36th percentile. This contraction in the labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, making it challenging for businesses to find and recruit new talent.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Columbus has decreased by -25.53% YoY, ranking it in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile. This sharp decline in permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which can lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Columbus is 40 days, with a YoY increase of +5.3%, ranking it in the top tier at the 84th percentile. This slower market means that workers relocating to Columbus may have an easier time finding a home, as properties are sitting on the market for longer periods.

Office Economy

Columbus has a relatively shallow professional talent pool, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile. This makes it less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, it may be more suitable for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as logistics or manufacturing.

In conclusion, Columbus offers a business a relatively affordable location with a tight labor market and moderate wage growth. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the city's shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply, which can pose significant challenges for hiring and accommodating a growing workforce.
