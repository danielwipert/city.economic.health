Economic Analysis

Columbus, with an overall grade of B and a composite score ranking it at the 51.7th percentile among 50 US metros, is characterized by its low unemployment rate of 2.70% and a labor demand composite score of 5.16, which places it near the median. The city's economic character is most defined by its tight labor market and moderate labor demand growth, with employment growing at a rate of 0.44% year-over-year. This combination of low unemployment and moderate labor demand suggests a competitive job market.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 0.44% and weekly hours deviation of 0.246% above trend indicate a moderate expansion of labor demand, signaling genuine demand for workers in the city. This combination suggests that businesses are adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours, indicating a healthy labor market. However, the near-median percentile rank of 52nd suggests that this growth is not exceptionally strong compared to other cities.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 2.70% is extremely low, ranking in the top tier at the 96th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring and retaining workers, as the pool of available labor is small. As a result, businesses may need to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract and retain talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of 3.49% is moderate, ranking near the median at the 48th percentile. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not exceptionally quickly. While this wage growth supports worker purchasing power, it may not be sufficient to drive significant economic expansion.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of 6.06, based on $208 per square foot and $34.31 per hour, Columbus ranks near the median at the 43rd percentile, indicating a moderately affordable city. However, the 1.0% year-over-year increase in PSF suggests that affordability may be decreasing. A high cost of living percentile rank would indicate greater affordability, but in this case, the city's rank suggests that it may not offer a significant talent attraction advantage without wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is contracting at a rate of -1.50% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile. This decline in labor force supply suggests that businesses may face structural headwinds in hiring, as the pool of available workers is shrinking.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is -25.53%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 10th percentile, indicating a significant tightening of housing supply. This suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 39 days, with an 8.3% year-over-year increase, ranking in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This suggests a slower market, where homes are taking longer to sell, making it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share of 1.40, ranking in the bottom tier at the 16th percentile, Columbus has a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This suggests that the city may be less suited for businesses that require a deep pool of specialized office workers, such as tech or finance companies.

In conclusion, Columbus offers a business a competitive labor market with moderate labor demand growth, but also presents challenges in terms of hiring and retaining workers due to its tight labor market. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the city's declining labor force supply, which may limit the availability of workers and drive up labor costs.
