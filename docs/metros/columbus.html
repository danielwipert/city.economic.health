Economic Analysis

Columbus, with an overall grade of B- and a composite score ranking it at the 52.8th percentile among 50 US metros, is characterized by its low unemployment rate of 3.30% and a labor demand composite score of 4.88, which ranks below average at the 36th percentile. The city's economic character is also defined by its stagnant labor force growth and declining building permits. Specifically, the labor demand and unemployment metrics suggest a complex labor market with both opportunities and challenges.

Labor Demand

Columbus has an employment growth rate of +0.06% and weekly hours deviation of +0.492% from its own trend, indicating a moderate expansion of labor demand. However, the combination of these metrics suggests that the city is experiencing a genuine demand expansion, albeit at a slow pace. This signals that businesses may face some competition for talent, but the market is not overly tight.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Columbus is 3.30%, ranking at the 96th percentile, which indicates a very tight labor market. This means that businesses may face significant challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is relatively small. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive wages and benefits to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Columbus is +4.15%, ranking near the median at the 56th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. At the same time, workers in the city are experiencing a moderate increase in purchasing power, which could support local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Columbus has a cost of living score that ranks at the 65th percentile, with a PSF of $205/sqft and an average hourly earnings of $34.84/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.88. This suggests that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. As a result, businesses may be able to attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Columbus is shrinking at a rate of -2.20% year-over-year, indicating a contraction in the supply of available workers. This decline in labor force growth poses a significant challenge for businesses looking to hire, as it may limit their ability to find qualified candidates.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Columbus has declined by -14.98% year-over-year, suggesting that the housing supply is tightening. This decline in building permits may lead to future affordability challenges and limit the city's ability to accommodate a growing workforce.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Columbus is currently 48 days, with a year-over-year increase of +6.7%. This suggests that the housing market is slowing down, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. However, the relatively slow pace of sales may also indicate a weakening demand for housing.

Office Economy

Columbus has a professional and office worker share that ranks at the 12th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. As a result, the city may be less suited for businesses that require a deep pool of specialized office or professional talent, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms.

In conclusion, Columbus offers businesses a relatively affordable cost of living and a moderate pace of wage growth, but its tight labor market and declining labor force growth pose significant challenges for hiring and expansion. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's shrinking labor force, which may limit the availability of qualified candidates and hinder business growth.
