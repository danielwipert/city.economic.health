Economic Analysis

Columbus, with an overall grade of B- and a composite score ranking it at the 52.8th percentile among 50 US metros, is characterized by its low unemployment rate of 3.30% and a labor demand composite score of 4.88, which ranks below average at the 36th percentile. The city's economic character is most defined by its tight labor market and moderate wage growth. Specifically, the combination of a +0.06% employment growth rate and +0.492% weekly hours deviation signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit a slow one.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Columbus is +0.06% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating +0.492% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, indicating that jobs are being added and hours are running above trend. However, the below-average labor demand composite score suggests that this expansion is not particularly strong.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Columbus is 3.30%, ranking at the 96th percentile, which means the market is extremely tight. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the low unemployment rate exerts upward pressure on wages. As a result, companies may need to offer competitive salaries to attract talent.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Columbus is +4.15% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 56th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. At the same time, workers in Columbus are experiencing an increase in purchasing power, which can benefit local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Columbus has a cost of living score that ranks at the 65th percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $205/sqft to $34.84/hr, or 5.88. This indicates that the city is slightly above average in terms of affordability. However, the fact that the PSF is not falling year-over-year means that affordability is not improving. Still, the relatively high affordability score suggests that Columbus may have a talent attraction advantage without needing to offer significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Columbus is shrinking at a rate of -2.20% year-over-year, ranking at the 18th percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is decreasing. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to compensate for this shrinking labor pool.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Columbus is decreasing by -14.98% year-over-year, ranking at the 26th percentile. This decline in permits suggests that housing supply is tightening, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for workforce accommodation in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Columbus is currently 48 days, with a year-over-year increase of +6.7%, ranking at the 71st percentile. This slower market means that homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it a more accessible market for relocating workers. However, this trend may also indicate a normalization of the market rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

Columbus has a professional and office worker share that ranks at the 12th percentile, with a composite score of 0.82. This suggests that the city's talent pool is not particularly deep in terms of professional and office sectors, making it less suited for businesses that require a high concentration of tech, finance, or consulting talent. Instead, Columbus may be more suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

In conclusion, Columbus offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market and moderate wage growth, but also poses challenges such as a shrinking labor force and tightening housing supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's limited labor force growth, which may require businesses to adapt their recruitment strategies to attract and retain talent.
