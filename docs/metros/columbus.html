Economic Analysis

Columbus, with an overall grade of B and a composite score ranking it 50.3th percentile among 50 US metros, is characterized by its low unemployment rate of 3.20% and a near-median labor demand composite score of 4.57. The city's economic character is also defined by its stagnant labor force growth and sharp decline in building permits. Specifically, the labor demand and unemployment metrics, with scores of 40th and 96th percentile respectively, set the tone for the city's current economic situation.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Columbus is +0.02% year-over-year, combined with a +0.246% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, signaling a genuine demand expansion, albeit a modest one. This combination indicates that while jobs are being added, the increase in hours worked suggests that the existing workforce is shouldering a slightly heavier load. The near-median percentile rank of 40th suggests that labor demand is neither exceptionally strong nor weak.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Columbus stands at 3.20%, ranking in the top tier at the 96th percentile, which signifies a very tight labor market. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is limited, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate suggests that workers have significant bargaining power.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Columbus is +3.31% year-over-year, placing it near the median at the 46th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not at an alarming rate. At the same time, workers are experiencing an increase in purchasing power, which can contribute to local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $205/sqft to $34.40/hr, and a year-over-year decrease of 0.5% in PSF, Columbus ranks in the near-median 59th percentile for affordability. This means the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without necessitating significant wage premiums. The falling PSF relative to wages is a key driver of this affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Columbus is shrinking at a rate of -1.35% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 22nd percentile. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of potential workers is decreasing. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to this shrinking labor market.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -37.46%, placing Columbus in the bottom tier at the 2nd percentile. This sharp decline signals a tightening housing supply, which could lead to decreased affordability and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The lack of new housing developments may constrain the city's ability to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

Homes in Columbus are currently sitting on the market for 44 days, with a year-over-year increase of 7.3%, ranking in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This slower market means that relocating workers may find it easier to find and purchase a home, as the market is less competitive. However, this could also indicate a normalization of the market rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

Columbus has a relatively shallow professional talent pool, ranking in the bottom tier at the 14th percentile. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses requiring a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. Instead, Columbus may be more accommodating to industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

In conclusion, Columbus offers a business a relatively affordable location with a tight labor market, which can be both a blessing and a curse. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the city's shrinking labor force and sharp decline in building permits, which may limit the availability of talent and housing for a growing workforce.
