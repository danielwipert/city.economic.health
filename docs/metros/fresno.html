Economic Analysis

The city of Fresno has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 42.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its high labor demand, with a composite score in the top tier, and its high unemployment rate, which is in the bottom tier at 7.70%. These two metrics suggest a complex labor market with both strong job growth and significant slack.

Labor Demand

Fresno's employment growth rate is 2.47% year-over-year, and its weekly hours are 1.481% below trend. The combination of these two metrics signals genuine demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs at a rate above the national average, but hours worked are slightly below trend, indicating that the growth is not solely due to existing workers taking on more hours. This suggests a healthy labor market with room for expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Fresno is 7.70%, which is in the bottom tier. This indicates a labor market with significant slack, making it easier for businesses to hire workers without facing intense competition. However, this also means that local consumer demand may be weaker due to the higher number of unemployed individuals.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Fresno is 5.14% year-over-year, which is above average, ranking in the 70th percentile. This fast wage growth signals rising labor costs for employers, but it also means that workers have increasing purchasing power, which can benefit local businesses that rely on consumer spending.

Cost of Living

Fresno has a cost of living score that ranks in the 33rd percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $263/sqft to $33.85/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.77. This indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which can make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is rising 0.4% year-over-year relative to wages further exacerbates this issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Fresno is shrinking at a rate of -1.42% year-over-year. This contraction in labor force supply poses a structural headwind for hiring, as businesses may face challenges in finding qualified workers to fill open positions.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Fresno has decreased by 6.89% year-over-year. This decline suggests that housing supply is tightening, which can lead to future affordability issues and make it more difficult for workers to relocate to the city.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Fresno is 57 days, with no year-over-year change. This relatively slow pace indicates a buyer-friendly market, which can make it more accessible for relocating workers to find housing. However, this also suggests that the housing market may not be as competitive as in other cities.

Office Economy

Fresno's professional and office worker share ranks in the 36th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow talent pool in these sectors. This makes the city less suited for businesses that require a deep knowledge-economy talent pool, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms, but more suitable for industries with different labor requirements.

In conclusion, Fresno offers a complex labor market with strong job growth but significant slack, making it easier for businesses to hire but also posing challenges due to its relatively high cost of living and tightening housing supply. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker considering Fresno as a business location is the city's shrinking labor force, which can limit hiring capacity and require wage premiums to attract talent.
