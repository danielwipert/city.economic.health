Economic Analysis

Fresno has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 49.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its high labor demand, with a composite score in the 78th percentile, and its high unemployment rate, which is in the bottom tier at 0th percentile, with a rate of 7.40%. These two metrics signal a complex labor market with both strong job growth and significant slack.

Labor Demand

Fresno's employment growth rate is 2.04% year-over-year, and its weekly hours are deviating from trend by -1.744%, still resulting in a labor demand composite score in the 78th percentile. This combination signals genuine demand expansion, as the city is adding jobs and hours are running above trend, indicating a strong labor market. The labor demand is robust, suggesting that businesses can find workers, but may face competition for talent.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Fresno is 7.40%, which is in the bottom tier at 0th percentile, indicating a significant amount of slack in the labor market. This means that the market is not tight, and businesses may find it easier to hire workers without facing intense competition. However, the high unemployment rate also suggests weaker local consumer demand, which could impact businesses that rely on local spending.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Fresno is 5.16% year-over-year, which is above average, ranking in the 66th percentile. This rate of wage growth is moderate to fast, suggesting that employer labor costs are rising, but worker purchasing power is also increasing. As a result, businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, but can also expect to benefit from a more affluent local workforce.

Cost of Living

Fresno has a cost of living score in the 53rd percentile, with a price-to-salary ratio of $263/sqft to $33.46/hr, resulting in a ratio of 7.86. This means that the city is near the median in terms of affordability, neither particularly expensive nor cheap compared to its peers. The stable year-over-year change in PSF (+0.0% YoY) suggests that affordability is not improving or deteriorating significantly. As a result, businesses may not need to offer significant wage premiums to attract talent, but may still face some challenges in retaining workers due to the relatively average cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in Fresno is growing at a rate of 0.00% year-over-year, which is ranked in the 66th percentile, indicating a stable labor supply. This means that the workforce is neither expanding nor contracting significantly, suggesting that businesses may not face significant challenges in hiring, but also may not benefit from a rapidly growing talent pool. The implication is that hiring capacity is unlikely to be severely constrained, but businesses should still plan carefully to attract and retain workers.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Fresno is decreasing by 6.89% year-over-year, which is ranked in the 38th percentile, indicating a tightening housing supply. This suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand. As a result, businesses may need to consider the potential impact of a tightening housing market on their ability to attract and retain workers.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Fresno is 55 days, with a year-over-year increase of 5.8%, resulting in a percentile rank of 54. This means that the market is slowing down slightly, making it more accessible for workers relocating to the city. Office Economy

Fresno has an office economy score in the 34th percentile, indicating a relatively shallow professional talent pool. This suggests that the city is less suited for businesses that require a deep pool of specialized office workers, such as tech or finance companies. However, the city may be more suitable for businesses that require a more general workforce, such as logistics or manufacturing companies.

In conclusion, Fresno offers businesses a complex labor market with both strong job growth and significant slack, as well as a relatively average cost of living. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for businesses is the tightening housing supply, which may impact future affordability and workforce accommodation. Decision-makers should carefully consider these factors when evaluating Fresno as a potential location for their business.
