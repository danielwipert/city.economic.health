Economic Analysis

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 52.0th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low unemployment rate of 3.20% and a labor force growth rate of 0.88% YoY. The city's economic character is defined by these two metrics, indicating a tight labor market with an expanding workforce. Specifically, the low unemployment rate and positive labor force growth suggest a competitive yet dynamic environment.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Atlanta is -0.11% YoY, combined with a weekly hours deviation of +0.454% from its own trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 3.69, which ranks in the bottom tier (12th percentile). This signals a contraction in labor demand, as the slight increase in hours worked is not enough to offset the decline in employment growth. The scenario suggests a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Atlanta is 3.20%, ranking in the top tier (84th percentile), indicating a tight labor market with little slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city will face significant competition for talent, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. The low unemployment rate implies that workers have more bargaining power, making it essential for employers to offer competitive compensation packages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Atlanta is +2.99%, ranking near the median (42nd percentile), indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively, while workers are experiencing some improvement in their purchasing power. The moderate wage growth may not be enough to keep pace with the cost of living, potentially affecting the attractiveness of the city to workers.

Cost of Living

Atlanta has a cost of living score that ranks in the 67th percentile, with a PSF of $195/sqft and average hourly earnings of $37.03/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.27. The city is more affordable than many of its peers, particularly since the PSF is falling by 0.5% YoY. This affordability advantage can be a significant talent attraction factor, as workers may not require wage premiums to maintain their standard of living in Atlanta.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in Atlanta is growing at a rate of 0.88% YoY, ranking in the top tier (82nd percentile), indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the city has a structural advantage in terms of hiring capacity, as the increasing labor force can provide a steady stream of potential employees for businesses.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Atlanta is increasing by 7.37% YoY, ranking above average (60th percentile), signaling an expansion in housing supply. This growth in permits suggests that the city is likely to experience improving affordability and a more accommodating environment for its workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market in Atlanta is 59 days, with a YoY increase of 3.5%, ranking near the median (48th percentile). This indicates a relatively competitive market for workers relocating to the city, as homes are selling at a moderate pace. The rising days on market may signal a healthy normalization of the housing market, rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

Atlanta has an office economy score that ranks in the 42nd percentile, indicating a moderate depth of professional talent. The city is suited for businesses that require a mix of office and non-office skills, but may not be the best fit for companies that rely heavily on specialized tech, finance, or consulting talent.

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market, moderate wage growth, and an expanding workforce, making it an attractive location for companies that can navigate the competitive hiring environment. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential for upward pressure on wages due to the low unemployment rate, which may impact labor costs and profitability.
