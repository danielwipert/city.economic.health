Economic Analysis

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 51.8th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile, and its tight unemployment market, with a rate of 3.40% ranking it in the top tier at the 84th percentile. Specifically, the labor demand is characterized by a -0.46% employment growth rate and a +0.595% weekly hours deviation from its own trend.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Atlanta is -0.46% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating +0.595% from its own 12-month trend, signaling a contraction in labor demand rather than genuine expansion. This combination indicates that while hours are slightly above trend, the overall job market is not adding positions, suggesting a potential survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. This metric is in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Atlanta is 3.40%, which ranks in the top tier at the 84th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with little slack. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, with more wage pressure as a result. For a company trying to hire in this market, it may be necessary to offer competitive wages to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Atlanta is +3.11% year-over-year, ranking below average at the 36th percentile. This moderate wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not as quickly as in other metros, and worker purchasing power is increasing, albeit at a slower pace. This could be beneficial for businesses looking to manage labor costs while still offering competitive compensation.

Cost of Living

Atlanta has a cost of living score that ranks above average at the 76th percentile, with a PSF of $194/sqft and average hourly earnings of $37.62, resulting in a ratio of 5.16. The fact that PSF is falling by -0.5% year-over-year contributes to its relatively high affordability score. This means that Atlanta has a talent attraction advantage due to its affordability, allowing businesses to attract workers without needing to offer significant wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force in Atlanta is growing at a rate of +1.18% year-over-year, ranking in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This positive growth indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a favorable condition for hiring. As the labor force grows, businesses in Atlanta may find it easier to fill open positions and expand their operations.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Atlanta has increased by +15.21% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 62nd percentile. This rise in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which could lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future. This is a positive sign for businesses considering relocation, as it indicates a potential for a more stable and affordable housing market for their employees.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Atlanta is 60 days, with a year-over-year change of +0.0%, ranking below average at the 36th percentile. This relatively fast market means that homes are selling quickly, which could make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the stable year-over-year change suggests a balanced market that is not overly competitive.

Office Economy

Atlanta's professional and office worker share ranks below average at the 32nd percentile, indicating a less deep talent pool in these sectors compared to other metros. This suggests that Atlanta is better suited for businesses that are not heavily reliant on tech, finance, consulting, or HQ operations, and may be more accommodating to industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market and moderate wage growth, along with an expanding labor force and improving housing affordability. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to consider is the low labor demand, which could signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to expand or establish a strong foothold in the market.
