Economic Analysis

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area has an overall grade of B-, ranking 52.3th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low unemployment rate of 3.20% and a cost of living score that indicates relative affordability. The city's economic character is defined by these two metrics, which signal a tight labor market with moderate affordability. Specifically, the cost of living index shows a PSF of $195/sqft, which has decreased by 0.5% YoY, combined with an average hourly wage of $37.62, resulting in a ratio of 5.18.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Atlanta is -0.46% YoY, while weekly hours are deviating 0.595% above the city's own 12-month baseline, indicating a scenario where hours are increasing despite job losses, which suggests a survivor squeeze. This combination signals that the labor market is not expanding in terms of job numbers, but existing workers are taking on more hours. The labor demand composite score of 3.06 falls into the bottom tier, at the 12th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Atlanta is 3.20%, which places it in the top tier, at the 84th percentile, indicating a very tight labor market. This tight market means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as there are fewer unemployed individuals seeking work, leading to potential wage pressure. The low unemployment rate suggests that businesses may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent.

Wage Growth

Wage growth in Atlanta is +3.11% YoY, which ranks below average, at the 36th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies that labor costs for employers are increasing, but at a slower pace than in many other cities. While this may not be ideal for employers, it does mean that workers have somewhat stronger purchasing power, which can be beneficial for local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Atlanta's cost of living score indicates that it is more affordable than many of its peer cities, with a percentile rank of 78th. The city's PSF of $195/sqft, which has decreased by 0.5% YoY, combined with average hourly earnings of $37.62, results in a ratio that suggests the city is relatively affordable. This affordability can be a significant advantage for talent attraction, as businesses may not need to offer wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Atlanta is growing at a rate of +0.88% YoY, which places it in the top tier, at the 82nd percentile. This positive growth rate indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a favorable condition for businesses looking to hire. An expanding labor force can reduce the challenges associated with a tight labor market, making it easier for companies to find the talent they need.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Atlanta has increased by +15.21% YoY, ranking above average, at the 62nd percentile. This increase in building permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which can lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce. As housing supply grows, it can help mitigate potential workforce attraction and retention issues.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Atlanta is 59 days, with a YoY increase of +3.5%, placing it near the median, at the 48th percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced market, neither extremely fast nor slow. For workers relocating to Atlanta, this means that the housing market is competitive but still accessible, with homes sitting on the market for nearly two months before going under contract.

Office Economy

Atlanta's professional and office worker share is below average, ranking at the 32nd percentile. This suggests that while there is a talent pool, it is not as deep as in other cities, making Atlanta less suited for businesses that require a large number of specialized, knowledge-economy workers, such as tech, finance, or consulting firms. However, it may be more suitable for businesses with less specialized labor needs.

The bottom line for businesses considering Atlanta is that the city offers a relatively affordable cost of living and an expanding labor force, which can be attractive for talent acquisition and retention. However, the single biggest risk or constraint is the tight labor market, with its low unemployment rate and potential for wage pressure, which businesses need to factor into their hiring and compensation strategies to remain competitive in this market.
