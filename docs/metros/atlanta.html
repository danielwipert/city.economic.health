Economic Analysis

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area has earned an overall grade of A, ranking in the 65.2th percentile among 50 US metros, with a strong labor demand and labor force growth being the two metrics that most define its current economic character, at 70th and 88th percentiles, respectively. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.66 and labor force growth rate of +1.93% YoY signal a thriving economy. With an employment growth rate of +0.41% YoY and weekly hours deviation of +0.789%, Atlanta's economy is characterized by genuine demand expansion.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +0.41% YoY and weekly hours deviation of +0.789% indicate a strong labor market with genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is adding jobs and workers are putting in more hours than the 12-month trend, suggesting a healthy and growing economy. The labor demand composite score of 5.66, at the 70th percentile, further reinforces this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 3.50% is relatively low, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The low unemployment rate also suggests that workers have more bargaining power.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +3.15% is near the median, ranking in the 42nd percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively, and workers have some, but not exceptional, purchasing power. The moderate wage growth may help attract and retain workers without breaking the bank for employers.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of 5.37, based on $197/sqft and $36.71/hr, and a percentile rank of 55th, Atlanta is relatively affordable compared to its peers. This affordability, combined with a stable PSF YoY change of +0.0%, makes the city an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The affordable cost of living can be a major draw for businesses looking to relocate or expand.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of +1.93% YoY, ranking in the 88th percentile, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This growth suggests that the city has a strong pipeline of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to hire and expand their operations. The growing labor force can help support business growth and expansion.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is +15.16%, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating an expansion of housing supply. This growth in permits suggests that the city is experiencing an increase in developer confidence, which can lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce. The expanding housing supply can help support the growing labor force.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 56 days, with a year-over-year increase of +3.7%, ranking in the 50th percentile. This suggests a relatively balanced market, neither extremely competitive nor excessively slow, making it accessible for workers relocating to the city. The moderate days on market can provide a healthy environment for businesses to attract and retain workers.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share of 3.06, ranking in the 64th percentile, Atlanta has a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. The city's professional talent pool is above average, making it an attractive location for businesses in these sectors. However, it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area offers businesses a strong labor market, growing workforce, and relatively affordable cost of living, making it an attractive location for relocation or expansion. However, the tight labor market and moderate wage growth may pose challenges for businesses looking to hire and retain workers. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the potential for rising labor costs and increased competition for talent in a tight market.
