Economic Analysis

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area has an overall grade of C, with a score of 42.9 out of 100, indicating a very poor performance compared to other metros. The city's economy is currently defined by its slow job growth, with employment increasing by only -0.1% over the past year, and a relatively low unemployment rate of 3.5%, which scores 60 out of 100, indicating a strength in the labor market.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is losing jobs, with a -0.1% increase in employment over the past year, and workers' hours are rising by 0.6%, but this is still a weakness, scoring 16 out of 100, indicating a slower hiring pace than most metros. This means that the job market is not as strong as it could be, with fewer jobs being added and workers not seeing significant increases in their hours.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Atlanta is 3.5%, which is lower than many other metros, scoring 60 out of 100, and making it a strength, as it indicates a tight labor market. This low unemployment rate makes it harder for employers to hire, as there are fewer workers available, and it also puts upward pressure on wages.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay in Atlanta rose by 2.9% over the past year, which is slower than many other metros, scoring 30 out of 100, and indicating a weakness, as it means that workers are not seeing significant increases in their paychecks. This slow wage growth also means that employers may not face significant labor cost increases, but it also limits workers' purchasing power.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Atlanta is about average, with a score of 45 out of 100, and a price per square foot of $193, compared to an hourly pay of $36.77, and the price per square foot is actually falling by 1.0% over the past year. This means that housing is becoming more affordable for local workers, which could be a talent attraction advantage for employers.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in Atlanta is growing quickly, with a 2.6% increase over the past year, scoring 94 out of 100, and indicating a strength, as it provides a large and expanding workforce for employers to hire from. This fast labor force growth is a positive sign for the city's economy, as it indicates a strong and growing workforce.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in Atlanta increased by 1.4% over the past year, scoring 54 out of 100, and indicating an average performance, as it suggests that the housing supply is expanding, but not as quickly as in some other metros. This moderate increase in building permits signals that the city's housing supply is growing, but it may not be enough to keep up with demand.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Atlanta are taking 61 days to sell, which is getting slower, with a 1.7% increase over the past year, scoring 28 out of 100, and indicating a weakness, as it suggests a softening housing market. This slower pace of home sales indicates that the housing market may be weakening, making it easier for buyers to find homes, but also potentially reducing demand.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The office-based workforce in Atlanta is smaller than in many other metros, scoring 28 out of 100, and indicating a weakness, as it limits the city's appeal to businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This smaller office workforce means that Atlanta may not be the best fit for companies that require a large pool of specialized office workers.

The bottom line is that Atlanta's biggest strength is its fast labor force growth, which provides a large and expanding workforce for employers to hire from, but its biggest risk is its slow job growth, which limits the city's ability to attract and retain businesses. Overall, Atlanta's economy is facing significant challenges, but its growing workforce and relatively low unemployment rate are positive signs that could help drive future growth.
