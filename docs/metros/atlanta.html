Economic Analysis

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area has earned an overall grade of A+ with a composite score of 70.6th percentile, ranking it among the top US metros. The city's economic character is largely defined by its strong labor demand, with a composite score of 5.35, and its low unemployment rate of 3.20%, which ranks in the 78th percentile. These metrics signal a city with a thriving job market and a tight labor pool.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Atlanta is 0.03% year-over-year, combined with a weekly hours deviation of +1.004% from its own 12-month baseline, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a period of job growth, with hours worked above trend, suggesting that businesses are hiring and workers are in high demand. The labor demand composite score of 5.35, ranking in the 70th percentile, further supports this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Atlanta is 3.20%, ranking in the 78th percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This means that businesses may face challenges in finding available workers, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract top talent. With such a low unemployment rate, the practical implication for a business trying to hire in Atlanta is that it may need to be prepared to offer higher salaries or benefits to attract and retain workers.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Atlanta is +4.31%, ranking in the 60th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a manageable pace, while workers are experiencing an increase in purchasing power. As a result, businesses may need to factor in higher labor costs, but can also expect to benefit from a workforce with increased disposable income.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $198/sqft to $36.88/hr, Atlanta ranks in the 71st percentile in terms of affordability, indicating that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers. This means that businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to offset high living costs. The affordability of Atlanta makes it an attractive location for businesses looking to relocate or expand.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Atlanta is growing at a rate of +1.58% year-over-year, ranking in the 86th percentile, indicating that the workforce supply is expanding rapidly. This growth suggests that businesses will have access to a increasing pool of potential workers, making it easier to hire and staff new positions. The implication for hiring capacity is positive, with a growing labor force providing a steady stream of new talent.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Atlanta is increasing at a rate of +8.79% year-over-year, ranking in the 68th percentile, indicating that housing supply is expanding. This growth in permits suggests that the city is experiencing an increase in developer confidence, which should lead to an expansion of housing supply and improved affordability in the future. As a result, businesses can expect that their workers will have access to a range of housing options, making it easier for them to relocate to the area.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Atlanta is 52 days, with a year-over-year increase of +2.0%, ranking in the 50th percentile. This suggests that the housing market is relatively balanced, with homes selling at a moderate pace. For a worker relocating to Atlanta, this means that they will have a reasonable amount of time to find a new home, without feeling rushed or facing intense competition.

Office Economy

With an office economy composite score ranking in the 66th percentile, Atlanta has a deep talent pool of professional and office workers, making it well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors. However, the city may be less suited for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors, which may require a different type of workforce. The presence of a strong office economy makes Atlanta an attractive location for businesses looking to establish or expand their headquarters or professional services operations.

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a strong labor market, low unemployment, and moderate wage growth, making it an attractive location for businesses looking to relocate or expand. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers to factor in is the potential for a tight labor market to drive up wages and make it challenging to attract and retain top talent, which could impact business costs and competitiveness.
