Economic Analysis

The Atlanta-Sandy Springs-Roswell metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 51.8th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand, with a composite score in the bottom tier at the 12th percentile, and its tight unemployment market, with a rate of 3.40% ranking in the top tier at the 84th percentile. These metrics signal a challenging environment for businesses looking to hire and expand.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Atlanta is -0.46% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating 0.595% above the city's own trend. This combination signals a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles, rather than genuine demand expansion. The labor demand composite score of 3.06 is in the bottom tier, indicating payrolls are contracting.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Atlanta is 3.40%, which is in the top tier at the 84th percentile. This signals a tight market with little slack, making it harder for businesses to hire and potentially leading to wage pressure. For a business trying to hire in this city, the tight labor market implies a competitive environment where attracting and retaining talent may be challenging.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Atlanta is +3.11%, which is below average at the 36th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate implies rising labor costs for employers, but it also means workers have somewhat stronger purchasing power. However, the pace of wage growth is not exceptionally high, suggesting a relatively stable cost environment for businesses.

Cost of Living

Atlanta has a cost of living score that ranks in the above-average tier at the 76th percentile, with a PSF of $194/sqft that is decreasing by 0.5% year-over-year, compared to hourly earnings of $37.62. This means the city is relatively more affordable than its peers, which can be a talent attraction advantage without needing significant wage premiums. The falling PSF relative to wages is a key driver of this affordability.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Atlanta is growing at a rate of +1.18% year-over-year, which is in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a growing pool of potential employees.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Atlanta is increasing by +15.21% year-over-year, ranking in the above-average tier at the 62nd percentile. This signals that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. The increase in building permits is a positive indicator for future workforce attraction and retention.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Atlanta is 60 days, with a year-over-year change of +0.0%, ranking in the below-average tier at the 35th percentile. This suggests a relatively fast-paced market that may be competitive for relocating workers, potentially making it tougher for them to find and secure housing.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in professional and office sectors in Atlanta ranks in the below-average tier at the 32nd percentile. This indicates a less deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, or HQ decisions, suggesting the city might be more suited for industries with fewer specialized roles, such as industrial or logistics. The city's economy is not as heavily weighted towards professional services as some of its peers.

In conclusion, Atlanta offers businesses a relatively affordable cost of living and an expanding labor force, but it also presents challenges such as a tight labor market and moderate wage growth. The single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker to factor in is the city's low labor demand, which could signal a challenging environment for hiring and expansion, potentially requiring strategic planning to attract and retain talent in a competitive market.
