Economic Analysis

The San Antonio-New Braunfels metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 49.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its below-average labor demand, with a composite score of 4.27, and its top-tier cost of living, with a ratio of $169/sqft to $32.51/hr, ranking it in the 86th percentile for affordability. The labor demand and cost of living metrics are crucial in understanding the city's current economic landscape, with the former indicating a slower job market and the latter suggesting an attractive environment for talent.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in San Antonio-New Braunfels is +0.36% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.533% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, indicating that the job market is not expanding rapidly. The below-average labor demand composite score of 4.27 further reinforces this notion, suggesting that the city is not experiencing genuine demand expansion.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Antonio-New Braunfels is 4.30%, ranking it in the 26th percentile, indicating a relatively loose labor market. This means that there is some slack in the market, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire, but also potentially leading to weaker local consumer demand. The practical implication for a business trying to hire in this city is that it may have a relatively easier time finding candidates, but may also face less competitive pressure to offer high wages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in San Antonio-New Braunfels is +3.90%, ranking it in the 54th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not extremely rapidly, while worker purchasing power is increasing at a steady pace. The moderate wage growth implies that businesses can expect to pay slightly higher wages, but the increase is not drastic.

Cost of Living

San Antonio-New Braunfels has a cost of living ratio of $169/sqft to $32.51/hr, with the price per square foot falling -3.4% year-over-year, ranking it in the 86th percentile for affordability. This means that the city is relatively affordable compared to its peers, making it an attractive location for talent without requiring significant wage premiums. The top-tier affordability score suggests that businesses can recruit and retain workers without breaking the bank.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Antonio-New Braunfels is growing at a rate of +0.19% year-over-year, ranking it in the 56th percentile, indicating a slow but positive expansion of the workforce supply. This implies that the hiring capacity in the city is increasing, but not rapidly, providing a moderate environment for businesses to find new talent.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in San Antonio-New Braunfels is increasing by +24.39% year-over-year, ranking it in the 82nd percentile, signaling a significant expansion of the housing supply. This suggests that the city is experiencing a surge in developer confidence, which will likely lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in San Antonio-New Braunfels is 74 days, with a year-over-year change of +0.0%, ranking it in the 30th percentile. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, making it challenging for relocating workers to find homes. The low percentile rank suggests that the market is competitive, and workers may face difficulties in finding suitable housing.

Office Economy

San Antonio-New Braunfels has an above-average share of professional and office workers, ranking it in the 76th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses that require specialized knowledge-economy roles, but may not be the best fit for industries with more industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The San Antonio-New Braunfels metro area offers businesses a unique combination of moderate wage growth, affordable cost of living, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the below-average labor demand, which may indicate a slower job market and potential challenges in finding skilled workers, despite the city's overall affordability and talent pool advantages.
