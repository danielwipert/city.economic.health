Economic Analysis

The San Antonio-New Braunfels metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it at the 51.1th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its high cost of living affordability, with a percentile rank of 92, and its near-median wage growth of 3.73% year-over-year. The labor demand composite score of 4.36, ranking at the 28th percentile, also plays a significant role in shaping the city's economic landscape.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in San Antonio-New Braunfels is 0.51% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.651% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a labor market that is not experiencing genuine demand expansion, as the hours worked are below trend despite job growth. The labor demand composite score of 4.36, ranking at the 28th percentile, further supports this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in San Antonio-New Braunfels is 4.50%, ranking at the 30th percentile, indicating a labor market with some slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city may find it relatively easier to staff, but may also face weaker local consumer demand. The unemployment rate suggests that the market is not overly tight, giving employers some flexibility in hiring.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in San Antonio-New Braunfels is 3.73%, ranking at the 50th percentile, indicating a moderate pace of wage increases. This rate of wage growth suggests that employer labor costs are rising, but at a pace that is neither extremely fast nor stagnant. Workers in this city can expect to see their purchasing power increase, but not dramatically.

Cost of Living

San Antonio-New Braunfels has a cost of living percentile rank of 92, making it one of the more affordable cities relative to its peers. The PSF to earnings ratio is $170/sqft vs $33.17/hr, resulting in a ratio of 5.13, and the PSF is falling by 3.4% year-over-year. This affordability advantage means that businesses can attract talent without needing to offer significant wage premiums to compensate for a high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in San Antonio-New Braunfels is growing at a rate of 0.23% year-over-year, ranking at the 68th percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, albeit slowly, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire in this city. The growing labor force suggests that hiring capacity is increasing, but may not be rapid enough to support very fast-paced growth.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in San Antonio-New Braunfels is increasing by 25.98% year-over-year, ranking at the 76th percentile. This significant increase in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which should improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce. The rising permits signal a positive outlook for future affordability and workforce accommodation in this city.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in San Antonio-New Braunfels is 71 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 2.7%. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, although the decrease in days on market is modest. For workers relocating to this city, the housing market is competitive, but not extremely so, making it accessible for those looking to move.

Office Economy

San Antonio-New Braunfels has an office economy percentile rank of 76, indicating a deep talent pool in professional and office sectors. This city is well-suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions, but may not be as suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The San Antonio-New Braunfels metro area offers businesses a unique combination of affordability and a growing labor force, making it an attractive location for companies looking to expand. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's labor demand, which is not experiencing genuine expansion, and may require businesses to adapt their hiring strategies to the local market conditions.
