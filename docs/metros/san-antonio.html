Economic Analysis

San Antonio-New Braunfels has an overall grade of C+, scoring 49.7 out of 100 across 50 US metros, with its economy defined by a mix of strengths and weaknesses, including a relatively affordable cost of living and a growing office workforce. The city's cost of living, in particular, stands out, with a score of 86, indicating that it is more affordable than most metros, with a price per square foot of $169 and hourly pay of $32.51.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs, but at a slower pace than most metros, with employment growth of 0.4% over the past year, and workers' hours are actually falling by 0.5%. This suggests a weaker job market, where hiring is not as strong as in other cities, with a score of 34, indicating a weakness.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in San Antonio-New Braunfels is 4.3%, which is higher than in most metros, making it easier for employers to hire, but also indicating a weaker local economy, with a score of 26. This higher unemployment rate means that employers may have an easier time finding workers, but it also suggests that the local consumer demand may be weaker.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 3.9% over the past year, which is about average for the 50 metros, with a score of 54, indicating that wage growth is neither particularly strong nor weak. This means that employers' labor costs are rising, but not as quickly as in some other cities, and workers' paychecks are growing, but not at an exceptional rate.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in San Antonio-New Braunfels is relatively affordable, with a price per square foot of $169 and hourly pay of $32.51, and it is getting even more affordable, with prices falling by 3.4% over the past year, earning a score of 86. This makes the city an attractive option for workers and businesses looking for a lower cost of living, with a significant advantage in terms of talent attraction.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in San Antonio-New Braunfels is growing, but at a slow pace of 0.2% over the past year, with a score of 56, indicating that the labor force is expanding, but not as quickly as in some other cities. This means that hiring may become more challenging over time, but for now, the labor force is still growing, albeit slowly.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in San Antonio-New Braunfels rose by 24.4% over the past year, with a score of 82, indicating a strong increase in home-building activity, which suggests that the city's housing supply is likely to expand in the future. This is a positive sign for affordability and workforce attraction, as more homes will become available, putting downward pressure on prices.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in San Antonio-New Braunfels are taking 74 days to sell, which is not changing much over the past year, with a score of 30, indicating a weaker housing market. This suggests that the housing market is not particularly healthy, with homes taking a relatively long time to sell, which may indicate a softening market.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

San Antonio-New Braunfels has a large office-based workforce, with a score of 76, indicating that the city has a deeper talent pool in tech, finance, and professional services than most metros. This makes the city a good fit for businesses in these sectors, such as tech, finance, and consulting firms, which require a highly skilled and educated workforce.

The city's biggest strength is its affordable cost of living, which makes it an attractive option for workers and businesses, while its biggest risk is its relatively weak labor demand, which may make it harder for businesses to find the workers they need. Overall, San Antonio-New Braunfels offers a mix of advantages and challenges, making it a fair option for businesses and workers, but not without its drawbacks.
