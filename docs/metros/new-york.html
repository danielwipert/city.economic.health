Economic Analysis

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 54.0th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average labor demand and labor force growth, at 72nd and 76th percentiles, respectively. The city's economic character is defined by its employment growth rate of 0.48% and weekly hours deviation of 0.430%, indicating genuine demand expansion. With a wage growth rate of 4.50%, the city also exhibits rising labor costs and increasing worker purchasing power.

Labor Demand

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an employment growth rate of 0.48% and a weekly hours deviation of 0.430%, signaling genuine demand expansion as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This combination indicates that the city is experiencing a real increase in labor demand, rather than just a survivor squeeze where remaining workers absorb the load of eliminated roles. The labor demand composite score of 5.40 places the city in the 72nd percentile, confirming this trend.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is 4.30%, ranking in the 44th percentile, which suggests a near-median level of unemployment. This rate indicates that the market has some slack, making it slightly easier for businesses to hire compared to tighter markets. However, the relatively low unemployment rate still implies some competition for talent, which could lead to moderate wage pressure.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is 4.50%, placing it in the 62nd percentile. This rate of wage growth is above average, signaling rising labor costs for employers. As a result, workers in the area are experiencing increasing purchasing power, which can be beneficial for local consumer demand but may pose challenges for businesses looking to control labor expenses.

Cost of Living

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has a cost of living ratio of $537/sqft to $40.69/hr, with a PSF increase of 3.3% YoY, ranking it in the 2nd percentile for affordability. This low percentile rank indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The rising PSF relative to wages further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is growing at a rate of 0.45% year-over-year, placing it in the 76th percentile. This positive growth rate indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a favorable condition for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a structural advantage in terms of hiring capacity.

Building Permits

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has seen a 15.42% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking it in the 78th percentile. This significant increase in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which could lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is 47 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 4.1%. This relatively fast pace of home sales, combined with the decreasing trend, indicates a competitive market that may be challenging for relocating workers to find housing quickly.

Office Economy

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an office and professional worker share of 2.82, ranking in the 56th percentile. This near-median rank suggests a moderately deep talent pool in professional and office sectors, making the city suited for a variety of businesses, including those in tech, finance, and consulting, but perhaps less ideal for industries requiring a large workforce in industrial or logistics roles.

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area offers businesses a dynamic environment with growing labor demand and an expanding workforce, but the high cost of living and competitive housing market pose significant challenges for talent attraction and retention. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's relatively low affordability, which may necessitate wage premiums to attract and retain top talent, potentially impacting business operational costs.
