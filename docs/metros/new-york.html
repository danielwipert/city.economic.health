Economic Analysis

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 52.9th percentile among 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average labor demand and labor force growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.42, combining a 0.96% employment growth rate and a -0.228% weekly hours deviation, suggests genuine demand expansion. The unemployment rate of 4.30% and labor force growth rate of 0.60% also define the city's economic character.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of 0.96% and weekly hours deviation of -0.228% indicate a moderate expansion of labor demand, signaling genuine demand growth as hours are not excessively above trend during job growth. This combination suggests that the city is adding jobs at a steady pace, with workers' hours not being overly stretched. The labor demand composite score of 5.42, ranking in the 60th percentile, reinforces this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 4.30% ranks in the 72nd percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market with some slack. This means that businesses may face moderate competition for talent, but it is still possible to hire without excessive wage pressure. The relatively low unemployment rate implies that workers have some bargaining power, but it is not extremely high.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of 1.95% is below average, ranking in the 28th percentile, indicating stagnant to moderate wage growth. This slow wage growth means that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but worker purchasing power is not increasing quickly either. The implication is a relatively flat cost environment for businesses, but with limited bargaining power for workers.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in New York Newark-Jersey City is relatively expensive, with a percentile rank of 6th, due to a PSF of $490/sqft and average hourly earnings of $40.39, resulting in a ratio of 12.13. Although the PSF is falling by 0.6% YoY, the city remains expensive, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The low affordability score means that businesses may need to compensate employees for the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of 0.60% year-over-year, ranking in the 72nd percentile, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This positive growth rate suggests that the city has a growing pool of potential employees, making it easier for businesses to hire and expand their operations. The increasing labor force supply is a favorable condition for businesses looking to grow.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is 33.43%, ranking in the 84th percentile, indicating a significant expansion of housing supply. This rapid growth in permits suggests that developer confidence is high, and the future housing supply is likely to increase, improving affordability and accommodating a growing workforce. The rising permits signal a positive outlook for future affordability and workforce accommodation.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 56 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 3.5%, indicating a relatively fast-paced market. The low percentile rank of 18th means that homes are selling quickly, making it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. The fast-paced market implies that workers may face competition when searching for homes, potentially affecting their decision to relocate.

Office Economy

The city has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share of 3.08, ranking in the 68th percentile. This suggests that the city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require specialized knowledge-economy talent. The city's strong office economy makes it an attractive location for businesses that rely on professional and office workers.

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area offers businesses a growing labor force, expanding housing supply, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for various industries. However, the city's high cost of living and relatively tight labor market are significant constraints that businesses should factor into their decision-making process, potentially requiring wage premiums to attract and retain talent.
