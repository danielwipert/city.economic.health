Economic Analysis

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 39.9th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a labor demand composite score of 4.80, which is below average at the 34th percentile. This city's economic character is most defined by its low labor demand and high cost of living, with a cost of living ratio of $494/sqft to $40.91/hr, resulting in a ratio of 12.08, which ranks in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile. The combination of low labor demand and high cost of living presents a challenging environment for businesses.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in New York Newark-Jersey City is +0.50% year-over-year, while weekly hours are -0.202% below trend, indicating a labor demand composite score that signals a contraction in genuine demand expansion. This combination suggests that the city is experiencing a slowdown in job growth, with hours worked below the 12-month baseline. The labor market is not demonstrating strong demand for workers.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in New York Newark-Jersey City is 4.70%, which is near the median at the 46th percentile, indicating a relatively balanced labor market with neither significant slack nor tightness. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate means that finding qualified workers may not be overly difficult, but it also does not indicate a surplus of available labor. The market conditions are average, with no significant advantage or disadvantage.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in New York Newark-Jersey City is +1.91%, which is below average at the 22nd percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth means that employer labor costs are not rising rapidly, but it also implies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. The slow wage growth may help keep labor costs in check, but it also suggests a lack of upward pressure on wages.

Cost of Living

New York Newark-Jersey City has a cost of living ratio of $494/sqft to $40.91/hr, resulting in a ratio of 12.08, which ranks in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, indicating that the city is expensive relative to peers. The low percentile rank means that the city is less affordable, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The high cost of living is a significant disadvantage for businesses trying to recruit workers.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in New York Newark-Jersey City is growing at a rate of -0.15% year-over-year, which is above average at the 64th percentile, indicating a contracting labor pool. The implication for hiring capacity is that the labor pool is shrinking, presenting a structural headwind for businesses trying to expand their workforce.

Building Permits

The strong growth in permits is a positive sign for the city's future housing market.

Days on Market

Office Economy

New York Newark-Jersey City has an office economy percentile rank of 72nd percentile, indicating a deep professional talent pool. The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area presents a mixed bag for businesses, with a strong office economy and expanding housing supply, but also a high cost of living and slow wage growth.
