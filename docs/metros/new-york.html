Economic Analysis

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 46.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its extremely low affordability, with a cost of living percentile rank of 0th percentile, and its strong labor force growth, at +0.45% YoY. The combination of these metrics suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to attract and retain talent without offering significant wage premiums.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in this metro area is +0.68% YoY, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.000%, indicating a near-stable labor demand environment. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit a modest one, with jobs being added and hours running slightly above trend. However, the labor demand composite score of 5.25 ranks only at the 56th percentile, suggesting that the demand is not exceptionally strong.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 4.30%, ranking at the 44th percentile, indicating a near-median level of slack in the labor market. This suggests that while the market is not extremely tight, it is also not overly loose, with some competition for talent still present. For a business trying to hire here, this means that they may face some challenges in attracting the right candidates, but it is still a manageable environment.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in this metro area is +2.82%, ranking at the 38th percentile, indicating a below-average pace of wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a moderate pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, but not rapidly. As a result, businesses may need to balance their labor costs with the need to attract and retain talent in a competitive market.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in this metro area is extremely high, with a PSF of $537/sqft and an earnings ratio of 13.25, ranking at the 0th percentile. This means that the city is very expensive relative to peers, making it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering significant wage premiums. The fact that PSF is rising by +3.3% YoY relative to wages further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in this metro area is growing at a rate of +0.45% YoY, ranking at the 76th percentile, indicating an expanding labor force supply. This suggests that the hiring capacity for businesses is increasing, with more workers available to fill open positions. However, the high cost of living may still pose a challenge in attracting and retaining talent.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in this metro area is increasing by +22.60% YoY, ranking at the 82nd percentile, indicating an expansion of housing supply. This suggests that the future affordability and workforce accommodation in the area may improve, as more housing options become available. However, the current cost of living remains a significant challenge.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in this metro area is 47 days, with a YoY decrease of -4.1%, ranking at the 12th percentile. This indicates a fast-paced and competitive housing market, making it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing. The low percentile rank suggests that homes are selling quickly, and workers may face significant competition in the market.

Office Economy

The professional and office worker share in this metro area is 3.08, ranking at the 66th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses that require specialized knowledge-economy talent, but may be less suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area offers businesses a strong and growing labor force, with an expanding housing supply and a deep professional talent pool. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the extremely high cost of living, which may require significant wage premiums to attract and retain talent, posing a challenge for businesses looking to establish or expand operations in this area.
