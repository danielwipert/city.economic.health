Economic Analysis

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 38.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its low labor demand and high cost of living. The labor demand composite score of 4.35, which combines a +0.25% employment growth rate and a -0.303% weekly hours deviation from trend, signals a contraction in genuine demand. The cost of living, with a PSF of $498/sqft and a ratio of 12.28 to hourly earnings of $40.54, ranks in the bottom tier at the 6th percentile.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +0.25% and weekly hours deviation of -0.303% from trend indicate a lack of genuine demand expansion, suggesting that the labor market is not experiencing significant growth. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, rather than a survivor squeeze, as hours are below trend during a period of slow job growth. The labor demand composite score of 4.35 is below average, ranking at the 36th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 4.50% is near the median, ranking at the 56th percentile, indicating a relatively balanced labor market with neither significant slack nor tightness. This rate suggests that businesses may face moderate challenges in hiring, but not extreme wage pressure. The market is not particularly tight, but it is also not overly slack, making it a relatively stable environment for hiring.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +1.79% is in the bottom tier, ranking at the 18th percentile, indicating stagnant wage growth. This slow wage growth suggests that labor costs for employers are not rising rapidly, but it also implies weak bargaining power for workers and potentially limited purchasing power. The slow wage growth may be a concern for businesses relying on local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

The cost of living in New York Newark-Jersey City is expensive, with a PSF of $498/sqft and a ratio of 12.28 to hourly earnings of $40.54, ranking in the bottom tier at the 6th percentile. However, the PSF is falling by -1.4% YoY, which may indicate some improvement in affordability. The high cost of living makes it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums, as the city's affordability is relatively low compared to peers.

Labor Force Growth

The labor force growth rate of -0.54% indicates a contracting labor pool, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile. This contraction suggests that the supply of workers is shrinking, which may create structural headwinds for hiring and business expansion. The declining labor force growth rate may limit the city's ability to support rapid business growth.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in building permits is +20.67%, ranking in the top tier at the 80th percentile, indicating an expansion in housing supply. This growth in permits suggests that developer confidence is high, and future housing supply is likely to increase, which may improve affordability and support workforce accommodation. The rising permits may help mitigate potential housing shortages and support business growth.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 53 days, with a -1.9% YoY change, ranking below average at the 22nd percentile. This relatively fast market suggests that homes are selling quickly, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. The fast-paced market may require businesses to provide relocation support or incentives to attract talent.

Office Economy

The share of professional and office workers is 2.60, ranking near the median at the 54th percentile, indicating a moderate depth of professional talent. This city is relatively well-suited for businesses that require a mix of office and non-office skills, but may not be the best fit for businesses that rely heavily on specialized professional or tech talent.

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area offers businesses a large and diverse labor market, but its high cost of living and slow wage growth pose significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's expensive cost of living, which may require businesses to offer wage premiums to attract and retain talent, potentially limiting their competitiveness and growth prospects.
