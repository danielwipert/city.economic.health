Economic Analysis

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking 53.8th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its above-average labor demand and labor force growth. The city's labor demand composite score of 5.40, combining a +0.48% employment growth rate and a +0.430% weekly hours deviation from its own trend, signals genuine demand expansion. Additionally, its labor force growth rate of +0.45% YoY indicates an expanding workforce supply.

Labor Demand

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an employment growth rate of +0.48% YoY and a weekly hours deviation of +0.430% from its own trend, indicating genuine demand expansion. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a period of job growth, with hours worked above the trend line, suggesting that employers are increasing their workforce and workload. This is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand or establish operations in the area.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is 4.30%, ranking near the median at the 43rd percentile. This rate indicates a relatively balanced labor market, neither too tight nor too slack, which means that businesses may face moderate competition for talent, but still have access to a sizable pool of potential employees. As a result, hiring in this market may require some effort, but it is not excessively challenging.

Wage Growth

The YoY wage growth rate in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is +4.50%, ranking above average at the 62nd percentile. This rate indicates moderate to fast wage growth, which may lead to rising labor costs for employers, but also contributes to strong worker purchasing power. As a result, businesses may need to budget for higher labor costs, but can also expect a skilled and productive workforce.

Cost of Living

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has a cost of living ratio of $537/sqft to $40.69/hr, with a PSF increase of +3.3% YoY, ranking at the 2nd percentile in terms of affordability. This low percentile rank indicates that the city is relatively expensive compared to its peers, which may make it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering higher salaries to compensate for the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is growing at a rate of +0.45% YoY, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This growth rate suggests that the city has a structural advantage in terms of hiring capacity, as the labor pool is increasing, providing businesses with a larger talent pool to draw from. As a result, companies looking to expand their workforce may find it easier to do so in this market.

Building Permits

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has seen a +15.42% YoY increase in residential building permits, indicating an expansion of housing supply. This increase suggests that developer confidence is high, and the future housing supply is likely to improve, which may lead to better affordability and workforce accommodation. As a result, businesses may expect a more stable and predictable housing market, which can support their growth plans.

Days on Market

The median days on market in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is currently 47 days, with a -4.1% YoY decrease, ranking at the 12th percentile. This low percentile rank indicates a relatively fast-paced market, where homes are selling quickly, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. As a result, businesses may need to consider offering relocation assistance or flexible housing options to support their employees.

Office Economy

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an office economy percentile rank of 56th, indicating a deep professional talent pool, suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses that require specialized office skills, but may be less suitable for companies with primarily industrial or logistics-dominant operations. As a result, businesses in the professional services sector may find it an attractive location, with access to a skilled and experienced workforce.

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area offers businesses a unique combination of above-average labor demand, labor force growth, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for companies in the professional services sector. However, the city's high cost of living and relatively fast-paced housing market may pose challenges for talent attraction and relocation, which businesses should carefully consider when making location decisions. Overall, the city's strengths make it a viable option for businesses looking to establish or expand operations, but its weaknesses require careful planning and consideration.
