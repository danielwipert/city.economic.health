Economic Analysis

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of C, ranking 39.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its below-average labor demand and high cost of living. The labor demand composite score of 4.80, which combines a +0.50% employment growth rate and a -0.202% weekly hours deviation from trend, signals a contraction in genuine demand expansion. The cost of living, with a PSF of $494/sqft and an earnings ratio of 12.08, ranks in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile, indicating a highly expensive environment.

Labor Demand

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an employment growth rate of +0.50% and a weekly hours deviation of -0.202% from its own trend, resulting in a labor demand composite score of 4.80. This combination signals a contraction in genuine demand expansion, as the hours worked are below trend despite some job growth. This suggests that the labor market is not as strong as the employment growth rate alone might indicate.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is 4.70%, ranking near the median at the 46th percentile. This indicates a relatively balanced labor market, neither too tight nor too slack. For a business trying to hire in this market, the moderate unemployment rate suggests that finding qualified candidates may be manageable, but may not be as easy as in areas with higher unemployment rates.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is +1.91%, ranking below average at the 22nd percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not as quickly as in other metros. For workers, this means that purchasing power is increasing, but not at a rapid pace.

Cost of Living

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has a cost of living ratio of 12.08, with a PSF of $494/sqft and earnings of $40.91/hr, ranking in the bottom tier at the 8th percentile. This indicates that the city is highly expensive relative to peers, making it challenging to attract talent without offering wage premiums. The fact that PSF is falling by -0.8% YoY slightly mitigates this, but the overall affordability remains a concern.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is growing at a rate of -0.15% year-over-year, ranking above average at the 64th percentile. Despite the negative growth rate, the labor force supply is still relatively stable, suggesting that hiring capacity is not severely constrained. However, the slight contraction in labor force growth may signal a potential structural headwind for future hiring.

Building Permits

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has seen a +27.93% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, ranking in the top tier at the 82nd percentile. This significant increase in permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which may improve affordability and accommodate a growing workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market in the New York Newark-Jersey City metro area is 56 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -3.5%. This relatively fast market, ranking below average at the 24th percentile, indicates that homes are selling quickly, making it competitive for relocating workers to find housing.

Office Economy

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has a deep professional talent pool, with an office/professional worker share of 2.92, ranking above average at the 65th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less ideal for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented workforces.

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area offers a business a highly skilled and professional workforce, with a growing housing supply that may improve affordability in the future. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for a decision-maker is the high cost of living, which may require significant wage premiums to attract and retain talent, potentially increasing labor costs and affecting the bottom line.
