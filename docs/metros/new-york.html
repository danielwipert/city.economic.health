Economic Analysis

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of C, with a score of 38.8 out of 100, indicating it performs worse than most of the 50 metros. The city's economy is currently defined by its slow job growth, with employment increasing by only 0.25% over the past year, and a high cost of living, with a price per square foot of $498 and hourly pay of $40.54.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs, but at a slow pace of 0.25% over the past year, and workers' hours are actually falling by 0.3%, indicating a weak labor market. This means that while there are some new jobs being created, the overall demand for labor is not strong, and employers may not be looking to hire as many new workers.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in the city is 4.50%, which is near the median of the 50 metros, with a score of 56 out of 100, indicating it is easier for employers to hire than in some other cities. However, this also means that the city's labor market is not particularly tight, and employers may not face as much pressure to raise wages to attract workers.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by 1.79% over the past year, which is a relatively slow pace, earning the city a score of 18 out of 100, and indicating that employers' labor costs are not increasing rapidly. This slow wage growth also means that workers' paychecks are not increasing as quickly as they might be in other cities, which could affect their purchasing power.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in the city is high, with a price per square foot of $498 and hourly pay of $40.54, earning a score of 6 out of 100, and indicating that housing is not very affordable for local workers. Although the price per square foot is falling by 1.4% over the past year, the city's cost of living remains a significant challenge for workers and employers alike.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in the city is actually shrinking, with a decline of 0.54% over the past year, and a score of 44 out of 100, indicating that the labor market may face some challenges in the future. This decline in the labor force means that employers may struggle to find the workers they need, which could limit the city's economic growth.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals increased by 20.67% over the past year, earning the city a score of 80 out of 100, and indicating that developers are confident in the city's future and are building more homes. This increase in building permits suggests that the city's housing supply may improve in the future, which could help make housing more affordable for workers.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in the city are taking 53 days to sell, which is a relatively slow pace, and the time to sell is actually decreasing by 1.9% over the past year, earning a score of 22 out of 100. This slow pace of home sales indicates a softening housing market, which could make it harder for workers to buy or sell homes and relocate to the city.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is near the median of the 50 metros, with a score of 54 out of 100, indicating that it has a sizable but not dominant tech, finance, and professional services sector. This means that the city is suited for a range of businesses, including those in the office and professional services sectors, but may not be as attractive to companies looking for a highly specialized workforce.

The city's biggest strength is its increasing housing supply, driven by a 20.67% increase in new home approvals over the past year, which could help make housing more affordable for workers in the future. However, the city's biggest risk is its high cost of living, which could continue to drive workers away and limit the city's economic growth, particularly if wages do not rise more quickly to keep pace with the cost of living.
