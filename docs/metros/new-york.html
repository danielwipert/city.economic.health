Economic Analysis

The New York Newark-Jersey City metro area has an overall grade of C, indicating a very poor performance compared to other metros, with a score of 38.8 out of 100. This city's economy is currently defined by slow job growth, with employment increasing by only 0.2% over the past year, and a relatively high cost of living, with a price per square foot of $498.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs, but at a slow pace of 0.2% over the past year, and workers' hours are actually falling by 0.3%, indicating a weak job market. This means that the demand for labor is not strong, and employers may not be eager to hire new workers or increase hours for existing ones.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in the city is 4.5%, which is about average compared to other metros, making it neither particularly easy nor hard for employers to hire. With a score of 56 out of 100, the city's unemployment rate is not a major concern, but it also does not provide a significant advantage for employers looking to hire.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Pay rose by only 1.8% over the past year, which is slower than most other metros, with a score of 18 out of 100. This slow wage growth means that employers may not face significant pressure to increase wages, but it also means that workers may not have much bargaining power or see significant increases in their paychecks.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in the city is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $498 and hourly pay of $40.54, and it is getting worse, with a score of 6 out of 100. This means that housing is less affordable for local workers compared to other metros, and the city may struggle to attract and retain talent due to its high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in the city is actually shrinking, with a decline of 0.5% over the past year, and a score of 44 out of 100. This means that the city may face challenges in hiring and filling open positions, as the labor force is not growing to meet demand.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals increased by 20.7% over the past year, with a score of 80 out of 100, indicating a strong and growing housing supply. This suggests that the city is investing in new housing and may see an increase in affordable options for residents in the future.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in the city are taking 53 days to sell, which is getting slower, with a decline of 1.9% over the past year, and a score of 22 out of 100. This indicates a weakening housing market, where buyers may be pulling back, and sellers may struggle to find buyers.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a relatively average-sized office-based workforce, with a score of 54 out of 100, making it suitable for a range of businesses, including tech, finance, and professional services. This means that the city has a deep talent pool in these areas, but may not stand out as a hub for these industries compared to other metros.

The city's biggest strength is its growing housing supply, with a significant increase in new home approvals over the past year, which could help address affordability concerns and attract new residents. However, the city's biggest risk is its high cost of living, which may deter businesses and workers from locating there, and could exacerbate existing challenges in hiring and retaining talent.
