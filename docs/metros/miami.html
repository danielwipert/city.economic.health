Economic Analysis

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking in the 50.5th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong wage growth and building permits expansion. The city's economic character is most defined by its 7.46% year-over-year wage growth and 20.81% increase in building permits, indicating a dynamic labor market and expanding housing supply. These metrics suggest a city with a growing economy, but also with some challenges in labor demand and cost of living.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is -0.30% year-over-year, combined with a -0.119% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, signaling a contraction in labor demand. This combination indicates a lack of genuine demand expansion, with hours below trend during a period of job losses, suggesting a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This metric ranks below average, in the 38th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 3.60%, ranking near the median in the 43rd percentile, indicating a relatively balanced labor market with neither significant tightness nor slack. This rate suggests that businesses may face moderate competition for talent, but without extreme wage pressure. The practical implication is that hiring may be manageable, but not exceptionally easy or hard.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 7.46%, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile, indicating fast and strong wage growth. This implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increased worker purchasing power, which can be beneficial for businesses relying on local consumer demand. The high wage growth rate suggests a competitive labor market where employers must offer attractive compensation packages.

Cost of Living

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has a cost of living score that ranks below average, in the 25th percentile, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $357/sqft to $34.29/hr, equal to 10.41. Although the PSF is falling by 1.1% year-over-year, the city remains relatively expensive compared to its peers. This means that attracting talent without offering wage premiums may be challenging, as the cost of living is a significant factor in relocation decisions.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is contracting at a rate of -0.93% year-over-year, indicating a shrinking labor pool. This contraction implies a structural headwind for hiring, as the supply of potential employees is decreasing. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to attract talent from a diminishing pool.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has increased by 20.81% year-over-year, signaling an expansion in housing supply. This growth suggests that future affordability may improve, and the city may become more attractive to relocating workers. The increase in building permits is a positive indicator for businesses concerned about workforce accommodation and housing costs.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 82 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 1.2%. This indicates a relatively fast-paced market, although the decrease in days on market is modest. For workers relocating to this city, the housing market may be somewhat competitive, but still accessible, with homes selling at a moderate pace.

Office Economy

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has an office economy score ranking above average, in the 70th percentile, indicating a deep talent pool in professional and office sectors. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions, but less ideal for industries with primarily industrial or logistics roles. The strong office economy is a significant advantage for businesses seeking specialized knowledge workers.

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth, expanding housing supply, and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain industries. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's relatively high cost of living and contracting labor force, which may require businesses to offer competitive compensation packages to attract and retain talent.
