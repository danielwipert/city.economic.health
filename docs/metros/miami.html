Economic Analysis

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 54.1th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its high wage growth rate of 7.68% year-over-year and its low cost of living percentile rank of 33, indicating a relatively expensive city. The combination of these metrics suggests a challenging environment for businesses looking to manage labor costs while attracting talent.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 0.35% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating 0.285% above the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours are increasing, indicating a growing need for labor. However, the near-median percentile rank of 40 suggests that this demand is not exceptionally strong compared to other cities.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 3.70%, ranking at the 66th percentile, which indicates a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight market also implies upward pressure on wages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 7.68%, ranking at the 96th percentile, indicating very fast wage growth. This rapid increase in wages suggests that employer labor costs are rising quickly, while worker purchasing power is also increasing. Businesses should be prepared for higher labor costs in this environment.

Cost of Living

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has a cost of living percentile rank of 33, indicating that it is relatively expensive compared to its peers, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $355/sqft to $35.24/hr, or 10.07. However, the PSF is falling by 0.8% year-over-year, which slightly mitigates the expense. This relatively high cost of living may make it challenging for businesses to attract talent without offering wage premiums.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is growing at a rate of 0.39% year-over-year, ranking at the 76th percentile, indicating an expanding workforce supply. This growth suggests that businesses will have a larger pool of potential workers to hire from, making it easier to find talent.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has decreased by 34.10% year-over-year, ranking at the 4th percentile, indicating a significant tightening of housing supply. This sharp decline in permits suggests that future affordability and workforce accommodation may be at risk, as the supply of new housing is not keeping pace with demand.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 85 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 4.5%, ranking at the 50th percentile. This indicates a relatively competitive market for workers relocating to the city, as homes are selling fairly quickly. However, the decrease in days on market suggests that the market may be heating up.

Office Economy

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has an office economy percentile rank of 67, indicating a deep talent pool in professional and office sectors. This city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but may be less suitable for industrial or logistics-dominant businesses.

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area offers businesses a growing labor force and a deep talent pool in professional sectors, but also presents challenges such as high labor costs and a relatively expensive cost of living. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tightening housing supply, which may impact future affordability and workforce accommodation, making it essential to factor this into long-term business plans.
