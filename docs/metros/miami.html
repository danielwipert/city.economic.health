Economic Analysis

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 42.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 7.02%, and its struggling labor demand, with a composite score of 4.52, ranking below average at the 34th percentile. The combination of these metrics suggests a complex economic environment.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 0.48% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.452% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a contraction in labor demand, as the modest job growth is not sufficient to offset the decline in working hours. This scenario may indicate a survivor squeeze, where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 3.90%, ranking near the median at the 46th percentile. This suggests a relatively balanced labor market with some slack, making it neither extremely tight nor overly loose. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate implies a manageable hiring environment, but not one with excessive wage pressure.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 7.02%, ranking in the top tier at the 94th percentile. This rapid wage growth indicates a strong labor market where workers have significant bargaining power, which can lead to rising labor costs for employers. However, it also means that workers have increasing purchasing power, which can benefit local businesses.

Cost of Living

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has a cost of living score ranking below average at the 29th percentile, with a PSF of $356/sqft and average hourly earnings of $34.68/hr, resulting in a ratio of 10.27. The PSF has decreased by 0.8% year-over-year, slightly improving affordability. However, the city remains relatively expensive compared to its peers, which may require businesses to offer wage premiums to attract talent.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has contracted by -0.14% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This slight decline in labor force supply may pose a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is not expanding. Businesses may need to adapt their recruitment strategies to attract talent from other areas.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has decreased by -30.46% year-over-year, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This sharp decline in building permits signals a tightening housing supply, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for housing in the future. This could pose a risk to workforce accommodation and attraction.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 85 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -3.4%. This relatively fast market, ranking below average at the 24th percentile, may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing, as homes are selling quickly. Businesses may need to provide relocation assistance or adjust their hiring timelines to accommodate the competitive housing market.

Office Economy

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has a professional and office worker share ranking above average at the 70th percentile, with a composite score of 3.10. This indicates a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. However, the city may be less suitable for businesses with primarily industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for knowledge-economy companies. However, the city's struggling labor demand, expensive cost of living, and tightening housing supply pose significant risks and constraints that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating this location for their business.
