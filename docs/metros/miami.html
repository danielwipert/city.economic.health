Economic Analysis

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area has earned an overall grade of A-, with a score of 65.2 out of 100, indicating it is a good location for businesses, with a strong job market and growing workforce. One of the key defining features of this city's economy is its fast wage growth, with hourly earnings rising by 8.8% over the past year, which is faster than most metros.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs, but at a slower pace than most metros, with employment growth of 0.4% over the past year, and workers' hours are not changing much, which suggests a relatively weak job market. This means that while there are some new opportunities, the city is not experiencing the kind of strong labor demand that would drive up wages and create a competitive hiring environment.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 3.2%, which is lower than most metros, making it a strength, and indicating that it may be harder for employers to find workers. With a score of 80, this low unemployment rate suggests that the job market is tight, which can drive up wages and make it more challenging for businesses to hire.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Hourly earnings in the city have risen by 8.8% over the past year, which is a strength, and faster than most metros, with a score of 96, indicating that workers are seeing significant increases in their paychecks. This fast wage growth is good for workers, but it also means that employers may face rising labor costs, which could impact their bottom line.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $358 and hourly pay of $35.70, which is less affordable than most metros, with a score of 35, and it's not getting better, as housing prices are not falling. This means that workers may need higher wages to afford the cost of living, which could be a challenge for employers.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in the city is growing at a rate of 0.9% over the past year, which is faster than most metros, with a score of 78, indicating a strong and expanding workforce. This growing labor force is a strength, as it provides businesses with a larger pool of potential employees to hire from.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in the city have increased by 11.3% over the past year, which is a strength, with a score of 66, indicating that developers are confident in the market and building more homes. This increase in building permits suggests that the city's housing supply is likely to expand in the future, which could help improve affordability.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

Homes in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach are taking 79 days to sell, which is a relatively healthy housing market, with a score of 86, and it's getting faster, with an 8.1% decrease in days on market over the past year. This suggests that the housing market is balanced, with neither buyers nor sellers having too much power, which is good for both homeowners and those looking to relocate to the area.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city has a larger office-based workforce than most metros, with a score of 66, indicating a strong presence of tech, finance, and professional services jobs. This makes Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach a good location for businesses in these sectors, as they can tap into a deep pool of skilled workers.

The biggest strength of the Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area is its fast wage growth, which is driving up workers' paychecks and making the city an attractive place to live and work. However, the biggest risk is the high cost of living, which could make it challenging for workers to afford the cost of living, and for employers to attract and retain talent without offering higher wages.
