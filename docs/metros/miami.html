Economic Analysis

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area has earned an overall grade of A-, with a score of 65.2 out of 100, indicating it is a good location for businesses, better than most of the 50 metros. What most defines this city's economy right now is its strong wage growth, with hourly earnings rising by 8.8% over the past year, and a low unemployment rate of 3.2%, which is better than 80% of the metros.

Labor Demand

Whether employers are adding jobs, based on job growth over the past year and changes in weekly work hours.

The city is adding jobs, albeit slowly, with employment growth of 0.4% over the past year, and workers' hours are steady, with no change from their 12-month average. This means the job market is stable, but not particularly robust, with a score of 40, indicating it is near the median of the 50 metros.

Unemployment

The share of people who want a job but can't find one; lower is better.

The unemployment rate of 3.2% makes it challenging for employers to hire, as it indicates a tight labor market, with a score of 80, better than 80% of the metros. This low unemployment rate suggests that employers may need to offer competitive wages to attract and retain workers.

Wage Growth

How much average hourly pay rose over the past year.

Hourly earnings in the city rose by 8.8% over the past year, which is a rapid pace, with a score of 96, indicating it is better than almost all of the 50 metros. This strong wage growth means that employers may face rising labor costs, but workers will have more purchasing power.

Cost of Living

How many hours a local worker needs to work to pay for one square foot of a home, and whether that's improving.

The cost of living in Miami is relatively high, with a price per square foot of $358 and hourly pay of $35.70, resulting in a score of 35, indicating it is below average. The cost of living is getting slightly more affordable, as the price per square foot has fallen by 0.6% over the past year.

Labor Force Growth

Whether the number of people working or looking for work is growing.

The pool of workers in the city is growing at a rate of 0.9% over the past year, with a score of 78, indicating it is above average. This growth in the labor force is a positive sign for hiring, as it suggests a steady supply of workers.

Building Permits

How many new homes builders got approval for, compared with a year ago.

New home approvals in the city have increased by 11.3% over the past year, with a score of 66, indicating it is above average. This rise in building permits suggests that the future housing supply will expand, which could improve affordability.

Days on Market

How long a typical home for sale takes to find a buyer; about 35 to 80 days is healthiest.

A typical home in the city takes 79 days to sell, and this time is getting shorter, with a decline of 8.1% over the past year, resulting in a score of 86, indicating it is better than most of the 50 metros. This relatively healthy housing market, with a score indicating it is top-tier, suggests that buyers have a reasonable amount of time to find a home.

Office Economy

The share of jobs in office-based fields like tech, finance and professional services.

The city's office-based workforce is sizable, with a score of 66, indicating it is above average, making it a good fit for businesses in the tech, finance, and professional services sectors. This strong office economy suggests that the city has a deep talent pool in these areas.

The bottom line is that Miami's biggest strength is its strong wage growth, which is driving up workers' purchasing power, but its biggest risk is its relatively high cost of living, which could make it challenging for employers to attract and retain workers. Overall, the city's economy is well-rounded, with a good balance of job growth, low unemployment, and a growing labor force, making it an attractive location for businesses.
