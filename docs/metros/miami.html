Economic Analysis

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 41.6th percentile out of 50 US metros. The city's economic character is most defined by its strong wage growth, with a year-over-year increase of 6.93%, and its struggling labor demand, with a composite score of 4.29 ranking below average at the 28th percentile. These metrics suggest a complex economic environment with both positive and negative trends.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 0.18% year-over-year, while weekly hours are deviating -0.262% from the city's own 12-month baseline. This combination signals a labor demand contraction, as the small employment growth is accompanied by a decrease in hours worked, indicating a lack of genuine demand expansion. This contraction may lead to a surplus of workers, making it easier for businesses to hire, but also potentially indicating a weaker local economy.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 3.90%, ranking near the median at the 46th percentile. This rate indicates a relatively balanced labor market with some slack, making it neither extremely tight nor extremely loose. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate means that finding qualified workers may be relatively manageable, but wage pressure may not be as high as in tighter labor markets.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 6.93%, ranking in the top tier at the 92nd percentile. This fast wage growth rate implies rising labor costs for employers, but also increasing worker purchasing power, which can be beneficial for local consumer demand. Businesses operating in this city should be prepared for higher labor costs, but may also benefit from a more affluent local workforce.

Cost of Living

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has a cost of living score ranking below average at the 31st percentile, with a PSF to earnings ratio of $356/sqft to $34.40/hr, or 10.35. Although the PSF is decreasing by 0.8% year-over-year, the city remains relatively expensive compared to its peers. This means that attracting talent without offering wage premiums may be challenging, as the high cost of living could offset the benefits of the city's strong wage growth.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is growing at a rate of -0.14% year-over-year, ranking near the median at the 40th percentile. This slight contraction in labor force supply may pose a structural headwind for hiring, as the pool of available workers is shrinking. Businesses should be aware of this trend and plan accordingly, potentially considering strategies to attract workers from other areas or invest in workforce development.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is -30.46%, ranking in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This sharp decline in permits signals a tightening housing supply, which may lead to decreased affordability and increased competition for housing in the future. This trend poses a risk to workforce accommodation and may make it more difficult for businesses to attract and retain talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 85 days, with a year-over-year decrease of -3.4%. This relatively fast pace of home sales indicates a competitive market, which may make it challenging for relocating workers to find housing. However, the decreasing trend in days on market may signal a normalization of the housing market, potentially leading to a more balanced and accessible environment for homebuyers in the future.

Office Economy

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has an office economy score ranking above average at the 76th percentile, with a share of professional and office workers indicating a deep talent pool. This city is well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which can leverage the available specialized workforce. However, it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented profiles.

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth and a deep professional talent pool, making it an attractive location for certain industries. However, the city's struggling labor demand, decreasing labor force supply, and tightening housing market pose significant risks and constraints that decision-makers should carefully consider when evaluating this location for their business operations.
