Economic Analysis

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking at the 53.9th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its high wage growth rate of 7.68% year-over-year and its low cost of living percentile rank of 33, indicating a relatively expensive market. The combination of these metrics suggests a city with strong labor cost pressures but limited affordability advantages.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 0.35% year-over-year, accompanied by a 0.285% deviation in weekly hours above its own trend. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours are increasing, indicating a growing need for labor. However, the near-median percentile rank of 40 suggests that this growth is not exceptionally strong compared to other metros.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in this metro area is 3.70%, ranking at the 66th percentile, which indicates a relatively tight labor market. This tightness implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The practical implication is that companies may experience upward pressure on wages and potentially longer recruitment times.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 7.68%, placing it in the top tier at the 96th percentile. This fast wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising rapidly, which can be beneficial for worker purchasing power but may strain business budgets. The high wage growth indicates a strong demand for labor, which can drive economic activity but also increases the cost of doing business.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living percentile rank of 33, Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is considered relatively expensive, especially given its PSF to earnings ratio of $355/sqft to $35.24/hr, equating to 10.07. Although the PSF is falling by 0.8% year-over-year, the city's affordability is still below average. This means that attracting talent without offering wage premiums may be challenging, as the high cost of living could offset the appeal of the area's other economic advantages.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in this metro area is growing at a rate of 0.39% year-over-year, ranking at the 76th percentile. This positive growth indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a favorable condition for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a larger pool of potential employees. The growing labor force can support business expansion and reduce the risk of labor shortages.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is -34.10%, placing it in the bottom tier at the 4th percentile. This significant decline signals that housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and challenges in accommodating a growing workforce. The sharp decrease in building permits is a concern for businesses reliant on a readily available and affordable housing stock for their employees.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 85 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 4.5%. This indicates a relatively fast-moving market, although the near-median percentile rank of 49 suggests it is not exceptionally competitive. For workers relocating to this city, the market is somewhat accessible, but the trend towards faster sales could indicate rising demand and potentially less favorable conditions for buyers in the future.

Office Economy

With an office and professional worker share percentile rank of 64, Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has a deep talent pool suited for businesses in tech, finance, consulting, and HQ operations. However, it may be less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles. The city's strong office economy is a significant advantage for businesses seeking specialized knowledge workers.

In conclusion, Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach offers businesses a unique combination of high wage growth and a tightening labor market, suggesting strong economic activity but also potential challenges in hiring and labor costs. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's relatively expensive cost of living and the sharp decline in building permits, which could exacerbate affordability issues and limit the availability of housing for a growing workforce.
