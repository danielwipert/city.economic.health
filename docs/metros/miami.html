Economic Analysis

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area has earned an overall grade of A- with a composite score ranking at the 65.2th percentile among 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its top-tier unemployment rate of 3.20% and exceptional wage growth of +8.76% year-over-year, indicating a highly competitive labor market. The combination of these metrics suggests a city with strong job demand and rising labor costs.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is +0.44% year-over-year, accompanied by a negligible deviation in weekly hours from its own trend at +0.000%. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, albeit at a moderate pace, as the city is adding jobs without overburdening its existing workforce. The labor demand composite score of 4.92 places it near the median, indicating a balanced labor market.

Unemployment

With an unemployment rate of 3.20%, Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach boasts a tight labor market, ranking in the top tier at the 80th percentile. This low unemployment rate implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring due to intense competition for a limited pool of available workers, potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. The practical implication for a business trying to hire here is the need for competitive compensation packages to attract top talent.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach stands at an impressive +8.76%, placing it in the top tier at the 96th percentile. This rapid wage growth signifies rising labor costs for employers but also enhances worker purchasing power, contributing to a robust local economy. As a result, businesses should anticipate higher labor expenses but also benefit from a workforce with increased disposable income.

Cost of Living

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has a cost of living score that ranks at the 35th percentile, indicating that it is below average in terms of affordability, with a PSF of $358/sqft and average hourly earnings of $35.70. Although the PSF has decreased by 0.6% year-over-year, the city remains relatively expensive compared to its peers. This means that attracting talent without offering wage premiums may be challenging, as the cost of living is a significant factor in relocation decisions.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is growing at a rate of +0.90% year-over-year, which is above average and ranks at the 78th percentile. This expansion in the labor force supply suggests that the city has a growing pool of potential workers, making it easier for businesses to find and hire new employees. The implication for hiring capacity is positive, with a structurally supportive labor market.

Building Permits

The city has seen a +11.27% year-over-year increase in residential building permits, indicating an expansion in future housing supply. This growth in permits signals developer confidence in the market and suggests that affordability may improve over time, supporting workforce accommodation and attraction. As housing supply expands, the city is likely to become more competitive in retaining and attracting talent.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 79 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 8.1%, placing it in the top tier at the 86th percentile. This relatively fast market means that homes are selling quickly, which can be challenging for relocating workers. However, the decreasing trend in days on market suggests a normalization of the housing market, potentially making it more accessible for new residents in the future.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share composite score of 2.88, Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has a deep talent pool that ranks above average at the 66th percentile. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors that rely on professional and office workers. Conversely, it may be less ideal for industries dominated by industrial or logistics roles.

In conclusion, Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach offers businesses a highly competitive labor market with strong job demand and rising labor costs, alongside a growing labor force and expanding housing supply. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the tight labor market and resulting upward pressure on wages, which may increase operational costs but also contribute to a vibrant local economy with high worker purchasing power.
