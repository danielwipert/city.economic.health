Economic Analysis

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area has an overall grade of B, ranking 50.7th percentile out of 50 US metros, with a composite score driven largely by its strong wage growth and building permits expansion. The city's economic character is most defined by its 7.46% year-over-year wage growth and 20.81% increase in building permits, indicating a mix of rising labor costs and expanding housing supply. These metrics suggest a city with a growing economy, but also with challenges in labor demand and cost of living.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is -0.30% year-over-year, combined with a -0.119% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, signaling a contraction in labor demand. This combination indicates a lack of genuine demand expansion, with hours below trend during a period of job losses, suggesting a survivor squeeze where remaining workers are absorbing the load of eliminated roles. This metric ranks below average, at the 38th percentile.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 3.60%, ranking near the median at the 44th percentile, indicating a relatively balanced labor market with neither significant tightness nor slack. For a business trying to hire in this city, the moderate unemployment rate suggests that finding talent may not be overly challenging, but it also does not indicate a large pool of readily available workers. This balance may lead to moderate wage pressure.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 7.46%, ranking at the 92nd percentile, indicating fast and strong wage growth. This rapid increase in wages suggests rising labor costs for employers, which could be a challenge for businesses looking to minimize expenses. However, it also means good news for worker purchasing power, as higher wages can lead to increased consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has a cost of living score that ranks below average, at the 24th percentile, with a PSF of $357/sqft and average hourly earnings of $34.29/hr, resulting in a ratio of 10.41. The city's PSF is decreasing by 1.1% year-over-year, which is a positive sign for affordability. However, the overall cost of living remains relatively high, making it less attractive for talent without wage premiums, as the high cost of housing relative to wages could deter some potential employees.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is growing at a rate of -0.93% year-over-year, indicating a contracting labor force supply. This negative growth rate suggests that the labor pool is shrinking, which could pose a structural headwind for hiring and business expansion in the area. Businesses may face challenges in finding sufficient talent to support their operations.

Building Permits

The number of building permits in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has increased by 20.81% year-over-year, ranking at the 84th percentile, indicating an expansion in housing supply. This significant increase in building permits signals developer confidence in the market and suggests that future housing supply will be more ample, potentially improving affordability and making it easier for workers to relocate to the area.

Days on Market

The current median days on market for homes in Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach is 82 days, with a year-over-year decrease of 1.2%, ranking below average at the 28th percentile. This relatively fast pace of home sales indicates a competitive market, which could make it challenging for relocating workers to find and secure housing. The decreasing days on market further suggests a hot market that may favor sellers over buyers.

Office Economy

Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach has an office economy score ranking above average, at the 70th percentile, with a share of jobs in professional and office sectors indicating a deep talent pool suited for tech, finance, consulting, and HQ decisions. This city is well-suited for businesses that rely on a knowledgeable and skilled workforce, particularly in professional services, but may be less ideal for industries that require large amounts of industrial or logistics space.

The Miami-Fort Lauderdale-West Palm Beach metro area offers businesses a unique combination of strong wage growth and expanding housing supply, making it an attractive location for companies looking to tap into a skilled and growing workforce. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's relatively high cost of living and contracting labor force supply, which could pose challenges in attracting and retaining talent without offering significant wage premiums.
