Economic Analysis

The Providence-Warwick metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 44.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its high labor force growth rate of +1.69% YoY and its low unemployment rate of 4.30%, which signals a tight labor market. The labor demand composite score of 5.47, near the median, also suggests a moderate level of job growth.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate of +0.60% YoY and weekly hours deviation of +0.326% from the city's own trend indicate a genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This combination signals that the city is experiencing a moderate increase in labor demand. The labor demand composite score of 5.47, ranking near the median at 54th percentile, further supports this assessment.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate of 4.30% is relatively low, ranking in the bottom tier at 18th percentile, which means the labor market is tight. This tight market implies that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as there are fewer available workers, and may need to offer higher wages to attract talent. As a result, businesses may experience upward pressure on wages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate of +3.09% is below average, ranking at 34th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace than in many other cities. While this may be beneficial for workers' purchasing power, it also means that employers may not face significant pressure to increase wages.

Cost of Living

With a cost of living ratio of $360/sqft to $36.41/hr, resulting in a ratio of 9.89, and a percentile rank of 2, the city is relatively expensive. The fact that PSF is increasing by +8.4% YoY further exacerbates the affordability issue. This means that the city may struggle to attract talent without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force is growing at a rate of +1.69% YoY, which is in the top tier at 92nd percentile. This expanding labor force supply is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it indicates a growing pool of potential workers. As a result, businesses may find it easier to fill open positions.

Building Permits

The year-over-year change in residential building permits is +18.42%, ranking above average at 68th percentile. This increase in building permits suggests that housing supply is expanding, which may lead to improved affordability and a more accommodating environment for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The current median days on market is 39 days, with a year-over-year increase of +8.3%, ranking in the top tier at 86th percentile. This slower market means that homes are sitting longer before being sold, making it a more accessible market for workers relocating to the city. However, this may also indicate a normalization of the market rather than a demand erosion.

Office Economy

With an office/professional worker share of 2.92 and a percentile rank of 64, the city has a deep enough professional talent pool to support businesses in the tech, finance, and consulting sectors. This makes the city well-suited for businesses that require specialized knowledge-economy talent, but less suited for those with primarily industrial or logistics-dominant workforces.

The Providence-Warwick metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market, moderate labor demand, and expanding labor force supply. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's relatively high cost of living, which may require wage premiums to attract and retain talent.
