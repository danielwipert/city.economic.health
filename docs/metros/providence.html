Economic Analysis

The Providence-Warwick metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 44th out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 4.30% and its high cost of living, with a PSF to wages ratio of 9.96, ranking it at the 0th percentile for affordability. The labor demand composite score of 5.37, near the median at the 56th percentile, also plays a significant role in shaping the local economy.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Providence-Warwick is +0.60% year-over-year, combined with a +0.226% deviation in weekly hours from its own trend, signaling a genuine demand expansion. This indicates that jobs are being added, and hours are running above trend, suggesting a healthy labor market. The near-median percentile rank of 56th for labor demand composite score reinforces this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Providence-Warwick is 4.30%, ranking it at the 22nd percentile, indicating a tight labor market with limited slack. This means that businesses trying to hire in this city may face challenges in finding available workers, leading to potential wage pressure. The low unemployment rate suggests that workers have more bargaining power, which could impact hiring strategies.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Providence-Warwick is +2.76%, ranking it at the 36th percentile, indicating moderate wage growth. This suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a pace that is not excessively high. Workers in this city are experiencing some improvement in their purchasing power, but it is not exceptionally strong.

Cost of Living

Providence-Warwick has a cost of living score ranking it at the 0th percentile, making it one of the most expensive cities relative to peers, with a PSF of $362/sqft and average hourly earnings of $36.34. This means that the city is at a significant disadvantage for talent attraction without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living. The 9.4% year-over-year increase in PSF further exacerbates the affordability issue.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Providence-Warwick is growing at a rate of +1.69% year-over-year, ranking it at the 92nd percentile, indicating a rapidly expanding workforce supply. This suggests that the city has a strong hiring capacity, with a growing pool of potential workers. Businesses looking to expand or relocate may find this growth beneficial for their staffing needs.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Providence-Warwick has increased by +5.95% year-over-year, ranking it at the 56th percentile, indicating a moderate expansion of housing supply. This growth suggests that the city is experiencing some relief in terms of future affordability and workforce accommodation, but it is not exceptionally strong.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Providence-Warwick is 39 days, with a year-over-year increase of +2.6%, ranking it at the 68th percentile. This indicates a relatively accessible market for workers relocating to the city, as homes are not selling excessively quickly. The rising days on market suggests a healthy normalization of the housing market.

Office Economy

Providence-Warwick has an office economy score ranking it at the 66th percentile, indicating a deep professional talent pool. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, or HQ sectors that require specialized knowledge workers. However, it may be less suitable for industries with more industrial or logistics-oriented economies.

The Providence-Warwick metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market, moderate wage growth, and a rapidly expanding workforce supply. However, the single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the city's extremely high cost of living, which could significantly impact talent attraction and retention strategies, requiring wage premiums to remain competitive.
