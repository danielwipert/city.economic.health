Economic Analysis

The Providence-Warwick metro area has an overall grade of B- with a composite score ranking it 44.7th percentile out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 4.30% and its high cost of living, with a PSF to wages ratio of 10.10, ranking it at the 0th percentile for affordability. The labor demand composite score of 5.37, near the median at the 56th percentile, also plays a significant role in shaping the local economy.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Providence-Warwick is +0.60% year-over-year, combined with a +0.226% deviation in weekly hours from its own 12-month baseline. This signals a genuine demand expansion, as both jobs and hours worked are increasing. The labor demand composite score of 5.37, near the median, further supports this interpretation.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Providence-Warwick is 4.30%, ranking it at the 28th percentile, indicating a relatively tight labor market. This means that businesses may face challenges in hiring, as the pool of available workers is smaller, and may need to offer competitive wages to attract talent. The tight market also implies upward pressure on wages.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth in Providence-Warwick is +2.76%, which is below average, ranking it at the 36th percentile. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but at a slower pace, and worker purchasing power is increasing, albeit not rapidly. This could impact businesses' decisions on staffing and compensation.

Cost of Living

Providence-Warwick has a cost of living score ranking it at the 0th percentile, with a PSF to wages ratio of 10.10, driven by a PSF of $367/sqft that has increased by 8.6% year-over-year, compared to hourly earnings of $36.34. This makes the city one of the more expensive places to live, which could hinder talent attraction without offering wage premiums to offset the high cost of living.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Providence-Warwick is growing at a rate of +1.69% year-over-year, ranking it at the 82nd percentile. This indicates that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire, as it suggests a larger pool of potential employees.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Providence-Warwick has increased by +5.95% year-over-year, ranking it near the median at the 56th percentile. This suggests that housing supply is expanding, which could improve affordability and accommodation for the workforce in the future.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Providence-Warwick is 38 days, with a year-over-year increase of +2.7%, ranking it at the 78th percentile. This indicates a slower market, which could make it more accessible for workers relocating to the city, as they have more time to find a home.

Office Economy

Providence-Warwick has an office economy score ranking it at the 66th percentile, with a share of professional and office workers indicating a deep talent pool. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, but less ideal for those in industrial or logistics-dominant economies.

The Providence-Warwick metro area offers businesses a growing labor force and an expanding housing supply, but its high cost of living and tight labor market pose significant challenges. The single biggest risk or constraint for decision-makers is the need to offer competitive wages to attract and retain talent in this expensive city, which could impact business profitability and growth plans.
