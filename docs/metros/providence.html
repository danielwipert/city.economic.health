Economic Analysis

The Providence-Warwick metro area has an overall grade of C+ with a composite score ranking it 42.5th out of 50 US metros. This city's economic character is most defined by its low unemployment rate of 4.30% and its high cost of living, with a PSF to wages ratio of 10.10. The labor demand composite score of 5.37, which is near the median, also plays a significant role in shaping the city's economic landscape.

Labor Demand

The employment growth rate in Providence-Warwick is +0.60% year-over-year, and weekly hours are deviating from the trend by +0.226%. This combination signals a genuine demand expansion, as hours are running above trend during a period of job growth. This indicates that the city is experiencing an increase in labor demand, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to expand.

Unemployment

The unemployment rate in Providence-Warwick is 4.30%, which is below average and corresponds to a percentile rank of 28th. This means that the labor market is relatively tight, making it harder for businesses to hire and potentially leading to upward pressure on wages. As a result, businesses may need to offer competitive salaries to attract top talent in this market.

Wage Growth

The year-over-year wage growth rate in Providence-Warwick is +2.76%, which is below average and ranks 36th in percentile terms. This moderate wage growth rate suggests that labor costs for employers are rising, but not excessively so. At the same time, workers in the city are seeing some improvement in their purchasing power, which could have a positive impact on local consumer demand.

Cost of Living

Providence-Warwick has a cost of living percentile rank of 0th, indicating that it is one of the most expensive cities in the country. With a PSF of $367/sqft and average hourly earnings of $36.34/hr, the city's high cost of living may deter some businesses from locating there, as they may need to offer wage premiums to attract talent. This could be a significant constraint for businesses considering relocation to this city.

Labor Force Growth

The civilian labor force in Providence-Warwick is growing at a rate of +1.69% year-over-year, which is a top-tier performance and ranks 82nd in percentile terms. This means that the workforce supply is expanding, which is a positive sign for businesses looking to hire and grow in the city. The increasing labor force can provide a larger pool of potential employees for businesses to tap into.

Building Permits

The number of residential building permits in Providence-Warwick has decreased by -8.13% year-over-year, which is below average and ranks 34th in percentile terms. This decline in building permits suggests that the housing supply is tightening, which could lead to future affordability issues and make it more challenging for workers to relocate to the city. This may be a concern for businesses that require a steady influx of new talent.

Days on Market

The median days on market for homes in Providence-Warwick is currently 38 days, with a year-over-year increase of +2.7%. This corresponds to a percentile rank of 78th, indicating a slower market. For workers relocating to the city, this means that they may have a more accessible and less competitive housing market, which could be a positive factor in their decision to move.

Office Economy

Providence-Warwick has an office economy percentile rank of 66th, indicating a deep professional talent pool. This makes the city well-suited for businesses in the tech, finance, consulting, and HQ sectors, which require a high concentration of specialized workers. However, the city may be less suitable for businesses in industrial or logistics-dominant sectors, which require a different type of workforce.

The Providence-Warwick metro area offers businesses a unique combination of a tight labor market and a growing labor force, which can provide a talented and dedicated workforce. However, the city's high cost of living and tightening housing supply are significant risks that businesses should factor into their decision-making process, as they may need to offer wage premiums and contend with affordability issues to attract and retain top talent.
