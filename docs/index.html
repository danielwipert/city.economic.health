Are Cities Adding Jobs?

We sort every metro by two things: whether it is adding or losing jobs, and whether workers' weekly hours are rising or falling compared with their usual level. Together they show whether hiring is healthy — or whether a city is cutting jobs while the remaining workers put in longer hours.

Strong: jobs and weekly hours are both rising.
Growing: jobs are rising while hours ease a little.
Squeeze: jobs are being cut while remaining workers work longer.
Weak: jobs and weekly hours are both falling.

How the Score Works

Eight measures of jobs, pay and housing, each weighted by how much it tells us about a city's economy.

8
Measures, One Grade
Labor demand, unemployment, wage growth, cost of living, labor force growth, building permits, days on market, and office economy — combined into one weighted score and a letter grade.
0–100
Scored Against the Other 49
Each measure is scored 0–100 by comparing a city with the other 49 metros. A score of 75 means the city does better than 75% of them on that measure, so one extreme city can't distort everyone else's score.
85 / 15
Jobs / Housing Split
85% of the grade comes from jobs, pay and affordability — the direct measure of a healthy job market. The other 15% comes from the housing market itself: how much is being built and how fast homes sell.
